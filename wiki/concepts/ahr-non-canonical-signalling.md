## Definition

AHR signalling that does not depend on the AHR–ARNT–XRE axis, including:
(i) direct AHR interaction with NF-κB subunits RelA/RelB to modulate inflammatory transcription;
(ii) AHR-induced SOCS2 to indirectly suppress TLR/NF-κB-dependent IL-6/IL-12/IL-23/TNF;
(iii) AHR as a substrate-specific subunit of a CUL4B E3 ubiquitin ligase that targets proteins for proteasomal degradation;
(iv) AHR-mediated c-Src sequestration controlling c-Src target phosphorylation (e.g., IDO1);
(v) ligand-independent AHR activation by shear and oxidative stress;
(vi) AHR co-activation of ESR / p300 on oestrogen-responsive elements.

## Intuition

Even when "off" transcriptionally, AHR is doing work: holding c-Src in reserve, acting as an E3 ligase, talking to NF-κB. Several of AHR's anti-inflammatory effects in macrophages and DCs go through this branch (KLF6, SOCS2) rather than through CYP1A1 induction.

## When to use

- When tissue-specific AHR effects don't track with classical CYP1A1 expression
- When designing AHR-targeted drugs aiming for E3-ligase or transcription-factor-crosstalk pharmacology rather than canonical agonism
- When interpreting AHR–HIF / AHR–NF-κB context-dependent immune outputs

## Known limitations

- Less well-mapped than canonical signalling — substrates of AHR-CUL4B remain incompletely catalogued
- Many "non-canonical" effects are mixed with canonical components in vivo

## Open problems

- Physiological relevance of ligand-independent activation under shear/oxidative stress
- Whether SAHRMs can selectively engage non-canonical over canonical branches
