## Definition

The suppression of de novo purine nucleotide biosynthesis (PPAT, MTHFD1/2, PAICS, ATIC pathway) in tumors exposed to systemic hypoxia, with a compensatory but insufficient increase in purine salvage, leading to tumor-wide depletion of adenine, adenosine, AMP and higher nucleotides and contributing to slowed tumor growth.

## Intuition

Building a purine ring de novo is energetically costly (≈4 ATP + 1 GTP per AMP). Under systemic hypoxia, tumors downregulate this pathway (associated with reduced Myc activity) and lean on salvage, but salvage cannot fully compensate in the poorly-vascularized in vivo environment, so nucleotide pools fall.

## Formal notation

De novo flux traced by gamma-15N-glutamine label into IMP/AMP; salvage traced by 8-13C-adenine label into AMP. Hypoxia ↓15N-IMP/AMP, ↑13C-AMP fraction.

## Variants

In vitro vs in vivo suppression; lineage-specific evasion (ccRCC lines maintain biosynthetic gene expression).

## Comparison

Distinct from aspartate-limitation models of hypoxic nucleotide deficit (aspartate not depleted in vivo here). Relates to in-vivo-specific purine dependence shown by CRISPR essentiality screens.

## When to use

When reasoning about nucleotide-metabolism vulnerabilities of tumors under low oxygen, and antifolate / purine-synthesis-targeting combinations.

## Known limitations

Causal role of Myc not yet established; correlational fitness associations from a single multiplexed assay.

## Open problems

Is Myc downregulation the driver? Can pharmacological de novo purine blockade phenocopy systemic hypoxia?
