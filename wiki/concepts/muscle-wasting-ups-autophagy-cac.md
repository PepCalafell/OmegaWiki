## Definition

The net loss of skeletal muscle in cancer-associated cachexia (CAC) arises from imbalance between protein synthesis and degradation, with contributions from the ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) — markers MURF1 (TRIM63) and Atrogin-1 (FBXO32) — autophagy-lysosomal flux (Beclin-1, MAP1LC3B), apoptosis (caspases 8/9 at late stages), and a uniquely human-prominent BLUNTED postprandial protein-synthesis response (anabolic resistance). Human evidence is more conflicting than mouse-model literature suggests, driven by definition heterogeneity across cohorts.

## Intuition

Mouse cachexia is dominated by UPS-driven proteolysis (clean MURF1/Atrogin-1 upregulation). Human cachexia is messier: UPS markers are sometimes elevated, sometimes not, depending on cancer type, weight-loss cutoff, and disease stage. What appears MORE consistent in humans is that the muscle CANNOT mount a normal anabolic response to feeding — postprandial protein synthesis is blunted in CRC/PDAC CAC — explaining why nutritional support alone fails.

## Formal notation

- UPS markers: MURF1 (TRIM63), Atrogin-1 (FBXO32), ubiquitinated-protein abundance, 26S proteasome proteolytic activity.
- Autophagy markers: Beclin-1, MAP1LC3B (LC3-II/LC3-I ratio) — note these are NOT flux measurements.
- Apoptosis markers: caspase-8, caspase-9, apoptotic myonuclei.
- Anabolic readouts: fed-vs-fasted myofibrillar fractional synthesis rate (FSR) from stable-isotope incorporation.

## Variants

- Early CAC: synthesis higher than controls but still < breakdown (MacDonald — net catabolism by quantitative balance).
- Advanced CAC (>15% weight loss): apoptosis becomes apparent.
- Sarcopenic obesity: muscle loss masked by stable weight.

## Comparison

vs starvation muscle loss: starvation triggers UPS predictably; CAC shows variable UPS and persistent anabolic resistance to feeding.

vs disuse atrophy: disuse is reversed by activity; CAC is only partially reversed by exercise + nutrition combined.

## When to use

When discussing the mechanism of muscle loss in CAC, when designing nutrition + anabolic-agent trials, or when evaluating UPS-targeted candidates (proteasome partial inhibitors, MURF1/Atrogin-1 antisense).

## Known limitations

- Beclin-1 / MAP1LC3B as autophagy proxies are POOR — they reflect autophagosome content, not flux. Most human studies do not measure flux.
- Cohort heterogeneity (cancer type, weight-loss cutoff, disease stage) dominates discrepancies.
- Most studies are cross-sectional snapshots; longitudinal change is rare.

## Open problems

- What is the relative contribution of UPS vs autophagy vs apoptosis vs anabolic resistance to net muscle loss across CAC stages and subtypes?
- Can anabolic resistance be overcome by combining feeding + exercise + drug (e.g., anabolic steroids, SARMs, anti-myostatin)?
- Do molecular subtypes (e.g., one-carbon-metabolism activation per [[papers/multi-omics-profiling-cachexia-targeted-tissues]]) stratify which mechanism dominates in a given patient?

## My understanding

Anabolic resistance to feeding is the most therapeutically actionable insight: it rationalizes the failure of nutrition-only trials and motivates combination strategies. The UPS/autophagy literature in humans is messier than meta-analyses suggest, and definition unification (TRACERx-style thresholds) is a precondition for resolving the conflicts.
