## Definition

AHR activation upregulates TIPARP (PARP7), which ADP-ribosylates TBK1 and suppresses type I interferon production. Many viruses (vesicular stomatitis virus, influenza, HSV-1, ZIKV, dengue, SARS-CoV-2) exploit this axis to dampen antiviral innate immunity. Pharmacologic AHR inhibition (CH-223191, HP163) reactivates type I IFN responses, suppresses viral replication, and in murine ZIKV congenital syndrome models reduces intrauterine growth retardation and microcephaly.

## Intuition

AHR's natural job is to brake type I IFN to prevent immunopathology — but viruses have learned to push this brake to evade detection. AHR antagonists release the brake selectively, enhancing antiviral responses without the toxicity of systemic type I IFN therapy.

## When to use

- Considering AHR antagonists as broad-spectrum antivirals
- Interpreting AHR upregulation in SARS-CoV-2 lung tissue and ZIKV-infected cells
- Designing PARP7 inhibitors for viral and oncologic indications

## Known limitations

- Sustained AHR inhibition risks compromising barrier-tissue homeostasis (loss of IL-22 / antimicrobial peptides)
- Translation to human antiviral therapy still preclinical

## Open problems

- Whether AHR antagonism plus existing antivirals (e.g., nirmatrelvir) gives synergistic effects in SARS-CoV-2
- Whether TIPARP-independent AHR antiviral mechanisms exist
