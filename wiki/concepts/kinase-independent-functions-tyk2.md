## Definition

A subset of TYK2-mediated regulatory effects on the transcriptome and chromatin of homeostatic immune cells that **persist** in a catalytically-inactive TYK2 (kinase-dead K923E) mutant while other effects are lost — implying TYK2 has scaffolding / non-catalytic regulatory functions beyond phosphotransfer.

## Intuition

JAK kinases are usually treated as pure catalytic enzymes — drug them and the pathway dies. Comparing full Tyk2-KO to Tyk2-inact (kinase-dead) shows many shared effects (catalytic loss is dominant) but a meaningful subset of effects requires the protein to be absent, not merely catalytically silenced. That subset reflects scaffold / interaction-dependent activity.

## Variants

- Catalytic-dependent TYK2: most ISG and IL-12-regulated genes.
- Catalytic-independent TYK2: a subset of IL-12-regulated genes and chromatin-accessibility regions are altered in Tyk2-KO but not Tyk2-inact.

## When to use

- Interpreting TYK2 inhibitor (e.g. deucravacitinib) data — kinase inhibition will not phenocopy full TYK2 loss.
- Designing experiments that distinguish JAK catalytic vs structural roles.

## Known limitations

- The molecular basis (which scaffolded interactions matter) is not characterized.
- Effects are observed in mouse spleen; relevance to human TYK2 deficiency / allosteric inhibitor responses is open.

## Open problems

- Identify TYK2-interacting receptor/signaling complexes that depend on TYK2 protein presence but not its catalytic activity.
- Predict which clinical phenotypes of TYK2 inhibition vs TYK2 loss-of-function variants will diverge.
