## Definition

The asparagine-642 → histidine substitution in STAT5B that stabilizes phosphorylated STAT5 dimers and drives T-cell leukemia/lymphoma. Beyond hyperactivation, the mutant acts as a **neomorphic** TF: its target-gene set is qualitatively different from wild-type STAT5 (very low correlation with STAT5-WT IL-2 response, low correlation with STAT5-KO loss of function) and is enriched for AURORA kinase, cell-cycle, E2F4, FOXM1 signatures plus chromatin opening at T-cell effector loci (Gzmk/Gzmb, Klrc1/Klre1, Mki67).

## Intuition

The convenient framing is "STAT5BN642H is just constitutively active STAT5". The data say otherwise: it is closer to a **different** transcription factor than to either STAT5-WT or STAT5-loss. The mutation rewires both protein-protein interactions and chromatin binding to a cancer-program-promoting target set.

## Variants

- **Germline / somatic** N642H in STAT5B — driver of human T-cell leukemia/lymphoma; mouse Stat5b-N642H knock-in recapitulates target rewiring.
- Related neomorphic STAT mutations may exist in other STAT family members.

## When to use

- Interpreting STAT5BN642H-driven tumours: target genes are **not** simply WT-STAT5 targets up-tuned.
- Designing therapies: blocking phospho-STAT5 binding alone may miss the rewired interaction surfaces.
- Cross-reading with the Chung 2025 [[atlas-guided-discovery-transcription-factors-cell]] framework: STAT5BN642H is a TEXterm-like "wire-changer" TF.

## Known limitations

- Mechanistic basis of the target rewiring (DNA-binding preference shift? protein partner change?) is not fully resolved.
- Mouse model results may not capture all features of human T-cell leukemia heterogeneity.

## Open problems

- Identify the protein-interaction partners that distinguish STAT5BN642H from STAT5-WT.
- Test whether N642H-specific chromatin opening at effector loci is reversible by JAK inhibitors.
- Cross-link neomorphic STAT5BN642H chromatin signature to TEXterm chromatin atlases.

## Key papers

- [[jak-stat-signaling-maintains-homeostasis-cells]] — Fortelny et al. 2024: epigenome+transcriptome evidence that STAT5BN642H targets qualitatively differ from WT STAT5 and STAT5 KO.
