## Definition

Mitochondrial fatty-acid β-oxidation (FAO), gated by CPT1-mediated FA transport into mitochondria and energetically coupled to AMPK, supports central-memory CD8⁺ T cells, tissue-resident memory T cells, Treg function, germinal-centre B cells, neutrophil and DC homeostasis. CPT1 blockade in macrophages promotes CD36-driven LDL uptake and atherosclerotic lipid accumulation.

## Intuition

FAO is the "slow burn" metabolic mode that immune cells adopt for long-lived, low-throughput functions: surveillance, tolerance, memory. AMPK senses energy depletion and licenses FAO; switching toward FAO is therefore a marker of memory/regulatory fate.

## Variants

- CPT1A-dependent FAO (most immune cells)
- FABP4/5-mediated lipid uptake (TRM, Treg)
- Efferocytosis-fuelled FAO (corpse-derived lipid → PPARδ; see [[concepts/efferocytosis-metabolic-rewiring-fao]])

## Comparison

Counterpoint to [[concepts/de-novo-lipogenesis-effector-immune-cells]] — FAO sustains long-lived/regulatory states, lipogenesis sustains effector/proliferative states. CAR-T metabolic conditioning ([[concepts/car-t-metabolic-conditioning-persistence]]) is partly an effort to bias toward FAO/memory.

## When to use

For CAR-T persistence engineering, vaccine memory formation, Treg-targeted autoimmunity therapies, and atherosclerosis macrophage biology.

## Known limitations

- The "memory ≡ FAO" framing has been challenged (etomoxir off-target effects; some memory subsets rely on glucose).
- Dose-dependent CPT1 inhibition outcomes in macrophages (atherosclerosis worsening) complicate FAO-targeted drug strategies.

## Open problems

- CPT1A vs CPT1B isoform-specific immune effects.
- Whether AMPK agonists (metformin) act via FAO or independent pathways for ICI synergy.
