## Definition

The tumorigenic effect of systemic hypoxia is both **tissue-specific** and **time-sensitive**. Hypoxia acts most potently on oxygen-sensing cells that are developmentally immature and not yet innervated — the adrenal medulla and sympathetic paraganglia from birth until ~day 7 of life. Hypoxia present since birth drives sympathetic (catecholamine-secreting) PPGL, whereas hypoxia of later (childhood/adult) onset is associated instead with parasympathetic carotid-body tumors.

## Intuition

Before splanchnic innervation, immature chromaffin cells autonomously sense oxygen and co-express EPAS1 and COX4I2; this is exactly the window when chronic hypoxia can rewire them toward tumorigenesis. After maturation these genes are downregulated and the window closes — EPAS1-mutant tumors reactivate this fetal-like program.

## Variants

- Congenital (since-birth) hypoxia → sympathetic PPGL (85%)
- Childhood/adult-onset hypoxia (e.g. Eisenmenger syndrome) → parasympathetic carotid-body tumors (64%)

## When to use

Use to explain tissue-of-origin and aggressiveness differences across PPGL subtypes by hypoxia onset timing, and to motivate early normoxia restoration (heart surgery) as a protective intervention.

## Known limitations

The "window" is inferred from clinical cohort correlations and developmental scRNA-seq, not from prospective developmental perturbation in humans.

## Open problems

- Precise molecular gating of the window's closure at chromaffin maturation
- Whether early surgical normoxia restoration causally lowers tumor risk
