## Definition

Predictive biomarkers to guide STING-pathway-directed therapy selection: (a) tumor cGAS/STING protein expression (IHC), (b) ENPP1 and TREX1 expression as negative-regulator load, (c) genomic alterations driving STING silencing — MYC overexpression (TNBC), STK11/LKB1 inactivation (KRAS-mutant NSCLC), IDH1 mutation (low-grade glioma), MTAP/CDKN2A co-deletion on chromosome 9p, (d) ISG signature RNA panels.

## Intuition

The single biggest gap in the cGAS-STING clinical-development field — no currently active trial pre-selects patients by intact tumor STING expression. The implication: most "STING agonist failed" trials may have been treating STING-silenced tumors, which are inherently unresponsive without epigenetic priming.

## Variants

- cGAS/STING IHC (Cleveland Clinic and DFCI panels) — most direct, requires standardization
- MTAP loss + 9p IFN gene co-deletion: confers STING-agonist resistance through IRF3 destabilization
- STK11 loss in KRAS-mutant NSCLC: predicts EZH2/DNMT1-driven STING silencing; rationale for derepression-first strategies
- IDH1 mutation: hypermethylates cGAS promoter; mutant IDH1 inhibitors restore dsDNA sensing
- MYC-high TNBC: epigenetic STING repression via DNMT1 induction

## When to use

In clinical trial design for any STING-pathway-directed agent — patient enrichment for intact tumor cGAS/STING expression (or, conversely, enrichment for STING-silenced tumors + epigenetic priming arm) should be the default rather than the exception.

## Open problems

- IHC scoring thresholds and reproducibility for cGAS/STING staining
- Composite biomarker panels (genomic + protein + ISG signature) vs single markers
- Validation in retrospective cohorts of completed Phase 1/2 STING-agonist trials

## My understanding

The biomarker absence is the single biggest reform needed in STING clinical development. Until trials enrich for cGAS/STING-intact tumors (or pair therapy with appropriate priming), the field will keep accumulating "STING agonist failed" data points that conflate biology with patient selection.
