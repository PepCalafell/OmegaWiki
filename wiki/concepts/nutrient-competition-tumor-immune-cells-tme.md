## Definition

Coordinated depletion, by tumour cells and stromal/myeloid neighbours, of essential nutrients within the TME — glucose, glutamine, arginine, methionine, asparagine, tryptophan — that starves infiltrating immune cells of substrates needed for activation, proliferation, and effector function.

## Intuition

The TME behaves as a metabolic battleground: cancer cells, cancer-associated fibroblasts and myeloid cells are the major consumers, leaving T cells and NK cells in a state of nutrient stress that engages stress-response pathways (NRF2, ATF4, IRE1α-XBP1) and inhibits TCR signalling.

## Formal notation

Per-nutrient gradient: [nutrient]TME ≪ [nutrient]plasma; uptake_immune ≫ uptake_supply ⇒ functional deficit.

## Variants

- Glucose competition (CAFs, myeloid > T cells).
- Glutamine competition via [[foundations/slc1a5-asct2-glutamine-transporter|SLC1A5]].
- Methionine competition via [[foundations/slc43a2-lat4-methionine-transporter|SLC43A2]].
- Asparagine restriction and tryptophan depletion (IDO1).
- Arginine depletion by MDSC ARG1.

## Comparison

- Complements but is distinct from lactate-mediated acidosis (see [[concepts/lactate-acidification-tme-immunosuppression]]).

## When to use

- Framing biomarker selection for nutrient-restoration combinations with ICB.

## Known limitations

- Direct in-vivo measurement of intratumoural nutrient concentrations is technically challenging.

## Open problems

- Localised nutrient restoration (e.g. intratumoural methionine, glutamine, arginine-producing bacteria).
- Identifying the dominant scarcity axis per tumour entity.
