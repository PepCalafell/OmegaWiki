## Definition

Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P), produced by sphingosine kinases (Sphk1/2), signals through GPCRs S1PR1–5 to control lymphocyte egress from lymphoid organs, neutrophil bone-marrow trafficking, macrophage polarization (M1→M2), and mast-cell degranulation. Downstream the receptors engage PI3K/AKT and MAPK/ERK pathways.

## Intuition

S1P is a "gradient compass" — high in blood and lymph, low in tissues — that immune cells follow via S1PR1 to leave tissues. Beyond trafficking, S1P also rewires macrophage inflammatory tone and is a survival signal secreted by Sphk1 from efferocytosing macrophages.

## Variants

- S1PR1 — T/B lymphocyte egress, neutrophil chemotaxis
- S1PR2 — peritoneal macrophage anti-inflammatory tone, neutrophil bone-marrow remodelling
- S1PR3–5 — DC and NK trafficking (less central)

## Comparison

Distinct from cytokine-driven trafficking (CCL/CXCL); S1P is a small-molecule lipid gradient. Fingolimod sequesters lymphocytes by inducing S1PR1 internalization.

## When to use

Whenever modelling lymphocyte egress, neutrophil bone-marrow release, atherosclerosis macrophage polarization, or mast-cell allergy biology; also relevant to multiple sclerosis therapeutics (fingolimod-class drugs).

## Known limitations

- Receptor-isoform specificity often inferred from KO phenotypes; agonist selectivity (SEW2871) limited.
- Most macrophage-polarization data from LDLR⁻/⁻ peritoneal models — generalization beyond atherosclerosis is incomplete.

## Open problems

- Whether S1P-S1PR2 macrophage tuning is exploitable for tumour-associated macrophage repolarization.
- Tissue-by-tissue S1P concentration mapping.
