## Definition

Efferoptosis is the TNF-driven conversion of homeostatic, anti-inflammatory efferocytosis (engulfment of dying cells) into a lytic, caspase-8- and gasdermin-D-dependent pyroptosis of the engulfing phagocyte, accompanied by inflammasome-independent IL-1β maturation. The term was coined by Muendlein et al. (2025).

## Intuition

Normally a macrophage that eats a dying neutrophil stays quiet and resolves inflammation. Add enough TNF — as in sepsis/SIRS — and that same act of eating becomes a death sentence for the macrophage: efferocytosis now licenses caspase-8 to drill GSDMD pores and mature IL-1β, amplifying inflammation rather than resolving it.

## Variants

Mechanistically related to caspase-8-mediated pyroptosis seen with TAK1 inhibition or *Yersinia* infection, but distinguished by (i) being initiated by phosphatidylserine recognition / efferocytosis and (ii) being GSDME-independent.

## Comparison

Versus homeostatic [[concepts/efferocytosis-anti-inflammatory-clearance]]: same engulfment trigger, opposite output (lytic death + IL-1β vs silent clearance). Versus canonical inflammasome pyroptosis: independent of NLRP3 and caspase-1.

## When to use

Use as the organizing concept when discussing how dying-cell clearance becomes pathogenic under high TNF (sepsis, SIRS, TNF-mediated inflammatory disease) and as a rationale for targeting PS receptors therapeutically.

## Known limitations

Demonstrated with bolus TNF and high-dose *E. coli* models; relevance to chronic/physiological inflammation remains to be shown.

## Open problems

Whether efferoptosis occurs in human disease in vivo; which efferocytic cell types beyond splenic macrophages are susceptible.

## My understanding

A clean conceptual inversion: efferocytosis as a context-dependent liability. The PS-receptor → PLCγ → IL-1β arm and the TAK1-loss → caspase-8 → GSDMD death arm are elegantly separable.
