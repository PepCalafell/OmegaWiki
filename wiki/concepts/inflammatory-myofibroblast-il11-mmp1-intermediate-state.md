## Definition

The F6 inflammatory myofibroblast is a disease-specific human fibroblast state expressing a myofibroblast core (ACTA2, COL/ECM, LRRC15, SFRP4) plus immune-related genes — interleukins (IL11, IL24), chemokines (CXCL5, CXCL8, CXCL13, CCL11), MMP1, and IL7R — with elevated JAK-STAT and hypoxic signaling. It characterizes early human skin wounds, inflammatory diseases with scarring risk, and cancer, and is predicted to recruit neutrophils, monocytes and B cells.

## Intuition

F6 is a fibroblast that is simultaneously becoming a myofibroblast and acting like an inflammatory hub — remodeling matrix (MMP1) while broadcasting chemokines to pull in immune cells. It appears transiently as an intermediate before terminal F7 myofibroblasts.

## Formal notation

Markers: IL11, IL24, MMP1, MMP3, CXCL5, CXCL8, CXCL13, CCL11, IL7R; iCAF-like (MMP1, MMP3, CXCL8, IL24). Receptor-ligand: CXCL5/6/8-CXCR2 (neutrophils), CCL5/26-CCR1 + CSF3-CSF3R (monocytes/macrophages), CXCL13-CXCR5 (B cells).

## Variants

Maps to inflammatory CAFs (iCAFs) in cancer; equivalent inflammatory myofibroblasts described in IBD.

## Comparison

Contrasts with F7 terminal myofibroblasts (ECM/TGFβ/PIEZO2-high, immune-low) and F8 fascia-like myofibroblasts.

## When to use

When identifying the inflammatory, immune-recruiting stromal state in wounds, scarring-risk inflammation, or tumor stroma across tissues.

## Known limitations

Immune-recruitment role inferred from receptor-ligand analysis; intermediate status inferred from trajectory + wound time-course, not lineage tracing.

## Open problems

Whether blocking F6 (or its chemokine output) reduces scarring or pathological immune infiltration.
