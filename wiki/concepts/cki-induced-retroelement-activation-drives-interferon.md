## Definition

The off-target phenomenon whereby certain clinical kinase inhibitors derepress transposable elements (especially ERVs/LTRs), generating cytoplasmic double-stranded RNA that triggers the TBK1–IRF3 axis and upregulates Ifnb1 and downstream interferon-stimulated genes — i.e., drug-induced viral mimicry as an unintended consequence of kinase inhibition.

## Intuition

A kinase inhibitor meant to dampen signaling can, paradoxically, switch on the interferon response by unmasking endogenous retroelements that the cell reads as a viral infection.

## Formal notation

- Seven CKIs that upregulated Ifnb1 (Omipalisib, BGT-226, Mubritinib, Lapatinib, IMD-0354, Lenvatinib, Tofacitinib) induced numerous TEs, particularly ERVs, in mouse macrophages.
- Mechanistic parallel: Trametinib (MEK1/2i) induces retroelements and IFNB1 in pancreatic cancer (Cortesi et al. 2024).

## Variants

- DNA-sensing viral mimicry via cGAS–STING (parallel to RNA-sensing route).
- IRF3-bound CRE hyperactivation (BGT-226, Lapatinib, Tofacitinib) as a related route to Ifnb1 upregulation.

## Comparison

Distinct from canonical IFN induction by the intended pathway: here interferon is an emergent off-target effect of the inhibitor.

## When to use

When interpreting unexpected pro-inflammatory/interferon phenotypes of kinase inhibitors, or considering repurposing for interferon-driven contexts.

## Known limitations

- Analysis does not pinpoint the specific repeat elements or the causal molecular mediators.
- TE quantification is mapping-ambiguous.

## Open problems

- Which off-target kinase(s) cause the derepression for each implicated CKI.
- Clinical relevance of drug-induced viral mimicry for efficacy and toxicity.

A striking demonstration that the epigenome-based screen can surface unexpected mechanistic off-target effects (viral mimicry) that a binding assay would never reveal.
