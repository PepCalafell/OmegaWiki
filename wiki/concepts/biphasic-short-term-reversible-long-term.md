## Definition

The observation that BCG vaccination produces two temporally distinct classes of DNA-methylation change: transient short-term changes at day 14 that largely revert by day 90, and lasting long-term changes at day 90 that constitute durable epigenetic memory — including a "late" reprogramming that develops after day 14.

## Intuition

Methylation does not simply move once and stay. An acute phase (T14) shows changes enriched in active-transcription states that then recover, mirroring the acute/resting stages of trained-immunity induction; a later phase (T90) shows new changes — often opposite in direction to T14 — that persist and map to infection/immune pathways.

## Formal notation

Defined over three contrasts: T14–T0 (acute), T90–T14 (recovery/"late"), T90–T0 (net long-term). Eight of 11 top CpGs reverse direction between T14–T0 and T90–T0; a majority of increased T90 CpGs first dipped at T14.

## Variants

- Acute/active-transcription demethylation (T14)
- "Late" reprogramming: decreased hypermethylation + increased hypomethylation (opposing aging)

## Comparison

Parallels the "immediate transcriptional vs late epigenetic" distinction; the late methylation effect runs counter to the hypermethylation trend of natural aging.

## When to use

When modeling the time course of epigenetic memory formation after a single training stimulus.

## Known limitations

Most temporal CpGs only reach suggestive significance (P < 1×10⁻⁵); no non-vaccinated control to exclude time/seasonal drift.

## Open problems

What molecular machinery drives the delayed "late" demethylation, and whether it is required for memory.

## My understanding

A two-wave model of vaccine-induced methylation: a reversible acute wave and a delayed durable wave that carries the memory signal.
