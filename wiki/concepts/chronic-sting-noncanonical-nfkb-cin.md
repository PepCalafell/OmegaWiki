## Definition

In chromosomally unstable (CIN-high) cancer cells, persistent cytosolic dsDNA from micronuclei biases STING signaling away from canonical TBK1-IRF3-IFN and toward STING-dependent, TBK1-independent non-canonical NF-κB → IL-6 → autocrine STAT3. The result is pro-tumorigenic instead of antitumor.

## Intuition

Same upstream signal (cytosolic DNA), opposite functional outcome. The C-terminal-tail (CTT) modular motifs of STING can route to either IRF3 or NF-κB; under chronic activation, cells "rewire" to the latter to escape antiproliferative IFN consequences while still benefiting from inflammatory cytokines that fuel growth and metastasis.

## Variants

- Bakhoum 2018 Nature: STING-NF-κB drives tumor cell-autonomous metastasis in CIN-high cancers
- Hong 2022 Nature: cGAS-STING-IL-6-STAT3 supports survival of chromosomally unstable cancers; tocilizumab + STING activation impair TNBC growth
- Li 2023 Nature: chronic CIN-cGAS-STING dampens type I IFN response and promotes ER stress to favor metastasis

## When to use

When interpreting TNBC, CIN-high colorectal, or chromosomally unstable tumor data — IL-6 and STAT3 readouts may dominate over IFN/ISG readouts.

## Open problems

- Whether biasing STING output toward canonical IRF3 (e.g., via dose, schedule, or co-treatment) can convert pro-tumor chronic activation into antitumor acute activation
- IL-6/IL-6R blockade (tocilizumab, siltuximab) as a STING-pathway-directed combination strategy

## My understanding

This concept demolishes the simplistic "STING ON = good" model and explains why patient outcomes diverge from preclinical predictions in CIN-high tumors. The therapeutic implication — combine STING modulation with IL-6 axis blockade — is concrete and testable.
