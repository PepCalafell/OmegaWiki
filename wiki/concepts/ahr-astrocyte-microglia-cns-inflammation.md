## Definition

AHR is expressed in CNS-resident astrocytes and microglia and is upregulated during neuroinflammation. Astrocyte- or microglia-specific AHR deletion exacerbates EAE through unleashed NF-κB-driven pro-inflammatory transcription. AHR activation in these glial cells suppresses NF-κB and dampens neuroinflammation. The microbiome-derived AHR ligand axis connects gut → CNS via tryptophan metabolites that cross the blood-brain barrier and modulate glial AHR.

## Intuition

The CNS isn't immune-privileged with respect to AHR — astrocytes and microglia themselves use AHR to keep NF-κB-driven inflammation in check. The gut microbiome influences CNS inflammation in part because it controls the systemic supply of AHR ligands that reach the brain.

## When to use

- Designing AHR-targeted therapies for MS, neurodegenerative disease, neuropsychiatric disorders
- Interpreting microbiome-CNS connections in MS through AHR-ligand availability

## Known limitations

- Many findings rely on conditional AHR knockout in glia in EAE; human translation early
- Cell-subtype heterogeneity within astrocyte / microglia populations not fully captured

## Open problems

- AHR-targeted CNS-penetrant agonists (e.g., laquinimod-like compounds) for progressive MS
- Whether AHR agonism in microglia can reverse chronic neurodegenerative inflammation
