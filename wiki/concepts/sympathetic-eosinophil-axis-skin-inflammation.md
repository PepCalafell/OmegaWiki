## Definition

A two-arm neuroimmune circuit in which skin-innervating Pdyn⁺ noradrenergic sympathetic neurons, activated by acute psychological stress, exacerbate atopic-dermatitis-like inflammation through:

1. **Chemotactic arm** — local release of CCL11 (eotaxin-1) that engages CCR3 on eosinophils, driving their recruitment from blood and bone marrow into the upper dermis.
2. **Effector arm** — local noradrenaline release that activates Adrb2 on recruited eosinophils, triggering degranulation (Epx, MBP) and release of itch/inflammation mediators (IL-31).

## Intuition

The "stress worsens dermatitis" clinical observation has historically been attributed to HPA-axis cortisol or general sympathetic tone. This axis sharpens the model: a specific sympathetic subtype, defined by Pdyn expression, large soma, and selective hairy-skin innervation, provides both the chemoattractant signal (CCL11) and the effector signal (noradrenaline → Adrb2) needed to convert stress into eosinophil-driven skin pathology.

## Formal notation

- Neuron subtype: Pdyn⁺ Th⁺ Slc18a2⁺ VIP⁻ Npy⁻ (sNuc-seq cluster 3 of stellate / T2 ganglia).
- Chemotactic signal: CCL11 → CCR3 (Gi-coupled).
- Effector signal: noradrenaline → Adrb2 (Gs-coupled / β-arrestin-2 in eosinophils).
- Target population: CD45⁺ CD11b⁺ Siglec-F⁺ dermal eosinophils.

## Variants

- **Optogenetic mimic** — local blue-light stimulation of Pdyn^Net-CatCh skin fibres recapitulates the axis without psychological stress.
- **Pharmacological dissection** — CCR3 antagonism (SB297006, anti-CCR3 mAb) blocks the chemotactic arm; eosinophil-specific Adrb2 KO blocks only the effector arm.

## Comparison

Distinct from the older HPA → cortisol model of stress-aggravated AD (whose ablation by adrenalectomy actually worsens, not improves, inflammation) and from generalised sympathetic vasomotor activation (mediated by Npy⁺ neurons that do not drive dermatitis). Conceptual cousin of the cardiac-sympathetic-leukocyte axis (Poller 2022) and of bone-marrow eosinopoiesis under sympathetic control.
