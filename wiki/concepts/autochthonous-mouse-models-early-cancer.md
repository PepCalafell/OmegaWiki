## Definition
Autochthonous models induce cancer in situ within the native organ of an immunocompetent mouse, either by genetic alterations (GEMMs) or by direct application of carcinogens, allowing the entire process from cancer initiation through advanced disease to be observed in the natural tissue microenvironment.

## Intuition
Unlike cell-line xenografts and PDX, autochthonous models preserve the immune system, vasculature, and stromal interactions needed to study the multi-step nature of early cancer.

## Variants
- GEMMs (constitutive, conditional, inducible, multi-allele)
- Carcinogen-induced (DEN, 4NQO, DMBA, PM2.5)
- Hybrid GEMM × carcinogen models
- CRISPR-Cas9 somatic in vivo editing

## Known limitations
Expensive and time-consuming; inter-animal heterogeneity in onset of cancer can span months to a year; mouse-human differences in immunity and tissue architecture limit clinical translation; alterations are typically introduced into entire lineages rather than rare clones as in human cancer.
