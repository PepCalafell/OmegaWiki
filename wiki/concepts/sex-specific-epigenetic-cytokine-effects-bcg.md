## Definition

The finding that BCG vaccination drives largely non-overlapping, often oppositely-directed DNA-methylation changes in males versus females, and that baseline methylation–cytokine associations are sex-specific — implicating sex hormones (e.g. cortisol, GnRH axis) in shaping trained immunity.

## Intuition

Men and women take different epigenetic trajectories after the same vaccine. Short-term female changes enrich for sex-hormone/GnRH pathways and male changes for nervous-system functions; long-term male changes enrich for infection/TLR pathways and female changes for neuronal pathways. Methylation–TI associations are far more numerous in males.

## Formal notation

Sex-stratified EWAS: e.g. 3579 baseline CpGs associated with TI(IFN-γ) in males vs 584 in females, no overlap; 45.3% of associations differ in direction between sexes. Female TI(IFN-γ) CpGs correlate with cortisol; male sites do not.

## Variants

- Short-term vs long-term sex-specific signatures
- Hormone-linked (female, cortisol/GnRH) vs receptor-tyrosine-kinase (male) enrichment

## Comparison

Consistent with prior reports of sex-dependent BCG inflammatory-protein changes and sex-dimorphic clinical protection.

## When to use

When designing or interpreting immune/vaccine studies where pooling sexes could mask or cancel effects.

## Known limitations

No genome-wide-significant sex-stratified BCG CpGs (FDR < 0.05); male-specific results lack external validation; small replication cohort (few males).

## Open problems

Causal role of sex hormones; whether sex-specific methylation explains sex-dimorphic clinical BCG outcomes.

## My understanding

A strong argument for sex-stratified analysis in immune-epigenetics; sex is a primary, not nuisance, axis of trained-immunity variation.
