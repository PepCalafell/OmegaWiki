## Definition

Despite robust antitumor activity of STING agonists across mouse syngeneic tumor models for over a decade, no STING agonist has progressed beyond Phase 2 clinical trials in oncology. Failures span direct CDN agonists (ADU-S100, MK-1454), non-CDN small molecules (MK-2118, SNX281), bacterial-vector delivery (SYNB1891), and ADC formats (TAK-500).

## Intuition

The translational gap reflects a stack of mismatches: (a) DMXAA-class compounds activate murine but not human STING; (b) syngeneic mouse models lack the autochthonous evolutionary history (epigenetic STING silencing, immune editing) of human tumors; (c) human T cells are sensitive to STING-induced cytotoxicity in ways masked in mouse models; (d) CDN pharmacokinetics are poor (rapid clearance, hydrolysis instability); (e) systemic toxicity from widespread STING engagement limits dose.

## Variants

- Failed by efficacy not safety: ADU-S100 phase 1 in advanced solid tumors and lymphoma (1/41 PR + 2 unconfirmed PR)
- Failed in combination with PD-1 (spartalizumab + STING agonist): minimal antitumor responses
- DMXAA: phase 3 NSCLC failure due to species mismatch
- TAK-500 (CCR2-STING ADC) + pembrolizumab: terminated for clinical futility

## When to use

As baseline context for any new STING-pathway-directed clinical program. Patient stratification by tumor STING/cGAS status (currently absent in trial design), delivery method, and cellular target (DC, myeloid, EC, NK vs T) must be considered upfront.

## Open problems

- Whether systemic agonists (CRD3874-SI, GSK3745417, TAK-676) can succeed where intratumoral failed
- Whether ADC-targeted (XMT-2056 HER2-STING) or indirect (ENPP1, TREX1 inhibitors) approaches close the gap
- Predictive biomarkers (cGAS/STING IHC, MTAP, STK11, MYC, IDH1) for trial enrichment

## My understanding

The "STING agonist crisis" is the dominant frame of the field today. Direct agonism is unlikely to deliver as monotherapy; the productive next decade will be defined by (a) indirect activation via negative-regulator inhibition (ENPP1, TREX1), (b) cell-type-targeted ADCs and LNPs, (c) epigenetic priming of STING-silenced tumors, and (d) NK-cell-centric combinations.
