## Definition

Somatic missense mutations (and rare in-frame deletions) clustered in the oxygen-dependent degradation domain (ODD) of HIF2α, around residues 529–542 (notably Pro531), that impair prolyl-hydroxylation and VHL-mediated proteasomal degradation. The result is a constitutively stabilized, normoxia-resistant HIF2α — a gain-of-function oncogenic driver. Recurrent alleles include L529P, A530P/T/V, P531A/R/L/S, Y532C, L542R/P, and P534_N536del.

## Intuition

The ODD is the molecular "off switch" that lets oxygen mark HIF2α for destruction. Mutating it removes the switch: HIF2α persists even when oxygen is present, and stabilizes further as oxygen drops from 21% to 1%, giving mutant cells an additional survival edge under hypoxia.

## Variants

- Pro531 hydroxylation-site mutations (direct loss of the hydroxyl-acceptor proline)
- Neighboring ODD residue mutations (530, 532, 542) that disrupt VHL recognition
- In-frame ODD deletions (P534_N536del)

## Comparison

Opposite in effect to the loss-of-function high-altitude population variants (promoter / H194R) that dampen HIF2α activity. See [[concepts/convergent-epas1-adaptation-high-altitude-populations]].

## When to use

Use when interpreting EPAS1 somatic mutations in PPGL or other sympathoadrenal tumors as activating pseudohypoxia drivers, and when stratifying patients for HIF2α inhibitor (belzutifan) therapy.

## Known limitations

Functional gain-of-function shown for selected alleles; the quantitative activation strength of each rare allele is not individually characterized.

## Open problems

- Allele-specific differences in HIF2α stability and downstream transcriptional output
- Whether ODD mutations sensitize uniformly to HIF2α inhibitors
