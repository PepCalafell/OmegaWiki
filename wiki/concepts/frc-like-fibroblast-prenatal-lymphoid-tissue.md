## Definition

A developmental hypothesis, supported by integrating adult and prenatal human skin (and intestinal) fibroblasts, that adult skin F3 FRC-like fibroblasts correspond to prenatal skin CCL19+ fibroblasts, which co-cluster with intestinal mesenchymal lymphoid-tissue-organizer (LTo) cells and express LTo markers (CCL21, CXCL13, MADCAM1, FDCSP, TNFSF11/RANKL). The LTo program is silent in healthy adult skin but re-activated in specific diseases (notably hidradenitis suppurativa).

## Intuition

Skin lacks Peyer's-patch-like structures, yet its FRC-like fibroblasts may derive from the same developmental LTo program that builds gut lymphoid tissue — a program that can be re-awakened in disease to support tertiary lymphoid structures.

## Formal notation

Adult F3 ↔ prenatal skin CCL19+ (gene-module correlation). Prenatal skin CCL19+ ↔ intestinal mLTo (co-clustering + shared LTo markers). LTo program (CXCL13, FDCSP) off in healthy adult skin, on in hidradenitis suppurativa.

## Variants

Human-specific: F3 FRC-like cells are enriched in human skin but rare/absent in mouse skin (correspond to mouse Ccl19+ fibroblasts found mainly in lymphoid organs).

## Comparison

Parallels intestinal FRC ontogeny from prenatal LTo cells in Peyer's patches; extends it to a non-canonical lymphoid tissue (skin).

## When to use

When reasoning about the developmental origin of immune-organizing stromal cells or tertiary lymphoid structure formation in peripheral tissue.

## Known limitations

Correlative/integrative evidence; no direct lineage tracing of prenatal-to-adult transition in humans.

## Open problems

Whether re-activated LTo programs causally drive TLS in human inflammatory disease.

## My understanding

A developmental coda to the immune-niche story: the human-skin-enriched FRC-like fibroblast may be a re-deployable LTo-derived lineage, explaining disease-associated lymphoid organization in skin.
