## Definition

In abdominal skin (n=13 donors), advancing donor age is correlated with a shift in neighborhood composition from the STROMA neighborhood (Spearman ρ = −0.70, P = 0.0083) to PERIVASC II (Spearman ρ = 0.70, P = 0.0076), driven by depletion of Retic Fib I (reticular fibroblast subset I) and expansion of pericytes, HECs, VECs and smooth muscle cells.

## Intuition

The known clinical reduction of dermal collagen with age has a cellular correlate: not just fibroblast hypofunction but a numerical loss of the dominant collagen-producing Retic Fib I subset, with the vacated space repopulated by perivascular and vascular cells.

## Variants

- Site-restricted: established for abdominal skin; whether the same shift holds in scalp / face / extremities at equivalent ages is open.
- Distinct from Solé-Boldo et al. (2020 inguinal) which reported per-fibroblast gene-expression decline rather than numerical depletion.

## Comparison

- Complements aging-fibroblast gene-expression studies by adding a compositional axis.

## When to use

When discussing dermal aging, anti-aging interventions targeting fibroblast subsets, or interpreting age-confounded skin spatial data.

## Known limitations

- Cross-sectional; no longitudinal data.
- Confounders (sun exposure, BMI, hormonal status) not exhaustively controlled.

## Open problems

- Is Retic Fib I numerical loss reversible (cell-therapy, dermal injection)?
- Does Retic Fib I loss drive or follow basement-membrane / DEJ changes with age?

## My understanding

Bridges a long-standing clinical observation (collagen loss with age) to a quantifiable cellular substrate. Should be considered when designing skin therapeutics that aim to "restore" dermal architecture in older patients.
