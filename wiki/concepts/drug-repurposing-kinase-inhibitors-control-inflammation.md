## Definition

The use of functional epigenomic profiling to identify clinical kinase inhibitors whose effects on macrophage activation programs make them candidates for repurposing to modulate inflammation — for example, compounds that selectively impair inflammatory (TLR4/LPS) versus resolution (IL-4) gene-expression programs, or that suppress interferon induction.

## Intuition

If a drug's measured cellular fingerprint shows it dampens a specific inflammatory program (even via off-target activity), it may be repositioned therapeutically for inflammatory disease regardless of its original oncology indication.

## Formal notation

- Some CKIs nearly selectively impaired H3K27ac changes elicited by one stimulus (LPS vs IL-4).
- Interferon-suppressing CKIs (e.g., Midostaurin via TBK1) versus interferon-inducing CKIs partition candidates by desired anti-/pro-inflammatory effect.

## Variants

- Suppressing classical inflammation (TLR4/NF-κB/IFN) vs modulating resolution (IL-4/STAT6) programs.

## Comparison

Complements target-based repurposing by ranking compounds on measured functional effect rather than annotated target.

## When to use

When prioritizing already-approved kinase inhibitors for anti-inflammatory indications based on their cellular macrophage-activation signature.

## Known limitations

- Cellular-effect evidence is correlative and context-specific (BMDM, two stimuli).
- Requires downstream in-vivo and clinical validation.

## Open problems

- Translating macrophage epigenomic signatures into predicted clinical anti-inflammatory benefit.
- Balancing desired anti-inflammatory effects against off-target interferon induction.
