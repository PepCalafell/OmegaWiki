## Definition

A stromal-immune feedback axis in which immune-cell-derived TNF acts on perivascular fibroblasts (Perivasc Fib I) to sustain CCL19 expression; CCL19 in turn recruits and retains CCR7+ lymphocytes and DCs, completing a homeostatic recruitment loop within the PERIVASC I neighborhood of human skin.

## Intuition

The axis re-uses the lymph-node FRC blueprint in non-lymphoid tissue. TNF — the apex of the inflammatory cytokine hierarchy — is repurposed as a homeostatic maintenance signal here, blurring the line between "homeostatic" and "low-grade inflammatory" stromal states.

## Variants

- Anti-TNF therapy as inadvertent disruptor: anti-TNF (psoriasis/HS/AD therapeutics) may attenuate the CCL19 program of perivascular fibroblasts and reshape PERIVASC I composition.

## Comparison

- Analogous to lymph-node FRC programs maintained by LT/TNF signalling but in skin perivascular setting.

## When to use

When framing the mechanism of skin perivascular niche maintenance, or when interpreting effects of anti-TNF biologics on skin stromal compartments.

## Known limitations

- Inference based on integrated scRNA-seq CellChat L-R analysis simulating PERIVASC I composition — not perturbational.
- Causality (TNF→CCL19) supported by L-R inference; direct functional validation in human skin still required.

## Open problems

- Do anti-TNF biologics reduce CCL19+ fibroblast abundance in vivo?
- Is TNFR1 or TNFR2 the relevant receptor on perivascular fibroblasts?
