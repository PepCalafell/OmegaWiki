## Definition

Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are the heterogeneous population of T, B and NK cells found within and around solid tumours. CD8+ effector T cells are the dominant cytotoxic subset; CD4+ helpers, Tregs, Bregs and exhausted T subpopulations co-exist depending on TME state.

## Intuition

TIL density and clonal diversity correlate with prognosis and ICB response. Adoptive transfer of ex vivo-expanded TILs has produced objective responses in PD1-resistant melanoma, cervical cancer and NSCLC.

## Variants

- **CD8+ effector TILs** — primary cytotoxic subset.
- **Exhausted CD8+ TILs** — CXCL13+, PD1hi, TIM3+.
- **Treg TILs** — FOXP3+, immunosuppressive.
- **CAR-TILs** — engineered for tumour-antigen specificity.
- **Reinvigorated TILs** — expanded with IL-2 + anti-PD1.

## When to use

- Predicting ICB response (correlate with CD8 density).
- Source for adoptive cell therapy.
- Readout for ex vivo immune organoid platforms.

## Known limitations

- TIL viability decays in culture without cytokine support.
- TCR repertoire bias during ex vivo expansion.

## Open problems

- Identifying clonotypes most predictive of clinical benefit.
- Spatial determinants of TIL function (TLS proximity, hypoxic niches).

TIL recapitulation is the central quality metric for any immune organoid platform; native immune organoids retain TCR clonotypes from cognate biopsies while reconstituted models do not.
