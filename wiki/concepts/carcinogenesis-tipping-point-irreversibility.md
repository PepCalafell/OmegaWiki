## Definition
The "tipping point" is the conceptual moment at which a cell carrying oncogenic alterations becomes irreversibly committed to a cancer trajectory, distinguishing it from the majority of driver-mutant cells in the same tissue that will self-renew, differentiate, or die without becoming cancer. Identifying the molecular nature of this commitment is the central open problem of early-cancer biology.

## Intuition
Milestone events such as p53 LOH, mutant-p53 stabilization, whole-genome amplification, and emergence of ecDNA are candidate markers that the cell has crossed the threshold. Event-based lineage tracing operationalizes the search for these milestones.

## Open problems
- Whether a single, universal tipping point exists or whether commitment is cancer- and lineage-specific
- How to non-invasively detect the moment of commitment in living tissue
- Whether commitment is truly irreversible or pharmacologically reversible
