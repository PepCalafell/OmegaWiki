# HIF-independent macrophage M2 transition during regeneration

After sterile tissue injury (skeletal muscle, myocardial infarction), infiltrating monocyte-derived macrophages mount a **day-1 glycolytic burst** with strong HIF-1α expression in the hypoxic wound, then **promptly downregulate glycolysis** by day 2–3 and upregulate TCA / ETC / FAO genes for the anti-inflammatory remodelling phase. Although HIF-1α/HIF-2α are required for **infiltration** of proinflammatory macrophages, conditional LysM-Cre HIF1α/HIF2α deletion **does not impair** the proinflammatory → anti-inflammatory transition itself or regeneration in two of three muscle-injury models — meaning the M2 transition occurs in a hypoxic niche but is **HIF-independent**.

The transition is supported by **AMPKα1** (essential for the anti-inflammatory phenotype), **PPARδ/PPARγ** (apoptotic-cell-induced), **resolvin D2 / GPR18** signalling between Ly6Cˡᵒ and Ly6Cʰⁱ macrophages, and **arginase-1**-driven ornithine→proline/polyamine synthesis that fuels collagen production. Macrophages also **synthesize and secrete glutamine** to support satellite-cell expansion; inhibiting glutamine dehydrogenase-1 in macrophages improves muscle regeneration.

The concept partitions HIF function: HIF for *infiltration*, but HIF-independent metabolic rewiring for *resolution*.
