## Definition

Activated STING in tumor endothelial cells (ECs) (a) upregulates T-cell adhesion molecules (ICAM-1, VCAM-1, SELL/L-selectin) overcoming "EC anergy", (b) drives vascular normalization with improved vessel integrity, (c) secretes CXCL10 and CCL5 chemoattractants, and (d) binds JAK1 directly to amplify pro-inflammatory JAK/STAT output. Host EC-STING is a dominant source of TME IFNβ in some tumor models.

## Intuition

ECs are the gateway between blood and tumor; tumor vasculature is normally dysregulated to exclude effector T and NK cells. STING activation in ECs reverses that exclusion — both by normalizing vessel architecture and by reprogramming the local chemokine landscape from immunosuppressive to immunostimulatory.

## Variants

- Murine EC STING knockout: dramatic reduction in CD8 T cell infiltration and poor antitumor responses
- STING–JAK1 direct binding (Wu 2024): non-canonical amplification of EC pro-inflammatory programme
- Combined with type I IFN: synergistic ICAM/VCAM upregulation

## When to use

When designing therapies to convert "cold" (T-cell-excluded) tumors to "hot" — EC STING is a potentially attractive cell-type-specific target for nanoparticle-based delivery.

## Open problems

- Whether antigen-presenting cells (APCs) or ECs are the dominant IFNβ source in human tumors — likely context-dependent
- EC-selective STING-agonist delivery vehicles
- Whether STING-driven vascular normalization improves drug penetration of chemotherapy and ADCs

## My understanding

A less-emphasized but mechanistically pivotal arm of STING TME biology. EC STING is the entry control point for the entire adaptive response and explains why hydrogel-sustained intratumoral STING activation can produce systemic immune effects.
