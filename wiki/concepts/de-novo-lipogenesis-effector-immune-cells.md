## Definition

Immune-cell activation requires the de novo synthesis of fatty acids and their derivatives: glucose → citrate → ACLY → acetyl-CoA → ACC → malonyl-CoA → FASN → palmitate; SCD converts SFAs into MUFAs; SREBP1 transcriptionally regulates the genes. The pathway sustains B-cell proliferation, plasma-cell program (Blimp-1), germinal-centre formation, CD8 IFN-γ output, Treg function, DC maturation, neutrophil survival.

## Intuition

Effector immune cells behave like rapidly proliferating cells — they need membrane lipid and FA-derived precursors fast. Blocking ACLY or FASN starves the activation program; SCD's oleic acid is specifically required for GC formation.

## Variants

- ACLY → acetyl-CoA → cytoplasmic FA synthesis (and protein acetylation)
- ACC → malonyl-CoA gate
- FASN → palmitate (membrane / signaling lipid pool)
- SCD → MUFA (oleic acid) for GC
- SREBP1 — master TF; activated by mTORC1/2

## Comparison

Counterpoint to [[concepts/fao-memory-cd8-trm-treg-program]] (catabolic, memory). FASN occupies a paradoxical position — required for memory CD8 survival but loss raises tumour MHC-I and enhances CD8 killing of cancer cells.

## When to use

For modelling B-cell maturation/plasma-cell output, GC dynamics, CD8 effector responses, DC vaccine biology, and FASN-targeted antitumor strategies.

## Known limitations

- FASN inhibitors lack immune-cell selectivity; dose-window for tumour benefit vs CD8 memory loss is narrow.
- SREBP1 has overlapping isoforms and SREBP2 cholesterol-targeting arm.

## Open problems

- Therapeutic dose-window of FASN inhibition: tumour benefit vs memory CD8 loss vs Treg suppression.
- SCD-MUFA selectivity for GC B cells in vaccine adjuvancy.
