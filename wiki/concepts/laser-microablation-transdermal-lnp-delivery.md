## Definition

A delivery strategy that pairs controlled laser microablation of the skin — generating transient micropores that extend into the viable epidermis — with topical application of mRNA-lipid nanoparticles, so that LNP cargo can reach and transfect the basal keratinocyte (skin stem cell) compartment that the intact barrier would otherwise exclude. Pore depth is tuned and verified non-invasively (reflectance confocal microscopy) to balance access against tissue integrity.

## Intuition

The skin barrier is the reason topical genome editing had lagged behind muscle, eye, and brain. LNPs cannot diffuse through an intact stratum corneum to reach the stem-cell niche. Microablation creates short-lived channels just deep enough to admit LNPs to viable epidermis while preserving overall tissue integrity — turning the skin's accessibility advantage into a usable delivery route without resorting to invasive injection or grafting.

## Variants

- **Laser microablation** (this concept) — micropores to viable epidermis, depth controlled by reflectance confocal microscopy
- **Microneedle arrays** — mechanical micropores (related barrier-modulation approach)
- **Chemical penetration enhancers** — solvent/surfactant barrier disruption
- **Iontophoresis / sonophoresis** — physical driving forces for transdermal transport

## Comparison

Versus systemic or intradermal injection: topical + microablation is non-invasive and spatially confined, limiting systemic dissemination of LNP and cargo. Versus unassisted topical LNP: barrier modulation is what enables stem-cell-compartment transfection at all (39.1% of basal keratinocytes in a 3D ARCI skin model vs negligible through intact barrier).

## When to use

When an LNP-delivered payload (genome editor, replacement-protein mRNA) must reach the regenerative basal layer of skin for durable effect, and an intact barrier blocks passive uptake. Pore depth must be controlled to reach stem cells without compromising tissue integrity.

## Open problems

- Scaling controlled microablation across large body-surface areas
- Reproducibility of pore-depth control across skin sites and patients
- Whether barrier modulation alters local immune responses on repeat dosing
- Translating from 3D skin models to in vivo human skin

## My understanding

This is the load-bearing innovation of the previewed study — the commentary explicitly notes the central advance "lies not in the editor itself but in its delivery." Controlled, non-invasive access to the skin stem-cell compartment is what converts a precise editor into a potentially curative skin therapy, and it is the most transferable piece of the platform to other genodermatoses.
