## Definition

AHR in endothelial cells is the critical compartment for resistance to Plasmodium chabaudi malaria: TEK-Cre conditional Ahr deletion phenocopies whole-body Ahr-KO (high parasitaemia, anaemia, mortality). In P. berghei ANKA cerebral malaria, Ahr-KO mice show increased leukocyte rolling/adherence and vascular permeability in the brain microvasculature. The likely endogenous AHR agonists during Plasmodium infection include host-derived bilirubin/biliverdin (released by haemolysis) and Trp-pathway metabolites.

## Intuition

Plasmodium infection releases haem from infected RBCs, which is metabolised to bilirubin/biliverdin (both AHR agonists). The resulting AHR signalling in endothelium maintains vascular integrity and limits cerebral inflammation — a protective tissue-homeostasis function distinct from AHR's classical immune-regulatory roles in T cells and ILCs.

## When to use

- For severe / cerebral malaria pathology mechanisms
- When framing endothelial AHR as a vascular-protection programme (parallels claims around FluV endothelial-AHR limiting lung leakage)
- For studying haem-pigment biology in infection

## Known limitations

- Only P. chabaudi has been formally tested with endothelial-conditional KO
- Bilirubin/biliverdin are competitive AHR ligands; direct binding still unmeasured

## Open problems

- Whether endothelial-targeted AHR agonists ameliorate severe cerebral malaria
- Whether bilirubin / biliverdin engagement of endothelial AHR is conserved across pathologies involving haemolysis (sickle cell, sepsis)
- Mechanistic identification of the AHR endothelial transcriptional programme that confers malaria resistance
