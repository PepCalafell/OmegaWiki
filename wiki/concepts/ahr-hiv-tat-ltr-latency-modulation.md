## Definition

AHR directly binds the HIV-1 5'LTR and the viral Tat protein, recruiting transcription factors to viral promoters and activating viral transcription. AHR knockdown (siRNA/shRNA) impairs HIV infectivity in Magi/CCR5, HEK293T and primary CD4+ T cells. KYN and FICZ AHR agonists reactivate latent HIV from PBMCs of cART-treated individuals; CH-223191 reduces HIC1 (a Tat-mediated repressor of viral transcription) and suppresses viral replication in CD4+ T cells from individuals on cART. Macrophages show the opposite outcome: FICZ-AHR signalling blocks HIV early replication.

## Intuition

HIV uses AHR as a co-factor at its own promoter — kynurenine, abundant in chronic HIV infection (positively correlated with viral load and negatively with CD4 counts), is the host-derived agonist that reactivates latent virus. This makes AHR antagonism a potential "block and lock" strategy: rather than triggering latency reversal, suppressing AHR could keep proviral DNA quiescent.

## When to use

- For HIV cure strategies (block-and-lock vs shock-and-kill paradigms)
- When framing tryptophan-kynurenine metabolic dysfunction as a driver of HIV pathogenesis
- For host-directed antiviral approaches independent of viral genome targeting

## Known limitations

- Opposite outcomes in macrophages vs CD4+ T cells limit a single therapeutic strategy
- Most evidence from cell lines and ex vivo PBMC; clinical AHR-targeting in HIV not yet trialed

## Open problems

- Whether AHR antagonism added to cART suppresses residual viral transcription and shrinks the reservoir
- Why macrophage AHR blocks HIV but T-cell AHR promotes HIV — what structural / co-factor difference?
- Whether human AHR allele variation correlates with HIV reservoir size in cohort studies
