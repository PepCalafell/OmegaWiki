## Definition

A mechanism explaining the clinical "obesity paradox" — that some obese patients respond better to ICB despite obesity being a major cancer risk factor. Obesity-induced chronic inflammation selectively triggers PD-1 expression on tumour-associated macrophages via the mTORC1-glycolysis axis. Anti-PD-1 releases the brake on TAMs, boosting their glycolysis, antigen presentation (CD86, MHC), and downstream CD8 T-cell activation.

## Intuition

ICB benefit isn't only about T-cell exhaustion — it's also about an inflamed myeloid compartment finally being unleashed. Myeloid-specific PD-1 KO recapitulates the effect.

## Formal notation

Obesity → chronic inflammation → mTORC1 → glycolysis → PD-1 (on TAM) ↑ → anti-PD-1 releases → CD86/MHC ↑ → CD8 activity ↑.

## Variants

- Specific tumour types (varying obesity-ICB benefit).
- Myeloid PD-1 vs T-cell PD-1 contributions.

## Comparison

- Contrasts with the canonical T-cell-centric view of ICB.

## When to use

- Interpreting clinical heterogeneity of obese-patient ICB outcomes.

## Known limitations

- Obesity paradox is heterogeneous; some tumour types do worse.

## Open problems

- Whether targeted TAM-PD-1 modulation benefits lean patients.

## Key papers

- Bader, Voss, Rathmell 2024; Ricci 2025 review.

## My understanding

A mechanistically satisfying explanation for a counterintuitive clinical observation — a model of how metabolic state of one compartment dictates ICB benefit.
