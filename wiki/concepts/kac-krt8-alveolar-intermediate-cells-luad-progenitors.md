## Definition

KAC (KRT8-high alveolar intermediate cells) — pathologically identifiable as reactive type II pneumocytes (RPII) — are a transient KRT8⁺ alveolar epithelial state positioned between AT2 and AT1 cells along normal alveolar repair, but co-opted in LUAD pathogenesis as the earliest precancerous progenitor.

## Intuition

In response to injury (e.g., bleomycin, IPF), AT2 cells transit through a KRT8⁺ intermediate before becoming AT1. In KRAS-mutant or tobacco-carcinogen-exposed alveoli, KACs accumulate, persist, and acquire tumor-associated meta-programs (MP6-tumor/KAC) while retaining partial alveolar identity (MP2-AT2). Histologically, they correspond to reactive pneumocytes (RPII) which appear morphologically subtle but molecularly malignant-leaning.

## Key papers

- [[papers/multimodal-spatial-omics-reveal-co-evolution]] — Peng et al. 2026 Cancer Cell: spatial-omics + snRNA-seq + Xenium mapping of KACs/RPII as earliest LUAD precursors, residing in IL1B-IL1R1 epithelial-proinflammatory niches across 56 human lesions, 36-lesion TMA, and Gprc5a−/− NNK mouse model.

## Variants

- Mouse Krt8high alveolar intermediate (Gprc5a−/−; KrtCreER lineage-traceable)
- AT2 inflamed subcluster (CXCL2⁺ NFKBIA/Z⁺) — proximal but distinct from KAC by lineage hierarchy
- LAMP3⁺/KRT8⁺ cells (immunofluorescence proxy)
