## Definition
A precancerous lesion is a morphologically or molecularly defined tissue state intermediate between normal epithelium and invasive cancer, in which cells already harbour cancer-driver alterations and exhibit abnormal proliferation but have not yet acquired the full malignant phenotype. The "malignant transformation cascade" is the multi-step trajectory by which a single ancestral cell of origin acquires drivers, undergoes clonal expansion, accumulates additional (epi)genetic events, traverses a precancerous state (with or without visible morphology) and ultimately progresses to invasive cancer.

## Intuition
Driver mutations are necessary but not sufficient: most cells carrying canonical drivers in normal tissues never become cancer. The contribution of intrinsic (genetic, epigenetic) and extrinsic (microenvironmental, macroenvironmental) factors is what tips selected lineages over the threshold to malignancy.

## Variants
- Precancerous lesions with clinically defined morphology (colon polyps, Barrett's oesophagus, PanIN, atypical adenomatous hyperplasia, leukoplakia, intraepithelial neoplasia, MGUS, smouldering myeloma)
- Precancerous cells without visible lesions (clonally expanded driver-mutant cells in phenotypically normal tissue)

## When to use
Whenever discussing the biology, sampling, or modelling of cancer initiation and early-stage progression, as distinct from advanced-stage cancer biology.

## Known limitations
The boundary between "precancerous" and "normal-with-drivers" is blurry: many drivers are present in aged normal tissue at frequencies far exceeding cancer incidence, so morphology and molecular criteria must be combined.
