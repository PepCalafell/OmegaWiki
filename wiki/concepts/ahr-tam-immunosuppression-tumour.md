## Definition

In multiple cancers (glioblastoma, breast, oral, gastric), cancer cells over-express IDO1 and TDO2 to produce Kyn, which activates AHR in tumour-associated macrophages (TAMs). AHR drives KLF4 expression in TAMs, suppressing NF-κB-driven pro-inflammatory cytokines while inducing CD39, which together with CD73 generates immunosuppressive adenosine and promotes CD8⁺ T-cell dysfunction. Chronic AHR activation also fosters stem-like cancer cell states and metastasis.

## Intuition

The tumour exploits the same metabolic immune checkpoint (Trp → Kyn → AHR) that the body uses to limit immunopathology in the gut, but redirects it locally — its TAMs and DCs become tolerogenic, T cells exhaust, and Tregs accumulate. AHR antagonists (or IDO/TDO/IL4I1 inhibitors) aim to break this axis.

## Variants

- Glioblastoma — AHR–KLF4–CD39 macrophage axis
- Breast cancer — AHR overexpression in tumour cells and stroma
- Oral/gastric cancers — TCDD-historic association; IDO1/TDO2 over-expression
- TAM phenotypes overlapping IL4I1⁺PD-L1⁺IDO1⁺ MoMac-VERSE clusters

## When to use

- Designing AHR antagonists for solid tumours (e.g., IK-175, BAY-2416964)
- Stratifying tumours by Trp-Kyn-AHR pathway expression for combination ICI therapy
- Connecting AHR biology to IL4I1/IDO1 single-cell pan-cancer macrophage atlases

## Known limitations

- AHR has both pro- and anti-tumour roles depending on tumour type and ligand context
- Clinical AHR antagonist trials are early; biomarker strategies needed

## Open problems

- Whether AHR antagonism synergises with IDO1/IL4I1 inhibitors or PD-1 blockade
- Spatial mapping of Trp-Kyn-AHR gradients within tumour micro-environments
