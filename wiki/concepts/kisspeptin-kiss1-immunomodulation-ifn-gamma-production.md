## Definition

The concept that kisspeptin signaling (KISS1/GPR54), classically reproductive, acts as an immunomodulator that inhibits IFN-γ production capacity, and that DNA methylation of *KISS1* may tune heterologous IFN-γ trained-immunity responses.

## Intuition

Pathway enrichment of baseline IFN-γ-associated CpGs pointed strongest to the kisspeptin receptor system. A direct functional test then showed recombinant kisspeptin-10 suppresses IFN-γ — implicating a neuroendocrine peptide in setting human innate/adaptive cytokine output.

## Formal notation

PBMCs preincubated 1 h with KP-10 (1–100 ng/mL), then stimulated with heat-killed *C. albicans* or *S. aureus*; IFN-γ measured at day 7. Effect: significant IFN-γ inhibition; IL-17/IL-22 unaffected.

## Variants

- Methylation-mediated (genomic, via *KISS1* CpGs) vs direct peptide signaling
- Sex-hormone axis crosstalk (GnRH) potentially linking to sex-specific effects

## Comparison

Adds a neuroendocrine node to cytokine-regulation concepts; selective for IFN-γ over other T-cell cytokines (IL-17, IL-22).

## When to use

When considering neuroendocrine–immune crosstalk as a modulator of trained-immunity cytokine responses.

## Known limitations

Single in vitro validation assay; mechanism downstream of GPR54 not resolved here; methylation→KISS1→IFN-γ chain inferred, not proven.

## Open problems

Whether *KISS1* methylation causally controls IFN-γ in vivo and the signaling intermediates involved.

## Key papers

- [[long-term-dna-methylation-changes-mediate]] — identifies kisspeptin pathway enrichment and functionally validates KP-10 inhibition of IFN-γ.

## My understanding

A novel, experimentally supported neuroendocrine brake on IFN-γ that may explain part of baseline-methylation-driven response heterogeneity.
