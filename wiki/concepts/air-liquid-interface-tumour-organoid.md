## Definition

An ALI tumour organoid is generated by embedding physically minced primary tumour fragments in a collagen gel inside an inner transwell whose upper surface is exposed to air while the bottom is bathed in medium in the outer dish. This geometry enables sufficient oxygenation and medium diffusion, allowing the tumour fragment to grow en bloc with endogenous stroma and immune populations.

## Intuition

Unlike submerged Matrigel PDOs, ALI cultures preserve native tumour architecture, mutational burden and immune infiltrate without enzymatic dissociation. The T cell compartment can be maintained for >70 days with IL-2 supplementation, and TCR clonotypes from the original biopsy are recapitulated.

## When to use

- Modelling ICB response in melanoma, NSCLC, RCC, basal cell carcinoma and other immune-infiltrated tumours.
- Long-term TIL studies where reconstituted models cannot preserve native clonotypes.
- Anti-myeloid therapy testing (e.g. anti-TREM2 in basal cell carcinoma).

## Known limitations

- Inter-organoid heterogeneity from spatially variable tumours.
- Low throughput vs reconstituted/MOS systems.
- Genetic engineering complicated by multicellularity and architecture preservation.
- TIL compartment declines over time; only partially preserved with cytokine supplementation.

## Open problems

- FNA-compatible ALI culture from minimal biopsy material.
- Cell-type-specific in situ perturbation (CRISPR delivery).
- Standardized prospective clinical correlation.

## My understanding

ALI organoids are the closest commonly available ex vivo system to a "living biopsy" and the most appropriate model for hypoxia-immune-evasion experiments because they retain native oxygen gradients and the full myeloid + lymphoid compartment.
