# Tissue-specific metabolic programming of macrophages

Each tissue-resident macrophage population (alveolar, Kupffer, red pulp, marginal zone, peritoneal, microglial, osteoclast, kidney-resident, white-adipose, cardiac, intestinal) executes a **distinct metabolic program** that is tuned to the metabolic resources and homeostatic demands of its niche, and that helps **define cellular identity** in concert with tissue-specific lineage-determining factors. The in-vitro M1/M2 paradigm captures only a fraction of this diversity: in vivo, tissue-instructed metabolism is the dominant axis.

Key examples crystallized by Wculek 2022:
- **Alveolar macrophages**: PPARγ-driven lipid catabolism and cholesterol handling for surfactant clearance; downregulated glycolysis; VHL/HIF tuning.
- **Red pulp macrophages / Kupffer cells**: SPI-C/NRF2/HO-1/ferroportin iron-recycling program plus LXRα/PPARγ lipid-cholesterol handling.
- **Large peritoneal macrophages**: glutamate as tissue-niche fuel, GATA6/RXR/C-EBPβ-controlled lipid handling, mitochondrial recruitment to phagosomes.
- **Osteoclasts**: high mitochondrial content with OXPHOS/FAO/glutaminolysis at rest; HIF-1α-driven glycolytic switch upon bone exposure.
- **Microglia**: high OXPHOS at steady state with plastic glucose↔glutamine fuel switching under aglycemia.

The concept implies that interpreting macrophage metabolism solely through BMDM in-vitro assays is misleading; therapeutic strategies must consider the in-situ metabolic state.
