# Osteoclast metabolic switch upon bone exposure

Mature osteoclasts contain more (and larger/more complex) mitochondria than other cells, reflecting their high energetic demand. RANKL+M-CSF-driven **osteoclastogenesis** requires mitochondrial biogenesis and OXPHOS (complex I), regulated by iron uptake, PGC-1β, and alternative NF-κB (RelB, NIK). At rest osteoclasts run OXPHOS fuelled by **glutaminolysis** through SLC1A5 / glutaminase-1 / c-Myc; glutamine withdrawal or c-Myc inhibition blocks differentiation. Upon **bone exposure**, osteoclasts **switch to HIF-1α-driven glycolysis with lactate production** to power resorption: collagen-degradation activity is reduced by glucose withdrawal and increased by complex I inhibition. The bidirectional metabolic switch couples local microenvironmental cues (bone matrix exposure, hypoxia) to functional output.
