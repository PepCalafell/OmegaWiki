## Definition

A coarse but operationally useful classification of NSCLC tumour microenvironments by immune cell density and activity. "Immune-rich" niches harbour dense T/B/NK/DC/CXCL9⁺ TAM infiltrates and frequently TLS, and tend to respond to ICIs. "Immune-poor" (or "desert"/"excluded") niches are dominated by fibrotic CAFs, hypoxic stroma, MDSCs, TANs, Treg cells, and SPP1⁺/COL1A1⁺/TREM2⁺ TAMs, and resist ICIs.

## Intuition

"Hot vs cold" tumours, sharpened by spatial transcriptomics: the classification is local rather than tumour-wide; multiple niches coexist in one tumour and influence each other. Immune desertification is not the *absence* of immune cells but the *active exclusion* of them by stromal architecture.

## Variants

- Topographical refinements (tumour core vs invasive margin vs peritumoural stroma)
- "Stem-immunity hub" microniche as a finer-grained immune-rich subtype
- LUAD histological subtypes (lepidic, acinar, papillary, micropapillary, solid) overlaying the niche classification

## When to use

For stratifying NSCLC biopsies prior to ICI therapy and for designing combination strategies aimed at converting immune-poor into immune-rich niches.

## Known limitations

- Binary categorisation oversimplifies a continuum.
- Niche assignment depends on platform (Visium vs CosMx vs MERFISH) and annotation.
- Reprogrammability of immune-desert niches into immune-responsive ones is not established.
