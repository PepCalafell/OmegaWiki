## Definition

CYP1A1 is a canonical AHR target gene that catalyses metabolic clearance of endogenous AHR agonists (FICZ, indoles), constituting an AHR negative-feedback loop. Transgenic constitutive intestinal CYP1A1 over-expression depletes physiological AHR agonists, eliminates AHR-dependent ILCs and Th17/IL-22 responses, and increases susceptibility to enteric infection. Conversely, pharmacological CYP1A1 inhibition raises AHR-agonist tissue levels, enhances AHR signalling, and increases IL-22 production.

## Intuition

The same gene whose induction is the best biomarker of AHR activation is also the off-switch that limits it. Drug design must consider whether the ligand is itself a CYP1A1 substrate (transient, e.g., FICZ) or not (persistent, e.g., TCDD).

## When to use

- Choosing AHR ligands for controlled-duration therapy
- Interpreting CYP1A1 status as both biomarker and modifier of AHR activity in tissues
- Considering combination of AHR agonist + CYP1A1 inhibitor

## Open problems

- Tissue-specific CYP1A1 dynamics in patients receiving AHR therapy
- Whether CYP1A1 inhibitors safely boost AHR-agonist efficacy
