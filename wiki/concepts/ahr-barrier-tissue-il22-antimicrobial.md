## Definition

AHR activation in Th17, Th22 and ILC3 cells, plus AHR signalling in epithelial cells themselves, drives IL-22 production and downstream antimicrobial peptide expression (REG3γ, Defensins), tight-junction repair, and intestinal stem-cell DNA-damage responses. Sustained AHR-IL-22 signalling is essential for clearance of barrier pathogens (segmented filamentous bacteria, Citrobacter rodentium) and prevention of colitis and IBD-associated tumorigenesis. Skin AHR similarly drives filaggrin/loricrin/involucrin/hornerin expression for epidermal barrier integrity.

## Intuition

If you want to understand AHR's "good" side in immunology, look at the barrier: gut, skin, and lung epithelia depend on AHR-driven IL-22 to keep their surfaces intact and antimicrobial-armoured. This is why dietary or microbial AHR-ligand depletion causes inflammation, and why tapinarof restores skin barrier proteins.

## When to use

- Designing AHR agonists for IBD, UC, atopic dermatitis, psoriasis, asthma
- Interpreting how environmental AHR antagonists worsen inflammation by suppressing this axis

## Known limitations

- IL-22 has tumour-promoting effects in colorectal cancer in some models — therapeutic window for AHR agonism in barrier tissues must avoid chronic over-activation
- Mouse-to-human translation of ILC3 biology is incomplete

## Open problems

- Optimum kinetics of AHR-IL-22 stimulation for barrier repair without tumour-promotion
- Cell-type-specific IL-22 outputs from AHR agonists in human tissues
