## Definition
Event-based lineage tracing is a class of GEMM reporter strategies that label only the rare cells which reach a defined, generally irreversible milestone along the carcinogenic trajectory (e.g. p53 LOH, mutant p53 stabilization, sustained ERK activation by oncogenic KRAS, elevated RAC activity following Apc loss, p16 induction). Genetically engineered fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters fire upon the event, allowing detection and isolation of precancerous cells destined for malignancy.

## Intuition
Standard lineage tracing labels all progeny of a recombination event. Milestone reporters add a second filter that ignores the bulk of mutant cells that will not progress, drastically enriching for the rare cells whose biology matters for transformation.

## Variants
- Genomic-event reporters (p53 LOH knock-in)
- Protein-stabilization reporters (mutant p53 fluorescent / Akaluc)
- Signalling-state reporters (ERK-KTR-mClover, RAC biosensor)
- Senescence-mark reporters (p16-reporter)

## When to use
To enrich for and characterize the rare precancerous cells in non-cancerous tissue that will progress to cancer, and to align independent carcinogenic trajectories across mice on a common temporal yardstick.
