## Definition

The observation that, within colorectal (colon adenocarcinoma) tumors, higher innate immune activation (clustered "innate groups" across the five PRR scores) co-occurs with greater tumor mutation burden, distinct driver-gene mutation patterns (lower P53/KRAS/APC, higher MUC16), more infiltration of neutrophils/DCs/CD8 T cells, and elevated immune-exhaustion markers (PDCD1, CTLA4, HAVCR2, LAG3).

## Intuition

"Hot" tumors that sense more danger also accumulate more mutations and neoantigens — but the same milieu drives T-cell exhaustion. Innate activation thus indexes immunogenicity while flagging tumors where adaptive immunity is being switched off, a phenotype relevant to checkpoint-blockade rationale.

## Comparison

Connects the classic mutation-burden→neoantigen→adaptive-recognition axis to *innate* sensing, suggesting PRRs may also help recognize self/neoantigen-associated danger, not just microbes.

## When to use

When stratifying colorectal tumors by inflammatory state, or building hypotheses linking innate sensing to immunotherapy responsiveness and antigen (e.g., MUC16/CA125) targeting.

## Known limitations

Correlational and within-cohort clustering; causality between innate activation, mutation burden, and exhaustion is not established. Mutation burden of the four driver genes was not associated with outcomes in this dataset.

## Open problems

Whether PRRs causally contribute to self/neoantigen recognition; whether innate-high/exhausted CRC tumors preferentially respond to checkpoint blockade; validity of MUC16 neoantigen as a vaccine target.

## My understanding

A useful correlative scaffold tying innate state to immunogenicity in CRC; the exhaustion co-association is the most actionable thread for immunotherapy framing.
