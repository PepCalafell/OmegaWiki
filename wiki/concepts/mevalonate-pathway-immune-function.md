## Definition

The mevalonate pathway (HMG-CoA → mevalonate → IPP → FPP → GGPP → cholesterol) provides cholesterol for membrane biogenesis and isoprenoids (GGPP, FPP) for protein prenylation in immune cells. CD40 stimulation upregulates the pathway in B cells; statins block HMG-CoA-reductase and dampen antigen presentation. Breg IL-10 output relies on GGPP-driven PI3Kδ-AKT-GSK-3 → Blimp-1, and Treg function on GGPP-enhanced STAT5 phosphorylation downstream of IL-2.

## Intuition

Statins are not metabolically inert in immune cells — by depleting the mevalonate pathway they suppress Treg and Breg arms but can enhance Th1/cytolytic T responses. This dual effect explains some paradoxical outcomes of statin use in autoimmunity and cancer.

## Variants

- Cholesterol branch — membrane biogenesis, lipid-raft formation
- GGPP / FPP branch — protein prenylation (small GTPases, STAT5)
- Inhibitor pharmacology — statins (HMG-CoA-reductase), bisphosphonates (FPP synthase)

## Comparison

Distinct from but cooperating with [[concepts/de-novo-lipogenesis-effector-immune-cells]] (ACLY-ACC-FASN). Mevalonate makes the sterol/isoprenoid backbone; de novo lipogenesis makes fatty acid carbon.

## When to use

Whenever statins / mevalonate-pathway drugs are considered in immune-modulating contexts (cancer immunotherapy, autoimmunity, vaccine adjuvancy).

## Known limitations

- Pleiotropic effects of statins confound dose-response and tissue specificity.
- Most data from in vitro B/T cell systems; in vivo translation is incomplete.

## Open problems

- Whether targeted GGPP-synthesis modulation (rather than blanket HMG-CoA-reductase inhibition) can selectively tune Treg vs Th1.
- Statin combination with checkpoint blockade — outcomes likely depend on Treg vs Th1 balance.
