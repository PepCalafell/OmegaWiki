## Definition

n-3 long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids — DHA (22:6) and EPA (20:5) — are integrated into immune-cell membranes and modulate CD4 T-cell activation (lowering MHC-II, CD40/CD80/CD86, IL-6, IL-12p70), promote Treg generation, suppress Th1/Th2 in food-allergy models, inhibit FcεRI-mediated mast-cell histamine release, and enhance perinatal neutrophil number/function. The n-3:n-6 ratio rather than absolute n-3 intake appears to be the operative dietary lever.

## Intuition

PUFAs are not merely energy substrates — by joining the membrane phospholipid pool they directly remodel TCR-, BCR-, and FcεRI-proximal signaling and the lipid-mediator pool (SPMs, eicosanoids).

## Variants

- DHA (22:6 n-3) — dominant CD4-suppressive PUFA
- EPA (20:5 n-3) — anti-allergy / SPM precursor
- n-6 PUFAs (linoleic, arachidonic) — opposing Th1-biased effects, DC glycolytic suppression
- SPM derivatives (resolvins, protectins, maresins) — pro-resolving mediators

## Comparison

Complements [[concepts/scfa-hdac-gpr-immune-regulation]] on the dietary axis but works through membrane integration rather than HDAC/GPR.

## When to use

Dietary immunology, allergy and asthma mechanisms, ICU nutrition for immune resolution, and metabolic biomarkers of inflammation.

## Known limitations

- Effects are dose- and ratio-dependent; absolute n-3 supplementation in clinical trials shows mixed outcomes.
- Most CD4-suppression data ex vivo / in vitro — human in vivo dose-response under-defined.

## Open problems

- Whether n-3:n-6 ratio biomarkers can predict ICI response.
- SPM (resolvin/protectin) pharmacology in chronic inflammation.
