## Definition

The differential reliance on glutamine across tumour cells (high uptake via SLC1A5, anaplerotic TCA), effector T cells (glutaminolysis for growth), M1-like macrophages (less dependent, glycolysis-dominant), M2-like macrophages (heavy glutaminolysis, α-KG drives polarisation), and MDSCs (glutamine-supported survival via STAT3).

## Intuition

Block glutaminolysis in tumours, you also hit M2/MDSC immunosuppression — but you potentially impair effector T-cell anaplerosis. The therapeutic window depends on tumour glutamine dependency, MDSC abundance, and T-cell state.

## Formal notation

GLS1: Gln → Glu (+ NH3) → α-KG (TCA) → m2 polarisation when α-KG/succinate high.

## Variants

- GLS1 inhibition (CB-839/[[foundations/cb-839-telaglenastat|telaglenastat]]).
- SLC1A5 inhibition ([[foundations/slc1a5-asct2-glutamine-transporter|SLC1A5/ASCT2]]).
- GLUL ablation in macrophages → M1 shift.

## When to use

- Designing GLS1-inhibitor + ICB trials.
- Selecting tumour types for SLC1A5-targeted therapy (liver cancer, breast cancer).

## Known limitations

- Tumour metabolic rewiring via glucose anaplerosis bypasses GLS1 inhibition.

## Open problems

- Selective glutamine modulation that suppresses tumour/MDSC/M2 without impairing effector T cells.

## Key papers

- Ricci 2025 comprehensive review.

## My understanding

The glutamine axis is the cleanest example of asymmetric tumour-immune dependency — a perfect substrate for combination therapy design once tumour glutamine dependency is biomarker-confirmed.
