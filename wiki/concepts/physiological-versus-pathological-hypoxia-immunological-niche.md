## Definition

A conceptual framework distinguishing two ends of a microenvironmental hypoxia spectrum at focal sites of immune activity ("immunological niches"). **Physiological hypoxia** is sustained, moderate and spatially ordered — a structural feature of healthy tissues (bone marrow, germinal centres, placenta, intestinal mucosa, renal medulla, retina) that supports immune homeostasis. **Pathological hypoxia** is severe, chaotic and often intermittent — arising in tumours and chronically inflamed, infected or ischaemic tissues — and drives immune cell dysregulation and disease.

## Intuition

The same oxygen-sensing machinery (HIF, PHD/FIH hydroxylases) operates in both settings; what differs is the *temporal, spatial and dynamic pattern* of the hypoxic stimulus and the accompanying niche features (inflammatory mediators, oxidative stress, gasotransmitters, metabolites). These determine the degree and kinetics of HIF activation and therefore whether the outcome is beneficial (barrier protection, controlled development) or harmful (tumour angiogenesis, tissue damage, bioenergetic crisis).

## Formal notation

niche hypoxia → {pattern, degree, co-mediators} → HIF activation profile → immune effector outcome ∈ {homeostatic … pathological}

## Variants

- Physiological immunological niches: bone marrow, germinal centre, placenta/fetus, intestinal mucosa, renal medulla, retina, carotid body.
- Pathological immunological niches: tumour, chronically inflamed tissue, infected tissue, ischaemic tissue.
- Intermediate states span the continuum between the two extremes.

## Comparison

Contrasts physiological, ordered oxygen gradients (e.g. the intestinal crypt–villus axis, germinal-centre light zones) with the unstructured, intermittent hypoxia of disordered tumour vasculature; see [[intermittent-versus-sustained-hypoxia-inflammation]].

## When to use

Use when reasoning about whether tissue hypoxia is supporting normal immune function or driving pathology — and therefore whether HIF should be activated or inhibited therapeutically.

## Known limitations

- The dichotomy is a deliberate reductionist simplification of a continuous spectrum.
- Boundaries are context- and cell-type-dependent; the same niche can shift between regimes (e.g. mucosa from physiological hypoxia to inflammatory hypoxia).

## Open problems

- Quantitative thresholds (pO₂, duration, oscillation frequency) that separate homeostatic from pathological HIF responses in vivo.

## My understanding

The organizing idea of the Taylor–Colgan review and a useful lens across the wiki's hypoxia corpus: hypoxia is not intrinsically pro- or anti-inflammatory; its immunological consequence is set by the niche.
