# Alveolar macrophage PPARγ-driven lipid catabolism

Alveolar macrophages (AMs) — fetal-liver-derived, CD11c⁺SiglecF⁺CXC3R1⁻ — are committed to **lipid catabolism and cholesterol handling** because their homeostatic task is clearance of pulmonary surfactant (~90% lipid). The signature transcription factor **PPARγ** is induced by epithelial GM-CSF (CSF2) and TGFβ during postnatal AM development; PPARγ⁻/⁻ AMs accumulate lipids, lose identity, and produce **pulmonary alveolar proteinosis**. The PPARγ program is reinforced by **BACH2** (lipid catabolism/transport), **LXRα/β**/ABCG1 (cholesterol efflux to HDL), **C/EBPβ** (lipid-handling genes), and **VHL/HIF** oxygen-sensing; SREBP1/2 (mTOR targets) are kept in check — fatostatin or rapamycin lowers AM numbers. Loss of any of these collapses surfactant clearance; PPARγ-, LXR-, or statin-based interventions ameliorate proteinosis preclinically.

Bioenergetically, AMs show **high basal OXPHOS** on glucose, glutamine, and fatty acids with **low glycolysis** — opposite to LPS-stimulated BMDMs — and this profile is imposed by the lung niche.
