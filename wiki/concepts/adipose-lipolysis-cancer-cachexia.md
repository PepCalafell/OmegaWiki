## Definition

Enhanced hydrolysis of stored triglycerides in adipose tissue to free fatty acids and glycerol, driving the adipose-wasting arm of cancer-associated cachexia (CAC). In human CAC, lipolysis-driven shrinkage of adipocytes (smaller size, unchanged number) is supported by elevated plasma glycerol/FFA and by stronger lipolytic responses to catecholamines and natriuretic peptides via hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL). Adipose triglyceride lipase (ATGL) has been implicated as primary mediator in mouse CAC models but human evidence is limited and inconsistent.

## Intuition

Adipose tissue in CAC is broken down faster than it is rebuilt. Catecholamines (epinephrine, norepinephrine) and natriuretic peptides activate β-adrenergic and NPRA receptors, raising cAMP, activating PKA, and phosphorylating HSL and perilipin — releasing fatty acids. Inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6) add further lipolytic drive. The result is net negative fat balance even when caloric intake is preserved.

## Formal notation

- Key enzymes: ATGL (PNPLA2; first step TAG → DAG), HSL (LIPE; DAG → MAG), MAG-lipase (MGLL; MAG → glycerol+FFA).
- Stimulators: catecholamines (β-AR/cAMP/PKA), natriuretic peptides (NPRA/cGMP/PKG), glucocorticoids, growth hormone, TNF-α, IL-6, lactate.
- Inhibitor: insulin (via PDE3B → cAMP degradation + HSL dephosphorylation).
- Biomarkers: plasma glycerol, FFA, adipocyte size on histology.

## Variants

- SAT-dominated lipolysis (the human-CAC primary signal).
- VAT-dominated reduced lipogenesis (separate adipogenic gene-program downregulation).
- Tumour-proximal adipose: locally reduced lipid anabolism (Dahlman et al.).
- WAT browning hypothesis (UCP1-driven REE elevation) — proposed in mice, contested in humans.

## Comparison

vs muscle wasting: lipolysis is more uniformly observed in human CAC than the proteolysis story, with smaller-but-not-fewer adipocytes a reproducible phenotype. Muscle wasting evidence (UPS markers) is more conflicting.

vs simple weight loss: in CAC, fat loss can occur with relatively preserved muscle (early CAC) or with prominent muscle loss (advanced CAC) — the body-compartment subtype framework matters.

## When to use

When mechanistic claims about adipose wasting in CAC are made, or when evaluating anti-lipolytic candidate therapies (β-AR antagonists, ATGL inhibitors).

## Known limitations

- ATGL primacy in mouse CAC has not been clearly recapitulated in human CAC.
- Conflicting evidence on whether impaired lipogenesis (reduced) also contributes — appears tumour-proximal but not systemic.
- WAT browning evidence in humans is conflicting (Petruzzelli vs 14,134-patient cohort).
- Tissue-depot heterogeneity (SAT vs VAT vs intermuscular) is rarely controlled across studies.

## Open problems

- Are β-AR antagonists (e.g., specific β3 antagonists) viable for human CAC, or do they trade adipose preservation against host energy demand?
- Does WAT browning contribute MEANINGFULLY to human CAC REE, or is it a minor/artefactual contributor (current consensus: probably the latter)?
- How does adipose lipolysis interact with the one-carbon metabolism axis (NNMT in adipose) described in [[papers/multi-omics-profiling-cachexia-targeted-tissues]]?

## My understanding

The most reproducible human-CAC phenotype on the adipose side. The therapeutic question is whether you can dampen lipolysis without making the patient hypoglycaemic or undermining anti-tumour responses — currently unresolved.
