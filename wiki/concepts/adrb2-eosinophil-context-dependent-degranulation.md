## Definition

A model in which Adrb2 signalling on eosinophils produces opposite outcomes depending on cellular and pathological context: anti-inflammatory in cytokine-primed eosinophils via Gs–cAMP; proinflammatory (degranulation, granule-protein release) in AD-context dermal eosinophils via β-arrestin-2. Determined empirically in Tian 2026, where Adrb2 agonists (salmeterol, tulobuterol) drive dose-dependent Epx release from AD-mouse eosinophils ex vivo, and eosinophil-specific Adrb2 KO blocks stress-aggravated dermatitis in vivo.

## Intuition

The literature has long disagreed on β2-agonists in allergic inflammation: salmeterol pre-treatment reduces serum ECP/Epx in asthma (Pedersen 1993), yet β2-agonists can increase sputum eosinophil counts and granule proteins (Aldridge 2002). The context-dependent branching of Adrb2 — Gs-cAMP (anti-inflammatory) vs β-arrestin-2 (proinflammatory) — provides a mechanistic frame for the conflict.

## Variants

- **Salmeterol** — long-acting Adrb2 agonist; degranulates AD-mouse blood eosinophils ex vivo dose-dependently.
- **Tulobuterol** — short-acting Adrb2 agonist; same outcome.
- **Epx^iCre-Adrb2^fl/fl** — eosinophil-specific Adrb2 KO; protects from RHS-driven AD exacerbation.

## Comparison

Distinct from the Adrb2-on-T-cell suppression model (Moriyama 2018) and from Adrb2-on-bone-marrow-progenitor mobilisation models. Open question: whether the same context-dependent branching governs eosinophil function in primary tissue from human AD patients.
