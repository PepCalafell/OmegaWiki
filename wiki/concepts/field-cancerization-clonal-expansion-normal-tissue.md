## Definition
Field cancerization is the observation that large stretches of phenotypically normal epithelium are colonized by clonally expanded cells carrying cancer-driver mutations, generating a patchwork of mutant clones from which a small subset eventually progresses to invasive cancer. Examples include TP53-mutant clones occupying 5–10% of normal oesophageal epithelium by middle age and 15–30% beyond age 70, against a <1% lifetime risk of oesophageal cancer.

## Intuition
Driver mutations are common but, by themselves, insufficient for cancer. Selection landscapes in normal tissue allow mutant clones to outcompete wild-type neighbours, but additional events (further mutations, ecDNA, microenvironmental change) are required to cross into malignancy. Carcinogen-induced models (DEN, 4NQO) recapitulate this patchwork of clones.

## Variants
- Spatially continuous field (oesophageal, oral SCC)
- Spatially scattered mutant mosaic (skin, blood)
- Carcinogen-induced field (DEN, 4NQO, DMBA, PM2.5)

## Known limitations
The factors driving fitness differences between mutant clones in normal tissue are still poorly understood; relationship between clone size and progression risk is not monotonic.
