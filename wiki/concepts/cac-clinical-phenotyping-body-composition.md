## Definition

The integrated assessment of cancer-associated cachexia (CAC) that combines body-composition imaging (CT-L3 SAT/VAT/SKM), patient-reported outcomes (FAACT, PG-SGA), clinician-reported outcomes (ECOG, KPS), and objective functional measures (handgrip strength, 6MWT, stairs climb). No single modality captures CAC fully; the trial-design field is converging on multi-modality endpoints stratified by body-composition subtype.

## Intuition

CAC is heterogeneous; a single endpoint (e.g., weight loss) misses the dimension being targeted (function, appetite, muscle, fat) and is not necessarily clinically meaningful to the patient. Multi-modal phenotyping enables matching the right intervention to the right deficit and provides multiple opportunities to detect treatment benefit.

## Formal notation

- PROs: FAACT (incl. Anorexia/Cachexia Subscale, ACS), EORTC QLQ-C30, PG-SGA.
- CROs: KPS (0-100), ECOG PS (0-5).
- Function: handgrip strength (HGS, dynamometer), 6MWT, stairs climb, gait speed; digital health (wearables).
- Body composition: CT-L3 SAT/VAT/SKM areas; DEXA total lean mass; BIA estimates.
- Biochemistry: CRP, albumin, IL-6, hemoglobin.

## Variants

- Combination scoring tools: CASCO (Argilés), Zhou staging score, mGPS (Silva, CRP+albumin).
- Digital phenotyping via wearable trackers (emerging continuous endpoint).
- Hypothalamic fMRI as an experimental neuroimaging endpoint for anorexia.

## Comparison

- vs weight loss alone: weight loss is too crude; fails in sarcopenic obesity and pre-CAC.
- vs CT-only: CT misses appetite, fatigue, functional status.
- vs PRO-only: PROs subject to patient bias; not all patients can complete questionnaires.

## When to use

Always — modern CAC trials should report a multi-modal endpoint set rather than weight loss as a single primary outcome.

## Known limitations

- Lack of consensus on which combination of endpoints is "the" CAC phenotype.
- HGS sensitivity to drug effect varies across trials (often non-significant when 6MWT/ECOG are positive).
- Wearable-derived endpoints are not yet regulatory-validated.
- PRO completion declines in late-stage disease, biasing endpoint availability.

## Open problems

- Which endpoint set should be prioritized for which mechanism-of-action?
- How should clinically meaningful change be defined (minimum clinically important difference) per endpoint?
- Can digital phenotyping replace clinic-based functional tests?

## My understanding

The pragmatic complement to molecular subtypes. The next decade of CAC trials will need to reconcile body-composition-defined subtypes (TRACERx style) with PRO/function endpoints to avoid endpoint-drug mismatch.
