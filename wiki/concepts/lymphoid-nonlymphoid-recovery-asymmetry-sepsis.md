## Definition

After sublethal LPS endotoxemia, nonlymphoid tissues (heart, lung, kidney, brain, liver) return to transcriptional steady state within 5 days, whereas lymphoid tissues (spleen, thymus, lymph nodes, bone marrow) do not, providing a transcriptional substrate for long-term sepsis-induced immunosuppression in survivors.

## Intuition

Survivors of sepsis carry long-lasting immune defects; the asymmetric resolution between organ classes localises where the residue lives — lymphoid compartments. This reframes "sepsis-induced immunosuppression" as a tissue-level recovery failure detectable in bulk transcriptomes.

## When to use

Use when discussing post-sepsis immunosuppression, late mortality after ICU discharge, or designing recovery-phase interventions.

## Open problems

- Cell-type identity of the persistent perturbation in each lymphoid tissue
- Whether antibiotic-treated CLP shows the same asymmetry as untreated CLP
- Whether IL-10/GDF15-like tissue-protective factors counteract the asymmetry
