## Definition

The AHR canonical pathway: agonist binding to the cytosolic AHR–HSP90–AIP–p23–c-Src complex induces conformational change, AIP dissociation, exposure of the nuclear-localisation signal, and importin-β-/transportin-mediated nuclear translocation. In the nucleus, AHR releases HSP90 and heterodimerises with ARNT; the AHR–ARNT complex binds 5′-TNGCGTG-3′ XRE/DRE motifs and recruits coactivators (p300) to drive transcription of canonical target genes including CYP1A1/A2/B1, NQO1, AHRR, TIPARP, IL-10, IL-21, IL-22.

## Intuition

Think of AHR as a chemical sensor with built-in dampers: agonist binding sends it to the nucleus, but the very genes it activates (CYP1A1 to clear ligand, AHRR to compete for ARNT, TIPARP to ADP-ribosylate AHR) shut signalling down. This is why brief endogenous ligands like FICZ produce transient signals while TCDD-like persistent ligands escape feedback and cause toxicity.

## Variants

- ARNT-restricted canonical (XRE/DRE-bound) — main pathway
- AHRR-competitive limiting feedback
- HIF-1α competing for ARNT under hypoxia (cross-talk node)
- Phosphorylation-regulated importin-β interactions (PKC sites in AHR)

## When to use

- When interpreting CYP1A1 induction as a biomarker of AHR target engagement
- When designing AHR ligands with controlled duration (CYP1A1-substrate ligands give self-limited responses)
- When studying AHR signalling in barrier tissues (IL-22 / antimicrobial peptide axis)

## Known limitations

- Many AHR effects (E3 ligase, NF-κB crosstalk) bypass this pathway → see [[ahr-non-canonical-signalling]]
- ARNT availability links AHR to hypoxia via competition with HIF-1α — pathway output is context-dependent

## Open problems

- Quantitative kinetics of AHR–ARNT vs HIF-1α–ARNT competition in hypoxic tumours
- High-resolution structures of full-length AHR in the cytosolic vs nuclear states
