## Definition
The cell of origin is the single ancestral somatic cell that acquires the initiating cancer-driver alteration(s) and through positive selection and clonal expansion seeds the entire malignant lineage. The cancer-initiating cell is the operationally identified progenitor that initiates the tumour in an experimental model; in many systems these are tissue-specific adult stem cells, although non-stem cells can acquire stem-like features and serve as origins.

## Intuition
Recombinase-based lineage tracing in GEMMs is the gold standard for identifying these cells: tissue-specific Cre drivers conditionally activate oncogenes and label progeny with fluorescent reporters, allowing forward tracking from the initiating event.

## Variants
- Resident tissue stem cells (Lgr5+ intestinal, Tff2+ pancreatic, Lgr6+ skin, AT2 lung, prostate progenitors)
- Non-stem cells acquiring stem-like signatures upon driver acquisition
- Inflammation- or damage-reprogrammed differentiated cells

## When to use
When attributing early-cancer phenotypes to specific cell lineages and designing models or therapies that target initiating populations.
