## Definition

Tumour-derived lactate, exported with H+ via MCT1/MCT4, acidifies the TME and inhibits cytotoxic T- and NK-cell proliferation and effector function while FOXP3+ Tregs (and many myeloid cells) are spared, producing an asymmetric immunosuppressive milieu.

## Intuition

It's not just the lactate molecule, it's the cotransported proton. Neutralising TME pH (bicarbonate, MCT/LDH inhibitors, SLC4A4 blockade in PDAC) can functionally re-arm CD8 and NK cells.

## Formal notation

Lactate−/H+ ⇌ lactate−/H+ symport via MCT1/MCT4 → TME pH ~6.0–6.8.

## Variants

- Glycolytic tumours: dominant MCT4-driven efflux.
- TAM-specific lactate sensing: see [[concepts/lactate-driven-tam-m2-polarization]].
- pH-rescuing interventions: sodium bicarbonate, SLC4A4 inhibition.

## Comparison

- Distinct from but cooperative with [[concepts/lactate-driven-tam-m2-polarization]] (TAM polarisation by lactate).

## When to use

- Designing pH-normalisation adjuvants for ICB.

## Known limitations

- Lactate also serves as a beneficial fuel for certain immune subsets.
- Systemic acid-base perturbation has narrow therapeutic window.

## Open problems

- Local pH-normalisation drug-delivery strategies.
- Lactate signalling (lactylation) vs acidosis: which contributes more to immunosuppression?

## Key papers

- Ricci 2025; Apostolova & Pearce review; PDAC SLC4A4 study.

## My understanding

Lactate-pH is one of the most addressable axes of metabolic immunosuppression — practical pharmacology (bicarbonate, AZD3965-class MCT inhibitors) already exists.
