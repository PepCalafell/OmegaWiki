# SPI-C / NRF2 / BACH1 iron-recycling axis

Red pulp macrophages (RPMs), Kupffer cells (KCs), and bone-marrow erythroid-island macrophages (EIMs) execute systemic iron recycling. The transcriptional core is the **BACH1 ⊣ SPI-C / NRF2 → HO-1 / ferroportin / ferritin** switch. Phagocytosed erythrocytes deliver heme/iron via CD163 or LRP1; intracellular heme **sequesters BACH1**, releasing repression and allowing SPI-C and NRF2 to induce HO-1 (heme→Fe²⁺ + biliverdin + CO), ferritin (storage), and ferroportin (export to transferrin). In RPMs, SPI-C is induced post-monocyte-to-RPM differentiation by the metabolite heme; in KCs, SPI-C is induced by sinusoidal-endothelial Notch ligands. KCs additionally suppress hepatocyte hepcidin to keep iron flowing out of macrophages. Spi-C loss collapses RPMs and EIMs but spares KCs; HO-1 loss broadly collapses spleen/liver/marrow macrophage populations.

The axis cross-talks with **lipid/cholesterol handling** (LXRα, PPARγ, SREBP) in the same cells, suggesting integrated heme–lipid–cholesterol metabolism for erythrocyte clearance.
