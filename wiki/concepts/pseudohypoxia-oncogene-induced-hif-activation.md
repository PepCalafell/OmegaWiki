## Definition

Pseudohypoxia is the activation of HIF1 (and downstream hypoxia gene program) under normoxic conditions, driven by oncogene activation (MYC, KRAS), loss of tumour suppressors (VHL, PTEN, TP53), or hormone/growth-factor signaling.

## Intuition

In VHL-null renal cancer, HIF is stabilized regardless of O2; in PTEN-null tumours, AKT-driven HIF1 translation is elevated. This produces a hypoxia-like transcriptome without low pO2 and is itself tumorigenic. Distinguishing true intratumoural hypoxia from pseudohypoxia requires isogenic systems decoupling HIF1 activation from O2 availability.

## Variants

- VHL-null pseudohypoxia (ccRCC paradigm)
- PTEN-loss / AKT pseudohypoxia
- MYC–HIF crosstalk (MAX-MXI1 axis)
- TP53-loss pseudohypoxia
- Hormone- or growth-factor–driven HIF1

## When to use

Use to qualify hypoxia-signature scoring in tumours with VHL/PTEN/MYC alterations — the score may reflect oncogene activity rather than O2 limitation.

## Known limitations

Hypoxia transcriptomic signatures cannot, alone, disambiguate hypoxia from pseudohypoxia; physical pO2 or pimonidazole staining is needed for ground truth.

## Open problems

- Quantitative dissection of pseudohypoxic vs true-hypoxic contribution to mutational signatures
- Whether pseudohypoxia produces the same DDR phenotype as true hypoxia

A confounder that any HypoxiaVERSE biomarker work needs to control for. Pseudohypoxic VHL-null ccRCC behaves transcriptionally like hypoxia but the cells are not necessarily oxygen-starved — implications for which DDR phenotypes are recapitulated.
