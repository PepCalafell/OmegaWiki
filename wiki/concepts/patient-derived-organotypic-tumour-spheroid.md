## Definition

PDOTS and the murine analogue MDOTS are 40-100 μm tumour fragments cultured within a collagen gel in the central channel of a three-channel microfluidic device flanked by parallel media channels. They retain autologous B, T cells and myeloid cells (monocytes, DCs, MDSCs, TAMs) and model dynamic immune-TME responses to immunotherapy.

## Intuition

Compared with ALI cultures, PDOTS use smaller fragments and a microfluidic chamber that simplifies imaging and media exchange, at the cost of shorter culture lifespan (1-2 weeks) and smaller per-fragment biomass.

## When to use

- Short- to medium-term ICB testing.
- Small-molecule screens for ICB-response enhancers (CDK4/6 inhibitors, TBK1 inhibitors).
- Glioblastoma fragment cultures (variant in serum-free medium preserves microglia and microvasculature for weeks).

## Known limitations

- Heterogeneous fragments → variable phenotypes.
- Difficult genetic engineering.
- Low throughput.

## Open problems

- Standardized fragment-size control.
- Larger validation cohorts for ICB prediction.

## My understanding

PDOTS occupy the middle ground between MOS (high-throughput, dissociated) and ALI (low-throughput, intact architecture), and have been particularly productive for ICB-combination small-molecule discovery.
