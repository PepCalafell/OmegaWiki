## Definition

AHR activation in dendritic cells (DCs) drives a tolerogenic programme: decreased MHC class II, CD80/CD86, IL-6/IL-12/TNF; increased retinoic acid; induced IDO1/IDO2 (converting Trp → Kyn, sustaining AHR); induced TGFβ and IL-27. The output expands Foxp3⁺ Tregs and IL-10⁺ Tr1 cells while suppressing Th17. The programme is ligand- and context-dependent: TCDD on bone-marrow DCs can instead increase MHC class II and TNF in vitro.

## Intuition

When AHR is activated in DCs by tolerogenic ligands (ITE, FICZ in some contexts, microbial indoles), the DCs present antigen "quietly" and pump out tolerance cytokines, so antigen-specific T cells get pushed to regulatory fates rather than effector fates.

## When to use

- Designing AHR-agonist therapies for autoimmune diseases (EAE/MS, IBD, atopic dermatitis)
- Interpreting why dietary AHR-ligand deprivation impairs Langerhans-cell migration and worsens allergic inflammation

## Known limitations

- Pro-inflammatory DC responses to AHR ligands also exist (BMDC + TCDD)
- Differences between human and mouse DC subsets in AHR responsiveness

## Open problems

- Translating AHR-tolerogenic DC induction to human therapy at scale
- AHR-DC effects on antigen presentation across DC subsets (cDC1, cDC2, pDC, Langerhans)
