## Definition

A two-axis metabolic switch within TAMs in which the relative tone of two TCA-derived metabolites — succinate and itaconate — biases macrophage output. Succinate stabilizes HIF-1α and drives IL-1β (pro-inflammatory); itaconate (produced by IRG1/ACOD1) alkylates KEAP1 to activate NRF2 and tempers type-I IFN responses (anti-inflammatory). Coexistence of both circuits within TAMs creates a predictable "set point" that can be therapeutically shifted.

## Intuition

The concept reframes TAM metabolic reprogramming as a dial, not a switch: blocking IRG1/ACOD1 raises succinate tone (pro-inflammatory bias); KEAP1/NRF2 activators or itaconate mimetics push the opposite way. This is more actionable than the M1/M2 polarization paradigm because it identifies enzymes (IRG1, SDH, NRF2 regulators) as druggable nodes.
