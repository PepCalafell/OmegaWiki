## Definition

Activation of host STING — particularly within CD11c+ dendritic cells — is required for tumor-associated tertiary lymphoid structure formation. STING agonists (ADU-S100, simultaneous LTβR co-activation) induce TLS in murine pancreatic, melanoma, and lung cancer models. TLS presence correlates with patient survival.

## Intuition

The bridge between innate STING activation and the durable adaptive responses needed for clinical efficacy — TLS provide in situ antigen presentation, B-cell responses, and effector T-cell generation. STING-induced TLS is a candidate mechanism for the abscopal effect of intratumoral STING agonism.

## Variants

- ADU-S100-induced TLS in pancreatic cancer (Sawada 2025) and melanoma (Chelvanambi 2021)
- LTβR + STING co-activation (Sawada 2025): induces B-cell responses within TLS
- cGAS overexpression in HSCs increases intratumoral TLS in murine lung cancer (Zhao 2024)
- Host STING requirement, particularly within CD11c+ DCs

## When to use

When interpreting TLS biology in tumors — STING activation status of the TLS-permissive DC compartment is a candidate biomarker. When choosing STING-agonist combination partners, LTβR agonists or B-cell-activating agents may amplify TLS-mediated benefits.

## My understanding

This is the strongest mechanistic link between innate STING activation and the kind of durable adaptive antitumor response clinicians actually need. TLS-centric STING combinations may unlock the durability that monotherapy STING agonism has so far failed to deliver.
