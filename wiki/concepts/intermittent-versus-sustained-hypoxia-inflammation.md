## Definition

The temporal pattern of a hypoxic stimulus — intermittent/cycling versus continuous/sustained — is itself a determinant of the immune outcome. Intermittent hypoxia (e.g. from irregular tumour blood flow, or the oxygen oscillations of obstructive sleep apnoea) tends to drive *greater* inflammatory responses, including NF-κB activation, than sustained hypoxia of comparable depth.

## Intuition

HIF activation is a temporally dynamic response with multiple inputs, not a binary on/off switch. Oscillating reoxygenation generates reactive oxygen species and repeatedly engages stress-responsive transcription (notably NF-κB), so the *kinetics* of hypoxia — how often oxygen returns — can matter as much as the absolute pO₂.

## Formal notation

same mean pO₂, different temporal profile → distinct HIF/NF-κB activation dynamics → intermittent ⇒ inflammation ↑ vs sustained ⇒ adaptive/homeostatic response

## Variants

- Intermittent/cycling: disordered tumour vasculature; obstructive sleep apnoea (adipocyte NF-κB sensitivity).
- Sustained/uniform: established chronic mucosal inflammation (e.g. IBD).

## Comparison

A temporal axis of the broader [[physiological-versus-pathological-hypoxia-immunological-niche]] framework; pathological niches skew toward chaotic, intermittent patterns.

## When to use

Use when the pattern (not just the level) of hypoxia is relevant — e.g. predicting inflammatory consequences in tumours with unstable perfusion or in cyclic-hypoxia disorders.

## Known limitations

- Mechanistic basis for the differential inflammatory output of intermittent vs sustained hypoxia is incompletely resolved.

## Open problems

- Defining the oscillation frequency/amplitude thresholds that maximise inflammatory signalling.

## My understanding

A reminder that hypoxia is a dynamic signal: reoxygenation cycles, common in pathological niches, are pro-inflammatory in a way that steady hypoxia is not.
