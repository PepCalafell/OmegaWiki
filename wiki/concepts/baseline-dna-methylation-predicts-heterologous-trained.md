## Definition

The finding that an individual's pre-vaccination (baseline) DNA-methylation profile is a strong predictor of how much their heterologous cytokine response — especially IFN-γ — will increase after BCG vaccination, outperforming genetic and protein predictors.

## Intuition

Inter-individual differences in trained-immunity magnitude are partly "written" in the methylome before any stimulus is given: baseline methylation status at certain CpGs facilitates (or limits) the later immune response, making it an intrinsic predictor of vaccine responsiveness.

## Formal notation

Variance-partition of TI(IFN-γ) FC across omics layers (age/sex, PRS, baseline proteins, metabolites, methylation); baseline methylation explains the largest single share, combined model adjusted R² ≈ 0.561.

## Variants

- Cytokine-specific: predictive for IFN-γ (and weakly IL-6), not IL-1β/TNF-α
- Sex-stratified predictive associations (stronger in males)

## Comparison

Contrasts with predictors based on circulating proteins or PRS, which explained less variance.

## When to use

When asking whether vaccine/trained-immunity responsiveness can be forecast from a pre-vaccination molecular profile for personalized medicine.

## Known limitations

41-CpG signal partly cell-composition driven; replication cohort small; young-adult population limits generalizability.

## Open problems

Causal validation that baseline methylation sets the response ceiling; portability across ages and ancestries.

## Key papers

- [[long-term-dna-methylation-changes-mediate]] — identifies 41 baseline CpGs associated with TI(IFN-γ) and shows methylation explains the most variance.

## My understanding

A predictive-biomarker framing of trained immunity: the methylome as a pre-existing readiness state for heterologous IFN-γ responses.
