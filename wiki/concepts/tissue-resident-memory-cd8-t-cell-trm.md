## Definition

Non-circulating CD8+ memory T cells that take up long-term residence in peripheral tissues (gut, lung, skin, brain, tumours). Marker phenotype CD69+CD103+ (Itgae+), with high expression of IFNγ, IRF1, BLIMP1 (PRDM1), BHLHE40, NR4A2, and the AP-1 family (FOSB, ATF3, JUN). High TRM infiltration correlates with better survival in solid tumours.

## Intuition

TRM cells are TEXterm's protective alter ego: similar transcriptome and chromatin, opposite functional fate. They share many TFs (BLIMP1, BHLHE40, NR4A2, HIC1, GFI1) with TEXterm but rewire connections towards TGFβ response, cell adhesion, and IL-2 production rather than catabolism and apoptosis.

## Variants

- Classic CD69+CD103+ TRM with strong tissue adhesion (small-intestinal IEL-like).
- TRM-Itgaelow subset (lower CD103, higher Ifng/Irf1) — early or transitional residents.

## When to use

Whenever interpreting CD8+ T cell phenotypes in barrier tissues or solid tumours, or designing adoptive cell therapies aimed at tumour residence rather than circulation.

## Known limitations

Distinguishing TRM from TEXterm by transcriptome alone is difficult — both occupy the tissue-resident niche and share key TFs.

## Key papers

- [[atlas-guided-discovery-transcription-factors-cell]] — Chung et al. 2025: KLF6 identified as TRM single-state TF; KLF6 OE produces ~42× more CD69+CD103+ TRM-like cells without exhaustion.

## Open problems

- Can enforced KLF6 expression be combined with CAR-T to boost tumour-resident anti-tumour memory?
- Do TRM-selective TFs differ between viral and tumour contexts?
