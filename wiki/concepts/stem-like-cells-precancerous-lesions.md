## Definition
A consistent finding across single-cell omics of precancerous lesions (colon polyps, gastric, lung adenocarcinoma, cervical, prostate, skin) is the emergence of cell populations expressing stem-cell-associated transcriptional programmes (e.g. WNT-target genes LGR5, OLFM4, ASCL2, AXIN2, RNF43, EPHB2). These can derive from clonal expansion and selection of bona fide tissue stem cells or from non-stem cells acquiring stem-like signatures.

## Intuition
Acquisition of stem-like signatures may be a permissive state that confers the long-term self-renewal capacity necessary for accumulating subsequent (epi)genetic events and for adaptability across the tipping point.

## When to use
When designing single-cell profiling, lineage-tracing, or targeted-therapy strategies for precancerous tissue, especially in epithelial cancers.

## Open problems
- Distinguishing tissue stem cells from non-stem cells with acquired stem-like signatures functionally
- Whether stem-like state per se is targetable for cancer interception
