## Definition

A therapeutic concept: IL-1β neutralization (alone or with anti-PD-1) administered during **precancerous** phases reduces KAC abundance, lung adenoma/adenocarcinoma volume, and pro-tumor inflammatory cytokines — whereas the same intervention is ineffective in established invasive LUAD.

## Intuition

The IL1B-IL1R1 epithelial-proinflammatory niche is **stage-specific**: it underwrites the early KAC→precursor transition, but dissipates by the time invasive LUAD is established. Therefore IL-1β blockade should be deployed in pre-invasive screening cohorts (e.g., AAH/AIS surveillance, CT-detected indeterminate nodules), not late-stage NSCLC.

## Comparison

- **CANTOS** (atherosclerosis cohort): canakinumab reduced incident lung cancer → consistent with precancer interception.
- **CANOPY-1/2/A** (advanced NSCLC, chemo–ICI ± canakinumab, adjuvant): no benefit → consistent with niche dissipation in invasive disease.
- **Anti-IL-1β + anti-PD-1** in NNK Gprc5a−/− mice: combination > monotherapy, particularly in the 3→7 month interception window.
- **Syngeneic LUAD model**: anti-IL-1β has no effect — confirms timing dependence.

## When to use

- High-risk lung-cancer screening cohorts (smokers, COPD, CT-detected AAH/AIS).
- Trials enriched for precursor-stage niche biomarkers (IL1R1⁺ epithelial signature, IL1B⁺ macrophage density).

## Open problems

- What is the optimal interception biomarker? IL1R1 IF on screening biopsies? Spatial niche signature on resected nodules?
- Risk-benefit of chronic IL-1β blockade in high-risk asymptomatic cohorts.
- Does combination with anti-PD-1 raise unacceptable autoimmunity risk in non-cancer prevention populations?
