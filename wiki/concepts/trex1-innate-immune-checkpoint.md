## Definition

TREX1 is an adaptive negative-feedback regulator of cGAS-STING. Induced downstream of STING-IFN signaling, it degrades cytosolic dsDNA and quenches the response. Tumor cells upregulate TREX1 to restrain STING-IFN activation — functionally analogous to PD-L1 on the adaptive arm.

## Intuition

The natural "off switch" for cGAS — when chronically engaged in tumors, it becomes a therapeutic target. Pharmacological TREX1 inhibition restores cytosolic-dsDNA-driven STING activation without externally adding an agonist, leveraging the tumor's own DNA damage as the stimulus.

## Variants

- Tumor-intrinsic TREX1 (Tani 2024 Cancer Discov): inhibition restores STING-IFN, recruits T and NK cells, sensitizes to anti-PD-1
- Systemic TREX1 inactivation (Marinkovic 2025; Xing 2025 Cancer Res): selective TME inflammation, spares T cells from STING-induced cytotoxicity, safe in preclinical models
- Combination with radiation, radioligand therapy, or DNA-damaging ADCs amplifies STING activation while restricting TREX1 inhibition's autoimmune risk

## When to use

For tumors with intact cGAS and STING expression but limited basal IFN output — TREX1 inhibition unleashes STING-IFN without external agonist delivery. Particularly attractive for radiation-combination strategies.

## Open problems

- Risk of inducing selective pressure for epigenetic cGAS/STING silencing as a resistance mechanism
- Dose/schedule optimization to balance antitumor effect against systemic interferonopathy (Aicardi-Goutières-like phenotype)

## My understanding

The "innate immune checkpoint" framing parallels PD-1/PD-L1 conceptually and is one of the cleanest therapeutic implications of the cGAS-STING-pathway field — TREX1 inhibitors should enter the clinic over the next 2-3 years and represent the most likely first STING-pathway-directed therapy to show efficacy in humans.
