## Definition

NK-f is a distinct NK cell polarization state induced predominantly by IL-18 in vivo. It is characterized by >1,000 upregulated genes (an order of magnitude more than IL-2/IL-12/IL-15-induced NK-e), partial overlap with IL-2/12/15/IFN states (Gzmb, Xcl1), and unique induction of growth/proliferation (Myc), DC recruitment (Xcl1), myeloid maturation (Csf2/GM-CSF), differentiation regulators (Kit, Batf) and TNF/NF-κB (Traf1, Cd83).

## Intuition

Reframes IL-18 from "IFNγ-inducing factor" to a master driver of NK cell polyfunctionality combining cytotoxicity with paracrine immune-orchestration capacity. Suggests IL-18-based NK therapeutics may engage broader immune programs than IL-2/IL-15.

## Variants

- IL-18 alone (the canonical NK-f)
- IL-18 + IL-12 synergy (classical IFNγ induction; may partially overlap)
- Engineered IL-18 (decoy-resistant IL-18 "DR-18") in preclinical immunotherapy

## When to use

Cite when discussing IL-18 cancer immunotherapy candidates, NK-cell engagers, or any analysis where NK cells show high Myc/Xcl1/Csf2 expression alongside cytotoxic markers.

## Open problems

- Persistence of NK-f state without continued IL-18 stimulation
- Translation to human NK cells
- Interaction with IL-18BP decoy axis
