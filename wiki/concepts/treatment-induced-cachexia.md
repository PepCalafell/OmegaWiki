## Definition

The bidirectional relationship between anticancer therapy (chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, radiotherapy) and cancer-associated cachexia (CAC): therapy can INDUCE or EXACERBATE body-compartment loss (treatment-associated cachexia), and pretreatment body composition predicts toxicity and survival outcomes. The pattern of body-compartment loss is regimen- and cancer-type-specific (e.g., FOLFIRINOX → greater SKM loss in male PDAC; GEM/NAB → greater adipose loss in both sexes).

## Intuition

Treatment is not neutral with respect to body composition. Cytotoxic agents distributed in LBM hit harder in patients with low SKM, raising toxicity; treatment-related side effects (mucositis, nausea, anorexia, microbiome disruption) drive intake-driven weight loss; conversely, weight loss after treatment may also reflect non-response (tumour progression) rather than treatment side effects alone. Pretreatment SKM stratification thus has dual value: it predicts toxicity AND tracks treatment response.

## Formal notation

- Toxicity correlates: low pretreatment BMI / SKM area / muscle attenuation → higher dose-limiting toxicity → dose reduction or termination → poorer outcomes.
- Regimen-specific patterns:
  - FOLFIRINOX (PDAC): SKM loss > adipose loss, predominantly male.
  - GEM/NAB (PDAC): adipose loss > SKM loss, both sexes.
  - Platinum-based (lung): SKM-confined loss.
  - Immunotherapy / melanoma: loss in all compartments.
- Dosing reform proposal: replace body surface area with LBM-based dosing (limited adoption).

## Variants

- Treatment-induced cachexia in previously stable patients.
- Treatment-exacerbated CAC overlaid on baseline CAC.
- Immunotherapy-related cachexia (mechanism unclear; possibly immune-related adverse events + checkpoint biology).
- Radiotherapy-induced regional muscle/adipose loss (e.g., head and neck).

## Comparison

vs tumour-progression-driven cachexia: difficult to dissociate clinically, since both happen on the same timescale.
vs anorexia-only cachexia: treatment effects can be intake-driven (mucositis) or tumour-driven (response failure).

## When to use

When evaluating CAC in patients ON anticancer therapy, when designing treatment regimens with awareness of body-composition effects, or when interpreting weight loss during treatment as response vs side effect.

## Known limitations

- Retrospective cohort designs dominate — few prospective trials with predefined body-composition endpoints.
- Mechanistic separation of "treatment effect" vs "non-response" requires concurrent tumour-response and body-composition data.
- LBM-based dosing has not been widely adopted despite supporting evidence.

## Open problems

- Should chemotherapy dosing be reformulated by LBM rather than BSA?
- Can pretreatment body composition be a TRIAL STRATIFICATION variable in cachexia interventions, or for adjusting the chemotherapy regimen itself?
- What is the mechanism of immunotherapy-induced cachexia, and does it differ from chemotherapy-induced cachexia?
