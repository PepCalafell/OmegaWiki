## Definition

Hypoxia transcriptionally, translationally and epigenetically suppresses key DNA repair pathways — homologous recombination (BRCA1, BRCA2, RAD51), mismatch repair (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6) and base excision repair — producing a transient "contextual DDR deficiency" that phenocopies germline BRCA/Lynch states without underlying mutations.

## Intuition

Chronic hypoxia (not cycling hypoxia) downregulates BRCA1/BRCA2/RAD51 mRNA and protein, producing PARPi sensitivity and SBS3/CN17 signatures. Independently, hypoxia silences MMR promoters in a HIF1α-dependent manner, yielding MSI and SBS6/SBS21/ID2 signatures. BER is suppressed at the translational level, accumulating residual base damage.

## Variants

- HR deficiency under chronic hypoxia (most consistent)
- MMR silencing (HIF1α-dependent, cell-line variable: HCT116 yes, HeLa no)
- BER suppression (chronic hypoxia, translational)
- NER and NHEJ effects under hypoxia: inconclusive and pathway-dependent

## When to use

Cite this concept whenever a tumour displays HRD/MMR-deficient signatures or PARPi-like vulnerability without germline DDR mutations, and a hypoxia score is elevated.

## Known limitations

Tissue and cell-line dependence; reversible upon reoxygenation; molecular mechanisms (transcription vs translation vs epigenetics) differ between BRCA2, RAD51 and MLH1.

## Open problems

- Quantitative kinetics of repair-pathway suppression as a function of pO2 and duration
- Whether transient hypoxia-induced HRD opens a therapeutic window for PARPi in non-BRCA-mutant tumours
- Mechanistic dissection of NER/NHEJ regulation under hypoxia

## My understanding

This is the mechanistic linchpin of the hypoxia → genomic-instability axis. For HypoxiaVERSE thesis work, this concept anchors why hypoxic subregions can mimic HRD/MSI without canonical DDR mutations, which has direct implications for biomarker-guided therapy stratification.
