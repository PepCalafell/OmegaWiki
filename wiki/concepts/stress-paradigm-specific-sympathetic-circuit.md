## Definition

Different acute psychological stressors recruit non-overlapping sympathetic subtypes, producing distinct peripheral immune signatures rather than a generic stress response. In mice, repeated high-platform stress (RHS) preferentially activates Pdyn⁺ skin-innervating sympathetic neurons and triggers eosinophil-mediated dermatitis exacerbation; restraint stress (PPR) and cage-change stress trigger HPA axis corticosterone elevation comparable to RHS but produce minimal or no eosinophilic dermatitis exacerbation despite similar overall stress hormone levels.

## Intuition

A single "fight-or-flight" lumped category obscures that the sympathetic outflow is wired into modality-specific channels (vasomotor, pilomotor, sudomotor, skin-immune). The stressor's modality biases which channel fires hardest. Therapeutic implication: targeting the circuit, not the global stress hormone, may decouple desired physiological adaptations from pathological inflammation.

## Variants

- **RHS** — sympathetic-dominant; activates Pdyn⁺ neurons; drives eosinophilic dermatitis.
- **PPR (restraint)** — mixed HPA + sympathetic; weak dermatitis effect.
- **Cage-change stress** — HPA-only; no dermatitis effect.

## Comparison

Generalises Zhang 2020 (Nature) and Poller 2022 (Nature) — both reported circuit-specific sympathetic outputs in CNS-targeting and bone marrow leukocyte trafficking respectively — to the skin/eosinophil arm. Distinct from generalised "stress axis" models that conflate all stressors.
