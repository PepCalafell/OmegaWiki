## Definition

STAT1 is expressed as two splice isoforms: full-length STAT1α (with the C-terminal transactivation domain) and the truncated STAT1β (lacking the TAD). Isoform-specific mouse models (Stat1a-only, Stat1b-only) reveal that the isoforms have **distinct and partially opposing** transcriptional effects under homeostasis, with STAT1α more closely resembling the full STAT1-KO transcriptional phenotype than STAT1β does.

## Intuition

STAT1 is not a single TF — it is two. The conventional reading "STAT1β is a dominant-negative" is incomplete: STAT1β can contribute to gamma-interferon-dependent innate immunity (Semper 2014), and in homeostatic immune cells the two isoforms produce non-redundant, partially opposing transcriptomes.

## Variants

- STAT1α — full-length, transactivation-competent.
- STAT1β — truncated, often acts as a modulator; produces a distinct (not dominant-negative) gene-regulatory signature under homeostasis.

## When to use

- Interpreting STAT1 KO phenotypes — beware aggregating effects of both isoforms.
- Designing JAK-STAT models in which isoform specificity matters (immunodeficiency variants, autoimmunity).
- Reading the context-deprivation data: IFN-β rescues most context-deprivation effects except STAT1β-dependent ones, implicating STAT1β as a homeostasis-specific (non-IFN-rescuable) regulator.

## Known limitations

- Isoform-specific mouse models are rare; most published STAT1 KO studies cannot disambiguate.
- Mechanistic basis for the partial opposition between isoforms is not fully resolved.

## Open problems

- Identify isoform-specific chromatin binding sites and protein partners.
- Test STAT1β-specific roles in human immune cells, where the literature is even sparser.
