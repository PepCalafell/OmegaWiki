## Definition
A class of preclinical strategies in which cancer is initiated within 3D organoids (or transplanted stem cells) by introducing oncogenic drivers, carcinogen exposure or oncogenic viral genomes. Cancer-initiating cells then undergo ex vivo or orthotopic in vivo evolution, recapitulating early carcinogenesis with longitudinal sampling and lineage-tracing access.

## Intuition
Organoids complement GEMMs by allowing the entire carcinogenesis trajectory to be reconstructed in a human-cell setting closer to a healthy organ than monolayer or PDX cultures, and at lower cost and ethical burden.

## Variants
- Genetically engineered organoid (sequential driver introduction)
- Mutant organoid orthotopic transplantation
- iPSC-derived organoid with patient-specific background
- Optogenetics-controlled mini-colons (Opto-Cre)
- Stem-cell graft models (HSC, NSC)

## Known limitations
Most organoid systems lack the full stromal and immune microenvironment unless explicitly cocultured or transplanted; carcinogenesis timelines may not match in vivo dynamics.
