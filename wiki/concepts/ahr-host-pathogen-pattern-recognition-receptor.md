## Definition

The AHR-as-PRR concept reframes the aryl hydrocarbon receptor as a non-classical pattern recognition receptor that senses microorganism-associated chemistry — pigments (phthiocol, pyocyanin), quorum-sensing molecules (HHQ, 3-oxo-C12-HSL, 1-hydroxyphenazine), indole metabolites (microbiota Trp catabolites), and fungal/parasite-derived ligands — and elicits antimicrobial gene programmes. Unlike TLRs/NLRs (cell-surface or endosomal protein-binding receptors), AHR-PRR signalling is cytosolic, ligand-promiscuous, and ligand-chemistry-specific in transcriptional output. Originally proposed in 2014 by Moura-Alves and colleagues; consolidated across bacteria, fungi, parasites and viruses in Barreira-Silva 2025.

## Intuition

Think of AHR as a chemical PRR: TLRs see molecular shapes (LPS, flagellin, dsRNA), but AHR "tastes" small organic molecules produced by microbes — phenazines, indoles, naphthoquinones, kynurenines. This lets AHR distinguish microbial states (early growth phase vs late growth phase in Pseudomonas) and integrate host-, diet- and microbe-derived signals into a single transcriptional response.

## When to use

- When framing AHR as a mucosal-immunity / antimicrobial sensor (gut, skin, lung)
- When designing AHR-targeted host-directed therapies for infection
- When discussing convergence of microbiota-derived metabolite signalling with antimicrobial defence
- For evolutionary arguments: the C. elegans AHR-1 sensing P. aeruginosa phenazines anchors the PRR function in pre-vertebrate lineages

## Known limitations

- AHR-PRR responses overlap with — and are not always separable from — TLR/NLR signalling in macrophages and epithelial cells
- The output of AHR-PRR signalling is ligand-, tissue- and host-state-dependent; no single "AHR-PRR signature" exists
- Many AHR ligands proposed in the literature lack rigorous binding evidence (the field commonly relies on CYP1A1 induction alone)

## Open problems

- Whether AHR-PRR signalling is additive or synergistic with TLR/NLR in cells co-stimulated by, e.g., phthiocol + LPS
- Identification of pathogen-derived AHR ligands from parasites (none described yet) and most viruses
- Whether human AHR polymorphisms (rs2066853, V570I) phenocopy mouse Ahrd-threshold biology in infection susceptibility
