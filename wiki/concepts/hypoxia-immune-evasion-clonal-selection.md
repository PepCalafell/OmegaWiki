## Definition

Hypoxic subregions of tumours shield mutator clones from immune elimination via five non-exclusive mechanisms: (1) recruitment and reprogramming of MDSCs, TAMs and Tregs; (2) upregulation of PD-L1 and CD47; (3) ECM-mediated physical/functional T-cell exclusion; (4) adenosine, lactate and acidification of the TME suppressing effector function; (5) inhibition of type-I interferon signalling. Additionally, hypoxia drives T-cell exhaustion.

## Intuition

The paradox: hypoxic tumours have elevated TMB and neoantigens (which predicts ICB response) but are clinically refractory to checkpoint inhibition. Resolution: hypoxia protects high-TMB clones from immune surveillance, so the neoantigens don't translate into productive T-cell killing.

## Variants

- Cell-recruitment immune suppression (MDSCs, TAMs, Tregs)
- Checkpoint molecule upregulation (PD-L1, CD47)
- Metabolic immune suppression (adenosine, lactate)
- Type-I IFN suppression
- T-cell exhaustion within hypoxic niches

## When to use

Explains why hypoxia scores predict ICB resistance, why hypoxic tumours accumulate MSI/HRD-like mutational signatures without immune clearance, and why hypoxia-modifying therapy may sensitize to immunotherapy.

## Known limitations

Mechanisms are correlative; relative contributions per tumour type unresolved; hypoxia-driven T-cell-exhaustion mechanism is unclear.

## Open problems

- Whether reversing hypoxia in situ rescues ICB efficacy
- Which immune-evasion axis dominates per tumour type
- Hypoxic cell fate-mapping in syngeneic models to test whether immune surveillance constrains clonal outgrowth
