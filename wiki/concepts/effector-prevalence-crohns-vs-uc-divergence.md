## Definition

The observation that commensal T3SS effectors are enriched in the gut metagenomes of
individuals with Crohn's disease but depleted in ulcerative colitis, an opposing pattern
that — together with effector targeting of CD- vs UC-associated host proteins — invites
the hypothesis that effectors shift homeostasis to increase Crohn's risk while
decreasing ulcerative colitis risk.

## Intuition

Two IBD subtypes that both show increased Pseudomonadota abundance nonetheless diverge
in effector prevalence, suggesting effector content (not just taxon abundance) carries
disease-relevant information, mirroring the differential efficacy of anti-TNF therapy
(effective in CD, not UC).

## Formal notation

In >800 IBD individuals (504 CD, 302 UC) vs 334 healthy controls, 64 HuMMI effectors
were significantly more prevalent in CD and depleted in UC (FDR < 0.01); network
neighbourhoods were enriched for CD (P = 8.5 × 10⁻⁵) but not UC.

## Variants

Direct disease-protein targeting (e.g. COG6, TNIP1) vs network-neighbourhood
enrichment; metagenomic prevalence vs GWAS-variant enrichment.

## Comparison

Parallels the clinical anti-TNF response dichotomy between CD and UC.

## When to use

When linking commensal effector content to IBD subtype aetiology or therapy response.

## Known limitations

Associative/observational; mechanistic causality in IBD not established.

## Open problems

Do effectors causally raise CD risk and/or protect against UC?
