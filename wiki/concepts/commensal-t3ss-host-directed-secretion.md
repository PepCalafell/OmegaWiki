## Definition

The concept that type III secretion systems (T3SS) — historically framed as virulence
determinants of pathogens — are in fact common among commensal Pseudomonadota of the
healthy human gut, where they constitute an under-appreciated route of direct,
protein-mediated microorganism–host communication rather than (only) a pathogenicity
mechanism.

## Intuition

If ~80% of healthy-gut Pseudomonadota carry intact T3SS and inject effectors into host
cells, then "needle-and-syringe" injection is a feature of normal cohabitation, not just
infection — paralleling beneficial T3SS use by plant and insect symbionts.

## Formal notation

T3SS+ strain = genome with a complete, intact T3SS. Host-directed secretion systems
also include T4SS and T6SS.

## Variants

Heterologous-host injection (via Salmonella) vs native-strain secretion (e.g.
Edwardsiella tarda); opportunistic pathobiont vs adaptive-commensal interpretations.

## Comparison

Contrasts with the classical pathogen-centric view of [[type-iii-secretion-system]];
extends plant/insect symbiosis findings to the human gut.

## When to use

When reasoning about microbiome–host molecular communication beyond metabolites and
MAMPs, or about how T3SS prevalence reframes commensal biology.

## Known limitations

Detection tools may miss divergent systems; whether secretion reflects adaptive
commensal function or opportunistic pathobiont stress responses remains open.

## Open problems

Do host cells actively signal to induce commensal T3SS, as plant hosts do? What cues
activate commensal secretion in vivo?

## Key papers

- [[effector-host-interactome-map-links-type]] — shows ~79–80% of healthy-gut
  Pseudomonadota encode complete T3SS and that commensal effectors are injected into
  human cells.

## My understanding

A genuine conceptual reframing: secretion systems as modulators of mutualism,
competition and host physiology, not only as virulence factors.
