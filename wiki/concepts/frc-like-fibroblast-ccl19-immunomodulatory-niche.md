## Definition

The F3 FRC-like fibroblast is a human skin fibroblast subtype transcriptomically resembling lymphoid-organ fibroblastic reticular cells (FRCs), marked by CCL19, CD74, HLA-DRA/DRB1, CH25H, IL33, IL15, TNFSF13B and VCAM1. It localizes to the superficial perivascular region near immune cells and is predicted to maintain a perivascular immune niche by attracting/compartmentalizing immune cells, supporting their survival, and presenting antigen.

## Intuition

Skin contains a fibroblast that behaves like a lymph-node stromal cell: it organizes a local immune compartment (T cells, migrating DCs) in the superficial dermis, an analogue of the T-zone reticular FRC, mediating T–DC interactions via CCL19-CCR7 and CXCL12-CXCR4.

## Formal notation

Markers: CCL19, CXCL12, CH25H (chemoattraction); IL33, IL15, TNFSF13B, VCAM1 (immune survival); CD74, HLA-DRA, HLA-DRB1 (antigen presentation). Activated/disease state: CXCL9+/ADAMDEC1+.

## Variants

Healthy F3 vs disease-activated F3-like (CXCL9+/ADAMDEC1+). Conserved across tissues (lung, intestine T-reticular cells).

## Comparison

Equivalent to intestinal T-reticular cells (an FRC subset) and to mouse Ccl19+ fibroblasts; uniquely abundant in human skin relative to mouse skin.

## When to use

When characterizing immune-organizing stromal cells in non-lymphoid tissue or the superficial perivascular immune infiltrate of inflammatory skin disease.

## Known limitations

Functional roles (antigen presentation, niche maintenance) are predicted from expression and receptor-ligand inference, not functionally proven.

## Open problems

Whether F3 FRC-like fibroblasts drive tertiary lymphoid structure formation in human disease; their causal role in immune-mediated pathology.
