# LXR-dependent function of marginal zone macrophages

Marginal-zone macrophages (MZMs, SIGNR1⁺) and marginal metallophilic macrophages (MMMs, CD169⁺) sit at the white-pulp/red-pulp border of the spleen, capturing blood-borne pathogens. Their development and immune function are controlled by **LXRα/β**, but — unlike LXR's classic role in reverse cholesterol transport in foam-cell macrophages — LXR activity here is **directly required for phagocytic clearance of microbes and apoptotic cells**. LXRα/β-deficient mice show increased susceptibility to bacterial and parasitic infection; intracellular bacteria and apoptotic cells activate LXRs in cultured macrophages, which is required for optimal clearance. The axis is a clean example of nuclear-receptor co-option of a lipid sensor for an immune-effector program.
