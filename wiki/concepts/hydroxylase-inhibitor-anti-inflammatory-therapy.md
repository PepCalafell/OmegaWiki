## Definition

The therapeutic strategy of pharmacologically activating the HIF pathway — by inhibiting the HIF hydroxylases (PHDs and FIH) — to suppress inflammation. Across many preclinical models the net effect of hydroxylase inhibition is profoundly anti-inflammatory, principally through epithelial barrier protection and modulation of immune cell function.

## Intuition

Counterintuitively, switching *on* the hypoxic response (rather than blocking it) calms inflammation in barrier tissues: stabilized epithelial HIF induces barrier-protective genes and tilts immune cells toward resolution. Clinically validated HIF prolyl-hydroxylase inhibitors already exist for renal anaemia, making drug repurposing for immune-related disease plausible — but the effect is context-dependent.

## Formal notation

PHD/FIH inhibitor (e.g. DMOG, roxadustat) → HIF ↑ → barrier-protective genes + immune modulation → inflammation ↓ (context-dependent)

## Variants

- Research tool: DMOG (pan-hydroxylase) — dominates preclinical colitis/ischaemia/infection models.
- Clinical PHD inhibitors: roxadustat (FG-4592), daprodustat, vadadustat — approved/advanced for CKD anaemia.
- Tissue-targeted delivery: gut-targeted AKB-4924, topical/inhaled approaches to limit systemic erythropoiesis.

## Comparison

Opposite therapeutic direction to HIF *inhibitors* (e.g. HIF2α antagonists belzutifan/PT2385) used in renal cancer; the choice depends on whether niche hypoxia is physiological-protective or pathological.

## When to use

Invoke for inflammatory/ischaemic barrier disease (IBD, renal ischaemia–reperfusion, transplant) where boosting HIF is protective.

## Known limitations

- Context-dependent: hydroxylase inhibitors protect against sterile LPS sepsis but *exacerbate* lethal bacterial sepsis (caecal ligation/puncture).
- Systemic HIF activation risks unwanted erythropoiesis and theoretical tumour/angiogenesis promotion.

## Open problems

- Cell-type- and tissue-targeted delivery; isoform-selective (HIF1 vs HIF2; PHD-specific) reagents; human efficacy in inflammatory disease.
