## Definition

In respiratory viral infection, AHR drives pathogenic mucin overproduction in alveolar epithelial cells. The pathway: viral infection → IFN-β and IFN-γ → IDO upregulation → kynurenine production → AHR activation → mucin gene transcription. Pharmacologic AHR antagonism (CH-223191) reduces mucins and ameliorates lung pathology in SARS-CoV-2-infected mice. AHR is also exploited by SARS-CoV-2 (upregulated in nasal swabs/BAL of high-viral-load patients; CH-223191 suppresses replication in vitro) and modulates influenza outcomes in ligand-specific ways (TCDD increases titres, FICZ does not; endothelial AHR can be protective).

## Intuition

Respiratory viruses turn AHR into a mucus pump by exploiting the IFN-IDO-KYN endogenous-agonist axis. Mucin overproduction obstructs gas exchange and exacerbates ARDS. AHR antagonism is therefore an attractive host-directed therapy because (i) it acts on the host, not the virus (no resistance), (ii) CH-223191 is already a validated tool molecule, and (iii) related antagonists (IK-175, BAY-2416964) are in clinical development.

## When to use

- When discussing host-directed antiviral therapy for SARS-CoV-2 or influenza
- When framing IDO-KYN-AHR as a pathology axis (alongside the cancer-immunosuppression KYN-AHR axis)
- For ARDS/mucus-driven respiratory failure mechanisms

## Known limitations

- Predominantly murine evidence; clinical translation in humans not yet shown
- Cell-type-specific outcomes: alveolar epithelium drives pathology, but endothelial AHR can be protective in FluV (ref 126 in source) — context matters

## Open problems

- Whether CH-223191 / IK-175 / BAY-2416964 reduce ARDS pathology in clinical COVID-19
- Whether the AHR-mucin axis is similarly active in RSV, parainfluenza, or chronic bronchitis
- How to spatially restrict AHR antagonism to alveolar epithelium without blocking endothelial-protective AHR
