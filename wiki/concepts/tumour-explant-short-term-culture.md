## Definition

Tumour explants and organotypic slice cultures are short-lived small tumour pieces or slices (48-96 h) that mimic the TME for hours to days, retaining living tumour cells, stroma and immune cells. They can be cultured free-floating in medium, on membranes, in collagen gels or embedded in artificial ECM.

## Intuition

Compared with native immune organoids (weeks-months), explants trade duration for fidelity: they preserve all cellular and architectural components but are limited to short-term assays. They are particularly useful for ICB and adoptive-therapy testing with rapid clinical-correlation potential.

## When to use

- ICB response prediction across mixed tumour types (chordoma, melanoma, NSCLC, breast, ovarian, RCC).
- Novel immunotherapy combinations (IL-10 blockade + CAR-T in CRC liver metastases).
- Multi-fragment-per-patient pooled cytokine/chemokine readouts.

## Known limitations

- Inherently short-term — no longitudinal studies.
- Heterogeneity between fragments → pooled-analysis requirement.
- No tumour cell proliferation studies.

## Open problems

- Standardized fragment selection and pooling protocols.
- Linking pooled biomarker readouts to clinical outcomes prospectively.

## My understanding

Explants are the fastest path from biopsy to immunotherapy decision; they cannot replace native immune organoids for mechanistic dissection but are the most clinically deployable platform short-term.
