## Definition

Gain-of-function mutations in IDH1 (R132) and IDH2 (R140/R172) generate the oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate (2HG), a competitive inhibitor of α-KG-dependent dioxygenases. 2HG drives DNA and histone hypermethylation, blocks differentiation, and silences the cytosolic dsDNA sensor cGAS, producing a "cold" T-cell-excluded TME. mIDH-selective inhibitors restore α-KG biology and T-cell infiltration.

## Intuition

The first oncometabolite. Targetable, immunologically meaningful, and clinically approved across AML, ICC, and glioma — a model for biomarker-driven metabolic oncology.

## Formal notation

α-KG + NADPH → D-2HG (neomorphic mIDH); D-2HG inhibits TETs and KDMs → hypermethylation → cGAS silencing → T-cell exclusion.

## Variants

- Ivosidenib (mIDH1, peripheral) — [[foundations/ivosidenib-ag120|FDA approval]].
- Enasidenib (mIDH2).
- Vorasidenib (mIDH1/2, brain-penetrant) — [[foundations/vorasidenib|recent FDA approval]].

## Comparison

- A defined mutational subgroup that contrasts with the broader [[concepts/oxphos-vs-glycolytic-tumor-metabolic-heterogeneity|OxPhos vs glycolytic stratification]].

## When to use

- IDH-mutant glioma, AML, ICC, chondrosarcoma management.
- Combination with ICB to leverage restored T-cell infiltration.

## Known limitations

- IDH-isoform switching as resistance mechanism.
- Heterogeneity of downstream methylation effects.

## Open problems

- Optimal sequencing of mIDH inhibitors with ICB.
- Translatable 2HG biomarkers.

## Key papers

- Ricci 2025; INDIGO vorasidenib trial; ivosidenib pivotal trials.

## My understanding

mIDH inhibitors are the closest example to-date of biomarker-driven metabolic oncology that delivers in patients; vorasidenib in particular is a watershed.
