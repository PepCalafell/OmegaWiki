## Definition

A core MDSC effector mechanism in which ARG1 (and ARG2) hydrolyse L-arginine to ornithine in the TME, depleting arginine for infiltrating T cells. Low TIL arginine impairs TCR-zeta-chain synthesis, proliferation, memory formation, and antitumour cytotoxicity. ARG inhibition restores function in preclinical models; systemic arginine depletion via ADI-PEG20 has the opposite (immunosuppressive) effect on TILs.

## Intuition

Cancer-associated myeloid cells weaponise arginase. Local depletion suppresses T cells; restoration (via ARG inhibitors or engineered arginine-producing bacteria intratumourally) is being studied in combination with ICB.

## Formal notation

[Arg]TME ↓ → TCRζ synthesis ↓ → T cell proliferation/cytotoxicity ↓.

## Variants

- ARG1-targeted small molecules (CB-1158/INCB001158, OATD-02).
- Engineered intratumoural bacteria synthesising arginine.
- Systemic arginine depletion (ADI-PEG20) for arginine-auxotrophic tumours.

## Comparison

- Asymmetric to [[concepts/methionine-competition-slc43a2-stat5-tcell|methionine-STAT5]] and [[concepts/asparagine-tcell-activation-lck-axis|asparagine-LCK]]: target-class is myeloid, not tumour cell.

## When to use

- ICB-combination design in MDSC-rich tumours.

## Known limitations

- ARG2 compensation; off-target effects of systemic arginine modulation.

## Open problems

- Tissue-selective ARG inhibition that spares hepatic urea cycle.

## Key papers

- Ricci 2025 review and the broader arginine immunometabolism literature it cites.

## My understanding

The arginine axis exemplifies why local vs systemic delivery matters: same molecule, opposite effects on tumour and TILs.
