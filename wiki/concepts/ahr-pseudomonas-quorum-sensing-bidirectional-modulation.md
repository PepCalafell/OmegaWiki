## Definition

P. aeruginosa produces both AHR-activating ligands (pyocyanin, 1-hydroxyphenazine, phenazine-1-carboxamide, phenazine-1-carboxylic acid) and AHR-inhibiting ligands (3-oxo-C12-l-HSL and 2-heptyl-4-quinolone, HHQ). The activating phenazines accumulate at late exponential / stationary phase; the inhibitory QS molecules are highest at early/mid log phase. Both populations of molecules can bind AHR competitively, and the QS antagonists can suppress phenazine-driven AHR activation dose-dependently. The net effect on host infection outcome therefore depends on bacterial growth phase as much as on AHR status.

## Intuition

P. aeruginosa effectively "talks" to AHR with two opposite chemical signals depending on its density. At low density (early growth), HHQ and 3-oxo-C12-HSL silence AHR; at high density (late growth), phenazines fire it up. The host immune response, gated by AHR, is therefore titrated to the bacterial growth state — paradoxically, WT mice are worse than Ahr-KO mice at clearing early-phase bacteria (because AHR is being inhibited) but better at clearing late-phase bacteria.

## When to use

- When discussing P. aeruginosa pathogenesis, especially CF airway infection or wound infection
- When framing AHR as a density / growth-phase sensor of bacterial communities
- For drug discovery: targeting either branch (agonist mimetics or antagonist blockers) gives opposite therapeutic effects depending on infection stage

## Known limitations

- Chronic biofilm-phase Pseudomonas (CF lung) expresses heterogeneous QS profiles, complicating clean model-to-clinic translation
- Direct binding data exist for PYO and 3-oxo-C12-l-HSL but not for all QS-related AHR modulators

## Open problems

- Whether other Gram-negative pathogens with QS systems (Vibrio, Burkholderia) use a similar AHR-bidirectional strategy
- Structural basis for agonist vs antagonist binding in the AHR PAS-B pocket
- Whether AHR-QS dialogue is exploitable as a therapeutic checkpoint in CF lung disease
