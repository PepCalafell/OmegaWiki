## Definition

A tertiary lymphoid structure is an organized lymphoid aggregate that forms ectopically within the tumour, composed of a T lymphocyte zone (CD8+ T cells and type 1 dendritic cells) and a follicular zone with B lymphocyte germinal centres. TLSs locally educate tumour-reactive effector and memory T and B cells.

## Intuition

TLS density and maturity within tumours predict favourable prognosis and response to ICB across multiple cancer types. They mark a "hot" TME and serve as in situ amplifiers of adaptive antitumour immunity.

## Variants

- **Mature TLS** — distinct B and T zones with germinal centres.
- **Immature TLS** — disorganized lymphoid aggregates.
- **Peritumoural vs intratumoural TLS** — location may affect functional impact.

## When to use as a biomarker

- Histopathological prognostic feature in melanoma, NSCLC, sarcoma, urothelial cancer.
- Mechanistic correlate of ICB response.

## Known limitations

- Standardized quantification across cohorts remains imperfect.
- Mechanistic drivers of TLS formation poorly understood.

## Open problems

- Causal role of CXCL13+ exhausted T cells in TLS induction (MIBI evidence in melanoma).
- TLS-targeted therapeutics.

## My understanding

TLSs are a leading candidate for the next generation of organoid-derived prognostic biomarkers — provided the organoid model can recapitulate their structure, which native immune organoids may begin to address.
