## Definition

A protective tumor-immune niche in which Folr2⁺Cd163⁺Mmp9⁺ CD206hi interstitial macrophages positioned along bronchial airways, bronchovascular bundles and the visceral pleura locally produce CXCL13, CXCL9 and CXCL10 to recruit lymphocytes and nucleate tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) that restrain lung tumor growth.

## Intuition

Xenium spatial transcriptomics shows Cxcl13 enriched along bronchial airways coincident with Cd163⁺Folr2⁺ CD206hi IMs lining Epcam⁺ epithelium. Genetic depletion of these IMs collapses B/T cell aggregates (~80%), abolishes TLS, drops lung CXCL9/CXCL10/CXCL13 protein, and accelerates tumor growth — establishing the niche as a causal immunologic scaffold rather than a bystander correlate.

## Formal notation

CD206hi IM (airway/vessel/pleura) → CXCL13 (B-cell/CXCR5), CXCL9/CXCL10 (T-cell/CXCR3) → lymphocyte recruitment → TLS → tumor control.

## Variants

- Across B16F10 melanoma metastasis, KPAR1.3 transplanted adenocarcinoma, and AgerCreERT2KP spontaneous adenocarcinoma.
- Melanoma model forms peribronchial aggregates rather than compact TLS; adenocarcinoma models form architecturally organized TLS.

## Comparison

Provides a cellular source and spatial address for the [[cxcl13-cxcr5-tls-recruitment]] axis and for [[tertiary-lymphoid-structure]] biology, attributing TLS organization to a defined resident-macrophage subset.

## When to use

When asking which cell organizes peritumoral/bronchovascular TLS in lung cancer and how to preserve it therapeutically.

## Known limitations

- Mouse lung models; targeted spatial panel.
- The CD206hi compartment is depleted as a whole, so individual Cxcl13⁺/Cxcl9⁺/Cxcl10⁺ subset contributions are not separated.

## Open problems

- Whether boosting or preserving this niche enhances checkpoint blockade or vaccination in vivo.
- Human translation at subset and spatial resolution.

## My understanding

This niche is the protective arm of the IM division of labor — a spatially fixed, macrophage-anchored lymphoid-organizing center whose loss is sufficient to accelerate tumors.
