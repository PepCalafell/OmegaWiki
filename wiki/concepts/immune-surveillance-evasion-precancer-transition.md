## Definition
A consistent transition observed by single-cell and spatial omics across precancerous lesions of colon, lung, cervical, head-and-neck, oral, multiple myeloma and other cancers: an initial increase in immune cell infiltration (compatible with surveillance) gives way to immunosuppression, marked by conversion of CD4+ T cells to Treg cells, exhaustion or depletion of CD8+ cytotoxic T cells and NK cells, and emergence of immunosuppressive monocyte/macrophage subpopulations.

## Intuition
The same observation across many tumour types suggests immune evasion is not a late event of established cancer but a hallmark of the precancer-to-cancer transition itself.

## When to use
When formulating early-intervention or cancer-interception strategies that target the precancerous immune niche before invasion.

## Known limitations
Most evidence comes from cross-sectional snapshots; few studies have longitudinally tracked the same patient or animal through the transition.
