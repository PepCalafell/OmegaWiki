## Problem

Encounters between hosts and microorganisms span pathogens (must be controlled), environmental microbes (tolerated within bounds), and symbiotic microbiota (actively supported). How a single host receptor distinguishes "threat" from "tenant" and shapes appropriate immune responses across this entire spectrum is unresolved. The aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) — historically known as the xenobiotic/dioxin receptor — was reclassified by the authors in 2014 as a pattern recognition receptor (PRR). This review consolidates how AHR senses microbial ligands across bacteria, fungi, parasites and viruses, what immune programmes it elicits, and how this can be exploited for host-directed therapy of infectious diseases.

## Key idea

The AHR functions as a non-classical PRR whose output is exquisitely context-, ligand- and tissue-dependent. Unlike TLRs/NLRs which trigger stereotyped inflammatory programmes, AHR integrates host- (haem catabolites, kynurenines, FICZ), microbiota- (indoles from dietary Trp metabolism) and pathogen-derived (phthiocol, phenazines, malassezin, quorum-sensing molecules) ligands into ligand-specific transcriptional outputs. The same receptor can confer resistance or susceptibility to the same pathogen depending on ligand chemistry, growth-phase chemistry of the pathogen, infected tissue, and AHR affinity. Pharmacologic AHR agonists (tapinarof) and antagonists (IK-175, BAY-2416964, CH-223191) are now in clinical development — including for SARS-CoV-2 and ZIKV — and represent a host-directed therapeutic axis for infectious disease.

## Method

Narrative review covering: (i) the bHLH-PAS architecture of AHR and its evolutionary conservation; (ii) the canonical (XRE/DRE) and non-canonical (NF-κB / KLF6 / CUL4B-E3-ligase / SRC tyrosine kinase) AHR signalling pathways; (iii) a curated table of 40 microorganism-associated AHR ligands (Table 1) with their AHR modulation, source, and binding evidence (direct vs competitive); (iv) AHR's role across infections with bacteria (M. tuberculosis, P. aeruginosa, L. monocytogenes, Citrobacter, Salmonella, S. agalactiae, S. pneumoniae, Streptococcus mitis, S. aureus/epidermidis, H. pylori, A. salmonicida, M. marinum), fungi (Malassezia, C. albicans, A. fumigatus, P. brasiliensis), parasites (H. polygyrus, C. tyzzeri, L. major, T. cruzi, P. berghei, P. chabaudi, T. gondii) and viruses (FluV, coronaviruses incl. SARS-CoV-2 and MHV, HIV, EBV, HCV, ZIKV, DENV); and (v) ongoing and prospective AHR-targeting drug development relevant to infection. Mechanism diagrams in Figs. 1–5 summarise AHR modulation, ligand activation/inhibition, experimental model (KO vs antagonist vs agonist) and infection outcome (resistance vs susceptibility).

## Results

This is a review; the synthesis is the result. Highlights:

- AHR ligands of microbial origin span all four phyla of pathogens — bacteria (phthiocol, pyocyanin, 1-hydroxyphenazine, HHQ, 3-oxo-C12-HSL, indoles), fungi (malassezin, indirubin, indolo[3,2-b]carbazole, tryptanthrin), and indirectly through Trp catabolism in parasites and viruses.
- Same-receptor, opposite-outcome rule: in P. aeruginosa, AHR is protective when bacteria express late-growth-phase phenazine agonists but is detrimental when bacteria express early-phase quorum-sensing antagonists. The host phenotype depends on the bacterial growth phase, not just AHR status.
- AHR resistance to bacterial infection is mediated by IL-22-producing RORγt+ ILC3s (C. rodentium), neutrophil-recruiting cytokines (P. aeruginosa), reactive oxygen species (L. monocytogenes), and AHR-dependent CD8+ memory T-cell formation.
- In SARS-CoV-2, the AHR-IDO1-KYN axis drives pathogenic mucin overproduction in alveolar epithelial cells, and CH-223191 reduces mucins and ameliorates lung pathology — establishing AHR antagonism as a host-directed antiviral strategy.
- In HIV, AHR binds the 5'LTR and Tat protein, activating viral transcription and reactivating latent HIV from PBMCs of cART-treated individuals — contradicting older work in which FICZ-activated AHR blocked HIV early replication in macrophages, illustrating cell-type-specific AHR functions in the same infection.
- Threshold-of-activation principle: in T. cruzi, mice carrying the Ahrd low-affinity allele (which transduces a lower-magnitude AHR signal) achieve a balanced inflammatory response, restrict parasite replication, and survive longer — providing genetic evidence that AHR signal magnitude, not on/off state, determines outcome.

## Discussion captured

### Authors' interpretation

The authors frame AHR as a "non-classical pattern recognition receptor" whose evolutionary role is to interface host metabolism with microbial chemistry. Three principles emerge: (i) AHR is ligand-promiscuous but ligand-specific in output — TCDD, FICZ, ITE, PCB126, kynurenine, microbial indoles and pathogen pigments each engage distinct downstream programmes; (ii) AHR is tissue-context dependent — the same ligand can be protective in lung epithelium and pathogenic in alveolar mucin biology; (iii) AHR can be exploited by pathogens — SARS-CoV-2 and HIV co-opt AHR for replication, EBV reroutes AHR to escape growth arrest, while M. tuberculosis and Pseudomonas use AHR-activating pigments to elicit clearance only at high pathogen density. Targeting AHR pharmacologically should therefore be tissue-, ligand- and infection-stage-specific.

### Comparisons with prior literature (made by authors)

- Authors cite their 2014 paper (Moura-Alves Nature) classifying AHR as a PRR.
- Foundational AHR-PRR ligand work: Rannug et al. 1987 (FICZ/UV-Trp); Stockinger group (Trp-microbiota-AHR-IL-22); Vogel group (TCDD-immunology); Lawrence group (TCDD-influenza); Zelante 2013 Cell (L. reuteri indoles-IL-22).
- Quintana group's AHR-CNS-EAE work (astrocyte/microglia AHR limiting inflammation).
- Recent endothelial-AHR work (Cibrian et al.) and SARS-CoV-2-mucin work (Liu et al. 2024).

### Mechanistic hypotheses proposed

- "Bacteria that produce a high level of AHR inhibitory molecules and a low level of virulence factors (in the early growth stage) are tolerated, whereas bacteria that generate virulence factors at high abundance in the late growth stage are rapidly killed" — proposes AHR as a quorum-sensing surveillance device that times the host immune response to bacterial density.
- "Activation of the AHR is exploited by SARS-CoV-2 as a virulence mechanism" — proposes AHR antagonism as a host-directed antiviral strategy.
- Ahrd low-affinity allele finding suggests "the threshold of AHR activation" — not its presence/absence — is the relevant pharmacological variable, motivating partial-agonist SAHRM development.

### Caveats and self-criticism

- "There is often a lack of standardization for the definition of an AHR ligand": many compounds labelled "AHR ligand" only induce CYP1A1 or reporter genes; binding evidence (direct ligand binding assays vs displacement of TCDD/FICZ) is uneven.
- Proligands like indole-3-carbinol are not themselves AHR binders but generate ICZ upon acid catalysis; misidentification has confounded the field.
- CYP inhibitors can act as apparent AHR ligands by allowing endogenous agonists to accumulate.
- Cross-study inconsistencies (especially in L. monocytogenes, T. cruzi, FluV models) reflect ligand, dose, route and timing variability, complicating meta-interpretation.

### Future directions suggested

- Develop standardised AHR-ligand validation pipelines (direct binding + competition + enzymatic activity).
- Test AHR antagonists (CH-223191, IK-175, BAY-2416964) for host-directed therapy of TB, SARS-CoV-2 and ZIKV.
- Probe whether AHR signal-magnitude modulation (partial agonism / SAHRMs) outperforms full agonism in infection contexts.
- Test microbiota-engineering (indole-producing probiotics) as AHR-based barrier therapy.
- Define AHR's role in vaccination and antimicrobial memory formation.

## Limitations

- Narrative review with author selection bias toward AHR-PRR framework (the authors proposed it in 2014).
- Coverage of Supplementary Table 1 (the ligand–outcome database) and Figs. 3–5 (infection summaries) compresses heterogeneous experimental designs, doses and time-points; the reader cannot derive quantitative comparisons.
- Many cited findings rely on mouse Ahr-KO models with variable backgrounds (C57BL/6 vs BALB/c — same paper notes opposing L. major outcomes on different backgrounds), and translational extension to human disease is largely speculative.
- Pathogen-derived AHR ligands have been described in detail only for a handful of organisms; absence of evidence (e.g., no parasite-derived AHR ligand to date) is not absence of mechanism.
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure: P. Moura-Alves and S. Kaufmann have prior commercial interests in AHR-related host-directed therapy programmes.

## Open questions

### Open questions raised by authors

- What microbe-derived AHR ligands remain unidentified — particularly from parasites, where none has yet been described?
- Can AHR-targeting host-directed therapies (agonist or antagonist) achieve clinical efficacy in TB, COVID-19, or Zika?
- How do bacterial growth-phase QS signals integrate with host AHR to time immune responses?
- Why does AHR confer resistance in some C57BL/6 infection models and susceptibility in BALB/c models (L. major case)?
- How does AHR ligand affinity (Ahrd vs Ahrb alleles) translate into immune-response titration in humans?

### Open questions identified during ingest

- Does the AHR-PRR concept overlap or compete with TLR/NLR-mediated antimicrobial defence in the same cell? Are there integrated AHR-TLR signalling nodes?
- The AHR-IDO-KYN-mucin axis in SARS-CoV-2 (claim c16) overlaps with the AHR-TIPARP-TBK1-IFN evasion concept ([[concepts/ahr-tiparp-antiviral-type-i-ifn-evasion]]) — are these distinct branches or one integrated AHR-anti-IFN programme?
- For TIER_1 pathogens in immunology, how would the AHR-PRR framework integrate with the [[concepts/pattern-recognition-receptors-macrophage]] canonical PRR signalling? Are AHR-PRR responses additive, synergistic, or antagonistic to TLR responses in macrophages co-stimulated with phthiocol + LPS?
- Does the AHR-Th17/Treg/Tr1 logic catalogued in [[papers/aryl-hydrocarbon-receptor-rehabilitated-target-therapeutic]] map cleanly to bacterial-clearance contexts (e.g., S. agalactiae)?

## My take

This review is the canonical reference for AHR's role in infectious-disease immunology. Together with [[papers/aryl-hydrocarbon-receptor-rehabilitated-target-therapeutic]] (the 2025 Quintana NRDD review) and [[papers/complex-biology-aryl-hydrocarbon-receptor-activation]] (the 2025 NRDA mechanism review), it completes the picture: Quintana 2025 = mechanism + drug development; this paper = host–microbe interface + pathogen-specific outcomes; NRDA = molecular structure and ligand chemistry. The "AHR signal-magnitude not on/off" principle (Ahrd allele in T. cruzi) is the most actionable claim for thesis work: it predicts that partial AHR agonists / SAHRMs should outperform full agonists in infection therapy, and it constrains how I think about AHR-TAM signalling in tumours (where the same logic likely applies). The SARS-CoV-2 mucin axis (claim c16) is concrete and clinically translatable — CH-223191 is already a known antagonist with a defined preclinical use case. The HIV-LTR-Tat-AHR finding (claim c17) is striking and underappreciated — AHR antagonism may be relevant to HIV cure strategies.
