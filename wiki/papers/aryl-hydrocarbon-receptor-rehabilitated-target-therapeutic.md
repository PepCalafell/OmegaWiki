## Problem

For decades, AHR activation was avoided in drug development because the prototypical agonist TCDD is teratogenic and carcinogenic. Yet AHR is also a physiological immune regulator activated by diet-, host-, and microbiota-derived metabolites. The unresolved problem reviewed here: how to translate AHR's tissue-protective immune-regulatory biology into safe therapeutics without recapitulating dioxin-like toxicity, across autoimmune, neoplastic, and infectious disease.

## Key idea

AHR is a "rehabilitated" target. Its toxic legacy is a feature of one specific ligand chemistry (persistent, CYP1A1-resistant dioxin-like agonists), not of AHR signalling per se. Endogenous and dietary AHR ligands are short-lived and tissue-restricted, and modern medicinal chemistry — guided by cryo-EM structures of AHR–HSP90–XAP2 complexes — can produce selective AHR modulators (SAHRMs) with defined kinetics. The FDA approval of tapinarof for plaque psoriasis (2022) is clinical proof-of-concept; multiple AHR-agonist (autoimmune, infectious-disease) and AHR-antagonist (cancer, antiviral) programmes are now in trials.

## Method

This is a narrative review (Nature Reviews Drug Discovery). Methods are literature synthesis with mechanism diagrams. The review covers: (i) AHR molecular biology and structural biology of the AHR-HSP90-XAP2 complex; (ii) AHR ligands (Table 1: dioxins, tryptophan metabolites, dietary, others); (iii) canonical and non-canonical AHR signalling; (iv) AHR in DCs, ILCs, CD4+ T cell subsets, CD8+ T cells, B cells; (v) AHR-targeting therapeutics in inflammatory disease (skin, MS, SLE, IBD, asthma/COPD); (vi) AHR-targeting in infections (ZIKV, SARS-CoV-2, influenza) and cancer (glioblastoma, breast); (vii) other small molecules, AHR-targeting probiotics, and AHR-targeting nanoparticles.

## Results

This is a review; the synthesis is the result. Key syntheses (referenced in claims below):

- Structural biology: cryo-EM AHR-HSP90-XAP2-indirubin defines a large, ligand-promiscuous PAS-B pocket; full-length AHR structures remain a gap.
- Pharmacology: ligand-biased AHR responses (TCDD vs FICZ vs ITE vs tapinarof) explain context-dependent immune outcomes.
- Therapeutic landscape: tapinarof (psoriasis approved; atopic dermatitis sNDA 2024); ITE / VAF347 / FICZ-mimetics preclinical for autoimmunity; CH-223191 / HP163 / IK-175 / BAY-2416964 antagonists for cancer and antiviral indications; PARP7 inhibitors (RBN-2397) targeting the AHR-TIPARP arm.
- Translational opportunities: AHR-targeting probiotics (Lactobacillus indole-producers), AHR-targeting nanoparticles for tolerogenic vaccination, dietary AHR-ligand restoration in dysbiotic disease.

## Discussion captured

### Authors' interpretation

The authors interpret the totality of evidence as a "paradigm shift": AHR, once considered an off-target hazard, is now a validated drug target. They argue this rehabilitation rests on three pillars: (i) recognition of physiological, microbiota-/diet-derived AHR ligands distinct from dioxins; (ii) elucidation of AHR's tissue- and ligand-context-dependent immune regulation, especially Treg/Tr1/Th17 balance and barrier homeostasis; (iii) clinical validation of tapinarof, which crossed the regulatory threshold by leveraging transient AHR agonism and tissue-restricted delivery to avoid dioxin-like toxicity. They explicitly frame tapinarof as a template ("the rehabilitation… is exemplified by tapinarof") and forecast follow-on programmes for AHR agonists in IBD, MS, atopic dermatitis, and antagonists in cancer and viral infection.

### Comparisons with prior literature (made by authors)

The authors situate the work alongside: Poland & Glover 1976 (cytosolic dioxin receptor identification); Quintana et al. 2008 Nature and Veldhoen et al. 2008 (foundational AHR-Treg/Th17 dichotomy); Apetoh 2010 Nat Immunol (AHR-IL27-Tr1); Rothhammer 2016/2018 (astrocyte/microglia AHR-EAE); Zelante 2013 Cell (microbial indoles-AHR-IL22); Lamas 2016 Nat Med (CARD9-microbiota-AHR-IBD); Schiering 2017 Nature (CYP1A1 negative feedback); Takenaka 2019 Nat Neurosci (AHR-TAM-KLF4-CD39 in glioma); Sadik 2020 Cell (IL4I1 as AHR activator); Smith 2017 (tapinarof mechanism); Lebwohl 2021 NEJM (PSOARING phase III); Giovannoni 2020 (ZIKV-AHR-HP163), Giovannoni 2021 (SARS-CoV-2-AHR-CH-223191); Yamada 2016 (AHR-TIPARP-TBK1-IFN).

### Mechanistic hypotheses proposed

- AHR-TIPARP suppression of TBK1 / type I IFN is "evolutionarily selected to limit immunopathology but is hijacked by viruses to promote viral replication and infectivity" (p.620) — motivates AHR antagonism as broad-spectrum antiviral.
- "AHR could potentially limit the establishment of p300-driven long-lived epigenetic programmes that promote chronic tissue inflammation" (p.614) — proposes AHR as an epigenetic moderator of inflammation.
- Tapinarof's clinical "remittive effect" (p.618) is hypothesised to reflect AHR-driven memory T-cell modulation (CD69⁺CD103⁺ tissue-resident T cells) — a mechanism to be exploited in other epithelial diseases.

### Caveats and self-criticism

The authors acknowledge: lack of high-quality structural data on full-length AHR limits rational SAHRM design; pro- vs anti-tumour effects of AHR are tissue- and tumour-type-specific and not fully understood; in vivo significance of ligand-independent AHR activation by shear/oxidative stress remains to be established; pro-inflammatory DC responses to AHR ligands (e.g., BMDC + TCDD) coexist with the predominant tolerogenic phenotype.

### Future directions suggested

- Develop selective AHR modulators (SAHRMs) for tissue-restricted immune modulation
- AHR agonists as non-steroidal options for asthma, COPD, IBD via inhaled / colon-delivery formulations
- AHR-targeting probiotics and engineered indole-producing strains for inflammatory disease
- AHR-targeting nanoparticles for tolerogenic vaccination
- AHR antagonists in oncology combined with ICI; PARP7 inhibitors as TIPARP-arm therapeutics
- AHR ratio (Th17 vs Treg) as prognostic biomarker in SLE

## Limitations

- Narrative review: no systematic search methodology disclosed; selection bias toward author's own and collaborating-lab work is unavoidable in a field as long-running as AHR
- Limited coverage of negative or contradictory data (e.g., trials where AHR agonists failed)
- Mouse-to-human translation issues across DC subsets, ILC biology, and Treg/Tr1 definitions glossed over in places
- Quantitative ligand-pharmacokinetics across tissues not discussed in detail
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure: two authors (McHale, Rubenstein) are from Dermavant Sciences, the tapinarof developer

## Open questions

### Open questions raised by authors

- How to design SAHRMs that engage tolerogenic AHR functions without off-target dioxin-like effects
- Whether AHR ratio in Th17 vs Treg is a clinically useful biomarker in SLE
- How CNS-penetrant AHR-targeted molecules can be developed for progressive MS
- How AHR-targeting probiotics or postbiotics can be standardised for clinical use
- Whether AHR antagonism + ICI is synergistic in cancer

### Open questions identified during ingest

- How does the AHR-TIPARP axis interact with cGAS-STING signalling — is there cross-regulation of type I IFN?
- Do CD8⁺ tissue-resident memory T cells, central to tapinarof's "remittive effect", behave the same way in IBD or atopic dermatitis?
- Can the AHR-driven Th17 → Tr1 conversion be pharmacologically enforced as an autoimmunity therapy?
- How does the AHR-HIF1α competition for ARNT affect tumour macrophage biology in [[hypoxia-pd-l1-tam-immune-evasion]] contexts?
- Where does IL4I1 fit relative to IDO1/TDO2 in AHR-ligand generation in human tumours (cf. [[papers/cross-tissue-single-cell-landscape-human]] IL4I1⁺PD-L1⁺IDO1⁺ MoMac-VERSE cluster)?

## My take

A comprehensive, authoritative reference for the AHR-immune-drug-discovery field by the leading lab (Quintana). The clearest single source linking AHR pharmacology, T-cell / DC / ILC biology, microbiome-AHR-ligand axis, and clinical translation (tapinarof + emerging antagonists). For my thesis, the most directly relevant nodes are: (i) the AHR-TAM-KLF4-CD39 axis in glioblastoma, which connects to my interest in macrophage immunosuppression in tumours; (ii) the IDO1/TDO2/IL4I1 → Kyn → AHR cancer axis linking to IL4I1⁺ macrophage atlases; (iii) the AHR-HIF1α competition for ARNT, which sits at the AHR/hypoxia interface; (iv) the AHR-CYP1A1 negative-feedback architecture as a model for ligand-self-limited transcription factor pharmacology. The conflict-of-interest from Dermavant authors is mild but worth noting when reading the tapinarof sections.
