## Problem

Cancer immunotherapies (immune-checkpoint blockade, bispecific T cell engagers, CAR-T and adoptive cell therapies) have transformed oncology but exhibit variable, transient and idiosyncratic responses confounded by resistance. Conventional 2D cell lines and standard 3D organoid models contain only tumour epithelium without immune or stromal components, so they cannot model immune-driven tumour biology or predict immunotherapy responses on a per-patient basis. There is an urgent need for personalized in vitro tumour models that incorporate the immune tumour microenvironment (TME) to support patient selection, target identification and definition of resistance mechanisms in immuno-oncology.

## Key idea

Two complementary classes of tumour-immune organoid platforms now bridge this gap:
1. **Reconstituted immune organoid models** — conventional patient-derived tumour organoids (PDOs) supplemented with engineered/expanded immune cells (PB lymphocytes, TILs, CAR-T) or droplet-based micro-organospheres (MOS) co-encapsulating tumour and stromal cells.
2. **Native architecture immune organoid models** — air-liquid interface (ALI) PDOs and patient-derived organotypic tumour spheroids (PDOTS/MDOTS) generated by minimal mincing of tumour fragments, preserving native tumour-stroma-immune architecture, ECM and TCR/BCR repertoires.

Combined with advanced imaging (CODEX, MIBI, BEHAV3D light-sheet, scission-accelerated fluorophore exchange) and single-cell analyses (scRNA + V(D)J seq, mass cytometry, spatial transcriptomics) plus organoid genetic engineering (CRISPR-HOT, perturb-seq, CROP-seq), these systems enable mechanistic dissection of the immune TME and ex vivo testing of immunotherapies on patient-matched material.

## Method

This is a Nature Reviews Cancer review article that surveys ~250 primary references. Methodologically it:
- Compares reconstituted vs native immune organoid platforms in terms of throughput, native-architecture preservation, ECM context, immune diversity and engineering tractability.
- Catalogues imaging modalities (live confocal, light sheet, SAFE, CODEX, MIBI, mLSR-3D + STAPL-3D) and single-cell analyses (flow / mass cytometry, scRNA-seq, V(D)J seq, spatial transcriptomics) applicable to immune organoids.
- Reviews genetic engineering options (lentiviral, Cre-lox, CRISPR-Cas9, CRISPRa/i, CRISPR-HOT, perturb-seq, CROP-seq) and notes their limitations in native immune organoid systems.
- Summarizes clinical correlative evidence for organoid-based prediction of chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy responses, including reconstituted PDO + PBMC studies, MOS prospective work, PDOTS small-molecule combination screens and ALI PDO ICB studies.
- Adds a side panel on short-term tumour explants and organotypic slice cultures as a parallel modality (48-96 h) for ICB testing.

## Results

Key empirical landmarks summarized by the review:

- ALI PDOs from melanoma, RCC and NSCLC preserve CAFs and a broad immune spectrum (CTLs, T helpers, TAMs, NK, B, NKT cells); the T cell compartment can be maintained >70 days with IL-2 and cytokine supplementation, and TCR clonotypes from the original biopsy are recapitulated.
- In approximately one-third of ALI PDOs, anti-PD1/anti-PDL1 ICB triggered patient-specific T cell activation and tumour killing; nivolumab response in clear-cell RCC ALI PDOs depended on CD8+ T cell infiltration rather than PDL1 levels.
- PDOTS and MDOTS retain autologous lymphocytes and myeloid cells; small-molecule screens in PDOTS identified CDK4/6 and TBK1 inhibitors as ICB-response enhancers in melanoma and MSI-high CRC.
- Anti-TREM2 in BCC ALI organoids induced epithelial cell death by depleting TREM2+ macrophages.
- BEHAV3D live confocal imaging of breast cancer organoids + γδ CAR-T identified type I interferon signalling as a sensitizer to CAR-T killing.
- Reconstituted CRC PDO + autologous PB T cell co-culture did not predict combination-ICB response in MSI-CRC cohorts, while MOS reaggregation that preserved infiltrating immune cells correlated with anti-PD1 and bispecific antibody clinical responses.
- Patient-derived tumour fragment explants pooled across multiple fragments per patient (cytokine + chemokine + T cell activation marker readouts) predicted ICB response across melanoma, NSCLC, breast, ovarian and RCC in a validation cohort.
- Conventional epithelial-only CRC organoids predicted irinotecan-based chemotherapy response but failed for 5-FU + oxaliplatin and for an mTOR/PI3K/EGFR/MEK/VEGFR intervention trial.
- CODEX defined cellular neighbourhoods stratifying CRC progression risk and predicting ICB response; MIBI quantified 35 markers in FFPE breast cancer and revealed CXCL13+ exhausted T cells driving TLS formation in immune-infiltrated melanoma.

## Discussion captured

### Authors' interpretation

The authors frame native immune organoid systems (ALI, PDOTS) as a paradigm shift over conventional epithelial-only PDOs because they preserve patient-specific TCR repertoires, immune cell types and TME architecture, enabling mechanistic dissection of ICB and CAR-T responses on patient-matched material. They argue that the discordance between conventional CRC PDO trials and clinical outcomes is explained by the absence of immune TME components and that this absence "may inefficiently predict long-term patient outcomes" (p.531). They highlight short-term explants and slice cultures as a complementary, faster modality with proven correlative power but inherently limited culture lifespan.

### Comparisons with prior literature (made by authors)

- Compare ALI PDOs (ref. 89, Neal et al. 2018, Cell) and PDOTS (refs. 148-151, Jenkins / Aref) with conventional Matrigel submerged PDOs (refs. 88, 109-115).
- Compare patient-derived tumour fragment explant studies (refs. 218-219) against organoid-based predictors of ICB.
- Note BEHAV3D (ref. 159, Dekkers et al.) and CRISPR-HOT (ref. 160, Artegiani et al.) as enabling technologies.
- Reference seminal CODEX (ref. 167, Goltsev) and MIBI (ref. 169, Angelo) work for translation to organoid imaging.

### Mechanistic hypotheses proposed

- "Type I interferon signalling sensitization of cancer organoids to CAR T cell therapy" (p.529) — proposed as a tractable adjuvant axis for boosting cellular immunotherapies.
- The discrepancy between PDL1 IHC and nivolumab response in RCC ALI PDOs is hypothesized to reflect CD8 infiltration as the dominant determinant (p.531).
- CXCL13+ exhausted T cells are proposed as drivers of TLS formation in immune-infiltrated melanoma (p.530).

### Caveats and self-criticism

- "These studies have often utilized small numbers of patient samples, increasing the risk of publication bias, and the accuracy of long-term therapy response prediction has not yet been established" (p.531).
- Native immune organoid heterogeneity, low throughput and engineering challenges remain unresolved (p.526).
- Single-cell suspension methods (MOS, dissociated melanoma) "disrupt native spatial relationships, which may influence in vitro recapitulation of key immune cell interactions" (p.526).

### Future directions suggested

- Pair organoid models with spatial transcriptomics, perturb-seq and CROP-seq for cell-type-specific perturbation of the immune TME.
- Develop FNA-derived ALI/microfluidic organoids to lower the tissue threshold for personalized immunotherapy testing.
- Establish prospective trials linking immune organoid readouts to patient outcomes — the next milestone before clinical adoption.
- Adapt CRISPR functional genomic screens to native immune organoids to identify tumour-immune evasion genes ex vivo.

## Limitations

- Conventional epithelial-only PDOs lack immune and stromal context and have under-predicted clinical outcomes in CRC ICB and prospective targeted-therapy trials.
- Native immune organoid systems are heterogeneous between organoids when derived from spatially variable tumours, are low-throughput, and are difficult to genetically engineer in cell-type-specific ways.
- Tumour explants are inherently short-term (48-96 h), precluding longitudinal studies.
- The TIL compartment of ALI PDOs decays over time, only partially rescued by IL-2 supplementation.
- Most ICB correlative studies use small patient cohorts, risking publication bias; long-term predictive accuracy of organoid readouts is unproven.

## Open questions

### Open questions raised by authors

- Can FNA-derived ALI or microfluidic organoids generate sufficient biomass to recapitulate tumour architecture and immune populations from minimally invasive biopsies?
- Can perturb-seq / CROP-seq be extended to non-reconstituted native immune organoids despite multicellularity?
- Which combination immunotherapies will reliably convert "cold" tumours to "hot" in immune organoid platforms?
- How can larger prospective trials definitively correlate native immune organoid readouts with patient outcomes?

### Open questions identified during ingest

- How do hypoxia and metabolic gradients within native immune organoid systems compare to in vivo tumour hypoxia? (relevance to the user's hypoxia project — none of the platforms reviewed explicitly model oxygen gradients).
- Could native immune organoid platforms be combined with hypoxia tracers (FMISO, EF5) to identify hypoxic niches modulating T cell exhaustion ex vivo?
- Is the CXCL13+ exhausted T cell → TLS axis identified by MIBI reproducible in ALI PDOs from immune-infiltrated tumours?

## My take

For the thesis on hypoxia-driven immunosuppression, this review's most actionable contribution is the ALI organoid + PDOTS platform map. Native immune organoid systems offer a tractable ex vivo testbed for the question "does hypoxia-driven PD-L1 upregulation on TAMs causally suppress CD8 T cell killing?" — a question that current human studies can only address correlatively. Worth following up on the Neal et al. ALI protocol and the recent Dekkers BEHAV3D paper as anchors for an organoid-based hypoxia-immune evasion experiment. The Calvin Kuo lab papers are the canonical source for any ALI-based protocol design.
