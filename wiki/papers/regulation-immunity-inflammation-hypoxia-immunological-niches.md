## Problem

Effective immunity depends on focal sites of immune activity ("immunological niches") whose microenvironment shapes immune cell function. Hypoxia is a recurrent feature of these niches in both health and disease, yet its immunological consequence is not fixed: the same low-oxygen condition can support homeostasis or drive pathology. The review's problem is to organize how hypoxia — acting largely through hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) — regulates immunity and inflammation differently across physiological versus pathological niches, and whether hypoxia-sensitive pathways can be targeted therapeutically.

## Key idea

Hypoxia is a shared microenvironmental feature of physiological immunological niches (bone marrow, germinal centres, placenta, intestinal mucosa) and pathological ones (tumours, chronically inflamed/infected/ischaemic tissues). The *pattern* (sustained-and-ordered vs severe-chaotic-intermittent), *degree*, and *co-mediators* (inflammatory cytokines, oxidative stress, gasotransmitters, immunometabolites) determine the HIF activation profile and therefore the immune outcome. Because pharmacological hydroxylase inhibition (HIF activation) is broadly anti-inflammatory and clinically tolerated for renal anaemia, the HIF pathway is a promising — if context-dependent — target for immune-related disease.

## Method

Narrative review (*Nature Reviews Immunology*). Synthesis with mechanism diagrams and one summary table. Structure: (i) transcriptional responses to hypoxia and the HIF/PHD/FIH/pVHL oxygen-sensing pathway; (ii) HIF modulation of immune cell function via metabolism; (iii) niche features that shape the HIF response (oxygen-gradient pattern, degree of hypoxia, inflammatory mediators, oxidative stress, gasotransmitters); (iv) hypoxia in physiological niches (bone marrow, germinal centre, placenta, intestinal mucosa); (v) hypoxia in pathological niches (tumours, inflammation, bacterial infection, ischaemia); (vi) targeting the HIF pathway therapeutically; (vii) perspective.

## Results

This is a review; the synthesis is the result. Key syntheses (expanded in claims below):

- Oxygen sensing: PHD and FIH hydroxylases repress HIFα in normoxia (degradation via pVHL + transcriptional repression); hypoxia inhibits them. The hydroxylases double as metabolic sensors (2-OG, succinate, 2-HG, ROS).
- Immune regulation: HIF is a central, cell-type-specific regulator of innate and adaptive immunity, acting largely by reprogramming metabolism (glycolysis). Inflammation feeds back to raise HIF (HIF–NF-κB coupling), forming a feedforward loop.
- Niche framework: physiological niches sustain ordered, moderate hypoxia supporting homeostasis (HSC quiescence, B cell antibody control, placental immune privilege, gut barrier); pathological niches show severe/intermittent hypoxia driving angiogenesis, immune dysregulation and tissue damage.
- Therapeutics: hydroxylase inhibition is broadly anti-inflammatory (Table 1) but exacerbates live bacterial sepsis; clinical PHD inhibitors (roxadustat, daprodustat, vadadustat) are tolerated in CKD anaemia, motivating repurposing.

## Discussion captured

### Authors' interpretation

The authors interpret hypoxia as a unifying microenvironmental regulator of immunity whose effect is determined by the niche rather than by oxygen tension alone. They argue that physiological and pathological niches engage largely the same machinery (HIF, hydroxylases, NF-κB crosstalk), and that the temporal/spatial/dynamic profile of the hypoxic stimulus — together with co-mediators — dictates the degree and kinetics of HIF activation and thus the immune outcome. They frame HIF as a node coupling immunity to metabolism (a feedforward loop), and present pharmacological hydroxylase inhibition as a rational, clinically de-risked anti-inflammatory strategy whose chief caveats are context dependence (sepsis) and systemic side effects (erythropoiesis).

### Comparisons with prior literature (made by authors)

The review synthesises, among others: Kaelin & Ratcliffe 2008 (oxygen sensing by the HIF hydroxylase pathway); Semenza 1994 (HIF control of glycolytic genes); Tannahill et al. *Nature* 2013 (succinate→IL-1β via HIF1α); Tyrakis et al. *Nature* 2016 (S-2-hydroxyglutarate→CD8⁺ T cell fate); Cho et al. *Nature* 2016 and Abbott et al. 2016 (germinal-centre hypoxia and antibody quality); Karhausen et al. 2004 (protective epithelial HIF1 in colitis); Campbell et al. *Immunity* 2014 (neutrophil oxygen depletion shapes mucosal resolution); Corzo et al. 2010 (HIF1α in MDSC function); Cho et al. *Nature* 2016 and Chen/Cho et al. *Nature* 2016 (HIF2α antagonists in renal cancer); Provenzano 2016 / Brigandi 2016 / Pergola 2016 (clinical PHD inhibitors in CKD anaemia).

### Mechanistic hypotheses proposed

- The dynamic/temporal profile of hypoxia (not a binary switch) sets which HIF target genes are transcribed and thus the immune effector outcome (p.776-777).
- Inflammation and HIF form a regulatory feedforward loop, with the metabolic microenvironment counter-regulating immune cell function via HIF (Boxes 2-3).
- "Inflammatory hypoxia" from neutrophil oxygen consumption is a deliberate signal that drives barrier-protective, pro-resolution epithelial HIF programmes (p.781-782).

### Caveats and self-criticism

- The physiological/pathological dichotomy is an explicit reductionist simplification of a continuum (p.774).
- The role of HIF in HSC maintenance is acknowledged as controversial (conflicting HIF2α data) (p.779).
- The impact of HIF on germinal-centre class-switch recombination is "controversial" (p.780).
- The mechanism(s) by which ROS regulate HIF are "unclear" (p.778).
- The pharmacological effects of HIF2α-specific inhibitors on immunity/inflammation are "largely unknown" (p.782).

### Future directions suggested

- Tissue-targeted delivery of hydroxylase inhibitors (gut, skin, lung) to avoid systemic erythropoiesis.
- Isoform-specific reagents (PHD-selective; HIF1 vs HIF2 inhibitors) and cell-type-targeted (surface-antigen-directed) delivery.
- Clinical evaluation of HIF-modulating drugs in inflammatory, infectious and autoimmune disease.

## Limitations

- Narrative review: scope and emphasis reflect author selection (notably the authors' own mucosal/HIF programme); no systematic methodology or quantitative meta-analysis.
- Mechanistic evidence is predominantly murine / preclinical; human efficacy of HIF modulation in inflammatory disease is not established.
- 2017 vintage: predates much subsequent single-cell/spatial dissection of niche hypoxia.

## Open questions

### Open questions raised by authors

- Which HIF isoform and downstream programme governs HSC quiescence in vivo (controversy).
- Whether GC hypoxia decreases or potentiates class-switch recombination.
- How ROS mechanistically regulate HIF expression/activity.
- The effects of HIF2α-specific inhibitors on immunity and inflammation.
- Whether hydroxylase-inhibitor tolerability in CKD anaemia translates to anti-inflammatory benefit.

### Open questions identified during ingest

- How the niche framework maps onto single-cell/spatial resolution of intratumoural and mucosal hypoxia (cf. the wiki's spatial-omics and tumour-hypoxia corpus).
- Whether "inflammatory hypoxia" (neutrophil oxygen depletion) operates analogously in non-mucosal pathological niches.
- Reconciliation of the HIF–NF-κB hydroxylase-coupling mechanism with the cooperative chromatin-binding model captured elsewhere in the wiki.

## My take

A foundational synthesis (595 citations) that established the physiological-vs-pathological niche framing now common across hypoxia immunology. For this vault it is a hub: it underpins the mechanistic vocabulary (HIF/PHD/FIH/pVHL, HIF–NF-κB crosstalk, immunometabolism, inflammatory hypoxia) that the more recent single-cell, spatial and tumour-hypoxia papers build on. Its therapeutic thesis (hydroxylase inhibition as anti-inflammatory, bounded by the sepsis caveat) remains a clean, testable translational claim.
