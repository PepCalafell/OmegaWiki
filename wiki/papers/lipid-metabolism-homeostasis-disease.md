## Problem

Lipid metabolism is increasingly recognized as a master regulator of immune cell behaviour, yet immune-cell research has historically focused on glucose/amino-acid metabolism while lipid biology was confined to cardiovascular and metabolic medicine. A unified, immune-cell-centric synthesis of how fatty acids, cholesterol, bile acids, phospholipids, sphingolipids and lipid rafts shape macrophage polarization, T/B cell differentiation, neutrophil and innate effector function — and how dysregulated lipid metabolism couples to autoimmunity, cancer, neurodegeneration, cardiovascular disease, aging and metabolic disease — has been missing. The authors set out to provide such a synthesis and to identify therapeutic targets at the intersection of lipid metabolism and immunology.

## Key idea

Lipid metabolism regulates immune cells along three interlocking axes that the review treats as a single framework:

1. **Circulating lipids as signals**: SCFAs, LCFAs (PUFAs/MUFAs/SFAs), cholesterol and bile acids signal through dedicated receptors (GPR41/43/109A, CD36, TGR5, FXR, EBI2) and chromatin enzymes (HDACs) to direct macrophage polarization, T-helper subset balance, B-cell humoral output, neutrophil trafficking/NETosis, DC maturation and mast-cell activation.
2. **Membrane lipids as signaling platforms**: phospholipids feed PI3K/AKT, IP3/NFAT, DAG→PA→NF-κB cascades downstream of TCR/BCR; sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) via S1PR1–5 controls immune cell trafficking and macrophage polarization; lipid rafts cluster BCR/TCR coreceptors to license proximal signaling.
3. **Intracellular lipid reprogramming as a fate switch**: de novo lipogenesis (ACLY-ACC-FASN-SCD), the mevalonate pathway (HMG-CoA-reductase→GGPP), and FAO (CPT1) determine whether immune cells become effector or memory/regulatory; SREBP1, PPARs, FABPs and lipid droplets are the transcriptional and storage nodes that integrate these flows.

A unifying theme is that the SAME lipid often has OPPOSITE effects across cell types (acetate vs propionate/butyrate; hydrophilic vs lipophilic bile acids; cholesterol promoting macrophage NLRP3 vs CD8 ER stress) — context defines outcome.

## Method

Narrative review. No new wet-lab data. The authors curate primary literature spanning ~520 citations across immunology, cell biology, lipidomics and clinical pharmacology, organized into: history/timeline of lipid metabolism research; circulating lipid effects on each immune lineage; membrane-lipid signaling; intracellular reprogramming (synthesis/catabolism/storage); multilevel regulation (genetic/epigenetic/posttranscriptional/posttranslational); and disease- and therapy-focused syntheses including ongoing global clinical trials.

## Results

### Circulating lipids — cell-by-cell effects

- **SCFAs (acetate, propionate, butyrate)** from gut-microbiota fermentation of dietary fibre signal through GPR41/43/109A and inhibit HDACs. Butyrate inhibits M1 polarization and drives M2 via NF-κB p65 acetylation and Akt/mTOR/FoxO3a; in T cells, HDAC inhibition raises p70 S6K acetylation and rS6 phosphorylation to support Th1/Th17; in B cells, SCFAs promote plasma-cell generation via mitochondrial OXPHOS+glycolysis; acetate→acetyl-CoA→TCA→protein acetylation induces IL-10⁺ Breg differentiation.
- **LCFAs**: PUFAs (n-3 DHA/EPA; n-6) integrate into membranes and suppress CD4⁺ T cell activation (lower MHC-II, CD40/CD80/CD86, IL-6, IL-12), promote Treg, suppress Th2/Th1, and inhibit FcεRI mast-cell activation. MUFA oleic acid boosts human Treg via FAO→OXPHOS→Foxp3/STAT5. SFAs (palmitic acid) act as TLR agonists driving M1 macrophage TNF-α/IL-1β/IL-6; lauric acid drives Th1/Th17 via p38/MAPK and worsens EAE.
- **Cholesterol**: hepatocyte-derived cholesterol uptake by macrophages enhances TLR signaling and primes NLRP3-inflammasome activation by cholesterol crystals in C5aR1/ROS-dependent fashion; CD8⁺ T-cell cholesterol accumulation in tumours causes ER stress and exhaustion; oxysterol 7α,25-OHC signals via EBI2/GPR183 to localize activated CD4⁺ T cells to B–T interface and seed Tfh; high-cholesterol diet drives neutrophil hepatic infiltration and NETosis in atherosclerotic plaques.
- **Bile acids**: DCA and LCA expand granulocyte-macrophage progenitors; DCA polarizes macrophages to M1 via TLR2 transactivation by M2 muscarinic receptor; LCA derivatives 3-oxoLCA and isoLCA are TGR5 agonists promoting M2. 3-oxoLCA antagonizes RORγt and blocks Th17; isoalloLCA induces mitochondrial ROS and NR4A1 binding at the Foxp3 locus to drive Treg differentiation (Foxp3 CNS1-independent of vitamin D / FXR CNS3 enhancer). Hydrophilic BAs (UDCA) suppress mast-cell histamine release; lipophilic BAs (CDCA, DCA) activate it.

### Membrane lipids — signaling platforms

- **Phospholipids**: PLC hydrolyses PIP2→IP3+DAG; IP3 raises Ca²⁺ → CaN dephosphorylates NFAT → NFAT-driven T-cell transcription. DGK converts DAG→PA → NF-κB activation; DGK overexpression cripples TCR signaling, deficiency boosts CD8 effector but blunts memory CD8 recall. PI3K→PIP3→AKT phosphorylates BAD/FOXO/MDM2/IKK/ACLY in B cells, controlling cycle, survival, lipogenesis, and Ig production. FOXO deletion blocks B-cell development at EBF1/IL-7R/RAG/AID stages.
- **Sphingolipids**: S1P generated by Sphk1/2 binds S1PR1–5. S1PR activation switches M1→M2 in LDLR⁻/⁻ peritoneal macrophages, alleviating atherosclerosis (SEW2871 agonist confirms; S1PR2 KO blunts effect). S1P-S1PR1 drives CD4 Th1/Th17 differentiation and Th17 intestinal egress to kidney via CCL20/CCR6; S1PR2 governs neutrophil bone-marrow trafficking and NETosis; Sphk2 KO impairs mast-cell degranulation.
- **Lipid rafts**: cholesterol- and sphingolipid-rich microdomains. Upon BCR engagement, BCR clusters into rafts, recruiting Lyn → ITAM phosphorylation → SYK → BLNK → BTK/PLC-γ2 → NF-κB/PI3K-AKT for B-cell activation; Raftlin scaffolds Lyn/GM1 in rafts. TCR clusters with Lck on activation; Lck phosphorylates CD3/ζ ITAMs → ZAP-70 → LAT → Gads/SLP-76/PLC-γ1 → Ca²⁺/PKC/MAPK. Glycosphingolipid depletion in CD4 rafts impairs TCR signaling and Th17 differentiation; α2,6-sialylation loss alleviates ulcerative colitis by mis-localizing TCR.

### Intracellular reprogramming

- B-cell activation upregulates ACACA, Elovl1/5/6, FASN, SCD2; PPP→NADPH and citrate→ACLY→acetyl-CoA support FA and cholesterol synthesis. ACLY inhibition cripples B-cell proliferation, endomembrane expansion, CD138/Blimp-1; SCD-derived oleic acid is essential for GC formation.
- Mevalonate pathway is induced on CD40 stimulation; HMG-CoA-reductase generates cholesterol/GGPP; statins inhibit this and impair B-cell antigen presentation; Breg IL-10 output depends on a GGPP-initiated PI3Kδ-AKT-GSK-3 cascade driving Blimp-1.
- CD8⁺ T-cell effector responses require ACLY-derived acetyl-CoA for IFN-γ output (especially under IL-12 stimulation and nutrient restriction); ACC inhibition reprograms CD8 metabolism, raises survival and antitumor multi-functionality; FASN loss raises MHC-I on tumours (enhances CD8 killing), but FASN is needed for memory CD8 survival and for Treg function (loss → tumour suppression).
- Mevalonate pathway / GGPP→STAT5 phosphorylation maintains Treg function; statins suppress Treg activity and conversely enhance Th1/cytolytic-T antitumor responses.
- Neutrophil maturation requires FASN (deficiency → apoptosis); Atg7⁻/⁻ myeloblast block can be rescued by exogenous FFAs.
- DCs synthesize palmitate via FASN downstream of ACC1/SREBP1 to remodel ER/Golgi for antigen presentation; FASN inhibition impairs maturation and antitumor function in the TME.
- FAO via CPT1 supports central-memory CD8 (linked to AMPK), TRM, GC B cells, neutrophil/DC homeostasis; FABPs (FABP4/5) supply lipid to TRM and Treg.

## Discussion captured

### Authors' interpretation

- Lipid metabolism is positioned by the authors as a single integrative regulator of immune function rather than a passive nutrient pool: signalling, membrane organization, and biosynthesis/catabolism converge on the same fate-determining nodes (SREBP1, PPARs, mTOR, NF-κB, lipid rafts).
- They argue that the same lipid produces opposite phenotypes depending on cell type (acetate vs propionate/butyrate; hydrophilic vs lipophilic bile acids; cholesterol promoting macrophage NLRP3 vs CD8 ER-stress exhaustion), making cell-resolution lipid-immunology mandatory for translation.
- They contrast immune cells with non-immune cells (muscle, endothelium, adipocyte) that use lipids predominantly for storage/energy, framing immune lipid metabolism as uniquely dynamic.

### Comparisons with prior literature (made by authors)

- They build on Wculek et al. and other tissue-macrophage metabolism reviews to extend immunometabolism beyond glucose/glutamine into a lipid-first framework.
- They cite the SCFA-HDAC body of literature (Smith/Furusawa/Park) for Treg/Th17 control, and the Schoggins/Bensinger/Schultze line for cholesterol immunometabolism.
- They specifically credit Hang/Huh laboratories for the discovery of 3-oxoLCA / isoalloLCA as endogenous Th17/Treg rebalancers and Song et al. for the NR4A1–Foxp3 mechanism.

### Mechanistic hypotheses proposed

- That cholesterol-crystal IL-1β secretion requires intracellular C5aR1→mitochondrial ROS as a necessary second signal for NLRP3 (p.7).
- That isoDCA suppresses DC immunostimulatory function in an FXR-dependent way to enable peripheral Treg expansion, with DC-specific FXR deletion phenocopying isoDCA (p.7).
- That an n-3:n-6 PUFA ratio rather than absolute n-3 intake dictates dietary immune modulation (p.6).

### Caveats and self-criticism

- The authors note that lipid raft research in immune-cell therapies remains underdeveloped relative to soluble-lipid mediators and call for more functional work on rafts in disease.
- They acknowledge ongoing debate about whether SCFAs directly drive peripheral CD4→Foxp3⁺ Treg differentiation (vs only enhancing Treg function via GPR43/HDAC).
- They flag that many findings rely on mouse models, with limited human validation for several bile-acid and oxysterol axes.

### Future directions suggested

- Combining lipid-targeting agents (statins, S1PR modulators, FASN/ACC inhibitors, FXR/TGR5 modulators) with immune checkpoint blockade.
- Lipidomic biomarkers for autoimmunity and cancer staging.
- Engineering gut-microbe consortia to tune bile-acid pools (e.g., isoDCA producers) for inflammatory bowel disease and Treg-deficient states.
- Targeting raft components and α2,6-sialylation for ulcerative colitis and autoimmunity.

## Limitations

- Narrative review without systematic search, predefined inclusion criteria or meta-analytic synthesis; selection bias is not discussed.
- The "+/-" Table 1 summary collapses dose-, time- and concentration-dependent effects into binary up/down arrows, masking the dual modulatory behaviour the authors themselves note (e.g., SCFAs).
- Many mechanistic claims are derived from murine or in-vitro systems; human translation for several axes (isoalloLCA Treg, n-3 PUFA Th2/Th1 balance) remains incomplete.
- The PDF available to ingest is truncated mid-FAO section, so the ingest captures the framework and circulating/membrane/intracellular axes thoroughly but only partially covers the multilevel regulation and the disease-by-disease therapy sections; figures (especially Figs 1, 3, 4, 5, 6) carry compact information that text alone underdetermines.

## Open questions

### Open questions raised by authors

- Whether and how SCFAs drive peripheral CD4 → Foxp3⁺ Treg conversion vs merely sustaining Treg function.
- Whether n-3:n-6 ratio rather than absolute n-3 intake is the operative dietary lever for immune modulation.
- What functional roles lipid rafts play in immune-cell-based disease therapies (called out as understudied).

### Open questions identified during ingest

- How does the cholesterol → ER-stress → CD8 exhaustion axis interact with the TREM2-LAM (lipid-associated TAM) program in solid tumours? Combined targeting potential.
- Are isoalloLCA / 3-oxoLCA findings reproducible in humans at physiological gut concentrations, or do they require engineered consortia for clinical effect?
- The dual M1-M2 modulatory pattern of SCFAs (acetate suppresses M2 in asthma but propionate/butyrate suppress M1) — is this concentration- or receptor-isoform-dependent (GPR43 vs HDAC)?
- How do FASN-loss CD8 anti-tumor benefits (raised MHC-I) reconcile with loss of memory CD8 survival? Is there a dose window for clinical FASN inhibition?
- Lipid raft α2,6-sialylation could be targeted in ulcerative colitis — is there a parallel sialylation handle in psoriasis / atopic dermatitis where Th17 is similarly central?

## My take

- For the thesis project, this review is a "lipid backbone" reference: it gives a cell-type-resolved map from SCFA→HDAC/GPR all the way to FAO/CPT1, which is exactly the framework needed to connect efferocytosis-fuelled FAO ([[concepts/efferocytosis-metabolic-rewiring-fao]]) and PPARγ-driven alveolar macrophages ([[concepts/alveolar-macrophage-lipid-catabolism-pparg]]) with the broader immunometabolic landscape.
- The mevalonate/GGPP→STAT5→Treg branch is particularly underweighted in cancer-immunology discussions and is worth pulling into discussions of metformin/statin combination immunotherapy ([[concepts/metformin-ici-synergy-treg-teff]]).
- The cholesterol → CD8 ER-stress exhaustion axis is a clean, testable claim that connects to TREM2 lipid-laden TAM biology ([[concepts/trem2-tam-hypoxic-niche-immune-suppression]]) and may suggest combination strategies.
- The "+/-" table is convenient but should not be used as a citation — go to the underlying primary papers, especially for any quantitative claim.
- For epigenetic-focused work, the HDAC-acetylation node SCFAs hit is a strong link to NF-κB p65 acetylation, plasma-cell program, and mTOR — relevant to projects mixing metabolism and chromatin.
