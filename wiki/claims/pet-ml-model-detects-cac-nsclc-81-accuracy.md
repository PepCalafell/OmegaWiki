## Statement

A machine-learning model trained on whole-body PET/CT imaging data from a retrospective lung-cancer cohort identifies cancer-associated cachexia at diagnosis with 81% accuracy. The most predictive features are PET-tracer uptake values in spleen, pancreas, liver and adipose tissue — suggesting that systemic metabolic redistribution detectable on routine PET imaging captures cachexia onset.

## Evidence summary

Retrospective single-cohort study; ML feature analysis.

## Conditions and scope

NSCLC only; single-centre retrospective design; needs prospective multi-centre replication.

## Counter-evidence

ML models trained on single-centre data often fail to generalize.

## Linked ideas

- Opportunistic use of routinely acquired imaging for CAC detection.

## Open questions

- Whether the model generalizes to other tumour types and to non-FDG tracers.
