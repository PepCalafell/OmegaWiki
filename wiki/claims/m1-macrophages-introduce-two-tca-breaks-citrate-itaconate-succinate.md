## Statement

Upon classical activation, M1 macrophages introduce two distinct breaks in the TCA cycle: (1) IDH1 downregulation accumulates citrate (fueling acetyl-CoA for histone acetylation and FAS) and feeds IRG1-mediated itaconate synthesis; (2) itaconate inhibits SDH/complex II, accumulating succinate which stabilizes HIF-1α and drives Warburg-like glycolytic reprogramming.

## Evidence summary

Established in multiple foundational immunometabolism studies (Tannahill 2013, Mills 2016, Lampropoulou 2016) and synthesized in [[papers/metabolism-tissue-macrophages-homeostasis-pathology]]. The two-break model is the canonical molecular basis of M1 macrophage immunometabolic reprogramming.

## Conditions and scope

- Most evidence is from in-vitro LPS-stimulated mouse BMDMs.
- Tissue-resident macrophages may not all execute the same program in vivo.
- Itaconate's SDH inhibition is one of several effects; KEAP1-modification → NRF2 activation is parallel.

## Counter-evidence

- Some succinate accumulation observed under inflammation is from non-glutaminolytic sources; quantitative flux contribution debated.
