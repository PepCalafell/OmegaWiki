## Statement

oxLDL engagement of CD36 on peritoneal macrophages first repurposes the ETC toward ROS/superoxide production (structural mitochondrial changes that suppress OXPHOS), then triggers a secondary glycolytic switch with NF-κB-driven inflammatory cytokine output — connecting lipid sensing to atherogenic inflammation.

## Evidence summary

Synthesized in [[papers/metabolism-tissue-macrophages-homeostasis-pathology]] (Chen 2020 and lineage).

## Conditions and scope

- Mouse peritoneal models; relevance to in-vivo atherosclerotic plaque macrophages corroborative but indirect.

## Counter-evidence

- Some macrophage subpopulations exposed to oxLDL upregulate cholesterol efflux without inflammatory shift.
