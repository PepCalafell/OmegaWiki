## Statement

In SARS-CoV-2 lung infection, type-I and type-II interferons drive IDO upregulation, which produces kynurenine, which activates AHR in alveolar epithelial cells to transcribe mucins. Pharmacologic AHR antagonism (CH-223191) reduces mucin output and ameliorates lung pathology in infected mice. The axis identifies AHR antagonism as a host-directed antiviral strategy for COVID-19 pulmonary disease.

## Evidence summary

Reported in [[papers/role-ahr-host-pathogen-interactions]] citing ref 132. Complementary observations: AHR is upregulated in nasal swabs / BAL of high-viral-load COVID-19 patients; CH-223191 suppresses SARS-CoV-2 replication in vitro (refs 127–129).

## Conditions and scope

Murine SARS-CoV-2 model; clinical translatability still being established.

## Counter-evidence

FICZ or omeprazole AHR activation in Vero E6 cells suppresses ACE2 and inhibits SARS-CoV-2 infectivity in vitro (ref 131) — illustrating ligand-specific divergence at the cellular vs whole-organism level.
