## Statement

Facilitated dissociation can drive previously inaccessible kinetic behaviours: a kinetically trapped target–effector relay that reconfigures through a chain reaction only when triggered, and rapid effector-induced breakage of an otherwise stable high-affinity split-enzyme (NanoBiT luciferase) complex.

## Evidence summary

Chain reaction: release of E2–target from AS114 and subsequent H2 switching is slow but "accelerates markedly upon addition of the original effector" (~56-fold acceleration, FRET; p.5). Split enzyme: NanoBiT-tagged AS1/target shows luciferase activity that "disappears much more rapidly upon addition of effector and excess untagged target than upon addition of excess untagged target alone" (16-fold acceleration; p.5).

## Conditions and scope

In vitro FRET and luminescence assays; designed AS114/AS1 systems.

## Counter-evidence

Demonstrated in defined in vitro systems; scaling to many chained orthogonal modules is proposed but not shown.
