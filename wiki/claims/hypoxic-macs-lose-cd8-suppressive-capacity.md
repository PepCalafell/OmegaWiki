## Statement

In a CFSE-based allogeneic CD8⁺ T-cell coculture assay, M-CSF-differentiated macrophages cultured under hypoxia (both unstimulated iMAC1 and LPS-activated mMAC1) suppress T-cell proliferation significantly less than their normoxic counterparts. This functionally inverts the conventional M-CSF MAC suppressive phenotype.

## Evidence summary

- Allogeneic CD8⁺ T cells + MACs, TCR-stimulated, CFSE proliferation readout (Calafell 2024 Fig. 1D, fig. S1A).
- Both steady-state (iMAC1 vs iMAC21) and activated (mMAC1 vs mMAC21) hypoxic MACs show reduced suppression.

## Conditions and scope

- Allogeneic in vitro coculture; not autologous tumor-antigen-specific.
- M-CSF-only ontogeny.

## Counter-evidence

- In vivo TAMs from hypoxic tumor regions are typically immunosuppressive in T-cell exclusion assays — the discrepancy may reflect MAC heterogeneity, chronic vs acute hypoxia, or tissue-resident vs MoDM differences.

## Linked ideas

- Mechanistic basis for mMAC1 ↔ T-cell crosstalk and the immune-hot BLCA correlation.
- Candidate functional assay for screening mMAC1-inducing compounds.

## Open questions

- Whether the reduced suppression is causally linked to C2 demethylation or to surface marker shifts.
- Tumor-antigen-specific T-cell readouts (not allogeneic).
