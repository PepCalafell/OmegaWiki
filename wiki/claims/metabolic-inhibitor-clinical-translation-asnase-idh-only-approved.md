## Statement

Despite dozens of metabolic inhibitors tested in clinical phases, only two classes have received cancer-treatment approval: L-asparaginase (ASNase) for ALL/NKTCL and mIDH1/mIDH2 inhibitors (ivosidenib, enasidenib, vorasidenib). Most others (notably mitochondrial complex I inhibitors) have failed due to toxicity, lack of efficacy, or tumour metabolic plasticity bypassing the targeted pathway.

## Evidence summary

- Explicit synthesis in Ricci 2025 of the past two decades of metabolic-inhibitor oncology trials.

## Conditions and scope

- As of 2025; emerging biomarker-stratified trials may shift this balance.

## Counter-evidence

- New brain-penetrant mIDH inhibitor (vorasidenib) shows recent success; biomarker selection may rescue prior failures.

## Linked ideas

- Lack of patient-stratification by metabolic phenotype is a major reason for past failures.

## Open questions

- Will biomarker-driven metabolic-inhibitor trials succeed where unselected populations failed?
