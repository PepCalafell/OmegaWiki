## Statement

The concentration of gene-study attention follows a preferential-attachment (rich-gets-richer) dynamic: the number of new yearly publications on a gene is linearly proportional to the size of its existing literature.

## Evidence summary

Quoted: "the number of new yearly publications focusing on a given gene is linearly proportional to the size of previous literature on it (see SI, Fig. S6)." (p.3) The authors attribute this to social factors rooted in funding, mentorship, tools, and citations.

## Conditions and scope

Observational bibliometric pattern; mechanism inferred from the authors' prior network-science work.

## Counter-evidence

Cannot fully separate preferential attachment from genuine biological importance driving repeated study.
