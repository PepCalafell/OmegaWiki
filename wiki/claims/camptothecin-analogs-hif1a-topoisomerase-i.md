## Statement

Camptothecin (CPT) and its FDA-approved analogs (SN-38, the active metabolite of irinotecan; topotecan; irinotecan) are Topoisomerase I inhibitors used clinically for colorectal, ovarian, lung, and other cancers. Beyond their canonical DNA-damaging mechanism, they also block HIF-1α expression — likely via transcriptional inhibition coupled with mRNA destabilization secondary to Topo-I-trapping-induced replication stress. The HIF-1α suppression in turn reduces cancer-stem-cell numbers (HIF-1α is a known CSC maintenance factor in many cancers). This positions Topo-I inhibitors as drug-repurposing candidates for hypoxia-targeted therapy, particularly for hypoxia-rich tumors. CRLX101 (also called NLG207) is a nanoparticle-drug conjugate (NDC) of CPT that delivers the drug specifically to tumor tissue via the EPR effect, reducing systemic toxicity. CRLX101 has been studied in phase I/II trials for solid tumors and hypoxia-driven cancers.

## Conditions and scope

- HIF-1α suppression is downstream of Topo-I inhibition; the mechanism is not strict HIF-1α-direct binding.
- Topotecan and irinotecan are already FDA-approved for cancer indications; CRLX101 is investigational.

## Counter-evidence

- The HIF-1α-suppressive effect requires sustained drug exposure; intermittent dosing schedules (clinical norm) may not achieve full HIF-1α suppression.
- CSC reduction is one of many off-target effects; the dominant clinical efficacy of Topo-I inhibitors comes from cytotoxic DNA damage.
