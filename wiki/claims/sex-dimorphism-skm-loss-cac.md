## Statement

Across various advanced cancer types, the loss of skeletal muscle mass during CAC is observed more consistently in male patients than in female patients, suggesting sex-dimorphic mechanisms.

## Evidence summary

Multiple cohorts in advanced lung, gastrointestinal and pancreatic cancers show this pattern; Klassen et al. specifically reported greater FOLFIRINOX-associated SKM loss in males.

## Conditions and scope

Advanced cancer; reduced power in single-sex cohorts; mechanism unclear (hormonal, body-composition-baseline, behavioural).

## Counter-evidence

Some cohorts show no clear sex difference; selection biases plausible.

## Linked ideas

- Sex-stratified trial analysis is warranted.

## Open questions

- Is sex dimorphism driven by androgen/oestrogen signaling, baseline muscle mass, or differential cytokine response?
