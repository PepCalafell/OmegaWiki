## Statement

Macrophage efferocytosis (engulfment of apoptotic cells) uses two parallel recognition systems for phosphatidylserine (PtdSer) on apoptotic cells: (1) TIM4 directly binds PtdSer; (2) TAM-family receptors (TYRO3, AXL, MERTK) bind PtdSer indirectly via bridging molecules — GAS6 (all three TAMs), protein S (TYRO3, MERTK), MFGE8 (αVβ3 integrin), and complement C1Q (complement receptors). The multi-receptor / multi-ligand architecture provides redundancy and ensures effective clearance under physiological conditions.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] presents the consensus efferocytosis machinery (Fig. 2) and references the relevant primary literature on TIM4, TAM receptors, MFGE8, GAS6, protein S, and C1Q. Single-knockout mice (MERTK, MFGE8, TIM4, C1Q) develop SLE-like autoimmunity, supporting non-redundant contributions despite overall pathway redundancy.

## Conditions and scope

- Murine genetic and biochemical evidence dominates.
- Human translation: C1Q-deficient patients develop SLE and chronic glomerulonephritis (Lazarov 2023 cites refs 186-187).
- Tissue context matters — some receptors are more important in some tissues (MERTK in retinal pigment epithelium for photoreceptor outer segment clearance, for example).

## Counter-evidence

- Single-receptor KOs are only partially penetrant for autoimmunity; double/triple KOs are needed for severe phenotype.
- Bridging-ligand pleiotropy — GAS6 / protein S also act on platelets and endothelium, complicating in-vivo interpretation.
- Some PtdSer-displaying targets (apoptotic neutrophils, lupus-associated debris) have receptor preferences that are not fully mapped.
