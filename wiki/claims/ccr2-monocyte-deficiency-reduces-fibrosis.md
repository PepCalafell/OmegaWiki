## Statement

Mice deficient in CCR2 (the receptor for CCL2/MCP-1; required for circulating monocyte egress from bone marrow) exhibit substantially milder fibrosis in injury models across multiple organs — liver, heart, kidney, and lung. This is operational evidence for the functional dichotomy between TRM (restorative) and BMDM (pro-fibrotic) macrophages: removing the recruited monocyte-derived population leaves repair largely intact while preventing fibrotic outcome.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] cites refs 160-163 (multiple primary studies on liver, heart, kidney, lung fibrosis in CCR2-KO mice). The dichotomy is consistent across organs and injury models.

## Conditions and scope

- Mouse genetic evidence (CCR2 KO) is the primary substrate.
- Translation to human disease: anti-CCR2 / anti-CCL2 drugs have been tested in clinical trials (cenicriviroc in MASH; results disappointing).
- Some injury contexts (severe acute injury, certain infections) require BMDMs for repair — CCR2 KO is not always beneficial.

## Counter-evidence

- Cenicriviroc (CCR2/CCR5 inhibitor) failed phase III trials in MASH (AURORA), suggesting the mouse-to-human translation is incomplete.
- Some studies report worse outcome in CCR2 KO under specific injury conditions (acute kidney injury — depends on timing).
- The CCR2-monocyte arm interacts with multiple other immune populations (NK cells, T cells); the fibrosis effect may not be purely macrophage-driven.

## Open questions

- Why anti-CCR2 therapy underperforms in human fibrotic disease.
- Whether tissue-specific or temporally-staged CCR2 blockade can yield better clinical results.
- The role of CCR2-independent monocyte populations (Ly6C-low non-classical) in fibrosis.
