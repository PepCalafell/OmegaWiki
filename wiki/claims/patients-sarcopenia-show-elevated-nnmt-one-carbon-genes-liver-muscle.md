## Statement

The one-carbon-metabolism signature documented in mouse cachexia models translates to humans: sarcopenic cancer patients show elevated NNMT and elevated one-carbon-signature gene expression in liver and muscle biopsies versus non-sarcopenic cancer patients — the principal patient-translation finding of the paper.

## Evidence summary

Quoted: "Sarcopenia was associated with increased gene expression of NNMT and increased expression of the signature gene set overall, in accordance with our previous observation linking this pathway to muscle wasting."

## Conditions and scope

Patient cohort sample size, cancer types, and sarcopenia-cut-off criteria detailed in Suppl Table 3; main-text presentation summarised.

## Counter-evidence

Patient cohort size limited; no metabolite-level (MNAM, sarcosine, methyllysines) measurements in patient tissue reported in main text.

## Open questions

- Whether plasma/urinary MNAM, sarcosine, methyllysines correlate quantitatively with sarcopenia severity in patients.
- Whether the human one-carbon signature predicts cachexia progression longitudinally.
