## Statement

Macrophages descended from haematopoietic stem cells (i.e. monocyte-derived macrophages, BMDMs) have substantially shorter half-lives than embryo-derived TRMs. Their tissue pool is sustained by continuous recruitment of CCR2-positive Ly6Chi inflammatory monocytes from blood and rapidly expandable under challenge — this is the functional opposite of TRM local self-renewal.

## Evidence summary

The review [[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] consolidates parabiosis, BrdU/EdU labelling, and shielded-irradiation chimerism evidence: monocytes turn over in days to weeks; BMDMs in inflamed or recovering tissues are replaced from blood within similar timescales; CCR2-deficient mice (lacking monocyte egress) lack the BMDM compartment in many models.

## Conditions and scope

- Steady-state and acute inflammation in mouse tissues; broadly transferrable to human.
- Gut lamina propria is an exception — BMDM-derived macrophages may self-maintain locally to some extent.
- Whole-body irradiation + BMT replaces TRMs with BMDMs that subsequently can persist long-term, but this is a technical artefact not a homeostatic regime.

## Counter-evidence

- Some long-residence BMDM populations have been reported in skin, gut, and post-injury heart, suggesting the dichotomy is gradient-like.
- Engrafted BMDMs in TRM-depleted niches can over time acquire features of resident macrophages, partially blurring the contrast.

## Open questions
