## Statement

Microglia engulf and eliminate synapses during developmental refinement of neural circuits using the classical complement cascade. C1Q is deposited on weak or unused synapses, activating downstream complement (C3 fragments). Microglia recognize complement-tagged synapses via complement receptors (CR3, CD11b/CD18) and engulf them. Loss of complement components (C1Q, C3) or microglia-specific phagocytosis machinery causes circuit-level connectivity defects and altered behaviour.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] cites Stevens 2007 *Cell* (complement-CNS synapse elimination), Paolicelli 2011 *Science* (synaptic pruning), and additional studies (Wake 2009, Tremblay 2010, Squarzoni 2014). The picture is well-established for developmental pruning; activity-dependent modulation is also documented (visual experience changes microglia-synapse interactions).

## Conditions and scope

- Developmental window (P10-P30 in mouse) is the strongest pruning phase.
- Adult ongoing pruning persists at lower rate.
- Activity-dependent — weaker synapses get C1Q-tagged preferentially.
- Disease relevance: aberrant pruning is implicated in schizophrenia (C4 SNP increases risk), Alzheimer's (C1Q-driven excessive synapse loss), West Nile virus encephalitis.

## Counter-evidence

- Complement is not the only synaptic-pruning mechanism — phosphatidylserine flipping on weak synapses can be recognized via PtdSer-receptor pathways (TIM4, TAM-bridging) without complement involvement.
- Astrocytes also prune synapses via MEGF10 and MERTK independent of microglia.
- Some studies have questioned whether microglia engulf functional synapses or only debris already disconnected.

## Open questions

- The molecular logic by which C1Q is selectively deposited on weak synapses.
- Whether complement-driven pruning in adult disease (Alzheimer's) re-uses developmental machinery or hijacks it pathologically.
- Pharmacological rescue strategies — anti-C1Q antibodies are in trials for ALS and Alzheimer's.
