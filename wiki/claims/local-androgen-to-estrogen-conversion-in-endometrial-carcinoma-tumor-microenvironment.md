## Statement

In an EEC Visium dataset, malignant regions show inferred activity in the androgen→estrogen biosynthetic step (consistent with local conversion of circulating androgens via aromatase) and significantly increased co-localization of estradiol biosynthesis with ESR1 receptor expression — supporting an intra-tumoral estrogen-signaling axis that may contribute to EEC tumorigenesis independent of (or in addition to) obesity-driven systemic estrogen.

## Evidence summary

scCellFie did not detect upstream cholesterol→sex-hormone biosynthesis in malignant regions, but did infer activity in the intermediate androgen→estrogen step (Supp Fig 9b-d). Spatial CCC analysis using the new estradiol-biosynthesis task and ESR1 receptor expression as ligand–receptor pair showed significantly increased co-localization in malignant regions (Fig 7e, Supp Fig 8c). Authors interpret this as local conversion of circulating androgens consistent with prior literature.

## Conditions and scope

Restricted to a single EEC Visium dataset; transcriptomic inference only; aromatase enzyme activity, circulating androgen levels, and in situ estradiol measurements are not provided.

## Counter-evidence

Population-level evidence that obesity-driven adipose-tissue estrogen synthesis is the dominant risk-factor mechanism for EEC; local-vs-systemic estrogen contributions remain debated in the literature.

## Open questions

- Does aromatase inhibition (letrozole, anastrozole) reduce local estradiol in EEC organoids or PDX where systemic estrogen is controlled?
- Is local androgen→estrogen activity associated with EEC stage, grade, or response to hormonal therapy?
