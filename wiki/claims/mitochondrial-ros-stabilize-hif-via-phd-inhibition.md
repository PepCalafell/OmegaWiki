## Statement

PHD enzymes are inhibited by ROS — most prominently mitochondrially-derived hydrogen peroxide and superoxide. ROS oxidize the Fe(II) in PHD catalytic centers to Fe(III), reducing enzymatic activity. This stabilizes HIF-α even in normoxic conditions when ROS are elevated, providing a redox-coupled input into oxygen sensing.

## Evidence summary

Mechanism supported by reductionist biochemistry (in vitro PHD assays) and cellular pharmacology (antioxidants block HIF stabilization). Luo et al. 2022 integrates this in the cross-talk model.

## Conditions and scope

- Antioxidants (NAC, mitoTEMPO) attenuate ROS-driven HIF stabilization
- Operative in viral infection (SARS-CoV-2 ORF3a → ROS → HIF-1α), inflammatory states, and cancer cell mitochondrial dysfunction

## Counter-evidence

Some hypoxia-dependent HIF-α stabilization is ROS-independent (oxygen-substrate limitation of PHD).
