## Statement

Macrophages — and within the tumor, TAMs — are the most abundant immune cell population in the tumor microenvironment (TME). They accumulate preferentially in hypoxic tumor regions (>100 µm from functional vasculature) rather than in normoxic perivascular regions, due to both active recruitment of monocytes (CCL2, CCL8, CSF1) and hampered egress from hypoxic niches (Sema3A-NRP1-PlexinA stop signal). Once entrapped, TAMs gradually polarize toward M2-like protumoral phenotypes. The spatial coincidence of hypoxic regions and M2 TAMs is associated with poor patient prognosis.

## Conditions and scope

- "Most abundant" applies to most solid tumors but not all (e.g. CLL, MM the abundance ranking differs).
- TAM phenotype heterogeneity in single-cell taxonomies (TREM2+ LAM, IL4I1+ Mac, etc.) is not yet integrated into this M1/M2 spatial paradigm.

## Counter-evidence

- Inflamed / "hot" tumors may have CD8+ T cells outnumbering TAMs in some subregions.
- Some studies report TAMs distributed throughout the tumor with only mild hypoxic-region enrichment, especially in highly vascularized tumors.
