## Statement

EMPs in the mid-gestation yolk sac give rise to PreMacs that disperse through the embryo concurrently with organogenesis. PreMacs differentiate into tissue-resident macrophages in their colonized tissues *before* the HSC-derived haematopoiesis becomes active in the AGM/fetal-liver axis. This temporal ordering — EMP-derived TRMs first, HSC-derived monocytes/BMDMs later — is the developmental basis of the resident-vs-monocyte-derived dichotomy.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] cites fate-mapping studies (Schulz 2012, Gomez Perdiguero 2015, Mass 2016) showing PreMac dissemination at E10.25 and earlier, prior to HSC-AGM emergence. The PreMac population already exhibits patchy lineage-determining-factor expression at this stage, supporting both the temporal precedence and the stochastic specification model the authors favour.

## Conditions and scope

- Mouse embryo. Inferred for human via cross-species concordance and human transplant chimerism studies.
- Specifically about the *temporal ordering*; spatial colonization patterns (which tissues receive PreMacs first) are tissue-dependent and partly distinct.

## Counter-evidence

- Whether the PreMac wave is fully homogeneous or contains internal sub-waves remains debated.
- Some HSC-derived contribution to early colonized tissues has been suggested by individual fate-mapping studies (acknowledged as outliers).

## Open questions

- Whether PreMacs settle in tissues by chemotaxis to specific niche cytokines or by passive vascular distribution followed by niche selection.
- Quantitative timing differences between organs in PreMac arrival and TRM specification.
