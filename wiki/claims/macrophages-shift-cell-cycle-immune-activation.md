## Statement
In response to immune stimulation, macrophages downregulate cell-cycle and metabolic maintenance genes (often only at the transcriptional level, preserving open chromatin) while upregulating immune-response programs. The retained chromatin accessibility at downregulated promoters preserves the potential for rapid reactivation after pathogen clearance.

## Evidence summary
Across all six stimuli more genes were downregulated than upregulated; downregulated genes (cell cycle, metabolism) frequently retained chromatin accessibility, whereas chromatin accessibility preferentially increased upon stimulation at immune loci ([[papers/integrated-time-series-analysis-high-content]], Figures 1D, 2).

## Conditions and scope
Murine BMDMs in vitro; first 24 h of response.

## Counter-evidence
None recorded.

## Open questions
Whether transcription-only downregulation with retained accessibility generalizes to in vivo tissue macrophages.
