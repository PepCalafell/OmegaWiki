## Statement

Infected tissues become hypoxic, and that hypoxia acts on both sides of the host–pathogen interface: it can bolster host immune cell function while simultaneously altering the pathogen (reducing P. aeruginosa virulence but increasing its drug resistance), thereby shaping disease progression.

## Evidence summary

Reviewed in [[papers/regulation-immunity-inflammation-hypoxia-immunological-niches]] (cites Schaible et al. work on P. aeruginosa). Links to [[foundations/pseudomonas-aeruginosa-pathogen]].

## Conditions and scope

Hypoxia at infection sites arises from bacterial oxygen consumption, oxygen-impermeable biofilms and inflammation-associated hypoxia.

## Counter-evidence

Net effect is dual-edged (host benefit vs pathogen drug resistance), not uniformly protective.
