## Statement

In peripheral blood monocytes from breast cancer patients, PD-L1 is upregulated during ex vivo maturation/differentiation without requiring IFN-γ. PD-L1 upregulation persists in serum-free medium under combined IFN-γ and IFN-γR blockade, and is enhanced upon adhesion and during M-CSF-driven macrophage differentiation. PD-L1 therefore behaves as a maturation marker rather than a strict inflammatory-response gene in the monocyte-macrophage lineage.

## Evidence summary

- Wang 2024 Fig. 5A-B (fresh vs rested), 5C-D (suspension vs adherent), 5E-F (IFN-γ/IFN-γR blockade), S9C (M-CSF differentiation).

## Conditions and scope

- Patient-derived monocytes (n=5–12 across panels).
- IFN-γ blockade verifies extracellular IFN-γ is not required; cell-intrinsic constitutive IFN-γ pathway activity is not ruled out at the gene level.

## Counter-evidence

- Textbook framing of PD-L1 as IFN-γ → JAK-STAT1-IRF1 inducible (Garcia-Diaz 2017) — the Wang 2024 finding does not refute this in cancer cells (where IFN-γ→pSTAT1→PD-L1 is confirmed in BC cell lines, Fig. 6L-M); it dissociates the monocyte/macrophage route specifically.
