## Statement

Tumor hypoxia is not a single state but is operationally divided into three subtypes:
1. **Chronic hypoxia** — diffusion-limited; tumor cells located >100 µm from functional vessels experience prolonged (>24 h) low pO₂. Drives HIF stabilization, EMT, glycolytic switch, and stem-cell maintenance.
2. **Acute hypoxia** — perfusion-limited; transient vasoconstriction or vessel occlusion produces short-duration (<24 h) hypoxia, often followed by reoxygenation. Drives ROS bursts upon reperfusion.
3. **Cyclic / intermittent hypoxia (IH)** — recurring hypoxia-reoxygenation (H-R) cycles on timescales of minutes to days. Drives sustained HIF-1α + NF-κB activation via ROS, distinct exosome cargos, and is linked clinically to obstructive sleep apnea–cancer comorbidity. NSCLC PD-L1 upregulation on TAMs is one example.

The three subtypes have overlapping but distinguishable molecular signatures. No agreed-upon standard exists for in vitro modeling (chamber settings, O₂ concentrations, H-R cycle duration).

## Conditions and scope

- Three indicators: total duration, oxygen concentration, frequency of H-R cycles.
- Cyclic hypoxia produces *quantitatively different* downstream outputs vs chronic hypoxia (ROS bursts, NF-κB priming).

## Counter-evidence

- "There is no unambiguous and uniform classification system" (Bai 2022, p.3) — the field's terminology is not standardized.
- Some authors use 2-class chronic/acute, others 3-class chronic/acute/cyclic, others 4-class adding "anoxia (pO₂ ~0)".
