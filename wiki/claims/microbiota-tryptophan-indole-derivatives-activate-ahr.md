## Statement

Gut microbiota convert dietary tryptophan into a spectrum of indole derivatives that act as AHR agonists. These ligands sustain mucosal AHR signalling, drive IL-22 production from CD4+/ILC3 cells, and promote barrier homeostasis. Species including L. reuteri, L. johnsonii and E. coli are documented producers; dysbiosis disrupts this axis.

## Evidence summary

Reviewed in [[papers/role-ahr-host-pathogen-interactions]] (Table 1, refs 47, 53, 161, 162). L. reuteri reprogrammes CD4+ T cells into immunoregulatory CD4+CD8αα+ IELs in a Trp-rich diet; E. coli indoles strengthen colon mucosal barrier and reduce TNF.

## Conditions and scope

Requires dietary Trp; effect strength varies by ligand chemistry (e.g., I3Ald induces IL-22; indoxyl-3-sulfate also activates AHR but is uraemic at high levels).

## Counter-evidence

Short-chain fatty acid effects on AHR are disputed — butyrate may act via HDAC inhibition rather than direct AHR binding (p.182, refs 58–59).
