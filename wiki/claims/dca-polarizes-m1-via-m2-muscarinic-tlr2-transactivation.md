## Statement

In colitis settings, DCA polarizes macrophages toward an M1 inflammatory phenotype, partly through M2 muscarinic-receptor-driven transactivation of TLR2 — a cross-receptor mechanism that links neurotransmitter and bile-acid signalling.

## Evidence summary

Reviewed in [[papers/lipid-metabolism-homeostasis-disease]].

## Conditions and scope

- DSS colitis and related models; gut-resident macrophages.

## Counter-evidence

- LCA derivatives (3-oxoLCA, isoLCA) act as TGR5 agonists and produce the opposite M2 phenotype, indicating bile-acid family heterogeneity.

## Open questions

- Whether selective M2 muscarinic-receptor antagonists can dampen DCA-driven colitis.
