## Statement

In autologous co-culture, anti-PD-L1 blocking antibody does not alter the immunostimulatory effect of PD-L1+ macrophages on CD8+ T cell proliferation, nor does it alter PD-L1−-macrophage-mediated suppression of BiTE-mediated CD8+ T cell killing. The immunoregulatory functions of PD-L1+/− TAMs are therefore not mediated by PD-L1:PD-1 engagement. This has direct clinical implications: anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy may not modulate the TAM-T cell axis observed here, and the survival benefit of PD-L1+ TAMs likely depends on other co-stimulatory or chemokine mechanisms (e.g., CD80 cis-heterodimer preserving CD28 co-stimulation; AREG-ICAM1; ANXA1; MIF).

## Evidence summary

- Wang 2024 Fig. 7D (PD-L1 blockade in T cell proliferation), 7G-H (PD-L1 blockade in BiTE killing), S11I (PD-1 expression confirmation).

## Conditions and scope

- Blocking antibody approach; small-molecule PD-L1 antagonists not tested.
- Patient-derived autologous system; n=6.

## Counter-evidence

- Clinical data showing anti-PD-L1 ICI response correlates with PD-L1+ TAM density (Ahmed 2020, ref 13) — Wang 2024 reconciles: PD-L1+ TAMs may be biomarkers of an immune-active TIME rather than the direct mechanistic target of ICI.

## Linked ideas

- Reframes anti-PD-L1 ICI mechanism: PD-L1+ TAMs may serve as biomarkers more than as direct targets.
- Predicts that pan-PD-L1 blockade should not abolish the protective effect of PD-L1+ TAM enrichment on prognosis.

## Open questions

- Whether PD-L1 blockade affects PD-L1+ TAM survival/abundance in vivo (TAM PD-L1 may protect against PD-1+ T cell killing).
- Whether anti-PD-1 (rather than anti-PD-L1) yields the same null result.
- Identification of the PD-L1:PD-1-independent co-stimulatory pathway.
