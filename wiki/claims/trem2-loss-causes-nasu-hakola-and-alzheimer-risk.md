## Statement

The TREM2 / DAP12 receptor-adapter complex underlies two distinct neurodegenerative diseases. (1) Bi-allelic loss-of-function mutations in *TREM2* or *TYROBP* (DAP12) cause Nasu-Hakola disease (also called PLOSL — polycystic lipomembranous osteodysplasia with sclerosing leukoencephalopathy), a rare autosomal recessive disorder combining presenile dementia and bone cysts. (2) Common variants of TREM2 (most prominently R47H) substantially increase risk of late-onset Alzheimer's disease in genome-wide association studies. The mechanistic substrate is microglial dysfunction — TREM2 senses lipid-rich substrates and is required for the disease-associated microglia (DAM) state that responds to amyloid plaques and demyelination.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] reviews the Mendelian and GWAS evidence and frames TREM2 as a critical microglial signalling node. The bone phenotype in Nasu-Hakola is interpretable because TREM2 is also expressed on osteoclasts (multinucleated macrophages of bone). Subsequent literature (Keren-Shaul 2017 *Cell* — DAM signature; clinical trials of TREM2-agonist antibodies) has expanded mechanistic understanding.

## Conditions and scope

- Bi-allelic LOF: rare, autosomal recessive; combined CNS + bone phenotype.
- Heterozygous risk variants (R47H, R62H, T96K): common in populations; ~3-fold AD risk increase.
- Some TREM2 variants may be protective — mechanism unclear.
- Mostly European-ancestry GWAS; non-European-ancestry data are limited.

## Counter-evidence

- TREM2's role in late-stage AD is debated — some evidence suggests DAM activation is *protective* early but maladaptive late.
- Conditional TREM2 KO mouse models give mixed amyloid and cognitive results depending on background.
- The mechanistic link between TREM2 lipid-sensing and amyloid-β plaque biology remains incompletely mapped.
