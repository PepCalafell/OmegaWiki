## Statement

White adipose tissue-resident macrophages (WAT-TRMs) — distinct from CCR2-recruited monocyte-derived obesity-associated macrophages — sense dietary nutrients and produce the growth factor PDGF-CC in response to dietary fat intake. PDGF-CC acts paracrinely on white adipocytes via PDGFR-α to promote lipid storage. Genetic loss of WAT-TRMs or of PDGF-CC redirects unstored energy to brown adipose tissue (BAT) thermogenesis, producing a lean phenotype on high-fat diet. The mechanism is non-inflammatory and is conserved between mouse and *Drosophila*.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] cites Cox et al. 2021 *Science* (the primary paper, co-authored by review author Nehemiah Cox) as the central evidence. The mechanism is genetically validated in mouse (PDGF-CC KO, conditional WAT macrophage depletion) and conservation in *Drosophila* fat body strengthens the evolutionary case.

## Conditions and scope

- Mouse and *Drosophila* evidence.
- Distinguishes WAT-TRMs (PDGF-CC-producing, anti-inflammatory) from CCR2-recruited obesity-associated BMDMs (TNF-producing, pro-inflammatory) — different lineages, different functions.
- Human translation not directly demonstrated; the lipodystrophy / cachexia space could be informed by this mechanism.

## Counter-evidence

- *Trib1*-deficient mice (which lack subsets of fat tissue macrophages) show lipodystrophy — consistent with reduced PDGF-CC, but suggests other factors also contribute.
- Some reports of fat tissue macrophages *suppressing* browning and thermogenesis (Pirzgalska 2017) — opposite direction; resolution requires distinguishing macrophage subsets.

## Open questions

- Whether human WAT macrophages produce diet-regulated PDGF-CC.
- Pharmacological exploitability — can PDGF-CC blockade safely mimic exercise/cold-induced thermogenesis?
- Role of WAT-TRM PDGF-CC axis in MASH / NASH (ectopic liver lipid).
- Cross-talk between WAT-TRM PDGF-CC and CCR2-BMDM TNF programmes in obesity.
