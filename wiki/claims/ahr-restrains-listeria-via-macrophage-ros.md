## Statement

In primary Listeria monocytogenes infection, AHR-driven ROS production in peritoneal macrophages restricts bacterial growth and prevents macrophage cell death — explaining the higher mortality and bacterial burden in Ahr-KO mice. Intraperitoneal FICZ administration after high-dose infection drastically reduces mortality in WT mice.

## Evidence summary

Reported in [[papers/role-ahr-host-pathogen-interactions]] citing refs 76 and 89.

## Conditions and scope

Established for primary infection; outcomes vary with AHR ligand (TCDD via lactating-mouse drinking water impairs neonatal clearance, p.187), route (subcutaneous DMBA increases mortality), and timing.

## Counter-evidence

Memory-related Listeria experiments suggest more complex AHR functions in re-infection (see claim [[claims/cd8-memory-formation-requires-ahr-listeria]]).
