## Statement
PAK2 and PKCα kinases are nominated as important regulators of the signaling state of in vitro immunosuppressive (M2-polarized) human macrophages.

## Evidence summary
- "Analysis of direct and indirect evidence of kinase activities suggested PAK2 and PKCα kinases as important regulators of in vitro immunosuppressive macrophages" (abstract).
- PAK2 had two activation-segment phosphosites upregulated in M2a; PKCα activation-loop T497 phosphorylated higher in both M2 states; PKCα was the highest-centrality node in the M2a network (p.10-13).

## Conditions and scope
Activity is inferred from phosphoproteomic footprinting and network analysis; the kinases were not knocked down or inhibited. Restricted to in vitro polarized macrophages.

## Counter-evidence
None within the paper, but the authors note neither PAK2 nor PKCα had previously been defined as central immunosuppressive-macrophage regulators — i.e. independent validation is pending.

## Open questions
Does inhibition or knockdown of PAK2/PKCα causally revert immunosuppressive macrophage polarization?
