## Statement

PD-L1+/hi monocytes and TAMs in human breast cancer have higher surface IFN-γR1 and a stronger phospho-STAT1 response to IFN-γ stimulation than PD-L1−/lo cells — i.e., the PD-L1+ subset is *primed* for IFN-γ signaling rather than refractory. The relationship reverses in cancer cells, where PD-L1−/lo cells are more IFN-γ-responsive (and presumably will upregulate PD-L1 upon stimulation). The monocyte/macrophage axis is therefore mechanistically distinct from the IFN-γ → PD-L1 axis in tumor cells.

## Evidence summary

- Wang 2024 Fig. 6A-C (monocyte phosflow), 6D-E (IFN-γ dose-response), 6F-H (TAM in situ), 6I-J (mIF tumor tissue), 6K-M (cancer cell inverse pattern).
- Replication via mIF in independent tumor tissue (Fig. 6I).

## Conditions and scope

- IFN-γ stimulation in ex vivo / in vitro contexts.
- IFN-γR1 surface protein quantification by flow + mIF — not by single-cell transcriptomics.

## Counter-evidence

- None within Wang 2024; the dissociation between monocyte vs cancer cell IFN-γ responsiveness explains historical confusion about IFN-γ → PD-L1 as a universal axis.

## Linked ideas

- Supports the dissociation between IFN-γ-dependent (cancer cells) and IFN-γ-independent (monocytes/macrophages) PD-L1 induction routes.
- Functionally implies PD-L1+ TAMs remain responsive to inflammatory IFN-γ — they are not in a desensitized state.

## Open questions

- Why IFN-γR1 protein levels are elevated on PD-L1+ monocytes despite simultaneous IFN-γ-induced downregulation (regulatory feedback dynamics).
- Whether the PD-L1+ TAM IFN-γ priming contributes to immunotherapy response prediction.
