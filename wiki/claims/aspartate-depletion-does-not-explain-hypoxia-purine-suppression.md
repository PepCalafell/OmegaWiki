## Statement

Unlike cultured hypoxic cells where aspartate becomes limiting, aspartate is not depleted in hypoxic tumor homogenates in vivo, so aspartate limitation does not account for the observed purine depletion.

## Evidence summary

- **systemic-hypoxia-suppresses-solid-tumor-growth** (p.9): "in the hypoxic tumor homogenates, aspartate levels were not depleted, suggesting that aspartate deprivation does not explain the depletion of purine nucleotides" — Tumor homogenate aspartate measurement (Fig.S4a) contrasts prior in vitro work (refs 10,45). (type: mechanistic, strength: moderate)

## Conditions and scope

Preclinical mouse models (syngeneic, GEMM, and human xenograft) under continuous inhaled hypoxia (typically 8% O2) or pharmacological hypoxia; single preprint source (not yet peer-reviewed).

## Counter-evidence

None recorded in the wiki yet. Some cell lines (notably ccRCC) deviate from the dominant response — see [[claims/ccrcc-and-select-lines-resist-hypoxia-tumor-suppression]].
