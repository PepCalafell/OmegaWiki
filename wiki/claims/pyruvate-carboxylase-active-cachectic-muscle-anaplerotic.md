## Statement

The M+3 isotopologue signature of TCA intermediates in cachectic muscle, combined with [1-13C]-pyruvate tracing in C2C12, establishes that pyruvate carboxylase (PC) is active in cachectic muscle and contributes anaplerotic carbon to the elevated TCA cycle flux — alongside PDH-driven acetyl-CoA flux.

## Evidence summary

Quoted: "the significant increase in M + 3 labelling of these metabolites in muscles of Cax mice indicates that PC is active... [1-13C]-pyruvate tracing in C2C12 myotubes... showed M + 1 labelling of TCA intermediates, confirming that PC is active in muscle cells."

## Conditions and scope

In vivo + in vitro complementarity; in vitro [1-13C]-pyruvate validates the mechanistic inference of PC activity from in vivo M+3 patterns.

## Counter-evidence

Other anaplerotic routes (glutamine, propionyl-CoA) also contribute and produce M+3 labelling under some conditions; full attribution to PC requires the formal INCA flux modelling (next claim).
