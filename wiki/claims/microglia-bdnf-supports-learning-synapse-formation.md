## Statement

Microglia produce brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) as part of their trophic support to neurons. Microglial BDNF is specifically required for motor-learning-dependent synaptogenesis (Parkhurst 2013) and contributes to the development of neuropathic pain via shift in neuronal chloride gradient (Coull 2005). Conditional deletion of BDNF from microglia (using Cx3cr1-cre) impairs learning-induced synapse formation without abolishing baseline synaptic density.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] cites Parkhurst 2013 *Cell* and Coull 2005 *Nature* as the principal evidence. Parkhurst showed that conditional BDNF deletion in microglia impairs motor learning and the associated synaptic plasticity; Coull showed that microglial BDNF is required for the GABA/glycine reversal in spinal cord that produces neuropathic pain after peripheral nerve injury.

## Conditions and scope

- Murine conditional KO + behavioural evidence.
- Effects are activity- and context-dependent (motor learning task; nerve injury for pain model) rather than baseline.
- BDNF is also produced by neurons; microglial BDNF is one of several sources.

## Counter-evidence

- Whether BDNF is the *primary* microglia-derived neurotrophin (vs NGF, neurotropin-3, IGF1, TGFβ) varies by context.
- Some studies have not reproduced motor-learning deficits in conditional microglial BDNF KO; reagent-specific or genetic-background differences are debated.
- Microglial BDNF role in adult cognition (vs developmental synapse formation) is less established.

## Open questions

- Whether boosting microglial BDNF therapeutically can enhance motor learning or rescue neurodegenerative deficits.
- The role of disease-associated microglia (DAM) state in modulating BDNF output during Alzheimer's.
- Whether neuropathic-pain-driving BDNF can be selectively blocked at microglia without disturbing learning-dependent BDNF.
