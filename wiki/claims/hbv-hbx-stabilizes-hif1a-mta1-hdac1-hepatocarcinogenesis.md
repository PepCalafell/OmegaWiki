## Statement

HBV X protein (HBx) stabilizes HIF-1α through multiple mechanisms: (i) MAPK pathway activation increases HIF-1α transcription; (ii) HBx induction of the MTA1/HDAC1 chromatin-remodeling axis enhances HIF-1α protein levels in HCC cells; (iii) HBx-driven NF-κB activation increases HIF-2α. The stabilized HIF-α subunits reciprocally activate the HBV basal core promoter, sustaining viral replication, and provide a hypoxic reservoir for HBV cccDNA that resists A3B-mediated immune clearance. HCV core protein similarly stabilizes HIF-1α (even under normoxia) and elevates VEGF, supporting HCV-induced hepatocellular carcinoma angiogenesis.

## Evidence summary

Multiple primary studies (Yoo et al., Wing et al., Hallez et al., Ripoli et al., Zhu et al.) integrated by Luo et al. 2022.

## Conditions and scope

- HBV-infected hepatocytes and HCC cell lines
- HCV core protein in Huh7.5.1 and HCV-infected primary hepatocytes
- Mechanism likely operative in chronic hepatitis B/C patients prior to HCC progression

## Counter-evidence

DNase I (cellular HBV restriction factor) is induced by HIF-1α — HIF-1α has anti-viral collateral effects, complicating therapeutic targeting.
