## Statement

The pro-tumorigenic role of tissue-resident macrophages generalises beyond NSCLC: in B16-OVA melanoma (intravenously seeded in the lung), TRM depletion (CD169-DTR + DT) reduces tumour burden, expands IFNγ⁺TNF⁺ effector CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells, reduces PD-1 MFI on T cells, reduces Tregs, and raises the CD8⁺/Treg ratio. Tumour reduction is not associated with increased OVA-antigen-specific OT-I or OT-II T cell expansion, indicating non-antigen-specific T-cell-tolerance effects.

## Evidence summary

- B16-OVA tumour burden reduced (P=0.0003)
- CD4⁺ T cell frequency ↑ (P<0.0001)
- PD-1 MFI on CD4⁺/CD8⁺ T cells ↓ (P<0.0001)
- IFNγ⁺TNF⁺ effector T cells expanded (CD4⁺ P=0.001, CD8⁺ P<0.0001)
- Tregs ↓ (P=0.0068); CD8⁺/Treg ratio ↑ (P=0.025)
- OVA-specific OT-I/OT-II not increased

## Conditions and scope

- Syngeneic mouse B16-BFP-F10/OVA model in lung (intravenous tail-vein)
- 2-week endpoint
- n=5-7 mice per group, two independent experiments

## Counter-evidence

- The mechanism in B16-OVA may differ in detail from NSCLC (different tumour cell biology, no EMT-driven invasiveness shown)
- OVA-antigen-specific response not amplified, which is unusual for an effective anti-tumour T cell response — points to antigen-non-specific T-cell-tolerance loss
- Limited n; large effect size warrants replication
