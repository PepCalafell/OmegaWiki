## Statement

Hypoxia signaling has context-dependent dual effects on apoptotic cell death. (i) Pro-survival: HIF-1α-induced BNIP3 promotes mitophagy that clears damaged mitochondria, suppresses ROS, and protects cells (e.g., in acute renal injury and hepatoma). (ii) Pro-apoptotic: HIF-1α also stabilizes the p53 tumor suppressor in a dephosphorylated state, driving p53-dependent apoptosis; Nix and BNIP3 themselves can permeabilize mitochondria when overexpressed, triggering apoptosis. Hypoxia-driven mitochondrial ROS additionally fuels apoptosis independently of HIFs.

## Evidence summary

Multiple primary studies cited in Luo et al. 2022. The bidirectionality reflects threshold effects (mild hypoxia engages survival programs; severe/prolonged hypoxia tips toward apoptosis).

## Conditions and scope

- Acute renal injury: HIF-1α/BNIP3 protective
- Embryonic stem cells, severe ischemia: HIF-1α loss → ROS-driven apoptosis
- Tumor cells: p53-WT contexts engage HIF-1α/p53/PUMA-BAX apoptosis

## Counter-evidence

In p53-mutant cancers, HIF-1α loses its pro-apoptotic arm and the BNIP3 survival arm dominates.
