## Statement

The number of discovered human protein-coding genes grew sharply during the HGP and then plateaued in the mid-2000s at about 20,000 — far below the ~100,000 figure many in the community had previously assumed.

## Evidence summary

Quoted: "It levelled out suddenly in the mid-2000s at about 20,000 protein-coding genes … far short of the 100,000-strong estimate previously adopted by many in the scientific community." (p.1)

## Conditions and scope

Applies to annotated human protein-coding genes; non-coding elements counted separately.

## Counter-evidence

None in source; gene-count remains mildly contested due to definition/boundary ambiguity.
