## Statement

PTEN loss facilitates HIF1-mediated gene expression and deregulates AKT, supporting clone expansion. In prostate and other cancers, hypoxic polyclonality associates with PTEN copy-number/expression loss and predicts poor prognosis.

## Evidence summary

Reviewed in [[papers/tumour-hypoxia-driving-genomic-instability-tumour]] (Suvac, Ashton & Bristow, *Nature Reviews Cancer* 2025).

## Conditions and scope

Localized prostate cancer; head-and-neck. Correlative with mechanistic support.

## Counter-evidence

None substantive in the reviewed literature, though see Limitations of source paper for cell-line/tissue dependence.
