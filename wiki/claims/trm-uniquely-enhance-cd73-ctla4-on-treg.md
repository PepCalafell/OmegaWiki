## Statement

Both tissue-resident macrophages (TRMs) and monocyte-derived macrophages (MDMs) can drive naive CD4⁺ T cells to differentiate into FOXP3⁺ regulatory T cells (Tregs) at comparable efficiency. However, only TRMs uniquely enhance the suppressive programme of those Tregs by upregulating the ectoenzyme CD73 (NT5E) and the co-inhibitory receptor CTLA-4. In vivo TRM depletion (CD169-DTR + DT) reduces both CD73 and CTLA-4 on remaining tumour Tregs, consistent with loss of TRM-driven licensing.

## Evidence summary

- Ex vivo Treg differentiation: TRMs vs MDMs co-cultured with naive CD4⁺ T cells
- Equal Treg-induction efficiency (FOXP3⁺ generation)
- Only TRMs upregulate CD73 and CTLA-4 on differentiated Tregs
- In vivo: TRM-depleted (CD169-DTR + DT) mice show reduced CD73/CTLA-4 on tumour Tregs

## Conditions and scope

- Mouse-derived sorted TRMs vs MDMs
- Naive CD4⁺ T cells from spleen / lymph nodes
- In vivo confirmation in KP NSCLC model

## Counter-evidence

- Mechanism (which TRM-secreted factor licenses CD73/CTLA-4) not identified
- Cross-species validation in human alveolar macrophage + Treg co-cultures untested
- Whether the same lineage selectivity holds in non-tumour contexts (e.g., lung tolerance) is not addressed here
