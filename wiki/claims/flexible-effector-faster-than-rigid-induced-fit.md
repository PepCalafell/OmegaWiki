## Statement

A flexible, intrinsically disordered peptide effector drives faster facilitated dissociation than a rigid, structured effector that makes nearly identical interface contacts — even though the rigid effector binds more tightly — because the flexible effector binds the closed state and folds upon binding to drive the conformational change (induced fit / power stroke).

## Evidence summary

The peptide and 3hb make nearly identical interactions with AS1, but unbound peptide is disordered (CD) whereas 3hb is structured. Apparent peptide on-rate increases linearly with [E] (induced-fit, binds state X); 3hb on-rate saturates hyperbolically at kswitch (conformational selection, binds only state Y). "a rigid effector provides reduced rate acceleration even though it binds more tightly" (p.6).

## Conditions and scope

Single designed system (AS1) with two matched-interface effectors.

## Counter-evidence

Demonstrated for one switch system; generality across designs not exhaustively tested.
