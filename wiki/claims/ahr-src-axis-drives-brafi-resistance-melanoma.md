## Statement

In the cytoplasm, AHR binds SRC and triggers SRC autophosphorylation and downstream EGFR/FAK activation — a non-genomic AHR function. In BRAF-mutant melanoma, sustained AHR-SRC signalling enables tumour escape from BRAFi (vemurafenib) and is a therapeutic vulnerability addressed by combined BRAF + AHR/SRC blockade. The same logic extends to prostate cancer, where dual AHR + SRC inhibition synergistically suppresses androgen receptor signalling.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/complex-biology-aryl-hydrocarbon-receptor-activation]] reviews Paris et al. 2022 EMBO Mol Med (DOI 10.15252/emmm.202215677) and Ghotbaddini et al. 2017 PLoS One.

## Conditions and scope

BRAF V600E-mutant melanoma context; prostate cancer cell lines. Need clinical validation.

## Counter-evidence

— none reviewed.

## Linked ideas

- Connects [[claims/vemurafenib-ahr-secondary-pocket-blocks-arnt]] mechanistically: vemurafenib itself can bind AHR, potentially modulating the very axis that drives its resistance.
- Suggests pocket-selective AHR antagonists that preserve canonical signalling but block SRC binding.

## Open questions

- Does the AHR-SRC axis explain primary BRAFi resistance, acquired BRAFi resistance, or both?
- Is dual BRAFi + AHR antagonist clinically tractable?
