## Statement

PD-L1−/lo macrophages — not PD-L1+/hi macrophages — suppress antigen-specific CD8+ T cell cytotoxicity in a controlled BiTE-mediated killing assay. This functionally inverts the prior assumption that PD-L1+ TAMs are the immunosuppressive subset. PD-L1 blocking antibody does not rescue PD-L1−/lo suppression nor affect PD-L1+/hi non-suppression, showing that the suppressive function of PD-L1− TAMs is mediated by other pathways (e.g., SPP1, VEGFA, ECM-receptor interactions identified in cell-cell communication analyses).

## Evidence summary

- Wang 2024 Fig. 7E (schematic), 7F (PD-1/CD137 induction), 7G-H (cytotoxicity quantification).

## Conditions and scope

- Defined antigen system (CD19+ K562 + bispecific CD3/CD19 antibody) — eliminates antigen-specificity confounder.
- Autologous monocyte system; n=6 patients.

## Counter-evidence

- Murine models with PD-L1 KO in TAM lineage (refs 5–7) report enhanced anti-tumor immunity, consistent with TAM PD-L1 being suppressive — Wang 2024 interprets this divergence as a species/model-specific phenomenon or as PD-L1 protecting suppressive PD-L1+ subsets in those models.

## Open questions

- Specific identification of the suppressive mediator(s) released by PD-L1− TAMs (SPP1 candidate).
- Whether PD-L1− TAM suppression is reversed by SPP1 / VEGFA blockade.
- Whether the suppression generalizes to tumor-antigen-specific (rather than BiTE-mediated) cytotoxicity.
