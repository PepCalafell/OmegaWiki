## Statement

AHRR hypomethylation — most prominently at CpG cg05575921 — is a reproducible epigenetic mark of cigarette smoke exposure, persisting after cessation and detectable in cord blood as a marker of maternal smoking. It correlates with reduced lung function, increased lung cancer risk, and smoking-related mortality. Paradoxically, AHRR is hypermethylated and transcriptionally silenced in many non-lung malignancies, where it likely serves as a tumour suppressor.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/complex-biology-aryl-hydrocarbon-receptor-activation]] reviews the smoking-related and cancer-related AHRR methylation literature.

## Conditions and scope

Methylation patterns are tissue- and cell-type-specific; the lung-cancer association is strongest with blood-based AHRR methylation signal. The tumour-suppressive role inferred from AHRR hypermethylation in other cancers is correlative.

## Counter-evidence

Smoking-related AHRR hypomethylation is opposite-direction from CYP1A1 hypermethylation under prenatal smoke exposure — the two AHR-axis loci respond divergently to the same exposure, suggesting distinct upstream mechanisms.
