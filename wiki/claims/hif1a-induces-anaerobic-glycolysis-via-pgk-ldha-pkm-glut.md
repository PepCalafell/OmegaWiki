## Statement

HIF-1α is the master transcription factor coupling hypoxia to glycolytic reprogramming. It binds HREs in the promoters of GLUT1/3 (glucose import), PGK1 (glycolysis), PKM (pyruvate generation), LDHA (pyruvate→lactate), and ancillary enzymes — collectively shifting cellular ATP production from oxidative phosphorylation toward anaerobic glycolysis. This program is required for tumor cell adaptation to hypoxic niches and underpins the Warburg phenotype.

## Evidence summary

Decades of HRE mapping and ChIP studies; pharmacological HIF-1α inhibition reduces glycolytic flux. Reviewed exhaustively in Luo et al. 2022.

## Conditions and scope

- Universal hypoxia response across cell types
- Co-regulated with PDK1-mediated PDH inhibition (TCA suppression)
- Synergistic with c-MYC in proliferating cells; HIF-1α represses MYC via MXI1 in quiescent contexts

## Counter-evidence

In normoxic non-cancer cells, basal HIF-1α is degraded — glycolytic baseline is HIF-independent.
