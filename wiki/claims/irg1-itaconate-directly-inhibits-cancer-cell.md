## Statement

Beyond macrophage-mediated effects, itaconate (endogenously via IRG1 overexpression or exogenously) directly and cell-autonomously suppresses lung cancer cell proliferation, challenging the view that itaconate acts on tumors only through macrophage paracrine immunomodulation.

## Evidence summary

- **irg1-itaconate-rewires-macrophage-lung-tumor** (p.1464, 1472) — mechanistic/pharmacological evidence.
  - Quote: "IRG1 overexpression reduced proliferation and cell numbers without affecting apoptosis in A549 and KEAP1-deficient H1650 cells."
  - Discussion: "itaconate and its derivative Octyl Ita exert direct anti-proliferative effects on cancer cells across in vitro, in vivo, and ex vivo lung cancer models."

## Conditions and scope

Epithelial cancer cells lack endogenous IRG1; exogenous itaconate is less potent than Octyl Ita (entry-limited). Complements paracrine model in [[claims/macrophage-secreted-itaconate-exported-abcg2-mediates]].
