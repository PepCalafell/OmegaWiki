## Statement

Gain-of-function IDH1/IDH2 mutations produce the oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate (2HG), which inhibits α-KG-dependent dioxygenases, driving DNA hypermethylation including silencing of the cytosolic dsDNA sensor cGAS. Result: T-cell exclusion from the TME. mIDH inhibitors (ivosidenib AG-120, enasidenib, brain-penetrant vorasidenib) restore α-KG biology, demethylate cGAS, induce T-cell infiltration and have received FDA approval for IDH-mutant glioma (vorasidenib), ICC and AML (ivosidenib), and AML (enasidenib).

## Evidence summary

- Mechanistic studies on 2HG-driven cGAS silencing.
- INDIGO phase 3 vorasidenib trial (NCT04164901) in residual/recurrent IDH-mutant glioma.
- Ivosidenib (AG-120) and enasidenib approvals.

## Conditions and scope

- mIDH-positive tumours (10–20% AML, 60–90% low-grade glioma, 10–30% ICC, 50–70% chondrosarcoma).

## Counter-evidence

- IDH inhibitors face IDH-isoform switching as a resistance mechanism.

## Linked ideas

- mIDH inhibitor + ICB to leverage T-cell re-infiltration.

## Open questions

- Quantitative threshold of 2HG that triggers immune exclusion.
