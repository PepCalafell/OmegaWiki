## Statement

Adult mouse osteoclasts are chimeric multinucleated cells. The original osteoclast pool is established embryonically from yolk-sac EMP-derived precursors and self-maintains by fusion. As the animal ages, HSC-derived monocytes that differentiate into osteoclast precursors integrate into existing osteoclast syncytia, rather than forming entirely new osteoclasts. The result: a single adult osteoclast contains nuclei from both EMP and HSC lineages.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] cites Jacome-Galarza 2019 *Nature*, which used genetic lineage-tracing (Csf1r-CreER, Cx3cr1-CreER) to track EMP and HSC contributions to adult osteoclasts and demonstrated chimerism. The fusion mechanism is consistent with osteoclast biology — multinucleation is achieved by precursor fusion driven by RANKL signalling.

## Conditions and scope

- Mouse lineage-tracing evidence; human direct evidence is limited.
- Distinct from "complete replacement" model — the original embryonic syncytium is preserved and merely accretes new nuclei.
- Implications for BMT therapy in osteopetrosis: HSC-BMT can restore some osteoclast function by contributing nuclei to existing chimeric syncytia.

## Counter-evidence

- The relative contributions of EMP-derived vs HSC-derived nuclei in different bones and ages may vary.
- Some HSC-derived osteoclasts may form de novo without integrating into pre-existing syncytia.

## Open questions

- Whether engineered EMP-derived osteoclast progenitors can rescue osteopetrosis better than HSC-BMT in genetic disease models.
- How the chimeric architecture is established — does fusion preferentially target existing syncytia or compete with de novo osteoclast formation?
- Whether human osteoclasts share the chimeric architecture (likely yes, but direct evidence is limited).
