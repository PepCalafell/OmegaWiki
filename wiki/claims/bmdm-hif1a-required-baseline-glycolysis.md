## Statement

HIF-1α is constitutively required in BMDMs for basal glycolytic flux and for resistance to mitochondrial ETC inhibition. Its loss collapses the glycolytic capacity that BMDMs normally maintain even under normoxia.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/hif-regulates-mitochondrial-function-bone-marrow]] Fig. 2F-J. Distinguished from siRNA knockdown of HIF-1α, which does not alter ECAR in the short term — long-term genetic loss is required to manifest the glycolytic deficit.

## Conditions and scope

In vitro BMDMs, genetic deletion model. Does not test recruited-monocyte-derived macrophages in vivo.

## Counter-evidence

Short-term siRNA HIF-1α knockdown in BMDMs (Suppl. Fig. 7A-C) does not reproduce the glycolytic deficit, suggesting the phenotype requires durable loss with time to rewire.
