## Statement

Across US counties, higher elevation is significantly negatively correlated with age-adjusted pan-cancer mortality rate, robust to adjustment for median income and obesity/diabetes prevalence, offering orthogonal human-population evidence.

## Evidence summary

- **systemic-hypoxia-suppresses-solid-tumor-growth** (p.13): "Higher altitude was significantly negatively correlated with AAMR ... the negative correlation between elevation and AAMR remained statistically significant" — ETOPO 2022 elevation vs CDC AAMR maps; multivariate regression (Fig.S6a-c). Ecological/correlational, confounding possible. (type: correlational, strength: weak)

## Conditions and scope

Preclinical mouse models (syngeneic, GEMM, and human xenograft) under continuous inhaled hypoxia (typically 8% O2) or pharmacological hypoxia; single preprint source (not yet peer-reviewed).

## Counter-evidence

None recorded in the wiki yet. Some cell lines (notably ccRCC) deviate from the dominant response — see [[claims/ccrcc-and-select-lines-resist-hypoxia-tumor-suppression]].
