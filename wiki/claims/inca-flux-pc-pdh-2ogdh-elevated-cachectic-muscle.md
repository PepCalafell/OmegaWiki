## Statement

Formal 13C metabolic-flux analysis (INCA) of cachectic GC muscle reveals simultaneously elevated PC, PDH and 2-OGDH fluxes plus elevated glutamine anaplerosis, with reduced fatty-acid / ketogenic-AA contribution to acetyl-CoA — a coherent flux signature of carbohydrate + glutamine-driven TCA hypermetabolism.

## Evidence summary

Quoted: "Relative flux through PC and PDH was increased in GC muscle (V9 and V10), indicating that more glucose enters the TCA cycle. By contrast, the relative flux filling the acetyl-CoA pool from fatty acid oxidation and ketogenic amino acids (V11) was decreased. We observed a significantly increased flux through 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase (V18)... significantly increased usage of glutamine (including also glucogenic amino acid backbones) as a substrate for the TCA cycle (V16 and V17)."

## Conditions and scope

Citrate synthase normalisation hides absolute-flux changes; CS is itself elevated in Cax muscle.

## Counter-evidence

INCA's assumed network topology and steady-state assumption may not perfectly match short-time-window in vivo tracing.
