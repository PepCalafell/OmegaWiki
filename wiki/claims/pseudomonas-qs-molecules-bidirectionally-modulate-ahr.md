## Statement

Distinct P. aeruginosa quorum-sensing molecules engage AHR with opposite effects: HHQ and 3-oxo-C12-l-HSL (early-growth phase) inhibit AHR while pyocyanin (PYO) and 1-hydroxyphenazine (late-growth phase) activate it. The QS antagonists can also suppress AHR activation by the agonists in a dose-dependent fashion, creating an integrated bidirectional AHR-QS signalling node.

## Evidence summary

Reviewed in [[papers/role-ahr-host-pathogen-interactions]] (Table 1; Figs. 3–4; refs 9–10). Direct ligand-binding evidence for PYO and 3-oxo-C12-l-HSL; competitive binding for HHQ and 1-hydroxyphenazine.

## Conditions and scope

Established in mouse and zebrafish infection models and in C. elegans. AHR is involved in PYO/phthiocol metabolism (epithelial degradation) but not 3-oxo-C12-l-HSL/HHQ degradation (p.182).

## Counter-evidence

None within the review's scope; broader QS biology (RhlR, LasR pathways) involves additional non-AHR receptors.
