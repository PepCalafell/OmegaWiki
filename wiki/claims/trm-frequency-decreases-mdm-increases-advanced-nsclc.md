## Statement

In advanced NSCLC tumours (day 30 in mouse models; clinical specimens in humans), the proportion of tissue-resident macrophages (group I) is markedly reduced compared to non-involved lung, while monocyte-derived macrophages (group II) increase and dominate the tumour macrophage compartment.

## Evidence summary

- scRNA-seq frequency analysis of monocyte/macrophage clusters in naive vs tumour-bearing mouse lungs (n=4 experiments, two pooled mice per experiment)
- Human NSCLC scRNA-seq across 19 paired non-involved/tumour samples confirms the same shift
- Genetically engineered SPC-Cre KP mouse model independently shows the same TRM redistribution
- Spatial imaging confirms TRM reduction within tumour parenchyma

## Conditions and scope

- Mouse KP orthotopic and genetic NSCLC; human paired NSCLC scRNA-seq
- Pertains to "advanced" tumours (day 30 in mouse, clinical specimens in human)
- Earlier time points (day 5-15) show TRMs still present and intratumoural

## Counter-evidence

- Some patient-level variability in macrophage compartment composition not fully explored
- Bulk deconvolution methods may underestimate TRM frequency in human samples
