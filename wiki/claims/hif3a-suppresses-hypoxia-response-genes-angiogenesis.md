## Statement

Unlike HIF-1α and HIF-2α, HIF-3α functions predominantly as a negative regulator of the hypoxia response. Its overexpression reduces transcription of hypoxia-responsive genes, blunts angiogenesis, and restrains cell proliferation. HIF-3α tissue distribution is restricted (vs. ubiquitous HIF-1α) and several splice variants act as dominant-negative competitors for ARNT/HIF-1β dimerization.

## Evidence summary

Cited in Luo et al. 2022 review of hypoxia signaling. The claim is grounded in prior literature on HIF-3α splice variants (e.g., IPAS) that lack a transactivation domain and titrate HIF-1β away from HIF-1α/2α.

## Conditions and scope

Variant-dependent. Some HIF-3α isoforms may have non-negative regulatory functions in specific contexts. Tissue expression is far narrower than HIF-1α.

## Counter-evidence

Some studies report HIF-3α-positive transcriptional activity in adipose tissue and chondrocytes — biology is splice-variant dependent.
