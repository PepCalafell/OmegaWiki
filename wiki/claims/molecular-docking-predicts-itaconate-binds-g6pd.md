## Statement

Molecular docking predicts that itaconate and Octyl itaconate occupy the G6PD active-site pocket, with Octyl itaconate showing modestly higher predicted binding affinity than itaconate.

## Evidence summary

- **irg1-itaconate-rewires-macrophage-lung-tumor** (p.1467) — quantitative (in silico) evidence.
  - Quote: "Molecular docking predicted that both Octyl Ita and itaconate bind to G6PD, with Octyl Ita showing a slightly higher affinity (−5.9 vs. −5.2 kcal/mol), which is consistent with greater enzymatic inhibition."

## Conditions and scope

Computational prediction; corroborated functionally by kinetic assays ([[claims/itaconate-non-covalently-inhibits-g6pd-reducing]]) but no experimental structure.

## Counter-evidence

Docking energies are model-dependent; confidence limited pending structural validation. Single-source claim from [[papers/irg1-itaconate-rewires-macrophage-lung-tumor]].
