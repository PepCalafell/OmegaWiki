## Statement

Macrophages — both TRMs and BMDMs — express five canonical PRR families that together cover the major pathogen-associated molecular pattern (PAMP) and damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) surfaces:

1. **TLRs** — Toll-like receptors (membrane-bound; bacterial/viral ligands)
2. **NLRs** — NOD-like receptors (cytosolic; intracellular bacteria, inflammasome triggers)
3. **RLRs** — RIG-I-like receptors (cytosolic RNA viruses)
4. **ALRs** — AIM2-like receptors (cytosolic DNA / inflammasome)
5. **CLRs** — C-type lectin receptors (carbohydrate ligands, fungi)

PRR engagement triggers production of TNF, IL-1, IL-6, type-I interferons and downstream phagocytic / antigen-presentation programmes.

## Evidence summary

The review [[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] consolidates the canonical PRR taxonomy from decades of innate-immunity literature (Janeway, Medzhitov, Akira, Hornung). The five-family classification is universal across the field.

## Conditions and scope

- Applies to all macrophage subsets, with tissue-specific variation in *which* family members dominate (e.g. microglia express TLR-rich profiles; alveolar macrophages emphasize CLRs).
- Macrophage PRR signalling intersects with MyD88/TRIF, RIP2, MAVS, ASC adaptor cascades.

## Counter-evidence

- Some authors include cyclic-GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS) as a sixth canonical sensor; the review uses the historical five-family taxonomy.
- Tissue-specific suppression of PRR responses (e.g. anti-inflammatory tone of intestinal macrophages, microglial restraint of TLR4 responses) qualifies the universal expression with functional state.

## Open questions

- Whether tissue-resident macrophage PRR profiles diverge systematically from BMDM profiles enough to drive different inflammatory thresholds.
- Whether the five-family taxonomy is sufficient or should be expanded to include cGAS-STING and emerging cytosolic DNA sensors as a sixth PRR class.
