## Statement

In macrophage–T-cell co-cultures, pharmacological inhibition of OGT increases the expression of co-stimulatory molecules on macrophages and results in increased T cell proliferation — suggesting O-GlcNAcylation restrains macrophage T-cell-stimulatory capacity.

## Evidence summary

Reported in [[papers/glcnacylation-shapes-macrophage-tissue-residency-alternative]] (commentary on Heieis et al.). Pharmacological complement to the genetic Ogt-deletion findings, implicating [[foundations/ogt-glcnac-transferase]] in tuning macrophage–T-cell crosstalk.

## Conditions and scope

In vitro co-culture; pharmacological (inhibitor not named in the commentary). Commentary-level confidence; off-target effects of OGT inhibitors not controlled in the secondary description.
