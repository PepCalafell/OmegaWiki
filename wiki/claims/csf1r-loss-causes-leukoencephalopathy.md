## Statement

Loss of CSF1R signalling in humans causes a phenotypic spectrum dependent on residual receptor activity. Bi-allelic loss-of-function (homozygous null) causes paediatric-onset leukoencephalopathy with a near-complete congenital absence of microglia and infant lethality. Hypomorphic or heterozygous CSF1R variants cause adult-onset leukoencephalopathy with axonal spheroids and pigmented glia (ALSP), a progressive neurodegenerative disorder. CSF1R bi-allelic LOF can additionally produce skeletal phenotypes consistent with osteopetrosis-like bone defects (because osteoclasts also require CSF1R).

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] cites Oosterhof 2019 *Am J Hum Genet* (paediatric-onset bi-allelic), Guo 2019 *Am J Hum Genet* (skeletal dysplasia + brain malformation), Rademakers 2012 *Nat Genet* (ALSP) and Nicholson 2013 *Neurology* (ALSP-POLD-HDLS unification). The phenotypic gradient maps cleanly to residual CSF1R kinase activity.

## Conditions and scope

- Rare Mendelian disorders.
- Genotype-phenotype: bi-allelic null = pediatric / lethal; hypomorph = adult-onset progressive; heterozygous LOF = ALSP-like.
- Mouse models recapitulate the gradient (Csf1r-KO has osteopetrosis + microglia absence; partial-function variants survive but with neurological defects).

## Counter-evidence

- Some patients with bi-allelic CSF1R variants survive longer than expected — residual activity from non-canonical splicing or alternative CSF1R-related pathways may rescue partially.
- The boundary between "ALSP" and "POLD" (pigmented orthochromatic leukodystrophy) clinical entities was unclear before the Nicholson 2013 unification.

## Open questions

- Whether HSC transplantation or microglia-replacement therapy can rescue bi-allelic CSF1R-null infants — early case reports suggest possible benefit but data are limited.
- The role of TRMs *outside* the CNS in CSF1R-LOF skeletal and visceral phenotypes — is the systemic phenotype microglia-driven or pan-TRM-driven?
- CSF1R-targeted small-molecule activators / agonists for ALSP — none clinically available.
