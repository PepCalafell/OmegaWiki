## Statement

The mouse-model paradigm in which white-adipose-tissue (WAT) browning (UCP1 induction) drives cancer-associated cachexia REE elevation does NOT translate clearly to humans. Petruzzelli et al. reported UCP1 in WAT of 7/8 CAC patients, but a retrospective cohort of 14,134 cancer patients showed brown-adipose-tissue (BAT) prevalence LOWER (not higher) in CAC at diagnosis with no association with survival; PDAC visceral adipose tissue shows no UCP1 differential. The browning-as-driver hypothesis is therefore challenged in human CAC.

## Evidence summary

Large-cohort BAT-prevalence imaging data + small-cohort PDAC VAT UCP1 measurements contradict the Petruzzelli paradigm.

## Conditions and scope

Imaging-based BAT detection has limitations; UCP1 protein measurement in WAT is technically challenging. The hypothesis is challenged but not definitively refuted.

## Counter-evidence

Petruzzelli et al. (CAC patients with WAT UCP1+); PTHrP-induced UCP1 in adipocyte cultures (mechanistic support).
