## Statement

In NSCLC patients, detectable circulating PTHrP (by immunoassay) correlates with lower lean body mass and higher resting energy expenditure. PTHrP induces UCP1 expression in primary brown and white adipocyte cultures, providing a candidate mechanism for tumour-driven host hypermetabolism via WAT browning.

## Evidence summary

Single NSCLC cohort; in-vitro adipocyte experiments.

## Conditions and scope

Restricted to PTHrP-detectable subset of NSCLC patients; broader WAT-browning evidence is conflicting in humans.

## Counter-evidence

A 14,134-patient cohort shows BAT prevalence LOWER in CAC (not consistent with strong tumour-driven browning); PDAC VAT shows no UCP1 differential.
