## Statement

In the tumour niche, hypoxia-stabilized HIF drives VEGF-dependent (leaky, pro-metastatic) angiogenesis and the OXPHOS→glycolysis Warburg switch, and dysregulates infiltrating immune cells — enhancing MDSC T cell suppression (HIF1-dependent arginase/iNOS) while also enabling CD8⁺ T cell tumour killing (via S-2-hydroxyglutarate).

## Evidence summary

Reviewed in [[papers/regulation-immunity-inflammation-hypoxia-immunological-niches]] (cites Corzo et al. 2010; Tyrakis et al. *Nature* 2016). Links to [[foundations/vegf]], concepts [[concepts/warburg-effect-hif1a-glycolytic-reprogramming]], [[concepts/tumour-immune-microenvironment]].

## Conditions and scope

Immune effects are bidirectional (suppressive via MDSCs; pro-cytotoxic via CD8 metabolite signalling), illustrating context dependence within the tumour niche.

## Counter-evidence

None within scope; the bidirectionality is itself noted.
