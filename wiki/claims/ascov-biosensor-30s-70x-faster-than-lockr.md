## Statement

A single-component biosensor based on facilitated dissociation (AScov), built by caging the SmBiT peptide within an effector and using a SARS-CoV-2 RBD binder, produces a 30-fold luciferase increase with a 30 s half-time on target addition — about 70 times faster than a previously designed LOCKR-based sensor that relies on slow conformational selection.

## Evidence summary

"the best sensor shows a 30-fold increase in luciferase activity, with a half-time of 30 s ... 70 times faster than a previously designed LOCKR-based SARS-CoV-2 sensor that relies on conformational selection" (p.6). Response time is limited by target association rather than internal conformational switching.

## Conditions and scope

16 AScov designs; in vitro NanoBiT luminescence.

## Counter-evidence

Speed-up is relative to one prior LOCKR sensor; absolute response is target-association-limited.
