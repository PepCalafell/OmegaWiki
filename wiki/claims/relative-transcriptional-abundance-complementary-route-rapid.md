## Statement
Beyond realizing pre-established epigenetic potential, macrophages can achieve rapid immune gene upregulation by establishing "relative transcriptional abundance" — transcription levels exceeding those typically seen for genes of comparable promoter chromatin accessibility.

## Evidence summary
Listeria triggered transcription beyond chromatin-accessibility-predicted levels for several immune genes; temporal clustering showed transitions between epigenetic potential and relative transcriptional abundance ([[papers/integrated-time-series-analysis-high-content]], Figure 3B-D; see [[concepts/relative-transcriptional-abundance]]).

## Conditions and scope
Murine BMDMs in vitro; concept defined relative to a population of genes with similar promoter accessibility.

## Counter-evidence
None recorded.

## Open questions
What post-transcriptional/elongation mechanisms (e.g., mRNA stability, Pol II elongation) drive relative transcriptional abundance?
