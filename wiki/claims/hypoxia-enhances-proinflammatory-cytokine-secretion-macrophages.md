## Statement

LPS-activated MACs differentiated under hypoxia (1% O₂) secrete higher levels of proinflammatory cytokines IL-6 and TNF-α and lower levels of anti-inflammatory IL-10 than their normoxic counterparts. They also express higher MHC-class-II HLA-DR, CD86, CD80 and lower CD14, CD206, CD163, consistent with enhanced antigen presentation. Functionally, hypoxic MACs lose their canonical capacity to suppress CD8⁺ T-cell proliferation in coculture.

## Evidence summary

- ELISA on culture supernatants (n=4): mMAC1 > mMAC21 for IL-6 and TNF-α; mMAC1 < mMAC21 for IL-10 (Fig. 1B).
- Flow cytometry MFI (n=4): mMAC1 > mMAC21 for HLA-DR, CD86, CD80; mMAC1 < mMAC21 for CD14, CD206, CD163 (Fig. 1C).
- CD8⁺ T-cell proliferation coculture (allogeneic, n=4): both iMAC1 and mMAC1 less suppressive than their normoxic counterparts (Fig. 1D, Fig. S1A).

## Conditions and scope

- Human M-CSF MACs from peripheral blood monocytes.
- LPS at 48 h activation.
- Allogeneic T-cell coculture proliferation assay.

## Counter-evidence

- Some prior literature reports hypoxia as broadly immunosuppressive in MACs (refs 15, 16, 29, 30 in the paper) — context-dependent contradiction.
- iMAC1 (resting hypoxic) shows partial down-regulation of p65-bound genes, hinting at a more nuanced phenotype than uniformly proinflammatory.
