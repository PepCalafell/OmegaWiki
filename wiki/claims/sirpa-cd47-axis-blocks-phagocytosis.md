## Statement

The SIRPα–CD47 axis is the principal macrophage 'don't-eat-me' signalling pathway. Healthy cells express CD47 on their surface; CD47 binds SIRPα (an ITIM-bearing receptor on macrophages and other phagocytes), which recruits SHP-1/SHP-2 phosphatases and inhibits the phagocytic synapse. Tumour cells exploit this pathway by overexpressing CD47, evading macrophage destruction. Therapeutic blockade (anti-CD47 antibodies, SIRPα-Fc fusions, anti-SIRPα antibodies) releases this brake and is in clinical trials across multiple cancer types.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] cites refs 193-194 for the SIRPα-CD47 axis as a cancer-immunotherapy target. The mechanism is well-established preclinically; clinical translation has produced mixed results — magrolimab (anti-CD47) showed early promise but late-phase trials in AML/MDS were halted in 2024 for lack of efficacy and safety concerns.

## Conditions and scope

- Mouse preclinical evidence is strong.
- Clinical: magrolimab + azacitidine in MDS (failed phase III), magrolimab + rituximab in NHL (modest signal), TTI-621 SIRPα-Fc in development.
- On-target toxicity: anaemia from RBC clearance — managed with priming-dose strategies.
- Tumour heterogeneity: not all tumours express enough CD47 to be vulnerable.

## Counter-evidence

- Magrolimab clinical failure in AML/MDS (2024) suggests the preclinical-to-clinical translation overstates efficacy in liquid tumours.
- Anti-CD47 monotherapy is generally ineffective; combination with opsonizing antibodies (rituximab) appears necessary for ADCP-driven tumour clearance.
- Macrophage activation state in tumour TME may render TAMs unresponsive even when CD47 is blocked.

## Open questions

- Why magrolimab failed in AML/MDS despite strong preclinical signal.
- Whether anti-SIRPα (rather than anti-CD47) circumvents on-target anaemia.
- Combination strategies — anti-CD47 + anti-PD-1 + chemotherapy combinations.
- Solid-tumour activity (clinical data still emerging).
