## Statement

Because the mechanism couples effector binding to target release through allosteric switching of steric clashes — rather than requiring the effector to directly overlap the target epitope — the force-generating switch can be fused to almost any binder, making facilitated dissociation a general way to dynamically regulate protein–protein interactions.

## Evidence summary

"because our allosteric mechanism of switching steric clashes places no specific requirements on the binder or target, our approach can be used to dynamically regulate protein–protein interactions quite generally: by fusing to our switch, almost any binding interaction can be made to switch off rapidly" (p.6–7). Demonstrated across an arbitrary designed heterodimer, a SARS-CoV-2 RBD binder (LCB1), and the IL-2 mimic Neo2.

## Conditions and scope

Three distinct binder systems; in vitro and cell-surface.

## Counter-evidence

Each new binder still requires sampling fusion geometries to set strain; modularity is demonstrated, not automatic.
