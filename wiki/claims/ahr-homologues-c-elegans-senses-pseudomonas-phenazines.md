## Statement

AHR is conserved across major animal phyla. In Caenorhabditis elegans the AHR orthologue AHR-1 senses Pseudomonas aeruginosa phenazines (including pyocyanin) and participates in host defence — establishing that AHR's pattern-recognition function predates the vertebrate adaptive immune system.

## Evidence summary

Reported in [[papers/role-ahr-host-pathogen-interactions]] citing refs 9–12. Drosophila and C. elegans AHR orthologues do not bind dioxin, suggesting xenobiotic sensing was acquired secondarily during vertebrate evolution.

## Conditions and scope

Direct phenazine binding by C. elegans AHR-1 is supported; the full ligand repertoire of invertebrate AHR remains unknown.

## Counter-evidence

None within the review's scope.
