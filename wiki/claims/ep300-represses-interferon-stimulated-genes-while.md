## Statement
EP300 (histone acetyltransferase) acts as a constrainer of the interferon response in macrophages — its loss upregulates ISGs and MHC-I cross-presentation genes — while it promotes inflammatory genes (e.g. Spp1). The authors propose EP300 balances HDAC activity (BRD4 sequestration model) and may act via non-histone acetylation.

## Evidence summary
Ep300 knockout produced a transcriptional signature distinct from all other regulators, with ISG upregulation and inflammatory downregulation ([[papers/integrated-time-series-analysis-high-content]], Figures 5G, S8; uses [[foundations/ep300-histone-acetyltransferase]]). EP300 had not been widely studied in macrophages, prompting validation (see companion pharmacological claim).

## Conditions and scope
Macrophage cell line; effect validated at gene and protein level.

## Counter-evidence
None recorded; mechanism (histone vs non-histone acetylation, HDAC balance) remains hypothesized.

## Open questions
Is EP300's ISG repression mediated by histone acetylation, non-histone (e.g. NF-κB/STAT) acetylation, or HDAC/BRD4 balance?
