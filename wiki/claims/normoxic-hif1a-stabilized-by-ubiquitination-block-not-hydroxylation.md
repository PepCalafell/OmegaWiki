## Statement

The transient normoxic stabilization of HIF-1α during the cell cycle in HCC cells arises from inhibition of HIF-1α ubiquitination/degradation rather than from a failure of PHD-mediated proline hydroxylation: normoxic G2/M HIF-1α is hydroxylated yet not ubiquitinated, coincident with a CDK1 activity peak.

## Evidence summary

- **oxygen-independent-expression-hif-during-cell** (p.3155) — immunoprecipitation of synchronized normoxic HIF-1α.
  - Quote: "Although HIF-1α was shown to be, at least partially, hydroxylated, no substantial ubiquitination could be detected ... suggesting that stabilization of HIF-1α at the particular cell cycle phase is not due to lack of hydroxylation but rather due to inhibition of its subsequent ubiquitination and degradation."
  - Appearance of HIF-1α coincides with a peak in CDK1, previously shown to modify HIF-1α (Ser668) and block its degradation in colon carcinoma.

## Conditions and scope

Single IP timepoint (8 h post-release); mechanistic inference (ubiquitination block) not tested by genetic perturbation of the E3 step or CDK1 in this paper.

## Counter-evidence

None recorded. The CDK1→HIF-1α causal link is imported from prior colon-carcinoma work, not directly perturbed here.
