## Statement

Re-analysis of a published metabolic CRISPR-knockout screen shows de novo purine synthesis genes are among the most essential for subcutaneous pancreatic tumor growth in vivo, yet far less essential in vitro, indicating the in vivo environment imposes dependence on de novo synthesis.

## Evidence summary

- **systemic-hypoxia-suppresses-solid-tumor-growth** (p.9): "genes involved in de novo purine synthesis were some of the most essential genes for tumor growth in vivo" — Adapted from Zhu et al. 2021 (ref 48) (Fig.5h); supports why hypoxic suppression of de novo synthesis impairs growth. (type: correlational, strength: moderate)

## Conditions and scope

Preclinical mouse models (syngeneic, GEMM, and human xenograft) under continuous inhaled hypoxia (typically 8% O2) or pharmacological hypoxia; single preprint source (not yet peer-reviewed).

## Counter-evidence

None recorded in the wiki yet. Some cell lines (notably ccRCC) deviate from the dominant response — see [[claims/ccrcc-and-select-lines-resist-hypoxia-tumor-suppression]].
