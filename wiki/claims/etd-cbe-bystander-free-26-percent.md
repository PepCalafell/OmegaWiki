## Statement

A next-generation high-precision cytosine base editor, eTD-CBE, corrected the most common *TGM1* splice-acceptor mutation (c.877-2A>G) at up to 26% efficiency at the target nucleotide while producing no detectable editing at the adjacent bystander nucleotide in ARCI patient-derived keratinocytes — in contrast to the standard BE4max-NG editor, which edited ~20% at both the target and the bystander base.

## Evidence summary

Reported in [[papers/editing-skin-place-vivo-genome-correction]] (Preview of Apaydin & Sadhnani et al.). Relates to [[concepts/bystander-free-precision-base-editing]] and [[foundations/cytosine-base-editor]].

## Conditions and scope

In vitro patient keratinocytes; figure-level numbers as relayed by the commentary, not extracted from the primary paper. Bystander-free editing is what makes a conserved splice site a viable target.
