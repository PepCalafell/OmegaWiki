## Statement

Vemurafenib binds AHR and triggers nuclear translocation, yet fails to promote AHR-ARNT heterodimerisation and does not induce CYP1A1 — strongly suggesting that vemurafenib occupies a binding position distinct from the canonical TCDD primary pocket, providing pharmacological evidence for pocket-selective AHR responses.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/complex-biology-aryl-hydrocarbon-receptor-activation]] (Opitz et al. 2023) reviews the original biochemical/displacement/proximity-ligation evidence and frames it as the clearest example that ligand identity can dissociate nuclear translocation from XRE-driven transcription.

## Conditions and scope

Holds for the specific BRAF V600E inhibitor [[foundations/vemurafenib]]. Does not yet generalise to all BRAFi or to all "biased" AHR ligands. Cryo-EM co-structure of vemurafenib with AHR would resolve.

## Counter-evidence

Cryo-EM data with indirubin shows only the primary AHR pocket is occupied by canonical ligands [[claims/ahr-pas-b-cryo-em-primary-secondary-pocket]] — direct structural evidence that vemurafenib occupies the secondary pocket is still missing.

## Linked ideas

- Pocket-selective AHR ligands could constitute a class of "biased AHR agonists" analogous to biased GPCR agonism.

## Open questions
