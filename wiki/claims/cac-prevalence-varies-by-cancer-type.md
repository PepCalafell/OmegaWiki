## Statement

The prevalence of cancer-associated cachexia (CAC) varies dramatically by tumour site, ranging from 15-30% in hematological, breast and prostate cancers to 40% in lung cancer and up to 80% in pancreatic cancer.

## Evidence summary

Multiple epidemiologic studies cited in the 2025 *Cancer Discovery* review converge on these prevalence ranges. Higher rates in upper GI, gastric and pancreatic cancer are consistent across decades and across diagnostic frameworks; lower rates in hematological and hormone-driven cancers are also reproducibly reported.

## Conditions and scope

The exact numbers depend on the diagnostic criterion (Fearon vs Evans vs CASCO) and on whether early-stage disease is included. Sex, age, ethnicity, and treatment modality further modulate observed prevalence.

## Counter-evidence

None at the population level; some single-centre cohorts report lower prevalence in pancreatic cancer when using strict Fearon criteria with short follow-up.

## Linked ideas

- Differentiated trial design by tumour type is needed.

## Open questions

- Are biological mechanisms underlying CAC fundamentally different across tumour types, or only quantitatively different?
