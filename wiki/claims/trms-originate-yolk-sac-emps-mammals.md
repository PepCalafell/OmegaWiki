## Statement

In mammals, the dominant developmental origin of tissue-resident macrophages is the yolk-sac erythro-myeloid progenitor (EMP) wave. EMPs arise from yolk-sac haemogenic endothelium in a RUNX1-dependent, MYB- and NOTCH1-independent manner; their PreMac descendants colonize the embryo during organogenesis and seed nearly every tissue with a long-lived, self-renewing macrophage population that persists into adulthood.

## Evidence summary

The reviewed body of work in [[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] points to (i) RUNX1 dependence of macrophage development, (ii) RUNX1-dependent / MYB-NOTCH1-independent EMP marker profile, (iii) fate-mapping with EMP-restricted Cre lines that label adult microglia, Kupffer cells, alveolar macrophages, Langerhans cells and others, and (iv) absence of significant fetal-HSC contribution to the resident pool in nearly all fate-mapping studies.

## Conditions and scope

- Applies to mammals; the dichotomy is conserved (with details differing) in zebrafish and Drosophila.
- Gut lamina-propria macrophages are HSC-derived and are an explicit exception.
- Some skin and liver TRMs receive partial HSC contribution under homeostasis or after stress/injury.

## Counter-evidence

- One fate-mapping study suggests fetal-HSC contribution to resident pools (review notes "All fate-mapping studies but one").
- Whether a primitive (RUNX1-independent) yolk-sac wave seeds adult microglia is hedged ("uncertain in the absence of further experimental evidence").

## Open questions

- Quantitative, lineage-resolved contribution of EMP vs HSC vs primitive yolk-sac progenitors to the adult resident pool — DNA-barcoding approaches were called out by the review as the way forward.
- Whether the EMP-vs-HSC functional divergence is preserved in human tissues at the same fidelity as in mouse.
