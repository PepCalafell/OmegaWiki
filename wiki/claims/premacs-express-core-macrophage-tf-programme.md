## Statement

Embryonic PreMacs already express a stable core macrophage transcriptional programme — PU.1 (SPI1), cMAF, and IRF8 — before tissue colonization and before tissue-specific lineage-determining factor (LDF) expression. Tissue-specific LDFs (SALL1 microglia, ID3 Kupffer, PPARγ alveolar, SPI-C red pulp, GATA6 peritoneal) are layered on top of this core programme during niche-driven specification.

## Evidence summary

The review [[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] consolidates ChIP-seq and KO-fate-mapping evidence (Mass 2016, Lavin 2014, Gosselin 2014, McKercher 1996 PU.1 KO) supporting PU.1 as the master regulator and cMAF/IRF8 as its core co-factors. PreMac scRNA-seq shows expression of all three factors before tissue colonization, with patchy LDF expression beginning at the same stage.

## Conditions and scope

- Mouse mid-gestation EMP-derived PreMacs. The dependence on PU.1 generalizes to all macrophages including HSC-derived BMDMs.
- The "core" programme refers to identity-defining factors; many additional TFs (KLF4, JunB, etc.) contribute to context-specific functions.

## Counter-evidence

- Whether all three TFs are strictly *necessary* in PreMacs (vs upregulated only at later stages) is not formally settled by single-cell data alone; PU.1 is the only one with a clear KO macrophage-loss phenotype.
- IRF8 has additional roles in dendritic-cell lineage that complicate interpretation of its PreMac function.

## Open questions

- The relative chromatin-priming roles of PU.1, cMAF, and IRF8 in establishing macrophage identity vs preparing for tissue-specific LDF binding.
- Whether the same core trio operates in HSC-derived monocyte differentiation, or whether monocyte fate uses a slightly different cofactor balance.
