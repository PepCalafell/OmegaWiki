## Statement

miR-133a-3p is the strongest hypoxia-associated miRNA in localized prostate cancer (TCGA: FDR=2.08×10⁻¹¹, ρ=−0.40), with replication in CPC-GENE and Taylor cohorts. In three PCa cell lines (22Rv1, DU145, PC3) it falls under 1% O₂ for 72h, and reintroducing miR-133a-3p mimic decreases viability in all three lines and decreases PC3 invasion (P=5.45×10⁻³). Validated correlated proteins (CPC-GENE) include the tumor suppressor BIN1 and the metabolic enzyme PGM5. Together these data position miR-133a-3p as a *functional* tumor suppressor microRNA whose loss under hypoxia contributes to aggressive PCa biology.

## Evidence summary

- [[papers/molecular-landmarks-tumor-hypoxia-across-cancer]] — three retrospective cohorts (n=330, 170, 97) + in vitro mimic rescue in three cell lines.
- Sakamuro 1996 *Nat Genet* — BIN1 as MYC-binding tumor-suppressor (mechanistic prior for the miR-133a-3p–BIN1 axis).
- Edwards 1995 *Genomics* — PGM5 chromosomal localization.

## Conditions and scope

- Localized prostate cancer cohorts; not metastatic/CRPC.
- In vitro validation in 3 cell lines; no in vivo mimic-delivery validation in this paper.
- Two genomic loci (MIR133A1, MIR133A2) confound locus-specific interpretation.

## Counter-evidence

- Mechanism of hypoxic downregulation (transcriptional vs processing vs stability) not characterized.
- Therapeutic mimic delivery in vivo unproven.
