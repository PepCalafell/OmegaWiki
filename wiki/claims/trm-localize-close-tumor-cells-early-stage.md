## Statement

Tissue-resident macrophages (TRMs) are spatially intratumoural during the earliest stages of NSCLC growth (days 5-15 in mouse models), in close contact with seeded KP tumour cells. As tumours expand, TRMs progressively redistribute to the tumour periphery, resembling the topology of human tuberculosis granulomas. This temporal redistribution is observed in both orthotopic transplant and genetic NSCLC mouse models, and in human NSCLC.

## Evidence summary

- Time-course confocal imaging (KP-GFP + MRC1, days 5-30); n=3-8 mice per time point
- Quantification: ~80% intratumoural TRMs at day 5 → <20% intratumoural by day 30
- Replicated in SPC-Cre KP genetic model and in human NSCLC tissue sections

## Conditions and scope

- Mouse KP orthotopic and SPC-Cre NSCLC; human NSCLC tissue sections
- "Early" defined operationally as days 5-15 post tumour injection in mouse
- Spatial imaging at whole-tumour level using MRC1 as TRM marker (also expressed on some MDMs but more strongly on TRMs)

## Counter-evidence

- MRC1 is not strictly TRM-specific; some MDMs and tumour-associated macrophages express MRC1
- Quantification is from 2D sections; 3D distribution may differ

## Open questions

- Whether the redistribution is active (chemokine-driven) or passive (tumour cell expansion displaces TRMs)
