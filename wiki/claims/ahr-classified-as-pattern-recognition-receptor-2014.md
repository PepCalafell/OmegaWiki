## Statement

The aryl hydrocarbon receptor functions as a non-classical pattern recognition receptor (PRR) that directly or indirectly senses microorganism-derived molecules — pigments, quorum-sensing molecules, indole metabolites — and initiates antimicrobial gene programmes. This reclassifies AHR alongside TLRs and NLRs as a microbial sensor.

## Evidence summary

Originally proposed by Moura-Alves et al. 2014 (Nature) and consolidated in [[papers/role-ahr-host-pathogen-interactions]] across bacterial (Mtb phthiocol, Pseudomonas phenazines), fungal (Malassezia indirubin/ICZ/malassezin/tryptanthrin) and microbiota-derived (indoles from L. reuteri, E. coli, S. epidermidis) AHR ligands.

## Conditions and scope

AHR-PRR function depends on ligand identity and tissue. Unlike TLRs, AHR has no extracellular pathogen-binding domain — sensing is via small-molecule diffusion to the cytosolic ligand-binding pocket.

## Counter-evidence

Classical PRR signalling (NF-κB, IRF3) is partially independent of AHR; many TLR/NLR responses occur in Ahr-KO cells. The PRR framing is therefore complementary rather than supersession.
