## Statement

Intranasal diphtheria toxin (DT) administration to CD169-DTR transgenic mice specifically depletes lung tissue-resident macrophages (alveolar TRMs) without affecting circulating monocytes or monocyte-derived macrophages. The specificity is supported by the observation that CD169 expression is negligible on Ms4a3-tdTom-traced monocyte progeny in naive lungs and early KP tumour lesions.

## Evidence summary

- CD169-DTR + intranasal DT (15 ng/mouse, days 0 and 3): TRM depletion in lung
- Flow cytometry confirms no effect on monocytes or MDMs
- Ms4a3-tdTom critical control: monocyte progeny do not express CD169 in naive or early-tumour lungs

## Conditions and scope

- Mouse C57BL/6 background; CD169-DTR transgenic
- Intranasal route (not systemic) restricts depletion to lung
- Validated in naive and early-stage NSCLC tumour conditions

## Counter-evidence

- In chronic inflammation or late-stage disease, MDMs may upregulate CD169, potentially compromising specificity
- DT-induced apoptosis releases DAMPs; possible secondary inflammatory effects
- Some splenic and lymph-node TRMs also express CD169; intranasal route mitigates but does not fully eliminate off-target depletion
