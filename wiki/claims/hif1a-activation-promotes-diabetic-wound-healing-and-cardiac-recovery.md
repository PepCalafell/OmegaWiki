## Statement

In diabetes, hyperglycemia destabilizes HIF-1α (via methylglyoxal-mediated CBP modification and other mechanisms), impairing the wound-healing hypoxia response. Pharmacological strategies that restore HIF-1α activity (PHD inhibitors, HIF-1α stabilizers) accelerate fibroblast proliferation, migration, and angiogenesis to close diabetic wounds, and improve cardiac functional recovery post-ischemia in diabetic models. Activation of HIF-1α signaling also prevents diabetic kidney disease progression in T2D animal models.

## Evidence summary

Decades of preclinical studies integrated by Luo et al. 2022; clinical translation via roxadustat (PHD inhibitor) for renal anemia is the first-in-class approval relevant to diabetes complications.

## Conditions and scope

- Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes preclinical models
- Wound healing assays and ischemia/reperfusion
- Hepatic HIF-2α (distinct from HIF-1α) ameliorates glucose tolerance

## Counter-evidence

In glomerular mesangial cells, high glucose activates rather than suppresses HIF-1α — cell-type heterogeneity within kidney.
