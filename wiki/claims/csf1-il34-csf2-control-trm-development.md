## Statement

The CSF1R / CSF2R signalling axis controls tissue-resident macrophage development, survival, and maintenance. CSF1 (broadly produced) and IL-34 (neurons, keratinocytes) bind CSF1R; CSF2 (lung type II pneumocytes) binds CSF2R. Microglia and Langerhans cells require IL-34 specifically and are spared in CSF1-deficient mice. Alveolar macrophages require CSF2 specifically. Most other TRMs (Kupffer, peritoneal, etc.) require CSF1. Bi-allelic LOF in CSF1R causes paediatric-onset leukoencephalopathy with absent microglia; hypomorphic CSF1R variants cause adult-onset ALSP; CSF2 / CSF2R / PPARG LOF causes alveolar proteinosis.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] consolidates the trophic-factor table with mouse genetic data (CSF1-KO, IL-34-KO, CSF2-KO, CSF1R-KO) and human disease genetics (CSF1R Mendelian disorders, alveolar proteinosis). The picture is well-established and pharmacologically validated by anti-CSF1R drugs in clinical use (pexidartinib, axatilimab) which deplete macrophages.

## Conditions and scope

- Murine genetic models are the primary substrate.
- Human translation is supported by both Mendelian disease (CSF1R-LOF leukoencephalopathy) and pharmacology (anti-CSF1R drug effects).
- Context dependencies: CSF1 isoforms (membrane-bound vs secreted) have tissue-specific rescue capabilities (membrane-bound rescues most tissues but not liver/spleen/peritoneum/adrenal).

## Counter-evidence

- IL-34's role in some peripheral tissues (e.g. some skin macrophages, gut) is incompletely mapped — it may have CSF1R-independent receptors (PTP-ζ proposed).
- CSF1R inhibitor clinical effects show heterogeneity across patients, suggesting context-dependence not fully captured by the canonical model.
