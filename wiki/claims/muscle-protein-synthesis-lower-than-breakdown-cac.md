## Statement

Stable-isotope measurements of myofibrillar protein synthesis in gastrointestinal cancer-associated cachexia show that synthesis is HIGHER than in weight-stable patients or healthy controls — yet remains LOWER than the simultaneously elevated protein breakdown rate, yielding net catabolism. This challenges the simple narrative that CAC muscle loss equals "low synthesis".

## Evidence summary

MacDonald et al. mechanistic study using deuterium-labelled amino acid tracers.

## Conditions and scope

GI cancer; breakdown rate inferred rather than directly measured.

## Counter-evidence

Earlier studies in mouse CAC emphasize reduced synthesis.

## Linked ideas

- Targeting breakdown (UPS / autophagy) may be needed in addition to anabolic stimulation.

## Open questions

- Quantitative relative contribution of synthesis vs breakdown across CAC stages.
