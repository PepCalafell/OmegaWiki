## Statement

Obesity-induced chronic inflammation selectively triggers PD-1 expression on tumour-associated macrophages via the mTORC1-glycolysis axis. PD-1 acts as a negative regulator on TAMs, dampening glycolysis, phagocytosis and T-cell activation. Anti-PD-1 therapy releases the brake: TAM glycolysis rises, CD86 and MHC molecules are upregulated, and CD8 T-cell activity increases. Myeloid-specific PD-1 deficiency slows tumour progression. This provides a mechanistic explanation for the clinical "obesity paradox" in ICB.

## Evidence summary

- Bader, Voss, Rathmell 2024 referenced.
- Multiple cohort analyses showing better ICB outcomes in some obese cancer patients.

## Conditions and scope

- Obese-host tumour models; subset of ICB-responsive cancer types.

## Counter-evidence

- The "obesity paradox" is heterogeneous across tumour types; opposite effects reported in some.

## Linked ideas

- Myeloid-specific PD-1 as predictive biomarker for ICB benefit in obese patients.

## Open questions

- Whether targeted TAM PD-1 modulation produces similar benefit in lean patients.
