## Statement

Topically applied mRNA-LNPs carrying base-editing cargo were locally and systemically well tolerated: across human skin explants, in vitro immune-cell models, and in vivo mouse studies they showed minimal immunogenicity and no local inflammation even after repeated administration, and in vivo imaging, qPCR, and desorption electrospray ionization (DESI) mass spectrometry confirmed that both the LNPs and the genetic cargo remained confined to the treated skin with limited systemic dissemination — supported by a strong genomic safety profile assessed by both sgRNA-dependent and sgRNA-independent methods.

## Evidence summary

Reported in [[papers/editing-skin-place-vivo-genome-correction]]. Relates to [[foundations/mrna-lipid-nanoparticle]]. Positions in situ skin base editing as a comparatively low-risk application of in vivo genome editing.

## Conditions and scope

Safety assessed across explants, in vitro immune models, and mouse; especially relevant for non-lethal disease where the safety bar is high. Local confinement is a deliberate advantage of the topical + barrier-modulation route.

## Counter-evidence

None within the source; long-term and large-surface-area safety untested.
