## Statement

In oropharyngeal candidiasis, AHR signalling promotes pathogenesis: it activates SRC-family kinases that phosphorylate EGFR and drive epithelial endocytosis of Candida albicans, increasing fungal invasion depth. CH-223191 pharmacologic AHR inhibition is protective in this tissue context — opposite to AHR's protective role in vulvovaginal candidiasis (where Ahr-KO mice show higher fungal burden and reduced IL-22).

## Evidence summary

Reported in [[papers/role-ahr-host-pathogen-interactions]] citing ref 101 (oropharyngeal) vs refs 102–103 (vulvovaginal).

## Conditions and scope

The opposite outcomes in oropharyngeal vs vulvovaginal candidiasis exemplify tissue-context-specific AHR biology.

## Counter-evidence
