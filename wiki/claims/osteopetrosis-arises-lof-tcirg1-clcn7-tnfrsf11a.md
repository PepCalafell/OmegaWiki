## Statement

Loss-of-function mutations in any of TCIRG1, CLCN7, TNFRSF11A (RANK), TREM2, or CSF1R cause osteopetrosis — defective osteoclast-mediated bone resorption leading to dense, brittle bone. The genes converge on three mechanisms: (i) acidification of the resorption lacuna (TCIRG1, CLCN7), (ii) osteoclast differentiation/survival signalling (TNFRSF11A/RANK, CSF1R), (iii) osteoclast multinucleation and lipid sensing (TREM2/DAP12).

## Evidence summary

The review [[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] synthesizes human genetics (Frattini 2000 TCIRG1, Sobacchi 2007 CLCN7) and mouse KO models showing dense bone, fractures, and CNS calcifications. TREM2 LOF combines osteoclast dysfunction (osteopetrosis-like) with microglial dysfunction (Nasu-Hakola), illustrating the macrophage-shared mechanism. CSF1R hypomorphs combine osteopetrosis with leukoencephalopathy.

## Conditions and scope

- Human and mouse. Phenotype severity depends on mono- vs bi-allelic state and on which gene.
- Some forms (e.g. CSF1R LOF) are systemic macrophage diseases with osteopetrosis as one organ manifestation; others (TCIRG1 acidification defect) are osteoclast-restricted.

## Counter-evidence

- Mild osteopetrosis-like phenotypes can also arise from non-genetic causes (drug-induced osteoclast suppression with bisphosphonates, denosumab) — these are pharmacological, not the inherited genetic disease.
- Genotype-phenotype correlation is imperfect; identical TCIRG1 variants can yield variable severity.

## Open questions

- Whether residual osteoclast function in hypomorphic alleles can be rescued pharmacologically (CSF1R hypomorph rescue, RANKL agonism).
- Why TREM2 LOF produces a distinct neurological-bone composite (Nasu-Hakola) while CSF1R LOF produces a leukoencephalopathy-bone composite — the differential macrophage subset dependencies remain incompletely mapped.
