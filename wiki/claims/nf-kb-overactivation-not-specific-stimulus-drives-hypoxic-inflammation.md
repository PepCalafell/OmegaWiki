## Statement

Multiple NF-κB-activating stimuli — PAMPs (LPS, P3C, CpG, polyI:C) and cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β) — converge on the same hypoxic boost of inflammatory gene expression. This indicates that NF-κB pathway activation itself, rather than a specific upstream receptor/ligand identity, is what couples hypoxia to inflammatory output.

## Evidence summary

- qRT-PCR on a representative panel of C2-associated genes (e.g., IL6, IRF1, NFKB1, CCL5) after each stimulus in MAC21 vs MAC1 (Calafell 2024 fig. S2E).
- Most genes / stimuli show the hypoxia × stimulus interaction, supporting the convergence model.

## Conditions and scope

- Limited to NF-κB-activating stimuli; non-NF-κB stimuli (IFN-α/β alone) not tested.
- Static 1% O₂.

## Counter-evidence

- Some stimulus × gene combinations did not show the boost — heterogeneity exists.

## Linked ideas

- Targeting NF-κB axis (rather than a specific receptor) is the relevant intervention point.

## Open questions

- Whether the convergence is at the IKK/NF-κB level or at the chromatin / TET2 recruitment level.
- Whether IFN-only stimulation (no NF-κB) phenocopies the C2 hypomethylation.
