## Statement

Macrophage engulfment of apoptotic cells (efferocytosis) is coupled to a *programmed* shift in cytokine output: secretion of anti-inflammatory mediators TGFβ, IL-10, and PGE2 is up-regulated, while pro-inflammatory mediators TNF, IL-1β, IL-6 (and type-I IFNs) are actively suppressed. The programme distinguishes efferocytosis from inflammatory phagocytosis and prevents chronic inflammation despite continuous high-volume apoptotic clearance.

## Evidence summary

[[papers/physiology-diseases-tissue-resident-macrophages]] presents the anti-inflammatory output as a defining feature of efferocytosis. Failure of efferocytosis (MERTK / MFGE8 / TIM4 / C1Q LOF) yields autoimmune phenotypes that are interpreted as chronic-inflammation states resulting from accumulated immunogenic debris. The mechanistic substrate involves TAM-receptor signalling that recruits SHP-1/2 phosphatases and induces SOCS1/3 to inhibit STAT1-driven inflammation.

## Conditions and scope

- Macrophage homeostatic clearance context.
- Tumour-context efferocytosis may have *immunosuppressive* output that is a *liability* (TAMs that efferocytose may be selectively immunosuppressive in cancer).
- Defects in the anti-inflammatory programme (e.g. NF-κB hyperactivation) may convert efferocytosis to inflammatory output.

## Counter-evidence

- Some efferocytosis events in cancer or chronic infection are not anti-inflammatory and may contribute to TAM-driven immune evasion.
- The relative contributions of different downstream pathways (SHP-1/2, SOCS, lipid mediators, metabolic shifts) to the cytokine switch are not fully mapped.

## Open questions

- Whether efferocytosis-coupled anti-inflammatory output is reversible or programmed for the macrophage's lifetime.
- Whether the cytokine-switch can be selectively reversed therapeutically (boost pro-inflammatory output of efferocytosing TAMs in cancer).
- The role of metabolic reprogramming (lipid handling from engulfed cells) in driving the cytokine switch.
