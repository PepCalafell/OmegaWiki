## Definition

*Staphylococcus aureus* is a Gram-positive bacterium that is both a common commensal and an opportunistic pathogen. In trained-immunity research, heat-killed *S. aureus* is a standard heterologous ex vivo stimulus for PBMCs, eliciting monocyte- and T-cell-derived cytokine production.

## Intuition

Because *S. aureus* is unrelated to the BCG training stimulus, the cytokine response it evokes serves as a readout of non-specific (heterologous) trained immunity rather than antigen-specific recall.

## Formal notation

Typical assay: PBMCs stimulated with ~10^6 CFU/mL heat-killed *S. aureus*; TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β measured in 24 h supernatants, IFN-γ at 7 days, by ELISA.

## Key variants

Heat-killed vs live; whole bacteria vs purified components (LTA, peptidoglycan).

## Known limitations

Ex vivo cytokine magnitudes depend on cell composition, donor variability, and assay timing.

## Open problems

Mapping which PRR pathways dominate the *S. aureus*-evoked trained response in humans.

## Relevance to active research

Reference heterologous stimulus for quantifying BCG-induced trained immunity in the 300BCG cohort.
