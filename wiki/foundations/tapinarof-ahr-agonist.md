## Definition

Tapinarof is a bacteria-derived stilbene that acts as a non-dioxin AHR agonist. It was approved by the US FDA in 2022 as a topical cream for adult mild-to-severe plaque psoriasis; ADORING-1/2 phase III trials met endpoints for atopic dermatitis. Mechanism: direct binding to AHR–ARNT heterodimers, induction of CYP1A1, suppression of IL-17A/F in T cells, induction of skin-barrier proteins (filaggrin, loricrin, involucrin, hornerin) and ceramides, and Nrf2 activation. Effects are AHR-dependent.

## Intuition

Tapinarof is the clinical proof-of-concept for AHR-agonist drug development. It works because (i) it gives transient AHR activation (unlike persistent TCDD), (ii) it repairs the epidermal barrier as well as suppressing inflammation, and (iii) it elicits durable "remittive" tissue memory in skin T cells.

## Key variants

- Tapinarof cream 1% (FDA-approved formulation)
- Synthetic stilbene analogues in preclinical development

## Known limitations

- Topical only; systemic AHR agonism in humans is untested
- Common AEs: folliculitis, contact dermatitis at application site

## Open problems

- Whether tapinarof's remittive effect generalises to other AHR agonists
- Translation to systemic indications (IBD, MS) via novel delivery routes
