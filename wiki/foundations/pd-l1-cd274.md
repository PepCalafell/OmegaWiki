## Definition

PD-L1 (CD274 / B7-H1) is a type-I transmembrane glycoprotein and the canonical ligand for PD-1 (PDCD1) on T cells. PD-L1 engagement of PD-1 recruits SHP-2 to TCR ITSM motifs, dampening TCR signaling and limiting cytotoxic T-cell activity. PD-L1 is constitutively expressed at low levels on a subset of antigen-presenting cells and rapidly upregulated by IFN-γ via JAK-STAT1-IRF1 and by additional cytokines through STAT3, NF-κB, HIF-1α, and MAPK pathways. In tumors, PD-L1 is expressed by cancer cells and by myeloid cells — particularly TAMs, which are the predominant PD-L1-expressing population in many human tumor types.

## Functional roles

- Trans interaction with PD-1 on T cells → ITSM-recruited SHP-2 → TCR signaling suppression (canonical T-cell checkpoint).
- Cis interaction with CD80 on the same cell → blocks PD-1:PD-L1 and CTLA-4:CD80, preserves CD28 co-stimulation.
- Cell-intrinsic survival signals via reverse signaling in some contexts.
- Possible protective role for PD-L1-expressing myeloid cells against PD-1+ T cell killing.

## Therapeutic relevance

- Target of anti-PD-L1 antibodies (atezolizumab, durvalumab, avelumab) and indirectly of anti-PD-1 antibodies (pembrolizumab, nivolumab).
- Myeloid (TAM) PD-L1 may be more predictive of immune checkpoint response than tumor-cell PD-L1 in several cancer types (bladder, urothelial, breast).
- PD-L1 IHC remains an imperfect biomarker due to staining heterogeneity, antibody platform variability, and ambiguous cell-of-origin attribution.
