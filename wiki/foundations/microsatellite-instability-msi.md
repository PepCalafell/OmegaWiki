## Definition

MSI is a hypermutable state of short tandem-repeat (microsatellite) regions caused by deficient DNA mismatch repair (MMR), typically loss of MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, or PMS2. It can be assessed by IHC (loss of MMR protein expression) or by genomic MSI scoring.

## Intuition

When the MMR system fails, replication slippage at repeats goes uncorrected, producing insertions/deletions and an elevated mutation burden — a marker of genomic instability and, in many cancers, of immunotherapy responsiveness.

## Key variants

- IHC-based detection (MLH1/MSH2/MSH6/PMS2 protein loss)
- Genomic MSI score from sequencing
- Occurs in ~3–4% of human tumors (≈20% in colorectal)

## Known limitations

IHC and genomic assays can disagree; loss of a single MMR protein does not always equal full MSI-high status.

## Open problems

- Mechanistic link between hypoxia and acquired MMR loss

## Relevance to active research

In Arenillas et al., most EPAS1-mutant sympathetic CCHD-PPGL tumors showed MSI / MMR-protein loss, linking systemic hypoxia to DNA-repair impairment and increased mutability.
