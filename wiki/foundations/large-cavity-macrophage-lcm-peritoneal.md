## Definition

Large cavity macrophages (LCMs, also called large peritoneal macrophages) are the dominant, long-lived tissue-resident macrophages of serous cavities (peritoneal, pleural). They are GATA6-dependent and, when mature, express TIM4 ([[foundations/tim-4-timd4]]). Small cavity macrophages (SCMs) and converting cavity macrophages (CCMs) are monocyte-derived, less mature populations along the replenishment trajectory toward LCMs.

## Intuition

The cavity macrophage compartment follows a monocyte → SCM → CCM → LCM maturation hierarchy: bone-marrow-derived monocytes ([[foundations/bone-marrow-derived-macrophage-bmdm]]) enter the cavity as SCMs/CCMs and gradually acquire the GATA6 ([[foundations/gata6-tf]])-driven, TIM4+ resident LCM identity. The mature LCM state is self-maintained by local proliferation rather than constant monocyte input.

## Key variants

- LCM / large peritoneal macrophage — mature resident, GATA6+, TIM4+.
- SCM (small cavity macrophage) — monocyte-derived, immature.
- CCM (converting cavity macrophage) — transitional intermediate.

## Known limitations

- Subset nomenclature is mouse-cavity-specific and does not map cleanly onto other tissue-resident macrophage compartments.

## Open problems

- What metabolic checkpoints gate the SCM→CCM→LCM transition and the acquisition of TIM4.

## Relevance to active research

[[papers/glcnacylation-shapes-macrophage-tissue-residency-alternative]] reports that myeloid Ogt deficiency depletes mature TIM4+ LCMs and shifts the compartment toward monocyte-derived SCMs/CCMs, with O-GlcNAcylation dynamically regulated along the monocyte–SCM–CCM–LCM transition.
