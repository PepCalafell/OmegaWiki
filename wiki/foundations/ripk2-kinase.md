## Definition

RIPK2 is a serine/threonine kinase with a C-terminal CARD domain that serves as the essential signal-transducing kinase downstream of the cytosolic pattern-recognition receptors NOD1 and NOD2. It drives activation of NF-κB and MAPK pathways and the production of proinflammatory cytokines.

## Intuition

RIPK2 is the adaptor-kinase that converts bacterial-peptidoglycan sensing by NOD1/2 into inflammatory transcription. Its catalytic activation is gated by autophosphorylation in its activation segment, making it a hallmark proinflammatory signaling node.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *RIPK2*; domain architecture: N-terminal kinase domain + C-terminal CARD
- Activation: NOD1/2 CARD-CARD recruitment → RIPK2 oligomerization, K63-ubiquitination, and activation-segment autophosphorylation (e.g. S176)
- Downstream: TAK1/IKK → NF-κB; MAPK activation

## Key variants

- Kinase-dead vs ubiquitination-deficient mutants dissect scaffolding vs catalytic roles
- Small-molecule RIPK2 inhibitors (e.g. GSK583) used to block NOD signaling

## Known limitations

- Kinase activity vs scaffolding contributions to downstream signaling are still debated
- Cross-talk with RIPK1/RIPK3 death-signaling complicates phenotypic attribution

## Open problems

- Precise role of RIPK2 catalytic activity in shaping human macrophage proinflammatory states
- Therapeutic value of RIPK2 inhibition in inflammatory disease vs anti-tumor immunity

## Relevance to active research

Phosphoproteomics of primary human macrophages found the RIPK2 activation-segment autophosphorylation site S176 — annotated as essential for catalytic activity — phosphorylated at higher levels in the proinflammatory M1 state, identifying RIPK2 as a central proinflammatory kinase upstream of NF-κB.
