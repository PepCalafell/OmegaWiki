## Definition

The International HapMap Project was a multi-country effort (launched 2003; Nature 426, 789–796) to catalogue common patterns of human genetic variation — the haplotype map of SNPs — across populations. Its third and final phase was completed in 2010.

## Intuition

HapMap slashed the cost of profiling common genetic differences across many individuals, providing the reference SNP catalogue that made genome-wide association studies practical.

## Formal notation

Not applicable.

## Key variants

- HapMap phases I–III, expanding population coverage.
- Succeeded/complemented by the [[1000-genomes-project]].

## Known limitations

- Focused on common variation; rare variants under-represented.
- Limited population diversity in early phases.

## Open problems

- Extending reference variation catalogues to under-represented populations.

## Relevance to active research

A key enabling resource for [[gwas-genome-wide-association-study]], cited in [[wealth-discovery-built-human-genome-project]] as one of the big efforts that, together with the HGP, ushered in the GWAS era.
