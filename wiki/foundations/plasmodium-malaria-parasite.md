## Definition

Plasmodium spp. are apicomplexan blood-stage parasites that cause malaria. Rodent species (P. berghei ANKA, P. chabaudi) are widely used as cerebral- and blood-stage-malaria models respectively. Acute Plasmodium infection produces high plasma levels of AHR ligands from haem (bilirubin, biliverdin) and Trp metabolism, providing the endogenous agonist supply for AHR-mediated endothelial protection.

## Intuition

Plasmodium causes pathology partly through haemolysis-released pigments (haem → biliverdin → bilirubin), all of which engage AHR. Endothelial AHR signalling is the protective compartment for parasite resistance and vascular integrity.

## Key variants

- P. berghei ANKA — experimental cerebral malaria
- P. chabaudi — blood-stage chronic malaria
- P. falciparum — human-relevant severe malaria

## Known limitations

- Mouse models incompletely recapitulate human falciparum pathology
- Drug resistance to artemisinin combinations is emerging

## Open problems

- Whether endothelial-targeted AHR agonists ameliorate severe cerebral malaria in humans
- Identification of parasite-derived AHR ligands (none described to date)
