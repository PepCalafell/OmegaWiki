## Definition

PDPK1 (PDK1) is a master serine/threonine kinase of the AGC kinase family that phosphorylates the activation loops of many downstream AGC kinases — including AKT, S6K, RSK, SGK, and several PKC isoforms — thereby acting as a central node of PI3K-dependent signaling.

## Intuition

PDK1 is the "kinase of kinases": by phosphorylating activation loops it licenses a whole tier of AGC effectors. Its position downstream of PI3K and upstream of AKT/PKC links growth-factor signaling to survival, metabolism, and polarization.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *PDPK1*; kinase domain + C-terminal PH domain (PIP₃ binding)
- Substrates: AKT (T308), PKCα (T497), S6K, RSK, SGK activation loops
- PIP₃ produced by PI3K co-localizes PDK1 with AKT at the membrane

## Key variants

- PH-domain mutants that disrupt PIP₃-dependent membrane recruitment
- PDK1 inhibitors explored in oncology

## Known limitations

- Constitutively active enzyme regulated mainly by substrate co-localization, so activity is hard to read from PDK1 phosphorylation alone
- Broad substrate set complicates pathway-specific attribution

## Open problems

- Contribution of PDK1 to macrophage immunosuppressive polarization via PKCα/AKT
- Therapeutic window for PDK1 inhibition in macrophage reprogramming

## Relevance to active research

Phosphoproteomic footprinting implicated PDPK1 (e.g. T513) as a kinase with higher activity in immunosuppressive M2a human macrophages; PDPK1 is the activation-loop kinase for PKCα and is reported to downregulate the mTOR pathway and promote M2-like polarization, placing it in the central M2 signaling network.
