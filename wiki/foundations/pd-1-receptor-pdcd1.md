## Definition

PD-1 (gene PDCD1, surface CD279) is an inhibitory immune-checkpoint receptor expressed on activated T cells, B cells, NK cells, and some myeloid populations. Engagement by its ligands PD-L1 (CD274) and PD-L2 (PDCD1LG2) recruits SHP-1/2 phosphatases via ITIM/ITSM motifs and dampens TCR signaling — a central mechanism of peripheral tolerance and tumor immune evasion. Therapeutic blockade (nivolumab, pembrolizumab, cemiplimab) restores anti-tumor T-cell function and is FDA-approved across many cancers.

## Intuition

The canonical T-cell "off-switch" exploited by tumors. PD-1 expression is induced upon TCR activation; in the tumor microenvironment, chronic stimulation drives sustained PD-1 expression that overlaps with exhaustion but is also a marker of recent activation. Anti-PD-1 antibodies are first-line systemic therapy across many solid tumors.

## Key variants

- Membrane PD-1 (full-length receptor)
- Soluble PD-1 (sPD-1, shed/spliced; less clinically used)
- PD-1hi T cells (sustained / exhaustion-skewed; mark therapeutic-target population)
- TCF1+ PD-1hi CD8 progenitor T cells (ICB-response-associated)

## Known limitations

- PD-1 expression marks both recently activated and exhausted T cells; biological interpretation is context-dependent.
- Anti-PD-1 monotherapy works in only ~20-30% of patients in most indications.

## Open problems

- Why some PD-1hi populations are reactivatable by ICB and others are terminally exhausted.
- Optimal combination partners (anti-CTLA-4, anti-LAG-3, anti-TIM-3, anti-TIGIT).
