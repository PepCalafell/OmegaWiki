## Definition

Candida albicans is a dimorphic fungus that exists as a commensal on mucosal surfaces but causes opportunistic disease (oropharyngeal, vulvovaginal, and disseminated candidiasis) in immunocompromised hosts. C. albicans induces AHR-dependent SRC-EGFR-driven epithelial endocytosis (oral mucosa); in contrast, AHR is protective in vulvovaginal candidiasis via IL-22 production.

## Intuition

C. albicans is the prototypical tissue-context-divergent pathogen for AHR biology: protective AHR in one mucosa, pathogenic AHR in another. The decisive factor is which AHR co-factors are available in the resident epithelial cell type.

## Key variants

- SC5314 reference strain (laboratory)
- Clinical isolates from immunocompromised patients

## Known limitations

- Azole-resistant Candida (e.g., C. auris) raises concern about generalisability of AHR-targeted approaches across Candida spp.

## Open problems

- How tissue context determines AHR pro- vs anti-fungal outcomes
- Whether topical CH-223191 could be efficacious in recurrent oropharyngeal candidiasis
