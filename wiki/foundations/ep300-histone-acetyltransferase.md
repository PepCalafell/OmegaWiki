## Definition
EP300 (p300) is a histone/protein acetyltransferase and transcriptional coactivator. It acetylates histones (e.g. H3K27ac at active enhancers/promoters) and numerous non-histone substrates (including transcription factors such as NF-κB and STAT proteins), and it bridges sequence-specific transcription factors to the basal transcription machinery. It is closely related to its paralog CBP (CREBBP).

## Intuition
EP300 is a hub coactivator: by writing acetyl marks on chromatin and on transcription factors, it both opens/marks active regulatory regions and tunes the activity of immune signaling factors. Because it acts broadly, its loss has wide-ranging and sometimes counterintuitive effects (e.g. de-repressing certain interferon-stimulated genes).

## Formal notation
Catalytic HAT (writer) domain plus a bromodomain (acetyl-lysine reader). Pharmacologically separable: A-485 inhibits the catalytic site; SGC-CBP30 targets the bromodomain reader.

## Key variants
EP300 vs paralog CBP/CREBBP; reader-domain vs writer-domain inhibition (distinct phenotypes); histone vs non-histone (NF-κB/STAT) acetylation roles.

## Known limitations
Pleiotropy makes phenotype interpretation difficult; reader- vs writer-domain inhibition can give divergent effects on specific genes (e.g. Spp1).

## Open problems
Whether EP300's repression of macrophage ISGs is mediated by histone acetylation, non-histone acetylation, or balancing HDAC/BRD4 availability.

## Relevance to active research
Identified as a constrainer of the interferon response in macrophages, validated by CRISPR knockout and small-molecule inhibition in [[papers/integrated-time-series-analysis-high-content]].
