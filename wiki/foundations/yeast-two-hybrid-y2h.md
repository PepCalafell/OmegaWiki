## Definition

Yeast two-hybrid (Y2H) is a genetic assay for detecting binary protein–protein
interactions. A "bait" protein is fused to a DNA-binding domain and a "prey" to a
transcription-activation domain; physical interaction reconstitutes a functional
transcription factor that drives a reporter gene in yeast.

## Intuition

Interaction = reporter ON. By screening one protein against an ORFeome library, one
can map a binary interactome at proteome scale.

## Formal notation

Assay sensitivity is benchmarked against positive (PRS) and random (RRS) reference
sets. yN2H is a nanoluciferase variant used as an orthogonal validation assay measuring
biophysical interaction quality.

## Key variants

yN2H (nanoluciferase readout), array vs pooled screening formats, multi-assay
mapping pipelines combining Y2H with orthogonal assays.

## Known limitations

Moderate sensitivity (~13–17.5% in this context); prokaryotic proteins can be harder
to test, requiring tailored reference sets. Misses interactions needing post-translational
modification or specific cellular context.

## Relevance to active research

Workhorse for systematic interactome mapping, including human reference interactome
(HuRI) and microbiome–host meta-interactome (HuMMI) projects.
