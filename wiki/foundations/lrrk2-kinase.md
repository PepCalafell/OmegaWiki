## Definition

LRRK2 is a large multidomain protein harboring both a GTPase (ROC-COR) and a serine/threonine kinase domain. It regulates vesicular trafficking, autophagy, and inflammatory responses, and its activating mutations are the most common genetic cause of familial Parkinson's disease.

## Intuition

LRRK2 couples a GTPase module to a kinase module, acting as a signaling rheostat in membrane trafficking and immune cells. In myeloid cells it modulates inflammatory responses, linking it to both neurodegeneration and inflammatory bowel disease.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *LRRK2*; domains: ARM–ANK–LRR–ROC–COR–kinase–WD40
- Substrates: a subset of Rab GTPases (e.g. Rab8a, Rab10)
- Pathogenic mutations (e.g. G2019S) increase kinase activity

## Key variants

- Risk/pathogenic alleles: G2019S, R1441C/G, etc.
- LRRK2 kinase inhibitors in clinical development for Parkinson's disease

## Known limitations

- Physiological substrate repertoire still incompletely mapped
- Functions span multiple organelles and cell types, complicating mechanism

## Open problems

- Role of LRRK2 in human macrophage immunosuppressive programming
- Whether its association with inflammation reflects a targetable macrophage axis

## Relevance to active research

Inferred by phosphoproteomic footprinting as a kinase with higher activity in immunosuppressive (M2a) human macrophages and flagged as a central regulator and possible reprogramming target, consistent with its documented role in regulating inflammatory responses.
