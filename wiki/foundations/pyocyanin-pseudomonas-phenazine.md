## Definition

Pyocyanin is a blue-green phenazine pigment produced by Pseudomonas aeruginosa at late exponential / stationary growth phase. It directly binds AHR and acts as an agonist (direct ligand binding ✓, competitive binding ✓; Table 1 of Barreira-Silva 2025). PYO and the related 1-hydroxyphenazine, phenazine-1-carboxamide, and phenazine-1-carboxylic acid are the AHR-activating arm of the Pseudomonas QS chemistry.

## Intuition

PYO is the iconic late-phase Pseudomonas virulence-factor pigment; its engagement of AHR signals the host that the bacterium has reached high density / virulence and should be cleared.

## Key variants

- Pyocyanin (PYO) — the prototypical AHR-activating phenazine
- 1-hydroxyphenazine (1-HP)
- Phenazine-1-carboxamide (PCN)
- Phenazine-1-carboxylic acid (PCA)

## Known limitations

- PYO is also a redox-active toxin; AHR-engagement studies must control for AHR-independent oxidative-stress effects
- Strain-to-strain variability in PYO production levels

## Open problems

- Whether PYO-mimetic AHR agonists could be designed as anti-Pseudomonas host-directed therapeutics
- AHR-mediated PYO degradation in epithelium — pharmacokinetic constraints
