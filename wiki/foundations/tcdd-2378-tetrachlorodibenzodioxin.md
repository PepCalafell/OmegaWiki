## Definition

TCDD is a halogenated polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon environmental pollutant; the prototypical high-affinity AHR agonist used since the 1970s to define AHR biology. Owing to its metabolic stability it produces prolonged AHR activation, sustained ARNT heterodimerisation, and strong XRE/DRE-driven transcription.

## Intuition

TCDD's planar, halogenated structure fits the AHR PAS-B ligand pocket tightly and is poorly metabolised by CYP1A1, so it overruns the AHR negative-feedback loop and produces persistent, often toxic, AHR responses.

## Key variants

- TCDD — type compound, dioxin family
- Related dioxin-like compounds: PeCDF, PCBs, hexachlorobenzene
- Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs): benzo[a]pyrene, 3-methylcholanthrene

## Known limitations

- Toxic (teratogenic, carcinogenic) — unusable as a drug
- Used in research only at controlled doses; off-target effects via prolonged AHR activation complicate mechanistic interpretation

## Open problems

- How TCDD bias toward Treg vs Th17 polarisation depends on dose and timing
- Whether TCDD-like sustained activation differs mechanistically from transient activation by FICZ/ITE beyond ligand pharmacokinetics

## Relevance to active research

Cited extensively in [[papers/aryl-hydrocarbon-receptor-rehabilitated-target-therapeutic]] as the historical reason AHR agonists were avoided in drug development, and as the experimental tool that first showed AHR-driven Foxp3⁺ Treg expansion (Quintana 2008).
