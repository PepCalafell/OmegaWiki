## Definition

O-GlcNAcylation is a reversible post-translational modification in which a single N-acetylglucosamine sugar is attached via an O-glycosidic bond to serine/threonine residues of intracellular proteins. It is written by [[foundations/ogt-glcnac-transferase]] (OGT) and erased by OGA, cycling far faster than the once-and-done glycosylation of the secretory pathway.

## Intuition

Unlike extracellular N-/O-glycosylation, O-GlcNAcylation is dynamic and competes with phosphorylation for the same or nearby Ser/Thr sites — a "yin-yang" crosstalk that lets nutrient state tune signalling, transcription, and cell-cycle proteins. Its donor sugar [[foundations/udp-glcnac-uridine-diphosphate-acetylglucosamine]] integrates glucose, amino-acid, fatty-acid, and nucleotide metabolism, so global O-GlcNAc levels report cellular nutrient abundance.

## Formal notation

Ser/Thr-OH ⇌ Ser/Thr-O-GlcNAc (OGT adds, OGA removes).

## Key variants

- Found on thousands of nuclear, cytoplasmic, and mitochondrial proteins involved in cell-cycle control, metabolism, and nutrient sensing.
- Site-specific O-GlcNAcylation of transcription factors and cell-cycle regulators modulates their stability and activity.

## Known limitations

- Site-mapping is technically demanding; functional consequences of individual sites are often unresolved.
- Global perturbation (OGT/OGA loss or inhibition) cannot isolate the responsible substrate.

## Open problems

- How dynamic O-GlcNAc cycling is choreographed across a differentiation trajectory (e.g. monocyte→tissue-resident macrophage).

## Relevance to active research

[[papers/glcnacylation-shapes-macrophage-tissue-residency-alternative]] reports O-GlcNAcylation as a central regulator of alternatively activated macrophages and macrophage tissue residency, dynamically modulated during the monocyte–to–large-cavity-macrophage transition. UDP-GlcNAc (the donor) is already known to be enriched in alternatively activated macrophages.
