## Definition

Indole-3-aldehyde (I3Ald) is a microbiota-derived tryptophan metabolite that activates host AHR. L. reuteri and Lactobacillus johnsonii are documented producers in the gut. I3Ald-driven AHR engagement induces IL-22 production from ILC3s and CD4+ T cells, promoting mucosal immune homeostasis. I3Ald also ameliorates vulvovaginal candidiasis via IL-22/IL-18 crosstalk.

## Intuition

I3Ald is one of the most-studied "good" microbial AHR ligands — a microbiota-derived signal that sustains barrier IL-22 production without dioxin-like toxicity. It is a prototype for AHR-targeting probiotic strategies.

## Key variants

- L. reuteri-derived I3Ald
- L. johnsonii-derived I3Ald
- Synthetic I3Ald for research

## Known limitations

- Bioavailability of orally administered I3Ald is limited
- Strain-specific production levels vary widely

## Open problems

- Whether engineered indole-producing probiotics standardised on I3Ald yields can be developed for IBD
- Pharmacokinetic profiles of I3Ald in human gut
