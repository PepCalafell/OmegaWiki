## Definition

PML is a tumor-suppressor protein and the scaffolding component of PML nuclear bodies — membraneless nuclear organelles that organize responses to stress, DNA damage, senescence, apoptosis, and antiviral/interferon signaling. PML is the fusion partner of RARA in acute promyelocytic leukemia.

## Intuition

PML nucleates nuclear bodies that act as hubs concentrating and post-translationally modifying partner proteins, coupling stress and interferon signaling to cell-fate decisions. Its phosphorylation status modulates nuclear-body dynamics and downstream outputs.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *PML*; TRIM/RBCC architecture (RING, B-boxes, coiled-coil)
- Forms PML nuclear bodies; heavily SUMOylated and phosphorylated
- Linked to the IFN-γ signaling pathway and to interferon-stimulated responses

## Key variants

- Multiple PML isoforms (I–VII) from alternative splicing
- Oncogenic PML-RARA fusion (target of ATRA/arsenic therapy in APL)

## Known limitations

- Complex isoform- and PTM-dependent functions resist simple readouts
- Nuclear-body composition is dynamic and context-specific

## Open problems

- Functional consequence of PML hyperphosphorylation in proinflammatory macrophages
- Whether PML nuclear-body dynamics gate interferon responses in macrophage states

## Relevance to active research

Among the most differentially phosphorylated proteins between macrophage states, PML carried 12 phosphoresidues with higher phosphorylation in the proinflammatory M1 state versus both M2a and M2c, consistent with its link to IFN-γ signaling.
