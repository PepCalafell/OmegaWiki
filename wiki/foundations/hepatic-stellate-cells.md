## Definition

Hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) are perisinusoidal mesenchymal cells that store retinoids (vitamin A) in cytoplasmic lipid droplets in the healthy liver and transdifferentiate into α-smooth-muscle-actin (Acta2)-positive, collagen-producing myofibroblasts upon liver injury. Activation is the central driver of liver fibrosis.

## Intuition

In a healthy liver, HSCs are quiescent and produce decorin (Dcn) and biglycan (Bgn), proteoglycans that sequester TGFβ and dampen pro-fibrogenic signaling. Chronic injury (alcohol, viral hepatitis, MASH, schistosomiasis) overrides this dampening, driving HSC activation, ECM deposition, sinusoidal capillarization, and progression to cirrhosis. NiCo (Agrawal 2024) identifies a homeostatic feedback loop where Kupffer-cell-derived Tgfb1 induces HSC Dcn, which then sequesters Tgfβ and prevents premature activation.

## Key markers

- Quiescent: Reln, Dcn, Bgn, Lhx2, Lrat, GFAP, retinyl-ester droplets, Pdgfra.
- Activated (myofibroblast): Acta2 (αSMA), Col1a1, Lox, Pdgfrb, Timp1, vimentin.

## Known limitations

- Activation states are reversible; subpopulation taxonomy still under refinement.
- Many "stellate-like" populations in other organs (pancreatic stellate, lung myofibroblasts) share markers but distinct origins.

## Relevance to active research

Central target for anti-fibrotic therapy; NiCo's Tgfb1–Dcn feedback hypothesis offers a homeostatic-signaling explanation for stellate quiescence in normal liver.
