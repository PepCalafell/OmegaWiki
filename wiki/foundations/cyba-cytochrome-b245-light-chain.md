## Definition

CYBA encodes p22phox, the light (alpha) chain of cytochrome b-245, an essential membrane component of the phagocyte NADPH oxidase (NOX2 complex). Together with gp91phox (CYBB) it forms the catalytic core that generates superoxide and reactive oxygen species (ROS) for microbial killing.

## Intuition

CYBA is part of the "respiratory burst" machinery: phagocytes use the NADPH oxidase to produce ROS that kill ingested pathogens. Loss-of-function mutations cripple this burst; altered expression shifts the ROS set-point of myeloid cells in barrier tissues.

## Formal notation

Membrane-bound p22phox heterodimerizes with gp91phox to form flavocytochrome b558, the redox core of NOX2.

## Key variants

- Autosomal-recessive chronic granulomatous disease (CGD) caused by CYBA mutations.
- Expression skewed toward monocytes/phagocytes.

## Known limitations

- ROS output depends on the whole NOX2 complex, not CYBA alone.
- Expression is not a direct readout of oxidase activity.

## Open problems

- Tissue-specific consequences of CYBA up/down-regulation in inflammatory disease.
- Whether circulating CYBA levels predict barrier-tissue disease subtype.

## Relevance to active research

Emerged as a top disease-discriminative gene in monocytes: high CYBA expression classifies intestinal IMIDs (UC, CD) whereas reduced CYBA classifies skin-related IMIDs (PS, PSA), linking the phagocyte ROS set-point to barrier-tissue disease localization.
