## Definition

CIITA is the master transcriptional coactivator that controls expression of MHC class II genes (HLA-DR/-DP/-DQ in human; H2-Aa/H2-Ab1/H2-Eb1 in mouse) and the invariant chain CD74. It does not bind DNA directly but is recruited to the conserved SXY module by the RFX complex, CREB, and NF-Y, acting as the rate-limiting switch for antigen presentation to CD4+ T cells.

## Intuition

MHC-II positivity of a macrophage — its capacity to present antigen — is essentially set by how much CIITA is on. Signals that raise CIITA (e.g. GM-CSF, IFN-γ) make macrophages MHC-II-high antigen presenters; signals that shut CIITA at the chromatin level (e.g. lactic acid + PGE2) drive the mutually exclusive MHC-II-low angiogenic state.

## Formal notation

Not applicable — a coactivator scored by MHC-II / CD74 target-gene expression and by chromatin accessibility at the CIITA locus and MHC-II promoters.

## Key variants

- Four promoters (pI, pII, pIII, pIV) drive cell-type-specific and IFN-γ-inducible CIITA isoforms.
- pIV is the interferon-γ-responsive promoter central to inducible MHC-II.

## Known limitations

- CIITA level does not capture post-transcriptional or peptide-loading regulation of surface MHC-II.
- Epigenetic silencing of the locus can uncouple CIITA from acute cytokine signals.

## Open problems

- How metabolic inputs (lactate, prostaglandins) are transduced to CIITA-locus chromatin closure.
- Reversibility of CIITA silencing when reprogramming angiogenic TAMs toward antigen presentation.

## Relevance to active research

CIITA/MHC-II is one pole of the angiogenic-versus-MHC-II TAM axis. Its accessibility is high in MHC-II+ TAMs and completely closed in angiogenic TAMs, making it a chromatin-level marker of the polarization state that LGP factors control.
