## Definition

A multifactorial wasting syndrome occurring in patients with cancer, defined by involuntary loss of body weight (and especially lean/skeletal muscle mass), progressive functional impairment, and a maladaptive metabolic component that cannot be reversed by conventional nutritional support. Reduced food intake (anorexia) is one component but not the sole driver. Consensus diagnostic criteria typically include weight loss > 5% over 6 months, low BMI with weight loss, or sarcopenia with weight loss.

## Intuition

Cachexia is the metabolic-syndrome dimension of late-stage cancer: a coordinated, tumour-driven reprogramming of host metabolism in muscle, liver, adipose and circulation that produces wasting independent of caloric intake. It is one of the strongest single predictors of cancer mortality.

## Formal notation

- Disease stages: pre-cachexia (early metabolic alterations, ≤5% BW loss) → cachexia (≥5% BW loss + sarcopenia / low BMI / appetite-and-inflammation features) → refractory cachexia (anti-cancer therapy unresponsive).
- Hallmark inflammatory drivers: IL6, TNF, IL1B (cytokine cocktail).
- Hallmark metabolic features: amino-acid depletion in plasma; one-carbon metabolism activation across host tissues; muscle proteolysis (ubiquitin-proteasome, autophagy); adipose lipolysis; hepatic acute-phase protein synthesis.
- Diagnostic codes: ICD-10 R64; common in pancreatic, gastric, lung, colorectal cancers.

## Key variants

- C26 mouse model (colon carcinoma); LLC (Lewis lung carcinoma); ApcMin (genetic, polyp-driven, chronic); KPP and Panc02/8025 (PDAC variants); SW480 (humanised xenograft cachexia).
- Patient subtypes: sarcopenic obesity, fast-progressing pancreatic cachexia, indolent CRC cachexia.

## Known limitations

- No FDA-approved cachexia-specific therapy with broad efficacy; anamorelin (ghrelin agonist) approved in some jurisdictions for NSCLC cachexia.
- Animal models (C26, LLC) develop cachexia faster and more uniformly than patients, limiting translation.
- Lack of validated early biomarkers for pre-cachexia detection.

## Open problems

- Whether one-carbon metabolism activation is a biomarker, driver or therapeutic target (the central question of [[papers/multi-omics-profiling-cachexia-targeted-tissues]]).
- Sex- and age-dependent variation in cachexia trajectories.
- Whether combining anti-IL6/anti-IL6R with metabolic interventions (MAT inhibition, methionine-restricted diet, glutaminase inhibition) outperforms either alone.
