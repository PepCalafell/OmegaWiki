## Definition

IRF2 is an interferon regulatory factor classically described as a transcriptional repressor that antagonizes IRF1 at shared ISRE elements, fine-tuning interferon and immune gene output.

## Intuition

By competing with IRF1, IRF2 restrains or redirects interferon-stimulated transcription, and its preferential activity can demarcate a distinct interferon-response cell state.

## Formal notation

IRF2 binds ISRE/IRF-E motifs and represses (or context-dependently modulates) IRF1-driven transcription; the IRF1:IRF2 ratio sets net ISG activation.

## Key variants

- Context-dependent activator roles for select IRF2 targets.

## Known limitations

- Regulon-activity inference is associative; repressor-vs-activator role is context-specific.

## Open problems

- Whether IRF2 actively specifies the IFIT1+/IFN-I TAM state.

## Relevance to active research

Identified as the TF preferentially active in IFIT1+ (IFN-I) TAMs, antagonistic to IRF1 in CXCL9+ (IFN-γ) TAMs, supporting distinct transcriptional programs for the two interferon-related macrophage states.
