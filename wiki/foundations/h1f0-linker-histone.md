## Definition

H1F0 (gene symbol H1F0, protein H1.0) is a replication-independent linker histone H1 variant. It is enriched in non-dividing or slowly dividing cells, contributes to chromatin compaction, and marks quiescent and terminally differentiated states across multiple tissues. In hematopoietic stem cells it is a quiescence-associated marker visible at the protein level.

## Intuition

Linker histones bind to the nucleosome dyad and to linker DNA, stabilizing higher-order chromatin compaction. H1F0 specifically accumulates in cells with low replication / transcription activity — making it a useful proxy for HSC quiescence on the protein layer.

## Formal notation

H1F0 protein: ~21 kDa, structured globular domain plus C-terminal tail. In [[papers/mapping-early-human-blood-cell-differentiation]], H1F0 abundance correlated with HSC identity at the protein level and CRISPR/Cas9 KO in CD34+ BM cells reduced LTC-IC frequency to 1/80 vs 1/14 in AAVS1 control.

## Key variants

- H1F0 (H1.0) — replication-independent.
- H1.1-H1.5 (HIST1H1A-E) — replication-coupled.
- H1.10 (H1FOO) — oocyte-specific.

## Known limitations

- Bulk-level changes are small relative to noise; scRNA-seq often misses H1F0 dynamics.
- Functional KO has cell-type-dependent effects.

## Open problems

- Mechanism by which H1F0 loss reduces long-term hematopoietic potential — chromatin accessibility shifts? Loss of quiescence?
- Therapeutic relevance in myeloid disease.

## Relevance to active research

- [[papers/mapping-early-human-blood-cell-differentiation]] — H1F0 identified as a top HSC quiescence marker on protein level; CRISPR KO reduces LTC-IC frequency.
