## Definition

¹⁸F-FMISO (fluoromisonidazole) is a 2-nitroimidazole radiotracer used in positron emission tomography (PET) to visualize and quantify tumor hypoxia in vivo. The nitroimidazole moiety is reduced selectively in hypoxic cells; under low pO₂, the nitro-radical is irreversibly bound to intracellular macromolecules, trapping the ¹⁸F label.

## Intuition

FMISO is the most clinically validated hypoxia PET tracer. Tumor-to-background ratios are modest (≈1.3–1.5 at standard imaging delay), so threshold calibration is critical. Used in head-and-neck cancer, glioma, NSCLC, cervical, hepatocellular carcinoma, and breast-cancer brain metastases (Table 2 of the review). Complementary tracers: ¹⁸F-HX4, ¹⁸F-EF5, ⁶⁴Cu-ATSM.

## Formal notation

- Chemical: 1-(2-nitro-1H-imidazol-1-yl)-3-[¹⁸F]fluoro-2-propanol
- Activation pO₂ threshold: <10 mmHg
- Imaging delay: 2–4 hours post-injection
- Half-life of ¹⁸F: 109.8 minutes

## Key variants

- ¹⁸F-FMISO — most clinical evidence
- ¹⁸F-HX4 — better pharmacokinetics, faster clearance
- ¹⁸F-EF5 — pentafluorinated, used in HNSCC
- ⁶⁴Cu-ATSM — non-nitroimidazole, distinct trapping mechanism

## Known limitations

- Low tumor-to-background ratio.
- Cannot distinguish chronic from cycling hypoxia (kinetic analysis required).
- Pseudohypoxic VHL-mutant tumors may be FMISO-low despite HIF activation.

## Open problems

- Prospective evidence that FMISO-guided dose painting improves outcomes.
- Concordance with mRNA hypoxia signatures (Buffa, Winter).
- Use as biomarker for HAP / belzutifan trials.

## Relevance to active research

In [[papers/hypoxic-microenvironment-cancer-molecular-mechanisms-therapeutic]], FMISO is one of three FDA-approved hypoxia tracers featured in the clinical-trial table for monitoring HAP / HIF-inhibitor response. For thesis work, FMISO + mRNA hypoxia signatures + spatial-WGS would form a multimodal hypoxia biomarker panel.
