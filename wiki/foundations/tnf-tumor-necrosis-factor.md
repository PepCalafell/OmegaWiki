## Definition

TNF is a pleiotropic pro-inflammatory cytokine signalling through TNFR1 (ubiquitous) and TNFR2 (restricted), activating NF-κB, MAPK and caspase-mediated death pathways. Produced mainly by macrophages early in infection and tissue injury.

## Intuition

In sepsis, TNF peaks in blood within 2 h of insult and is the proximal "alarm" cytokine; in chronic inflammation it sits at the apex of multiple cytokine hierarchies, explaining the broad clinical success of anti-TNF therapies across psoriasis, IBD, RA and AS — and their narrower failure in late-stage sepsis.

## Key variants

- Soluble TNF (cleaved by TACE/ADAM17)
- Membrane-bound TNF (mTNF; signals preferentially via TNFR2)

## Relevance to active research

TNF is the central node of the pairwise-cytokine hierarchy proposed by Takahama et al. 2024 for organism-wide sepsis response; its early kinetics underlie why TNF neutralization works as prophylaxis but not as treatment in sepsis trials.
