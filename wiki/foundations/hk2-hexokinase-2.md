## Definition

HK2 catalyses the first step of glycolysis (glucose → glucose-6-phosphate). It is the dominant hexokinase isoform in many tumours, contributes to the Warburg effect via mitochondrial-outer-membrane localisation, and has been identified as an AHR target gene that itself drives AHR promoter demethylation — creating a positive-feedback loop between AHR activity and cancer glycolysis.

## Intuition

A glycolytic enzyme that doubles as an epigenetic actor: HK2 expression reinforces AHR signalling by demethylating the AHR promoter, linking AHR pathway activity to the metabolic state of the tumour.

## Formal notation

- Reaction: glucose + ATP → glucose-6-P + ADP.
- Localisation: bound to mitochondrial VDAC via N-terminal hydrophobic anchor; protects against apoptosis.
- AHR target: induced by AHR ligands; in turn promotes AHR promoter demethylation in multiple cancers, with negative correlation between AHR promoter methylation and HK2 expression linked to worse overall survival.

## Key variants

- HK1, HK3, HK4 (glucokinase) — other hexokinase isoforms with distinct tissue distributions.

## Known limitations

- HK2 inhibitors have limited selectivity vs HK1.

## Open problems

- Mechanism by which HK2 expression drives AHR promoter demethylation (metabolite-driven? TET recruitment?).
- Therapeutic exploitation of the AHR-HK2 positive-feedback loop.

## Relevance to active research

AHR-driven cancer metabolism; epigenetic-metabolic feedback in tumours. See [[claims/hk2-ahr-target-promotes-ahr-promoter-demethylation]].
