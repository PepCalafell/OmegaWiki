## Definition

Short-chain fatty acids — acetate (C2), propionate (C3), and butyrate (C4) — are produced by gut microbiota through fermentation of dietary fibre. They signal through the GPCRs GPR41 (FFAR3), GPR43 (FFAR2), and GPR109A, and directly inhibit histone deacetylases (HDACs) at physiological intestinal concentrations.

## Intuition

The microbiota's most abundant signalling output — couples dietary fibre to systemic immune state.

## Key variants

- Acetate — promotes Breg/IgA, ambivalent macrophage effects, antiviral GPR43-STING.
- Propionate — M1 suppressor, anti-asthma in airway macrophages.
- Butyrate — strongest HDAC inhibitor, drives M2 polarization, Treg function, plasma B cells.

## Known limitations

- In-vivo SCFA concentrations vary by site (cecum → blood → tissue) by orders of magnitude.
- "Dose response" often differs between cell types (acetate enhances M2 in vitro but abrogates M2 in asthma).

## Open problems

- Cell-type-resolved SCFA sensors (GPR43 vs HDAC) for selective immunomodulation.

## Relevance to active research

Central to gut-immune axis, IBD, autoimmunity, and vaccine adjuvancy.
