## Definition

E6 and E7 are the principal oncoproteins of high-risk human papillomaviruses (HPV16, HPV18, etc.). E6 promotes ubiquitin-mediated degradation of p53 (via E6-AP); E7 binds and inactivates pRb, releasing E2F. Together, E6/E7 functionally inactivate the two main cell-cycle gatekeepers (p53 and pRb) without requiring mutations.

## Intuition

In HPV+ tumours (cervical, anal, oropharyngeal HNSCC, some skin SCC), the cell-cycle brakes are removed by viral proteins rather than by mutation. This produces highly proliferative tumours with intact TP53 and RB1 genes at the DNA level — a major diagnostic and therapeutic distinction from HPV− counterparts.

## Functional consequences

- pRb degradation → loss of G1/S restraint → strong **G1/S phase bias** in HPV+ tumours (3CA pan-cancer finding).
- p53 inactivation → impaired DNA-damage response.
- CDKN2A (p16) overexpression as compensatory feedback — used as a clinical surrogate for HPV+ status (e.g. in HNSCC).
- HPV+ tumours generally show better prognosis and ICB response than HPV− counterparts.

## Variants

- HPV16 E6/E7 — most common in oropharyngeal HNSCC and cervical cancer.
- HPV18 E6/E7 — frequent in cervical adenocarcinoma.
- Low-risk HPV E6/E7 (HPV6, HPV11) — much weaker p53/pRb binding, non-oncogenic.

## Known limitations

- E6/E7 expression cannot be inferred from standard DNA-mutation pipelines — requires RNA or HPV-specific assays.
- p16 staining is a surrogate, imperfect for HPV+ status.
- HPV-driven tumours can develop additional driver mutations that complicate the "wild-type TP53/RB1" picture.

## Open problems

- Why some HPV+ tumours still progress despite intact immune recognition.
- Whether the G1/S phase bias predicts CDK4/6 inhibitor response in HPV+ HNSCC.

## Relevance to active research

In 3CA v2 ([[curated-cancer-cell-atlas-provides-comprehensive]]), HPV+ HNSCC is the most proliferative cancer type (>45% cycling cells) and exhibits **opposite** phase bias from HPV− HNSCC, explained mechanistically by E7-driven pRb degradation. CDKN2A (p16) emerges as an HPV+ HNSCC malignant marker.
