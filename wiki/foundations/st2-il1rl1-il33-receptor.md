## Definition

ST2, encoded by Il1rl1, is the receptor for the alarmin cytokine IL-33. Signalling through ST2 (with the IL-1RAcP co-receptor) drives type-2 responses and contributes to the differentiation, self-renewal and tissue-repair functions of resident macrophages.

## Intuition

ST2 lets macrophages sense IL-33 released by damaged or stromal cells; through it, tissue cues are translated into resident-macrophage maturation and alternative activation. Loss of surface ST2 blunts the IL-33 arm of macrophage education while leaving IL-4Rα signalling comparatively intact.

## Formal notation

IL-33 + ST2/IL-1RAcP → MyD88 → NF-κB / MAPK.

## Key variants

- Membrane ST2 (signalling) vs soluble ST2 (decoy).

## Known limitations

- ST2 contribution to the residency transition appears tissue-specific (more relevant in peritoneum than universally).

## Open problems

- Whether reduced ST2 is a cause or correlate of the RTM differentiation block.

## Relevance to active research

Il1rl1 is among the transcripts whose ribosome engagement depends on hypusinated eIF5A; reduced ST2 protein in DHPS-deficient macrophages impairs IL-33-driven alternative activation and may contribute to the peritoneal monocyte-to-RTM defect.
