## Definition

Wnt3 is a secreted lipid-modified glycoprotein of the canonical Wnt family that binds Frizzled receptors (notably Fzd5, Fzd7) and LRP5/6 co-receptors to stabilize β-catenin, drive TCF/LEF-mediated transcription, and sustain proliferation/self-renewal of stem cells. In the small-intestinal crypt, Wnt3 is produced by Paneth cells and is essential for Lgr5+ intestinal stem-cell maintenance.

## Intuition

The canonical "stem-cell-niche Wnt": short-range, lipid-tethered, requires direct Paneth–stem contact. Loss of Wnt3 collapses crypt stemness unless compensated by exogenous Wnt or by Wnt2b from underlying stroma.

## Known limitations

- Soluble Wnt3 activity is hard to reconstitute in vitro because of lipid modification; afamin or detergent stabilization is needed.

## Relevance to active research

NiCo (Agrawal 2024) predicts Wnt3 (Paneth) — Fzd7 (stem/TA) as the canonical ligand-receptor pair underlying the Paneth–stem covariation in mouse small-intestinal MERFISH data.
