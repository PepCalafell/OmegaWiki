## Definition

SALT is a 1983 conceptual framework proposed by Streilein that posited the skin as an integrated immune compartment combining epidermal Langerhans cells, dermal lymphocytes, perivascular T-cell aggregates, vascular endothelium and draining lymph nodes — i.e. a peripheral lymphoid-tissue-like organ embedded in skin.

## Intuition

SALT predates molecular cytometry and was originally a hypothesis. It anticipated by decades the spatial discovery of perivascular CCL19+ fibroblast niches, FRC-like stromal scaffolds, and tertiary lymphoid structure formation in skin.

## Key variants

- SALT vs CLA (cutaneous lymphocyte antigen)-defined skin-homing T cell compartment.
- SALT vs canonical tertiary lymphoid structure (TLS).

## Known limitations

- The original SALT model lacked a defined stromal scaffold; until single-cell spatial methods, the physical correlate of SALT was undefined.

## Relevance to active research

- PERIVASC I in [[papers/single-cell-spatial-transcriptomic-analysis-human]] is proposed as the long-sought physical correlate of SALT.
