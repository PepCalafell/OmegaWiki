## Definition

LCA is a secondary bile acid produced from chenodeoxycholic acid by gut-microbial 7α-dehydroxylation. Subsequent microbial 3α/β-HSDH and 5α/β-reductase activity yield 3-oxo-, iso-, allo-, 3-oxoallo-, and isoalloLCA. Several derivatives act as immune-active ligands: 3-oxoLCA antagonizes RORγt (blocks Th17), isoalloLCA induces mitochondrial ROS and NR4A1-Foxp3 binding (promotes Treg), and 3-oxoLCA/isoLCA are TGR5 agonists (M2 macrophage).

## Intuition

A microbial chemistry "toolkit" — same parent scaffold, different stereochemistry / oxidation state, opposing immunological outcomes.

## Key variants

- 3-oxoLCA — RORγt antagonist (Th17 block)
- isoalloLCA — Treg inducer (NR4A1-Foxp3)
- isoLCA — TGR5 agonist (M2 macrophage)
- alloLCA — variable
- isoDCA — analogous DCA derivative (DC FXR-dependent Treg expansion)

## Known limitations

- Physiological human gut concentrations vary; clinical reproducibility uncertain.

## Open problems

- Engineered bile-acid-modifying microbial consortia for clinical Treg expansion.

## Relevance to active research

IBD therapeutics, gut-Treg engineering, autoimmunity, oncology (gut-immune-tumor axis).
