## Definition

Carbonic anhydrase IX (CA9 / CAIX) is a transmembrane zinc-metalloenzyme that catalyzes the reversible hydration of CO₂ to bicarbonate (CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCO₃⁻ + H⁺). It is one of the most strongly HIF1α-induced genes under hypoxia and a canonical immunohistochemical marker of tumor hypoxia. By exporting H⁺ at the cell surface, CAIX maintains intracellular pH in hypoxic cancer cells while acidifying the extracellular TME, contributing to immune suppression and chemoresistance.

## Intuition

In hypoxic cancer cells, glycolysis produces lactate and protons; CAIX (along with MCT1/4 and NHE1) dumps the acid load extracellularly, keeping intracellular pH near 7.2 while extracellular pH drops to 5.8–6.5. The acidic TME blunts CTL activation, blocks NK function, promotes M2 TAM polarization, and degrades the extracellular matrix to favor invasion. CAIX is also a serum biomarker for ccRCC and a direct target of small-molecule inhibitors (SLC-0111).

## Formal notation

- Gene: CA9 (chr9p13.3)
- HIF1α target via HRE in the proximal promoter
- Catalytic Zn²⁺-binding domain extracellular
- Substrate: CO₂ → HCO₃⁻ + H⁺ (proton export coupled to bicarbonate import)

## Key variants

- CAIX surface protein on tumor cells (classical hypoxia marker)
- Serum CAIX shed ectodomain (ccRCC biomarker)
- Small-molecule selective inhibitors: SLC-0111, U-104

## Known limitations

- Some tumors express CAIX constitutively (ccRCC via VHL loss → pseudohypoxia) regardless of true low O₂.
- Immunostaining intensity varies with fixation; mRNA scoring is more quantitative.
- Functional redundancy with CA12 in some tumors.

## Open problems

- Whether CAIX-targeted radioligands (antibody-drug conjugates, theragnostic imaging) outperform mRNA hypoxia signatures.
- Combination CAIX inhibitor + ICB to relieve TME acidosis.
