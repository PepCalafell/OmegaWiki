## Definition

A serine protease (encoded by PLAU) that cleaves plasminogen to plasmin upon binding its GPI-anchored receptor uPAR (PLAUR). Drives ECM remodelling, tumour-cell migration and invasion, and is a canonical HIF1α target gene. Recurrent across PDAC and other hypoxia-related prognostic signatures.

## Intuition

uPA-mediated pericellular proteolysis is the proximal step that lets hypoxic tumour and stromal cells degrade basement membrane and migrate; high PLAU expression marks invasive fronts and hypoxia-experienced TAMs.

## Formal notation

- Encoded at 10q22.2 in human.
- Secreted as pro-uPA, activated by cleavage to a two-chain form.
- HIF-responsive elements documented in PLAU promoter region.

## Key variants

- uPA / uPAR / PAI-1 axis with PLAUR (receptor) and SERPINE1 (PAI-1 inhibitor).
- Soluble vs membrane-bound (uPAR-bound) forms.

## Known limitations

- Pleiotropic: PLAU function depends on uPAR availability and on PAI-1 / PAI-2 balance.

## Open problems

- Tumour-cell PLAU vs TAM-derived PLAU contribution to bulk-tissue signatures is rarely deconvolved.

## Relevance to active research

PLAU is a recurring gene across PDAC hypoxia prognostic models (refs 27–29 in Ge 2025) and is one of 13 hub genes in the [[papers/development-hypoxia-responsive-macrophage-prognostic-model]] signature. Mechanistically it links the hypoxic-niche TAM transcriptome to invasive / metastatic biology.
