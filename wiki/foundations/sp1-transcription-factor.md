## Definition

SP1 (specificity protein 1) is a ubiquitously expressed zinc-finger transcription factor that binds GC-box / GC-rich promoter elements. Classically regarded as a constitutive "housekeeping" activator, SP1 also participates in inducible and cell-type-specific programs, including interferon-responsive and inflammatory gene regulation.

## Intuition

SP1 occupies GC-rich promoters of many genes; its functional output is shaped by post-translational modification, cofactor availability and chromatin context, so the same factor can drive heterogeneous, cell-type-specific target sets rather than a single fixed program.

## Formal notation

Binds the consensus GC box (5′-(G/T)GGGCGG(G/A)(G/A)(C/T)-3′) via three Cys2His2 zinc fingers.

## Key variants

- SP-family paralogs (SP2, SP3, SP4) with overlapping binding motifs.
- Phospho-/glycosylation-modified SP1 states altering activity.

## Known limitations

- Broad expression and motif make context-specific target assignment difficult.
- Regulon-based activity inference can conflate SP1 with GC-box-binding paralogs.

## Open problems

- How SP1 achieves cell-type-specific target selection in immune cells.
- Its causal role versus correlation in interferon-induced programs.

## Relevance to active research

Identified, alongside STAT1, as a primary regulator of the IFN-induced inflammation signature in circulating immune cells, with cell-type-specific and disease-specific (e.g. SLE flare) activity patterns distinct from canonical IFN signaling driven by [[stat1-tf]].
