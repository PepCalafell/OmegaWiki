## Definition

CTLA-4 is an inhibitory immune checkpoint receptor expressed on activated T cells and constitutively on Tregs. It binds CD80/CD86 with higher affinity than CD28, competing for co-stimulation and reducing T-cell activation. Antibody blockade (ipilimumab, tremelimumab) is FDA-approved for melanoma and combination ICI regimens.

## Intuition

If PD-1 brakes T cells at the effector site, CTLA-4 brakes them earlier (priming, in lymph nodes). Anti-CTLA-4 antibodies also deplete intratumoral Tregs via Fcγ-receptor-dependent ADCC in many preclinical models, contributing to efficacy.

## Key variants

- Membrane CTLA-4 (full length)
- Soluble CTLA-4 (alternative splicing)
- Anti-CTLA-4 antibody (clinical): ipilimumab (IgG1, ADCC-competent), tremelimumab (IgG2)
- Anti-CTLA-4 + anti-PD-1 combination — synergistic in melanoma, RCC, HCC
- Anti-CTLA-4 + MiTE-144 — preclinical synergy in MC38 (von Locquenghien 2025)

## Known limitations

- High immune-related adverse event rate (colitis, hypophysitis)
- Treg-depletion mechanism contested in patients (Fc-receptor engagement differs between mouse and human)
- Single-agent activity modest in many indications

## Relevance to active research

Repeatedly invoked as the canonical combinatorial partner for innovative TAM-targeted or cytokine-fusion therapeutics; in [[macrophage-targeted-immunocytokine-leverages-myeloid-nk]] the MiTE-144 + αCTLA-4 combination eradicated MC38 tumors in 6/7 mice.
