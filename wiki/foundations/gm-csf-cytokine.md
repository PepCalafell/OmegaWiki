## Definition

GM-CSF (CSF2) is a common-β-chain-family cytokine produced by T cells, macrophages, endothelial and stromal cells. It signals through CSF2Rα/βc → JAK2 → STAT5 and drives the production, survival, and activation of granulocytes, monocytes/macrophages, and dendritic cells. It is a key instructive signal for monocyte differentiation, including into inflammatory and tumor-associated macrophages.

## Intuition

GM-CSF is the myeloid mobilization/instruction cytokine. In tumors it is frequently overexpressed and recruits/differentiates monocytes into immunosuppressive tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs). In the Human Cytokine Dictionary its receptor expression and response are constrained largely to monocytes; huCIRA finds tumor-localized GM-CSF (and IL-3) activity reprogramming monocytes in NSCLC.

## Key variants

- Recombinant GM-CSF (sargramostim) — clinical myeloid support / vaccine adjuvant
- Anti-GM-CSF / anti-CSF2Rα antibodies (e.g. mavrilimumab) — inflammatory disease
- Shares βc with IL-3 and IL-5

## Known limitations

- Context-dependent: pro- vs anti-tumor depending on dose and setting
- βc sharing with IL-3/IL-5 complicates specificity

## Open problems

- Blocking GM-CSF to prevent monocyte-to-TAM polarization in solid tumors
- Spatially restricted GM-CSF activity in the TME
