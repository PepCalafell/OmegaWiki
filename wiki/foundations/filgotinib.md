## Definition

Filgotinib is a clinically approved, preferentially JAK1-selective inhibitor used for rheumatoid arthritis and ulcerative colitis. It blocks JAK1-dependent cytokine and interferon signal transduction (STAT phosphorylation).

## Intuition

A relatively JAK-specific inhibitor lacking TBK1 activity, making it a clean comparator for separating JAK–STAT effects from TBK1–IRF3 effects on interferon induction.

## Formal notation

- Designated clinical target: JAK1.
- Kinobeads (Klaeger et al. 2017): notably NOT found to bind JAK-family kinases — a discordance with its clinical annotation.

## Key variants

- Other JAK inhibitors: Tofacitinib (JAK3-preferring), Ruxolitinib (JAK1/2), Momelotinib, Pacritinib, BMS-911543.

## Known limitations

- Discordance between clinical JAK1 designation and kinobeads binding illustrates limits of in-vitro selectivity profiling.

## Open problems

- Which targets mediate its strong cellular STAT1/IFN inhibition given the absence of detected JAK binding in kinobeads.

## Relevance to active research

In [[papers/integrative-epigenome-based-strategy-unbiased-functional]], Filgotinib was among the strongest inhibitors of LPS-induced p-STAT1 and IFN-stimulated genes despite no detectable JAK binding in kinobeads; unlike Midostaurin/Momelotinib it did not reduce IFNB1 (no TBK1 activity) in human THP-1 cells but did reduce IFNβ-induced RSAD2, confirming JAK-specific action.
