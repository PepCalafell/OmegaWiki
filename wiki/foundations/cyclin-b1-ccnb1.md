## Definition

Cyclin B1 is the regulatory subunit that binds and activates CDK1 to form the mitosis-promoting factor (MPF). Its abundance rises through G2, peaks at the G2/M transition, and is abruptly destroyed by APC/C-mediated ubiquitination at the metaphase-to-anaphase transition. Because of this tightly timed expression, cyclin B1 is a canonical immunoblot marker for the position of a cell population within the cell cycle.

## Intuition

Cyclin B1 levels act as a "hand on the mitotic clock": a gradual rise-then-fall of cyclin B1 marks cells transiting G2→M→G1, which the source paper uses to time the transient normoxic appearance of HIF-1α.

## Formal notation

- Gene: CCNB1 (chr5q13.2); UniProt P14635
- Partner kinase: [[cdk1-cyclin-dependent-kinase-1]]
- Degraded via APC/C–Cdc20 at anaphase (D-box motif)

## Key variants

- Cyclin B2 (CCNB2) — related mitotic cyclin
- Cyclin E (CCNE1) — G1/S cyclin also reported to stimulate HIF-1α expression

## Known limitations

- A marker of cell-cycle position, not a direct functional readout of HIF-1α
- Asynchronous cultures average out its oscillation, masking G2/M-restricted events

## Open problems

- Precise temporal coupling between the cyclin B1/CDK1 peak and HIF-1α stabilization kinetics

## Relevance to active research

Used as the cell-cycle timing marker demonstrating that transient normoxic HIF-1α in Huh7 cells appears at the G2→M→G1 window. Relevant to cell-cycle and hypoxia-signaling themes.
