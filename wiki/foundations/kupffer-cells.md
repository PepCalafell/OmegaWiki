## Definition

Kupffer cells are the resident tissue macrophages of the liver, lining the sinusoidal lumen and performing scavenger and immune surveillance roles — bacterial clearance from portal blood, erythrocyte recycling, lipid and iron homeostasis, and complement-mediated opsonisation. Embryonically seeded from yolk-sac progenitors and largely self-maintained in homeostasis, with minor monocyte-derived contributions during inflammation.

## Intuition

Kupffer cells are the body's largest tissue-resident macrophage population and the first immune barrier between gut-derived antigens (portal blood) and systemic circulation. Identity is instructed by sinusoidal niche signals (TGFβ, LXR, ID3, BMP), with [[foundations/tgfb1-cytokine]] required for ID3-dependent KC core identity.

## Key markers

- Mouse: Clec4f (cardinal), Vsig4, Tim4, F4/80, CD68, Marco, ID3.
- Human: CD68, MARCO, VSIG4, CLEC4F (variable), CD163.

## Known limitations

- Activation states (e.g. lipid-associated KCs, KC-like monocyte-derived macrophages in MASH) blur the lineage definition.
- Single-cell resolution scRNA-seq atlases reveal multiple KC subsets that are not yet harmonized across the field.

## Relevance to active research

Kupffer cells participate in MASH/NASH progression, hepatocellular carcinoma immune escape, and the Tgfb1–stellate-cell feedback loop predicted by NiCo (Agrawal 2024) and validated by smHCR.
