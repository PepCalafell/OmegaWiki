## Definition

FIDAS-5 is a small-molecule inhibitor of methionine adenosyltransferase (MAT) — the enzyme converting methionine + ATP into SAM. By blocking SAM production, FIDAS-5 lowers cellular one-carbon metabolites and SAM-dependent methylation reactions in a dose-dependent manner.

## Intuition

If SAM is the universal methyl donor, FIDAS-5 turns off the production line. The functional consequence depends on the cell type and on the upstream stimulus: in C2C12 myotubes, FIDAS-5 reverses methionine- and IL6-induced atrophy + glucose hypermetabolism, producing instead a mild hypertrophic / hypometabolic phenotype.

## Formal notation

- Target: methionine adenosyltransferase (MAT2A primarily; MAT1A selectivity less characterised).
- Working concentrations in vitro (C2C12, 3T3-L1): 2-5 μM, 48 h.
- Stock preparation: DMSO 5 mM stocks, stored -20 °C.
- Commercial source: MCE HY-136144.

## Key variants

- Related MAT inhibitors: PF-9366 (an allosteric MAT2A inhibitor used widely in macrophage / cancer-metabolism literature).
- AG-270 (clinical-grade MAT2A inhibitor for MTAP-deleted cancers).

## Known limitations

- Selectivity profile (MAT1A vs MAT2A) at sustained doses not perfectly characterised.
- Pleiotropic effects expected: DNA methylation, histone methylation, polyamine synthesis, NNMT-driven NAM methylation — all are SAM-dependent.
- No published in vivo cachexia data with FIDAS-5 — translatability open.

## Open problems

- In vivo efficacy and safety of FIDAS-5 (or AG-270) in tumour-bearing cachexia mice.
- Whether FIDAS-5 combines additively with anti-IL6R (tocilizumab) in cachexia models.
- Selectivity over hepatocyte MAT1A — needed to avoid liver toxicity.

## Relevance to active research

Used in [[papers/multi-omics-profiling-cachexia-targeted-tissues]] to rescue L-methionine-induced and IL6-induced myotube atrophy and glucose hypermetabolism in C2C12 cells; the most direct druggable handle of the cachexia one-carbon axis in this paper.
