## Definition

G6PD is the rate-limiting enzyme of the oxidative branch of the pentose phosphate pathway (PPP). It catalyzes the NADP⁺-dependent oxidation of glucose-6-phosphate to 6-phosphogluconolactone, generating the first molecule of NADPH and committing glucose carbon to the PPP rather than glycolysis. Its products feed ribose-5-phosphate synthesis (nucleotide biosynthesis) and NADPH-dependent redox buffering (glutathione regeneration).

## Intuition

G6PD sits at the fork where a cell decides whether to burn glucose for ATP (glycolysis) or divert it toward building blocks and antioxidant power (PPP). Proliferating cancer cells lean heavily on this fork for ribose and NADPH; inhibiting G6PD starves them of nucleotide precursors and raises oxidative stress.

## Formal notation

Reaction: glucose-6-phosphate + NADP⁺ → 6-phosphogluconolactone + NADPH + H⁺. Enzyme activity is quantified spectrophotometrically by NADPH production (A₃₄₀), and kinetics are described by Michaelis–Menten Vmax/Km parameters.

## Key variants

- Human somatic G6PD (P11413), X-linked; inherited deficiency is the most common human enzymopathy.
- Recombinant G6PD used in cell-free inhibition assays.
- NRF2-driven transcriptional induction couples G6PD to antioxidant response programs.

## Known limitations

- Deficiency causes hemolytic anemia under oxidative stress, constraining therapeutic G6PD inhibition.
- Activity is regulated post-translationally (NADP⁺/NADPH ratio, acetylation) beyond protein abundance, so expression is an imperfect proxy for flux.

## Open problems

- Whether PPP/G6PD dependence varies across cancer genetic subtypes.
- Whether the growth-limiting consequence of G6PD inhibition is primarily ribose limitation or NADPH/redox loss.

## Relevance to active research

G6PD is elevated in many cancers and correlates with poor prognosis; it is the direct molecular target through which macrophage-derived itaconate and 4-octyl itaconate suppress lung cancer proliferation and reprogram tumor-associated macrophages ([[papers/irg1-itaconate-rewires-macrophage-lung-tumor]]).
