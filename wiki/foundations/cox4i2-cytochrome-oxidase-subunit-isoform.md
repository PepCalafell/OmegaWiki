## Definition

COX4I2 is the atypical, hypoxia-inducible isoform of subunit 4 of cytochrome c oxidase (mitochondrial respiratory complex IV, the terminal electron acceptor of the electron transport chain). It replaces the ubiquitous COX4I1 isoform under low oxygen.

## Intuition

Complex IV transfers electrons to molecular oxygen. The COX4I2 isoform has a lower oxygen affinity and alters complex-IV kinetics so that the ETC runs more slowly, matching oxygen consumption to a reduced oxygen supply.

## Key variants

- COX4I1 (ubiquitous, normoxic isoform)
- COX4I2 (hypoxia-inducible isoform; regulated by HIF and, per Arenillas et al., by EPAS1/HIF2α activation)

## Known limitations

Tissue-restricted expression; the precise structural basis of its altered oxygen kinetics is incompletely resolved.

## Open problems

- Therapeutic exploitability of the COX4I1↔COX4I2 switch
- Contribution to tumor fitness in vivo

## Relevance to active research

Central to the proposed mechanism by which EPAS1-mutant PPGL tumors survive hypoxia via oxygen supply–demand matching (see [[concepts/cox4i2-isoform-switch-balances-oxygen-consumption]]).
