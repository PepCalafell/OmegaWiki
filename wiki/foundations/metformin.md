## Definition

Biguanide antidiabetic drug that mildly inhibits mitochondrial respiratory-chain complex I, raises AMP/ATP ratio, activates AMPK, and lowers hepatic gluconeogenesis. Repurposed in oncology and immunometabolism as a metabolic conditioner.

## Intuition

Sub-lethal complex-I inhibition forces cells into a more "memory-like" metabolic state (fatty-acid oxidation, mitochondrial biogenesis) and curbs aerobic glycolysis — useful when training CAR-T cells for persistence.

## Formal notation

Indirect AMPK activation via mitochondrial complex I dampening and elevated AMP:ATP.

## Key variants

- Metformin (lowest potency, widest clinical safety).
- Phenformin (higher potency, withdrawn due to lactic acidosis).
- Newer biguanide-like analogues.

## Known limitations

- Lactic-acidosis risk in patients with renal impairment.
- Dose-response curve diverges between in-vitro effective concentrations and clinically achievable levels.

## Open problems

- Whether ex-vivo metformin conditioning of CAR-T translates to patients.
- Selectivity for tumour vs immune cell complex I.

## Relevance to active research

- Promotes T-cell memory formation in CAR-T manufacturing.
- Enhances HER2-CAR-T efficacy in xenograft lung adenocarcinoma.
- Mainstay of immunometabolism reprogramming.
