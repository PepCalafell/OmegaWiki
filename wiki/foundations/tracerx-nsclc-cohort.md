## Definition

TRACERx is a UK-wide prospective longitudinal study of operable non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) designed to characterize intra-tumour heterogeneity and tumour evolution through standard-of-care treatment. The cohort enrolls hundreds of patients with multi-region tumour sampling, blood-based ctDNA tracking, longitudinal imaging and clinical follow-up. The TRACERx body-composition arm (Al-Sawaf, Goncalves, Jamal-Hanjani and colleagues) uses serial CT-L3 segmentation to define cancer-associated cachexia thresholds and to link body-compartment loss to primary-tumour molecular features.

## Intuition

TRACERx provides the prospective longitudinal infrastructure that retrospective cachexia cohorts lack: serial CTs, multi-region tumour sampling, ctDNA, and PROs all on the same patients, enabling causal inference from tumour molecular state to body-composition trajectory.

## Formal notation

- Trial registry: NCT01888601 (TRACERx-Lung).
- Sister studies: TRACERx-Renal (NCT03226886), TRACERx-Melanoma, PEACE (autopsy programme).
- Body-composition CAC thresholds defined: >10% SKM loss, >20% SAT loss, >20% VAT loss, or grade-4 BMI-adjusted weight loss.

## Key variants

- TRACERx-Renal: renal-cell carcinoma evolution cohort.
- PEACE: paired post-mortem multi-region sampling.

## Known limitations

- Operable NSCLC only — early-stage bias; results may not generalize to unresectable / metastatic disease.
- UK-centric demographics.
- Retrospective body-composition arm depends on already-acquired clinical CT scans.

## Open problems

- Extension of TRACERx body-composition framework to other cancer types and to advanced-stage cohorts.
- Integration with multi-omics (multi-tissue) measures of CAC as in [[papers/multi-omics-profiling-cachexia-targeted-tissues]].
