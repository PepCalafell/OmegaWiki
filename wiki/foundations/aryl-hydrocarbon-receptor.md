## Definition

The aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR; gene symbol AHR; HGNC:348) is a ligand-activated basic-helix-loop-helix Per-ARNT-Sim (bHLH-PAS) transcription factor that mediates responses to a wide range of small-molecule ligands — environmental (TCDD, BaP, PAHs), dietary (indoles, flavonoids), microbial (indole-3-aldehyde), and endogenous (Trp catabolites including Kyn, KynA, FICZ, indirubin; eicosanoids; bilirubin; cAMP). It is the only bHLH-PAS family member activated by small molecules (the others — HIF1α, HIF2α, CLOCK, BMAL — sense oxygen, redox state, or circadian cues).

## Intuition

A single receptor that integrates xenobiotic, dietary, microbial, and endogenous signals to drive context-specific transcriptional and non-transcriptional outputs. Whether AHR promotes tumour progression, anti-tumour immunity, T-cell tolerance, or epithelial barrier integrity depends on which combination of layered regulators (ligand, cell type, ARNT/ARNT2/iso ratio, AHRR, HIFα crosstalk, PTMs, epigenetics) is active.

## Formal notation

- Protein domains: N-terminal bHLH (DNA binding) — PAS-A (dimerisation) — PAS-B (ligand binding) — glutamine-rich C-terminal transactivation domain.
- Inactive state: cytoplasmic complex with HSP90 dimer + AIP (XAP2/ARA9) + PTGES3 (p23) + SRC kinase.
- Canonical activation: ligand binding → NLS exposure → nuclear translocation → ARNT heterodimerisation → XRE binding (5'-TNGCGTG-3') → target gene transcription (CYP1A1, CYP1B1, AHRR, TIPARP, IL-22, …).
- Negative feedback: AHRR repression + TIPARP-driven degradation + CYP1A1-mediated ligand clearance.

## Key variants

- Human AHR vs mouse AHR: differ in ligand selectivity and transcriptional responses (a major source of translational discrepancies).
- High-affinity vs low-affinity ligands; primary-pocket vs secondary-pocket binders.

## Known limitations

- Species differences in ligand selectivity limit in-vivo cross-species translation.
- Cell-type and ligand context complexity makes "AHR antagonism" a blunt therapeutic concept.

## Open problems

- Cryo-EM of liganded AHR-ARNT-XRE complex.
- Pocket-selective AHR ligands (biased agonists).
- Single-cell atlas of AHR transcriptional output across human tissues.

## Relevance to active research

Central to AHR-pathway therapeutics (tapinarof, IDO1/TDO2 inhibitors, AHR antagonists in trials), cancer immunology (Treg/Th17 polarisation, TAM immunosuppression), barrier immunity (intestine, skin), and increasingly, BRAFi-resistance / non-genomic kinase signalling.
