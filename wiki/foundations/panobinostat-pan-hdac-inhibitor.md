## Definition

Panobinostat (LBH589) is a hydroxamic-acid pan-HDAC inhibitor that blocks class I, II and IV histone deacetylases, leading to global increases in histone acetylation (e.g. H3K27ac). Clinically approved for multiple myeloma; widely used experimentally to probe HDAC-dependent gene regulation.

## Intuition

By preventing removal of acetyl marks, panobinostat restores acetylation lost when HDAC activity rises (as it does under hypoxia). Restoring H3K27ac re-opens/reactivates genes silenced by deacetylation and can reverse hypoxia-driven phenotypes.

## Formal notation

- Mechanism: inhibition of HDAC catalytic activity → accumulation of acetyl-lysine on histones and non-histone substrates.
- Experimental dose in glioma microglia context: 10 nM.

## Key variants

- Other HDAC inhibitors: vorinostat (SAHA), trichostatin A, entinostat. See [[foundations/hdac-histone-deacetylase]].

## Known limitations

- Pan-specificity: affects many HDAC isoforms and non-histone substrates, so phenotypic reversal cannot be attributed to a single locus.
- Cytotoxicity at higher doses confounds interpretation.

## Open problems

- Which HDAC isoform(s) mediate the hypoxia-induced acetylation loss in microglia, and whether isoform-selective inhibition reproduces the effect.

## Relevance to active research

Used in [[papers/hypoxic-stress-dysregulates-functions-glioma-associated]] to show that restoring H3K27ac reverses hypoxia-induced Lgals3 upregulation, rescues Tmem119, and blocks lipid-droplet accumulation in hypoxic GAMs — establishing a causal HDAC/acetylation link to the hypoxic myeloid phenotype.
