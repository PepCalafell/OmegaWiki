## Definition

Kisspeptins are peptides encoded by the *KISS1* gene that signal through the G-protein-coupled receptor GPR54 (KISS1R). Originally identified as metastasis suppressors, they are best known for triggering gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) secretion and controlling puberty and reproduction. An emerging literature documents immunomodulatory roles.

## Intuition

Beyond the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis, kisspeptin/GPR54 signaling modulates immune responses — restraining antiviral innate immunity and dampening inflammatory signaling in macrophages — positioning the peptide at a neuroendocrine–immune interface.

## Formal notation

KP-10 (kisspeptin-10) is the active C-terminal decapeptide used experimentally (typical PBMC assay range 1–100 ng/mL).

## Key variants

- Kisspeptin-54, -14, -13, -10 (cleavage products of the same precursor)
- Reproductive vs immunomodulatory vs anti-metastatic functions

## Known limitations

Immunomodulatory effects are context- and dose-dependent and less characterized than reproductive signaling; cell-type-specific receptor expression is incompletely mapped.

## Open problems

Whether DNA-methylation control of *KISS1* tunes IFN-γ production in humans, and the downstream signaling (calcineurin, NF-κB, MAPK) bridging kisspeptin to cytokine output.

## Relevance to active research

Identified as a candidate pathway whereby baseline DNA methylation shapes heterologous IFN-γ trained-immunity responses after BCG vaccination.
