## Definition

Sarcosine is the N-methyl derivative of glycine, produced by SAM-dependent methylation of glycine by glycine N-methyltransferase (GNMT). Sarcosine is itself oxidised back to glycine + 5,10-methyleneTHF by mitochondrial sarcosine dehydrogenase (SARDH), feeding the folate cycle.

## Intuition

Sarcosine is a flux marker for one-carbon metabolism: its abundance reflects the balance between GNMT-driven SAM-overflow methylation and SARDH-driven oxidative consumption. Elevated sarcosine signals either increased GNMT activity, increased SAM availability, or decreased downstream consumption.

## Formal notation

- Formation: glycine + SAM →(GNMT)→ sarcosine + SAH.
- Degradation: sarcosine →(SARDH)→ glycine + 5,10-methylene-THF.
- Plasma/serum sarcosine: low baseline (μM range); rises in conditions of SAM excess or GNMT induction.

## Key variants

- Sarcosine has had controversial standing as a prostate-cancer biomarker (Sreekumar 2009 Nature); the prostate-cancer claim has been challenged.
- Sarcosine accumulation in inborn errors of metabolism (sarcosinemia, mostly benign).

## Known limitations

- Sarcosine quantitation by MS requires careful method (low abundance, similar mass to other small AAs).
- Interpretation requires concurrent GNMT/SARDH expression or pathway-level context.

## Open problems

- Whether plasma sarcosine has diagnostic utility for cachexia (suggested by [[papers/multi-omics-profiling-cachexia-targeted-tissues]]).
- Whether sarcosine has independent biological activity beyond being a flux marker (e.g., effects on glycine signalling or NMDA-receptor co-agonism).
