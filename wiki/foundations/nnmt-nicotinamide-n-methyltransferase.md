## Definition

NNMT catalyses the SAM-dependent N-methylation of nicotinamide (NAM) to 1-methylnicotinamide (MNAM), producing SAH as the methyl-acceptor by-product. NNMT activity diverts NAM from the NAD+ salvage pathway (NAMPT) and consumes cellular SAM, making it a key node coupling methyl-group homeostasis, nicotinamide/NAD+ pools, and one-carbon metabolism.

## Intuition

NNMT acts as a methyl-group sink: each turn consumes one SAM and one NAM, producing one SAH and one MNAM. In tissues where NNMT is highly expressed (cancer cells, obese adipose, cachectic liver), this can deplete SAM and lower the SAM/SAH ratio for other methylation reactions, and divert NAM away from NAD+ regeneration. MNAM is excreted in urine (a non-invasive biomarker of NNMT activity).

## Formal notation

- Reaction: nicotinamide + SAM → MNAM + SAH.
- Tissue expression: liver, adipose, kidney, many tumour types.
- Clinical/biomarker relevance: urinary/plasma MNAM as NNMT activity readout.
- Inhibitors: small-molecule NNMT inhibitors in development (e.g., 5-amino-1MQ).

## Key variants

- Hepatic NNMT (high baseline, further induced in cachexia, NAFLD).
- Adipocyte NNMT (linked to obesity / insulin resistance).
- Tumour-cell NNMT (linked to aggressive phenotype in multiple cancers).

## Known limitations

- NNMT inhibitors not yet clinically approved.
- Pleiotropic effects of NNMT modulation due to coupling to both SAM and NAM pools.

## Open problems

- Liver-specific Nnmt KO in tumour-bearing mice — does it rescue cachexia phenotypes?
- Quantitative relationship between plasma/urinary MNAM and cachexia severity in patients.
