## Definition

Sarcopenia is a clinically defined syndrome of low skeletal muscle mass + low muscle strength (or low physical performance), measurable by DEXA / CT (lumbar L3 SMI), grip strength, gait speed. In cancer patients, sarcopenia overlaps with but is not synonymous with cachexia: a patient may be sarcopenic without overt weight loss (sarcopenic obesity), or cachectic with not-yet-sarcopenic muscle mass.

## Intuition

Sarcopenia is the muscle-mass-and-function dimension that is *measurable* in patients, whereas "cachexia" is the metabolic syndrome that may or may not yet have produced detectable sarcopenia. In oncology, sarcopenia at diagnosis is a strong prognostic factor for chemotherapy tolerance and survival.

## Formal notation

- EWGSOP2 criteria (2019): low strength → confirmed sarcopenia if low muscle quantity/quality → severe sarcopenia if also low physical performance.
- Cancer-specific cut-offs: L3 SMI (skeletal muscle index) thresholds sex-specific (Prado et al.).
- Sarcopenic obesity: low muscle + high adipose — particularly poor prognosis in cancer.

## Key variants

- Primary sarcopenia (age-associated).
- Secondary sarcopenia (disease-associated: cancer, COPD, CKD, heart failure).
- Sarcopenic obesity.

## Known limitations

- DEXA/CT-based diagnosis requires imaging access and standardised cutoffs.
- Functional measures (grip, gait speed) limited in late-stage cancer patients.

## Open problems

- Whether one-carbon-metabolism biomarkers (NNMT activity, plasma MNAM, sarcosine) can stratify pre-sarcopenic cancer patients for early intervention.

## Relevance to active research

In [[papers/multi-omics-profiling-cachexia-targeted-tissues]], sarcopenic cancer-patient liver and skeletal muscle samples show ↑ NNMT and ↑ one-carbon signature gene expression — the principal patient-translation finding linking the mouse-model one-carbon-cachexia framing to human disease.
