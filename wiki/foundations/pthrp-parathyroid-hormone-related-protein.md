## Definition

PTHrP (encoded by PTHLH) is a tumour-secreted polypeptide structurally related to parathyroid hormone, originally identified as the cause of humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy. In cancer cachexia it is implicated as a tumour-derived factor that elevates host energy expenditure by inducing UCP1-mediated browning of white adipose tissue in mouse models. In humans, NSCLC patients with detectable plasma PTHrP exhibit lower lean body mass and higher resting energy expenditure, suggesting a role in human CAC but with smaller effect than in mice.

## Intuition

PTHrP is one of the candidate tumour-secreted factors that "broadcasts" cachexia signals to the host periphery, particularly adipose tissue. The cleanest functional readout is UCP1 induction in cultured adipocytes; clinical correlation in NSCLC reinforces the concept though the population fraction with detectable PTHrP is modest.

## Formal notation

- Gene: PTHLH (chromosome 12p11.2).
- Receptor: PTH1R (shared with parathyroid hormone).
- Functional readouts: UCP1 mRNA/protein in brown/white adipocytes; calcium homeostasis (the hypercalcemia mechanism).

## Key variants

- N-terminal PTH-like fragment (calcium effects).
- Mid-region and C-terminal fragments (distinct biology).

## Known limitations

- Plasma PTHrP not routinely measured; assay sensitivity variable.
- Human evidence for PTHrP-driven WAT browning is one supportive study; broader cohorts have not consistently confirmed.
- Targeting PTHrP pharmacologically risks disrupting calcium homeostasis.

## Open problems

- What fraction of CAC patients have detectable PTHrP, and does it map to a body-composition subtype?
- Are anti-PTHrP antibodies a viable therapeutic strategy, or do calcium side effects rule them out?
- Mechanism of PTHrP-induced UCP1 expression in human adipocytes.
