## Definition

MMP14 (MT1-MMP) is a membrane-anchored matrix metalloproteinase. It cleaves type I/II/III collagens, fibronectin, laminin, and activates pro-MMP2/pro-MMP13, driving extracellular matrix remodelling, cell invasion and angiogenesis. The catalytic domain is GPI-anchored / single-pass transmembrane with extracellular catalytic and hemopexin domains and a short cytoplasmic tail.

## Intuition

MMP14 is the "tumor invasion protease": cell-surface localisation lets cells punch holes through dense extracellular matrix. It is broadly expressed across mesenchymal cells but, critically for cancer immunotherapy, is TAM-specific and TME-restricted among immune cells.

## Key variants

- Wild-type membrane MMP14
- Soluble forms (shed catalytic ectodomain)
- TIMP2/3-inhibited form (TIMP2 is required for pro-MMP2 activation)
- Catalytic substrate-specific reporters (SGRSENIRTA, etc., from substrate specificity screens)

## Known limitations

- Broad substrate repertoire makes specificity claims for engineered cleavage linkers fragile
- Cancer-associated fibroblasts also express MMP14, partially limiting TAM-specific targeting
- KO mice die postnatally (skeletal phenotypes) — confounds developmental vs adult biology

## Relevance to active research

Used as the protease "switch" for tumor-restricted activation of MiTE immunocytokines: a linker (SGRSENIRTA) selectively cleaved by MMP14 unmasks IL-2 superkine within the TME ([[macrophage-targeted-immunocytokine-leverages-myeloid-nk]]). Co-expressed with TREM2 in TAMs, making it a TAM-specific activation marker.
