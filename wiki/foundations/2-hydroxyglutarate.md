## Definition

Stereoisomer (D-/R-2HG) of α-ketoglutarate produced at supraphysiological levels by mutant IDH1/IDH2 enzymes. As a competitive inhibitor of α-KG-dependent dioxygenases (TET2, KDM lysine demethylases, prolyl hydroxylases), 2HG drives DNA and histone hypermethylation, blocks cellular differentiation, and contributes to tumour immune exclusion.

## Intuition

The first identified oncometabolite. It rewires the epigenome by hijacking the cellular methylation potential, then propagates into the immune compartment by silencing cGAS and pseudohypoxic responses.

## Formal notation

α-KG + NADPH → D-2HG + NADP+ (catalysed by neomorphic mIDH1/2).

## Key variants

- D-2HG (mIDH1/2-derived, oncogenic).
- L-2HG (hypoxia-induced, milder phenotype).

## Known limitations

- Difficulty measuring intratumoural 2HG non-invasively (MRS imaging used).
- Heterogeneity of downstream epigenomic readouts.

## Open problems

- Quantitative threshold of 2HG that triggers immune exclusion.
- How 2HG cross-talks with HIF-1α (succinate/fumarate axes).

## Relevance to active research

- Substrate of mIDH inhibitors (ivosidenib, enasidenib, vorasidenib).
- Mechanistic link between tumour metabolism and the cold TME.
