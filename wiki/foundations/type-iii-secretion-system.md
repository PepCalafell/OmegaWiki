## Definition

The type III secretion system (T3SS) is a multiprotein, syringe-and-needle-like
molecular machine used by many Gram-negative bacteria (Pseudomonadota) to inject
effector proteins directly from the bacterial cytoplasm into the cytosol of a
eukaryotic host cell. It is evolutionarily related to the flagellar export
apparatus.

## Intuition

A "molecular syringe": the basal body spans both bacterial membranes, a needle
projects outward, and a translocon pore inserts into the host membrane, creating
a continuous conduit through which unfolded effectors are threaded into the host.

## Formal notation

T3SS+ denotes a genome/strain encoding a complete, intact T3SS. Effector substrates
are the translocated cargo proteins; meta-effectors regulate other effectors.

## Key variants

Distinct T3SS families exist (e.g. Ysc, Inv-Mxi-Spa/SPI-1, Ssa-Esc/SPI-2, Hrp in
plant pathogens). Related host-directed injection machineries include T4SS and T6SS.

## Known limitations

Detection tools (e.g. EffectiveDB) may miss divergent systems, underestimating true
prevalence. Activation often requires specific host/environmental cues that are hard
to reproduce in vitro.

## Relevance to active research

Historically framed as a virulence determinant studied in pathogens (Yersinia,
Salmonella, Shigella, Pseudomonas). Recent work shows T3SS are also common in
commensal gut Pseudomonadota and may mediate beneficial or immune-modulatory
microorganism–host interactions.
