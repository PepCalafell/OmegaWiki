## Definition

HSF1 is the master transcription factor of the cytoprotective heat-shock response, inducing heat-shock protein (HSP) chaperones under proteotoxic stress. Beyond proteostasis, HSF1 supports malignant phenotypes and, in tumor-associated macrophages, is associated with more aggressive tumor growth.

## Intuition

HSF1 is the cell's proteostasis commander; cancers and protumoral stromal cells co-opt its chaperone program to tolerate stress. Its activity in TAMs links the stress response to immunosuppressive, pro-tumor states.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *HSF1*; binds heat-shock elements (HSE: nGAAn repeats) as a trimer
- Activity gated by stress-dependent oligomerization and extensive phosphorylation
- Induces HSP70, HSP90, HSP27 and a broader cancer-supportive transcriptome

## Key variants

- HSF family (HSF1, HSF2, HSF4) with distinct stress/developmental roles
- Phospho-regulated activating vs repressive sites on HSF1

## Known limitations

- Phosphorylation code is complex and context-dependent
- Pleiotropic targets blur proteostasis vs oncogenic functions

## Open problems

- How HSF1 phosphorylation shapes immunosuppressive macrophage programs
- Whether HSF1 inhibition repolarizes TAMs without compromising normal proteostasis

## Relevance to active research

Phosphoproteomics of primary human macrophages found HSF1 (e.g. S292) with upregulated phosphosites in the immunosuppressive M2a state; HSF1's elevated activity in TAMs associates with aggressive tumor growth.
