## Definition

Autosomal recessive congenital ichthyosis (ARCI) is a clinically and genetically heterogeneous group of inherited disorders of cornification (a class of genodermatosis) characterised by generalised scaling, skin-barrier dysfunction, water loss, and, in severe forms, collodion-membrane presentation at birth. It is caused by biallelic loss-of-function mutations in any of several epidermal-barrier genes, most prominently *TGM1*.

## Intuition

ARCI is a barrier-failure disease: the outermost skin cannot assemble a sealed cornified envelope, so the skin scales, cracks, and loses water. Because most cases stem from single-gene loss-of-function mutations with a quantifiable enzymatic deficit, ARCI is conceptually well suited to gene-correction therapy — if an editor can be delivered to skin stem cells, partial restoration of the defective protein should yield durable barrier improvement.

## Formal notation

- Inheritance: autosomal recessive (biallelic)
- Causal genes: *TGM1* (~1/3 of cases), *ALOX12B*, *ALOXE3*, *NIPAL4*, *CYP4F22*, *ABCA12* (harlequin), *PNPLA1*, others
- Most common *TGM1* allele: c.877-2A>G (splice-acceptor disruption)
- Phenotypic spectrum: lamellar ichthyosis ↔ congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma ↔ harlequin ichthyosis

## Key variants

- **Lamellar ichthyosis** — large dark plate-like scales
- **Congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma (CIE)** — fine scaling on erythematous skin
- **Harlequin ichthyosis** — severe *ABCA12* form
- *TGM1*-associated ARCI — the subtype targeted for base-editing correction

## Known limitations

- Genetic heterogeneity complicates a single therapeutic approach
- Whole-body surface involvement challenges local delivery strategies
- Current care is largely symptomatic (emollients, retinoids), not curative

## Open problems

- Scaling gene-correction therapy from focal skin areas to whole-body surface
- Durability of correction given epidermal turnover and stem-cell competition
- Extending a modular barrier-modulation + editor-delivery platform across the genodermatosis spectrum
