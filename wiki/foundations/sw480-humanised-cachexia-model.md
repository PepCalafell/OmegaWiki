## Definition

The SW480 humanised cachexia model uses xenotransplantation of the human SW480 colon-carcinoma cell line into immunodeficient (e.g., NSG) mice and produces a clear cachexia phenotype with body-weight loss, tissue-mass loss, and transcriptomic/metabolomic features paralleling murine cachexia models — making it a bridge model for studying cachexia mechanisms driven by human tumour-secreted factors.

## Intuition

A humanised cachexia model lets a study test whether mechanisms identified in syngeneic mouse models (C26, Panc02, 8025) generalise when the *tumour* is human and secretes human ligands (notably human IL6). It complements patient-tissue analysis where direct in vivo perturbation is impossible.

## Formal notation

- Tumour cell line: SW480 (human colon adenocarcinoma).
- Host: immunodeficient mouse (typically NSG or athymic nude).
- Cachexia phenotype: significant BW + tissue-mass loss; conserved one-carbon enzyme/metabolite signature; conserved cardiac/skeletal muscle 13C-glucose TCA hypermetabolism.

## Key variants

- Other human tumour cell lines used in humanised cachexia models (e.g., human pancreatic xenografts).
- Patient-derived xenografts (PDX) as a more clinically faithful variant.

## Known limitations

- Immunodeficient host removes T-cell / NK-cell components of cachexia immune microenvironment.
- Single tumour-cell line; clonal limitation.

## Open problems

- Whether PDX cachexia models recapitulate the one-carbon signature identified in cell-line-based humanised model.

## Relevance to active research

Used in [[papers/multi-omics-profiling-cachexia-targeted-tissues]] to bridge from mouse models to patient relevance: SW480 cachectic mice show ↑ one-carbon enzymes (liver/muscle/adipose), ↑ one-carbon metabolites (plasma/liver/heart/muscle/adipose), and ↑ 13C6-glucose label in cardiac and skeletal muscle TCA — matching findings from C26 and PDAC models and from patient sarcopenia tissue.
