## Definition

SOD1 is a cytosolic Cu/Zn-dependent superoxide dismutase that catalyzes the dismutation of superoxide (O2•−) to hydrogen peroxide and molecular oxygen. It is a central component of the cellular antioxidant defense system. In hematopoiesis, SOD1 is enriched in HSCs and contributes to long-term hematopoietic potential, as shown by CRISPR-Cas9 KO experiments in human CD34+ BM cells.

## Intuition

HSCs reside in a low-oxygen bone-marrow niche and rely on glycolysis. Loss of cytosolic antioxidant capacity exposes them to oxidative damage when they exit quiescence — explaining why SOD1-KO HSPCs nearly fail to form colonies.

## Formal notation

SOD1 protein: ~16 kDa, homodimer with one Cu and one Zn per subunit. CRISPR KO in CD34+ BM cells in [[papers/mapping-early-human-blood-cell-differentiation]]: LTC-IC frequency dropped to 1/153 (vs 1/14 AAVS1 control); colony-forming capacity collapsed (only small CFU-Gs); 3-week stromal co-culture showed a block in granulocytic differentiation.

## Key variants

- SOD2 (manganese SOD, mitochondrial) — also assayed in the Furtwängler 2025 paper; KO replicates much of the phenotype but without proliferation arrest.
- SOD3 (extracellular Cu/Zn SOD).
- ALS-associated SOD1 mutations (separate disease context).

## Known limitations

- Sod1 / Sod2 null mice do not phenocopy human HSPC KO — Furtwängler 2025 explicitly highlights this species discrepancy.

## Open problems

- Why is the human HSPC phenotype not recapitulated in mouse?
- Therapeutic implications for SOD modulation in myeloid disease.
