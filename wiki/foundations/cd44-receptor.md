## Definition

CD44 is a cell-surface glycoprotein and the principal receptor for hyaluronan, with many splice-variant isoforms. It mediates cell-cell and cell-matrix adhesion and acts as a co-receptor and docking molecule for diverse ligands including collagens, fibronectin (FN1), osteopontin (SPP1), matrix metalloproteinases (e.g. MMP9), and growth factors (e.g. FGF1/FGF2). It is a widely used cancer stem-cell and tumour-invasion marker.

## Intuition

CD44 is a hub receptor of the extracellular matrix interactome. By binding and localising MMP9 to the cell surface it promotes ECM (collagen) degradation and invasion; by anchoring fibronectin it tethers cells to surrounding ECM; by interacting with FGFs it can amplify pro-proliferative signalling. Its many partners make it a recurring node in tumour cell-cell interaction networks.

## Formal notation

Not applicable (receptor protein). Appears in ligand-receptor analyses as the receptor in pairs such as COL*-CD44, SPP1-CD44, MIF-CD44, FGF1/2-CD44, FN1-CD44.

## Key variants

- CD44 standard (CD44s)
- CD44 variant isoforms (CD44v) from alternative splicing

## Known limitations

- Isoform complexity complicates antibody/probe specificity
- Broad ligand repertoire makes interaction attribution context-dependent

## Open problems

- Isoform-specific functions in different cancers
- Therapeutic targeting without disrupting normal adhesion

## Relevance to active research

A dominant receptor in melanoma cell-cell interactomes; CD44-FGF2, CD44-MMP9 and CD44-FN1 interactions are nominated and validated as candidate axes of melanoma invasion and potential therapeutic targets.
