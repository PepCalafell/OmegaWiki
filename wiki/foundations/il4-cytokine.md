## Definition

IL-4 is a four-α-helix-bundle cytokine produced mainly by Th2 cells, basophils, mast cells, and ILC2s. It signals through the type I receptor (IL-4Rα/γc, hematopoietic cells) or type II receptor (IL-4Rα/IL-13Rα1, non-hematopoietic and myeloid cells), activating JAK1/3 → STAT6. It drives Th2 differentiation, B-cell IgE class switching, and alternative (M2) macrophage activation, and shares receptor and signaling components with IL-13.

## Intuition

IL-4 is the master Th2/allergic and alternative-activation cytokine. In the Human Cytokine Dictionary it has a strong impact on essentially all PBMC cell types; in B cells it upregulates IGHE (isotype switch), its own receptor IL4R, and IL4I1. Its near-identical response to IL-13 (r≈0.94 in monocytes) reflects shared type II receptor signaling.

## Key variants

- IL-4 vs IL-13 (overlapping type II receptor signaling)
- Pitrakinra / dupilumab (IL-4Rα blockade) — therapeutic antagonists
- Engineered IL-4 superkines

## Known limitations

- Strong receptor/pathway overlap with IL-13 complicates attribution
- Pleiotropic, context-dependent effects across lineages

## Open problems

- Disentangling IL-4 vs IL-13 unique functions in vivo
- Role in tumor M2-TAM polarization as a therapeutic target
