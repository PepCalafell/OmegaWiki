## Definition

MAFB is a bZIP transcription factor of the large-MAF family that controls monocyte/macrophage differentiation and identity, and regulates anti-inflammatory and tissue-remodeling gene programs. Its elevated expression in tumor-associated macrophages is associated with more aggressive tumor growth.

## Intuition

MAFB is a lineage-defining macrophage transcription factor; in the tumor context its activity skews macrophages toward protumoral, immunosuppressive programs, making it a marker and candidate regulator of aggressive TAM states.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *MAFB*; large-MAF family (with MAF/c-MAF, MAFA, NRL)
- Binds MARE (MAF-recognition element) sites, often as homo- or heterodimers
- Functions in macrophage self-renewal and differentiation control

## Key variants

- Large MAFs (MAFA, MAFB, c-MAF, NRL) vs small MAFs (MAFF/G/K)
- Dosage-sensitive roles (MAFB haploinsufficiency phenotypes)

## Known limitations

- Overlapping functions with c-MAF in myeloid cells
- Cell-context-dependent pro- vs anti-inflammatory effects

## Open problems

- Mechanistic link between MAFB phosphorylation and immunosuppressive macrophage states
- Therapeutic targeting of MAFB-driven TAM programs

## Relevance to active research

Phosphoproteomics of primary human macrophages found MAFB (e.g. S7) with significantly upregulated phosphosites in the immunosuppressive M2a state; MAFB's elevated expression in TAMs is associated with aggressive tumor growth.
