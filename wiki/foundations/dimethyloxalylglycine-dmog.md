## Definition

Dimethyloxalylglycine (DMOG) is a cell-permeable competitive inhibitor of the 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases, including the HIF prolyl hydroxylase domain (PHD) enzymes and the asparaginyl hydroxylase FIH. By acting as a 2-oxoglutarate analogue it pharmacologically stabilizes and activates HIF under normoxia, mimicking the hypoxic transcriptional response.

## Intuition

DMOG is the prototypical, pan-hydroxylase research tool for "switching on" the HIF pathway without removing oxygen. It is non-selective (inhibits all PHD isoforms and FIH, plus other 2-OG dioxygenases), which makes it ideal for proof-of-concept studies but unsuitable as a clinical drug; selective PHD inhibitors (e.g. [[fg-4592-roxadustat]]) were developed for therapeutic use.

## Formal notation

DMOG ⊣ PHD1/2/3 and FIH (competitive vs 2-oxoglutarate) ⇒ HIFα stabilization + derepression of HIF transcriptional activity under normoxia.

## Key variants

- Hydrolysed intracellularly to the active species N-oxalylglycine (NOG).
- Clinically advanced, more selective PHD inhibitors in the same mechanistic class: roxadustat (FG-4592), daprodustat, vadadustat; the gut-targeted analogue AKB-4924; TRC160334; FG-4497.

## Known limitations

- Non-selective across the 2-OG dioxygenase family, confounding mechanistic attribution to HIF specifically.
- Systemic HIF activation has on-target liabilities (e.g. erythropoiesis, potential pro-angiogenic effects).

## Open problems

- Whether tissue-targeted delivery of hydroxylase inhibitors can decouple anti-inflammatory benefit from systemic HIF effects.

## Relevance to active research

DMOG is the dominant agent in preclinical demonstrations that pharmacological hydroxylase inhibition is anti-inflammatory across colitis, ischaemia, sepsis and infection models, as tabulated in [[regulation-immunity-inflammation-hypoxia-immunological-niches]].
