## Definition

Selective small-molecule inhibitor of mutant isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 (mIDH1 R132H/C/L/S/G). Reduces 2-hydroxyglutarate (2HG) production, restores α-KG-dependent DNA demethylation and induces tumour differentiation. FDA-approved for IDH1-mutant acute myeloid leukaemia (AML), intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), and myelodysplastic syndromes.

## Intuition

mIDH1 hijacks isocitrate to produce the oncometabolite 2HG, which blocks α-KG-dependent dioxygenases and causes hypermethylation that excludes T cells. Blocking mIDH1 restores cGAS expression and re-opens the tumour to T-cell infiltration.

## Formal notation

Allosteric inhibition of the neomorphic mIDH1 active site, suppressing α-KG → 2HG conversion.

## Key variants

- Ivosidenib (mIDH1).
- Olutasidenib (FT-2102, mIDH1) — separate molecule.

## Known limitations

- Differentiation syndrome in AML responders.
- IDH-isoform switching as resistance mechanism.

## Open problems

- Combination with ICB to leverage restored cGAS-driven T-cell infiltration.
- Use in IDH1-mutant glioma in combination with vorasidenib.

## Relevance to active research

- Approved metabolic targeted therapy.
- Bridge between oncometabolite biology and tumour immunology.
