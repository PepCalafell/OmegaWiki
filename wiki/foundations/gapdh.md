## Definition

Cytoplasmic glycolytic enzyme catalysing the oxidative phosphorylation of glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate, regenerating NADH from NAD+. Beyond glycolysis, GAPDH has well-documented "moonlighting" functions including RNA binding, nuclear translocation, NF-κB modulation, and apoptosis regulation.

## Intuition

GAPDH is the canonical housekeeping protein of mammalian cells but its protein-level expression varies markedly between tumours. Recent work in DLBCL shows GAPDH protein abundance discriminates "glycolytic" from "OxPhos" lymphomas, opening a biomarker route to metabolism-aware therapy.

## Formal notation

Reaction: G3P + NAD+ + Pi ⇌ 1,3-BPG + NADH + H+.

## Key variants

- Glycolytic GAPDH (cytoplasmic, NAD+-dependent).
- Nuclear GAPDH (apoptosis, transcription).
- Cytokine-translation-regulating GAPDH (binds 3′-UTR of IFN-γ mRNA under low glucose).

## Known limitations

- Variable utility as a loading control because its expression shifts with metabolic state.
- Moonlighting functions are context-dependent and incompletely mapped.

## Open problems

- Can GAPDH protein expression generalise as a metabolic biomarker beyond DLBCL?
- How is GAPDH 3′-UTR binding to IFN-γ mRNA regulated in vivo in TILs?

## Relevance to active research

- Marker of OxPhos vs glycolytic DLBCL phenotype.
- Lymphomagenesis: T-cell GAPDH overexpression drives angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma via NF-κB.
- Immune regulation: under low glucose in the TME, GAPDH suppresses IFN-γ translation in T cells.
