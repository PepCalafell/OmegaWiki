## Definition

MAT1A catalyses the ATP-dependent condensation of methionine to S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) — the same biochemistry as MAT2A but encoded by a distinct gene with hepatocyte-restricted expression. MAT1A produces the bulk of liver SAM (high tissue flux) and is the dominant MAT isoform in mature hepatocytes; MAT2A is the ubiquitous, growth-associated isoform expressed in most other tissues and in proliferating hepatocytes.

## Intuition

Liver is the body's major one-carbon metabolism hub, and MAT1A is the workhorse SAM-synthase that supplies that hub. Loss of MAT1A in liver disease (e.g., chronic liver disease, HCC) is associated with SAM depletion and impaired methylation reactions. In cachexia, MAT1A is induced — coupling to NNMT, GNMT and other methyl-acceptor enzymes.

## Formal notation

- Reaction: methionine + ATP → SAM + PPi + Pi.
- Tissue: hepatocyte-restricted (high baseline + further induced in cachexia).
- Isoforms encoded by MAT1A: MATI (homotetramer), MATIII (homodimer).
- Distinct from MAT2A (encoded by separate gene, ubiquitous expression, growth/proliferation-associated).

## Key variants

- MAT1A vs MAT2A switch: MAT1A dominates mature hepatocytes; MAT2A dominates proliferating/dedifferentiated cells (HCC, regenerating liver).
- MAT2B regulatory subunit modulates MAT2A activity but not MAT1A.

## Known limitations

- MAT1A-specific inhibitors not widely available; most MAT pharmacology targets MAT2A (e.g., PF-9366, FIDAS-5).
- FIDAS-5 selectivity over MAT1A vs MAT2A not fully resolved at therapeutic doses.

## Open problems

- Whether MAT1A induction in cachectic liver is a STAT3 / IL6 / NF-κB-mediated transcriptional event.
- Whether liver-specific MAT1A KO rescues cachexia metabolic phenotypes.
