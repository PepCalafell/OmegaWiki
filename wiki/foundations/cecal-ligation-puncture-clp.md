## Definition

CLP is a murine surgical procedure in which the cecum is ligated and punctured to release polymicrobial enteric contents into the peritoneum, producing a clinically relevant systemic polymicrobial sepsis. Severity is graded (mild/moderate/severe) by needle size, puncture count and fraction of cecum ligated.

## Intuition

CLP is the field's "gold-standard" sepsis model because it reproduces the polymicrobial, source-control-dependent nature of human peritonitis-driven sepsis, in contrast to single-PAMP LPS endotoxemia which is more synchronous but less clinically faithful.

## Known limitations

- Surgical variability between operators
- Severity tied to host genetic background
- Limited reproducibility of late-phase immunosuppression phenotypes

## Relevance to active research

In Takahama et al. 2024, severity-graded CLP profiles (severe/moderate/mild) showed up to 68% DEG overlap with sublethal LPS across organs, anchoring the claim that the LPS endotoxemia model captures most of the early organ-wide transcriptional response of bacterial sepsis.
