## Definition

IFITM1 is an interferon-stimulated, transmembrane restriction factor that inhibits viral entry by impeding fusion of the viral envelope with host membranes. It is part of the IFITM family (IFITM1/2/3) and is broadly induced by type I and type II interferons.

## Intuition

IFITM1 sits in cellular membranes and raises the energetic barrier to membrane fusion, blocking many enveloped viruses at the entry step. As an interferon-stimulated gene, its expression marks cells experiencing interferon signaling.

## Formal notation

Type II transmembrane protein localizing to plasma/endolysosomal membranes; restricts fusion-pore formation.

## Key variants

- IFITM1 versus IFITM2/IFITM3 (different subcellular localization and viral specificities).

## Known limitations

- Expression reflects interferon exposure, complicating disease-specific interpretation.
- Antiviral breadth is virus-dependent.

## Open problems

- Non-antiviral roles in lymphocyte adhesion/accumulation in chronic inflammation.
- Mechanistic link between IFITM1 and lymphoid-cell accumulation in COPD.

## Relevance to active research

Identified as a disease-discriminative gene in lymphoid cells (CD4 non-naive T cells, ILCs): high IFITM1 expression drives classification toward COPD and low expression toward asthma, suggesting interferon-driven lymphoid accumulation as a discriminating axis of chronic airway disease.
