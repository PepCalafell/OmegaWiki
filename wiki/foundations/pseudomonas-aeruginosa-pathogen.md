## Definition

Pseudomonas aeruginosa is an opportunistic Gram-negative bacterium that causes acute pneumonia, chronic CF airway infection, wound and burn infection, and bacteraemia. Its virulence is regulated by an integrated quorum-sensing (QS) network (Las, Rhl, PQS systems) that produces both pigment virulence factors (pyocyanin, 1-hydroxyphenazine, phenazine-1-carboxamide, phenazine-1-carboxylic acid) and signalling molecules (HHQ, 3-oxo-C12-l-HSL, PQS). Several of these compounds modulate host AHR.

## Intuition

Pseudomonas times its virulence-factor production to community density via QS. Early/mid-log bacteria mostly produce QS antagonists of AHR (HHQ, 3-oxo-C12-HSL); late-log/stationary bacteria produce phenazine AHR agonists. The host AHR pathway reads this chemistry as a proxy for pathogen density.

## Key variants

- Acute virulent strains (PAO1, PA14) in pneumonia models
- Chronic CF biofilm isolates with reduced acute virulence and persistent QS profiles

## Known limitations

- Antibiotic resistance (β-lactam, fluoroquinolone) is widespread; host-directed therapy via AHR modulation is an emerging alternative
- Strain-to-strain heterogeneity in QS molecule profiles complicates predictive modelling

## Open problems

- How AHR-QS dialogue maps to chronic CF biofilm vs acute pneumonia
- Whether AHR-modulating inhalants could synergise with antibiotics
