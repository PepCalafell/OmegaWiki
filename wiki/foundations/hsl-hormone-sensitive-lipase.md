## Definition

HSL is a rate-limiting cytosolic neutral lipase that hydrolyses diacylglycerol (DAG) to monoacylglycerol (MAG) and free fatty acid (FFA) in adipocytes, acting downstream of [[foundations/atgl-adipose-triglyceride-lipase]] in the lipolytic cascade. PKA-mediated phosphorylation of HSL and perilipin in response to β-adrenergic or natriuretic-peptide signaling translocates HSL to the lipid droplet to access substrate. In cancer cachexia, HSL-driven lipolysis appears to be stronger than in weight-stable patients.

## Intuition

HSL is the second cut on the way from stored triglyceride to free fatty acid. β-adrenergic / natriuretic-peptide signals turn the key (PKA phosphorylates HSL + perilipin), the lipid droplet surface opens, and HSL gets in. In CAC, this entire pipeline is more active.

## Formal notation

- Substrate: diacylglycerol (preferred) and cholesteryl esters; minor TAG activity.
- Activation: PKA phosphorylation at multiple Ser sites; perilipin phosphorylation removes the droplet barrier.
- Inhibition: insulin (via PDE3B → cAMP degradation → reduced PKA activity).

## Key variants

- Multiple tissue isoforms generated by alternative promoter/exon usage.
- Cholesteryl ester hydrolase activity in adrenal and gonadal tissue.

## Known limitations

- HSL-null mice are not protected from CAC adipose loss, suggesting redundancy with other lipases (ATGL, MGLL).
- Pharmacologic inhibitors specific to HSL are limited.
- Activity measurement requires careful preservation of phosphorylation status.

## Open problems

- Relative contribution of HSL vs ATGL to human CAC adipose wasting (mouse studies emphasize ATGL).
- Whether β-AR or NPR-A inhibition (upstream of HSL) is safer than direct lipase inhibition for CAC therapy.

## Relevance to active research

The downstream lipolysis effector implicated in human CAC, discussed centrally in [[papers/cancer-associated-cachexia-bridging-clinical-findings]] and operationally relevant to [[concepts/adipose-lipolysis-cancer-cachexia]].
