## Definition

UCP1 is an inner-mitochondrial-membrane proton transporter expressed in brown adipose tissue (BAT) and in "beige/brite" cells within white adipose tissue depots. By dissipating the proton motive force as heat instead of allowing ATP synthesis, UCP1 enables non-shivering thermogenesis. Its induction in white adipose tissue ("browning") is hypothesized to elevate resting energy expenditure and contribute to cancer cachexia in mouse models, but human evidence for browning-driven CAC REE elevation is weak and conflicting.

## Intuition

UCP1 is the molecular short-circuit that turns the mitochondrion from an ATP factory into a heater. If a tumour induces UCP1 in patient adipose tissue, the patient burns extra calories as heat — a plausible cachexia mechanism. In mice this happens cleanly; in humans, large CT-based BAT-prevalence studies show the OPPOSITE direction (less BAT in CAC patients), questioning the human relevance.

## Formal notation

- Family: SLC25 mitochondrial carrier; UCP1/2/3/4/5 paralogues with distinct functions.
- Activator: long-chain fatty acids; β-adrenergic signaling induces expression.
- Readouts: UCP1 mRNA/protein in adipose biopsy; 18F-FDG PET BAT activity; cold-induced thermogenesis.

## Key variants

- Beige/brite adipocytes: UCP1+ cells within WAT depots, inducible by cold or β3-AR agonists.
- Classical BAT: UCP1-high, interscapular in mice, cervical/supraclavicular in humans.

## Known limitations

- UCP1 protein measurement in clinical samples is technically challenging (low abundance in WAT).
- BAT mass in adult humans is small (<100 g) — quantitative impact on REE may be modest.
- WAT browning evidence in human CAC: Petruzzelli reported UCP1 in 7/8 CAC patients but a 14,134-patient cohort found BAT prevalence LOWER in CAC; PDAC VAT showed no UCP1 differential.

## Open problems

- Does WAT browning meaningfully contribute to human CAC REE elevation, or is it a minor / artefactual finding?
- Why do mouse and human evidence diverge?
- Are there CAC subtypes (e.g., the [[concepts/cac-clinical-subtypes-body-composition]] adipose-loss-only group) where browning is real?

## Relevance to active research

A historically central but increasingly contested mechanism in cachexia adipose biology. Discussed in [[papers/cancer-associated-cachexia-bridging-clinical-findings]] which compiles the human evidence against the mouse-derived browning paradigm, and linked to [[foundations/pthrp-parathyroid-hormone-related-protein]] as a candidate inducer.
