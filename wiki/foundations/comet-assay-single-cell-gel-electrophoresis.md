## Definition

The comet assay is a single-cell electrophoretic method for quantifying DNA strand breaks. Cells embedded in agarose are lysed and electrophoresed; fragmented DNA migrates away from the nucleus forming a "comet tail," whose intensity (% tail DNA, tail moment) scales with DNA damage.

## Intuition

More broken DNA migrates further under the electric field, so a longer/brighter tail = more damage. It is sensitive at the single-cell level and needs no sequencing.

## Key variants

- Alkaline comet assay (single- and double-strand breaks, alkali-labile sites)
- Neutral comet assay (double-strand breaks)

## Known limitations

Semi-quantitative; sensitive to procedural variation; does not identify lesion type.

## Open problems

- Standardization across laboratories

## Relevance to active research

Used by Arenillas et al. to confirm that prolonged hypoxia (1% O2, 36 days) increases DNA damage in PC12 PPGL-derived cells.
