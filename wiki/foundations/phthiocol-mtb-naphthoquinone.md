## Definition

Phthiocol (2-methyl-3-hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone) is a naphthoquinone pigment produced by Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It is a direct AHR ligand (binding and competitive assays both positive in Table 1 of Barreira-Silva 2025). AHR sensing of phthiocol contributes to host resistance to TB; the AHR also mediates phthiocol degradation in epithelial cells.

## Intuition

Phthiocol is one of the canonical examples of a bacteria-derived AHR ligand whose engagement of host AHR triggers a protective antimycobacterial programme. It is also a tool molecule for AHR-PRR studies.

## Key variants

- Native Mtb phthiocol
- Synthetic 2-methyl-3-hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone for in vitro use

## Known limitations

- Affinity and pharmacokinetic data in human cells are limited
- Phthiocol is one of several Mtb-derived AHR-active small molecules; the full repertoire is incomplete

## Open problems

- Whether phthiocol-mimetic AHR agonists could be developed as host-directed TB adjuncts
- Quantitative kinetics of AHR-mediated phthiocol degradation in epithelial vs macrophage cells
