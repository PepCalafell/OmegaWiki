## Definition

SRC is the founding member of the SRC-family non-receptor tyrosine kinases. It associates with the cytoplasmic AHR-HSP90-AIP-PTGES3 complex; AHR ligand activation triggers SRC phosphorylation and subsequent activation of downstream effectors including EGFR and focal adhesion kinase (FAK). The AHR-SRC axis is a non-genomic AHR function that has emerged as a therapeutic vulnerability mediating BRAF-inhibitor resistance in melanoma.

## Intuition

A canonical proto-oncogene that AHR uses as a downstream effector in the cytoplasm — explaining why AHR antagonism produces kinase-pathway phenotypes (BRAFi resistance) that would not be predicted from canonical CYP1A1 transcription alone.

## Formal notation

- SH3-SH2-kinase-CTT domain architecture; activated by autophosphorylation at Y416 and dephosphorylation at inhibitory Y527.
- AHR-SRC: SRC is in the cytosolic AHR chaperone complex; ligand activation triggers SRC phosphorylation cascade → EGFR, FAK, and downstream growth/survival signalling.

## Key variants

- SRC vs FYN, YES, LYN (other SFKs) — overlapping substrate preferences.

## Known limitations

- SRC inhibitors (dasatinib, saracatinib) hit multiple SFKs and other kinases.

## Open problems

- AHR-SRC complex stoichiometry and structural basis.
- Combined BRAFi + AHR-SRC blockade clinical translation.

## Relevance to active research

BRAFi resistance in melanoma (Paris et al. 2022 EMBO Mol Med); androgen receptor / prostate cancer signalling. See [[claims/ahr-src-axis-drives-brafi-resistance-melanoma]], [[concepts/ahr-non-genomic-cytoplasmic-effects]].
