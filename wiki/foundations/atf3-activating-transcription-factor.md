## Definition

ATF3 is a bZIP transcription factor of the ATF/CREB family and an adaptive-response gene induced by diverse cellular stresses, including hypoxia. It can act as repressor or activator depending on dimer partners (e.g. AP1/JUN) and context, and regulates metabolic and macrophage programs.

## Intuition

ATF3 is a stress-responsive "hub" TF: hypoxia and other stresses induce it, and via AP1-family partnerships it reshapes transcription, including tumor-supportive and lipid-metabolic macrophage functions.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *ATF3* / *Atf3*. bZIP; partners with JUN/FOS (AP1) at composite elements.

## Key variants

- Related stress bZIPs: ATF4, CHOP (DDIT3); AP1 subunits FOS/JUN ([[foundations/fos-transcription-factor]]).

## Known limitations

- Dual activator/repressor behavior makes locus-specific effects hard to predict.

## Open problems

- Whether ATF3 is required for hypoxic Lgals3 induction in microglia or only marks the accessible promoter.

## Relevance to active research

Its motif (with AP1) is enriched at the hypoxia-opened *Lgals3* promoter peak in [[papers/hypoxic-stress-dysregulates-functions-glioma-associated]], implicating stress-bZIP signaling in hypoxic myeloid marker induction.
