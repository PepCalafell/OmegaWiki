## Definition

DHPS (deoxyhypusine synthase) is the enzyme that catalyses the first and committed step of hypusine biosynthesis: it transfers the aminobutyl moiety of the polyamine spermidine onto a conserved lysine of eukaryotic translation factor eIF5A, producing deoxyhypusine. A second enzyme, deoxyhypusine hydroxylase (DOHH), then hydroxylates this intermediate to form the unusual amino acid hypusine.

## Intuition

DHPS is the gatekeeper of the only known post-translational hypusine modification. Because eIF5A has historically been considered the sole hypusinated protein, DHPS activity is effectively a master switch for hypusinated-eIF5A function — controlling translation of a subset of mRNAs that otherwise stall the ribosome.

## Formal notation

Spermidine + eIF5A(Lys) → deoxyhypusine-eIF5A (DHPS) → hypusine-eIF5A (DOHH).

## Key variants

- Genetic deletion models: floxed Dhps (loxP exons 2–7) crossed with myeloid ([[foundations/lysm-cre]]) or inducible ([[foundations/cx3cr1-creer-fate-mapping]]) Cre drivers.
- Pharmacological inhibitors: GC7 (N1-guanyl-1,7-diaminoheptane), a spermidine analogue.

## Known limitations

- Whether eIF5A is truly the only hypusinated substrate is unsettled; a DHPS-dependent effect on RIPK1 activation has been reported independently of eIF5A.
- Loss-of-function phenotypes can reflect both translational and putative non-eIF5A roles.

## Open problems

- Which mRNA motifs precisely require hypusinated eIF5A for efficient translation in a given cell type.
- How intracellular polyamine/spermidine availability gates DHPS flux in different biological contexts.

## Relevance to active research

DHPS deletion in myeloid cells blocks the monocyte-to-tissue-resident-macrophage transition across tissues, defining a cell-intrinsic, tissue-agnostic node for macrophage tissue residency. DHPS also shapes T-helper lineage fidelity and macrophage alternative activation through the polyamine–hypusine axis.
