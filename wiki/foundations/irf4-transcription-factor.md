## Definition
IRF4 is a member of the interferon regulatory factor family of transcription factors. Unlike type-I-IFN-driven IRFs, IRF4 is induced by immune signaling and cooperates with partners (e.g. PU.1, BATF) to control lymphocyte and myeloid differentiation. In macrophages it is a key driver of IL-4-induced alternative (M2-like) activation.

## Intuition
IRF4 acts as a context-dependent lineage and activation factor; its partnership with PU.1 at composite EICE/AICE motifs redirects myeloid gene programs toward alternative activation.

## Formal notation
DNA-binding via its IRF/Rel-like domain to ISRE/EICE/AICE composite motifs, frequently as a PU.1 or BATF heterodimer.

## Key variants
- PU.1–IRF4 (EICE) complexes
- BATF–IRF4 (AICE) complexes

## Known limitations
- Highly context-dependent; opposing roles across cell types

## Open problems
- Quantitative rules governing IRF4 versus IRF8 partner choice

## Relevance to active research
Identified as a predicted master regulator of the IL-4-activated (M_IL4) human macrophage state, binding accessible enhancers/promoters of network transcriptional regulators.
