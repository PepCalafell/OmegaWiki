## Definition

Cytosolic manganese-dependent hydrolase that converts L-arginine to L-ornithine and urea. Highly expressed in MDSCs and M2-like TAMs in the TME; ARG2 is its mitochondrial paralog. ARG-mediated arginine catabolism locally depletes the TME of arginine and impairs T-cell function.

## Intuition

Cancer-associated myeloid cells weaponise arginase: by stripping arginine from the TME they shut down TCR-zeta-chain synthesis and T-cell proliferation. Pharmacological ARG inhibition (CB-1158/INCB001158 etc.) restores arginine, T-cell function and tumour regression in preclinical models.

## Formal notation

L-arginine + H2O → L-ornithine + urea (Mn2+ cofactor).

## Key variants

- ARG1 (cytosolic, urea-cycle / MDSC).
- ARG2 (mitochondrial, broader tissue distribution).
- ARG inhibitors (CB-1158/INCB001158, OATD-02).

## Known limitations

- ARG2 compensation limits ARG1-selective inhibition.
- Systemic arginine depletion or restoration may have opposing effects on tumour vs immune cells.

## Open problems

- Combination with ICB in arginine-auxotrophic tumours.
- Tissue-selective delivery to MDSC-rich tumours.

## Relevance to active research

- MDSC effector mechanism.
- Therapeutic target balancing tumour and T-cell arginine needs.
