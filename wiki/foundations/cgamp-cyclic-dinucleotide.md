## Definition

Endogenous high-affinity STING ligand (Kd ≈ 4.59 nM) produced by cGAS upon dsDNA binding. The 2′3′ phosphodiester linkage distinguishes it from bacterial 3′3′ CDNs.

## Intuition

A diffusible cyclic dinucleotide "immunotransmitter" — beyond intracellular signaling, cGAMP is exported by tumor cells and imported by neighboring macrophages, DCs, NK cells, and endothelial cells via SLC19A1, SLC46A2, and LRRC8 volume-regulated anion channels, propagating innate-immune activation across the TME.

## Key variants

ENPP1 is the dominant extracellular cGAMP hydrolase. Synthetic phosphorothioate analogs (e.g., ADU-S100) are hydrolysis-resistant. Non-CDN small-molecule STING agonists (e.g., diABZI, MSA-2) avoid CDN delivery limitations.

## Relevance to active research

Hydrolysis-resistance and the export/import machinery are active therapeutic targets — ENPP1 inhibitors aim to preserve extracellular cGAMP and amplify endogenous STING activation rather than directly agonizing it.
