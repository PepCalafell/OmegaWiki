## Definition

Paneth cells are long-lived secretory epithelial cells localized at the bottom of small-intestinal crypts of Lieberkühn, interdigitating with Lgr5+ stem cells. They secrete antimicrobial peptides (defensins, lysozyme) and constitute the canonical stem-cell niche by providing Wnt3, EGF, Notch ligands, and TGFα to neighboring Lgr5+ stem cells.

## Intuition

Paneth cells co-localize with stem cells in 1:1 contact at the crypt base and supply the Wnt3 signal required for Lgr5+ stem-cell self-renewal. Recent lineage tracing (Böttcher 2021) shows Paneth cells can emerge directly from stem cells, suggesting a self-reinforcing niche where nascent Paneth-cell progenitors signal back to maintain stemness — a finding NiCo (Agrawal 2024) recapitulates by detecting covariation between stem/TA Fa1 (Olfm4/Lgr5/Hopx) and Paneth Fa1 (progenitor signature).

## Key markers

- Mouse: Lyz1, Defa-family α-defensins, Mmp7, Wnt3, EGF.
- Human: LYZ, DEFA5, DEFA6.

## Known limitations

- Paneth cell knockout has variable phenotype depending on remaining niche signals.
- "Deep crypt secretory cells" in colon are functionally analogous but distinct.

## Relevance to active research

Stem-cell niche biology, intestinal regeneration, IBD (Crohn's-disease Paneth-cell dysfunction), microbiome interface.
