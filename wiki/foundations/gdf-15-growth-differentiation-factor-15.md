## Definition

GDF-15 is a divergent TGF-β-superfamily cytokine secreted at low baseline levels by most tissues and dramatically upregulated by cellular stress, including tumour growth, inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction and chemotherapy. Its only known specific receptor, GFRAL, is expressed almost exclusively in hindbrain (area postrema and nucleus tractus solitarius) neurons; GDF-15→GFRAL→RET signaling drives anorexia and nausea. GDF-15 is among the most strongly upregulated circulating proteins in advanced cancer and is a leading therapeutic target for cancer cachexia anorexia.

## Intuition

GDF-15 is the body's "I am in trouble" signal that the brain reads as "stop eating". Tumours hijack this signal at very high circulating levels, producing chronic anorexia and weight loss. Pharmacologically blocking GDF-15 or GFRAL restores feeding in preclinical CAC models and is being tested clinically.

## Formal notation

- Receptor: GFRAL (GDNF family receptor alpha-like), co-receptor RET, hindbrain-restricted expression.
- Plasma reference range: ~200-1200 pg/mL in health; markedly elevated (often >5000 pg/mL) in cachectic cancer patients.
- Downstream: AP-NTS neuronal activation → projections to PBN and CeA → anorexia + conditioned taste aversion.

## Key variants

- Prodomain-bound vs mature GDF-15 (distinct ELISA detection considerations).
- Pharmacology: anti-GDF-15 (e.g., ponsegromab from Pfizer); anti-GFRAL antibodies; soluble GFRAL decoys.

## Known limitations

- GDF-15 also rises in pregnancy (hyperemesis gravidarum), metformin therapy, and many non-cancer conditions — not cancer-specific.
- Plasma levels do not always correlate with tissue source.
- Anti-GDF-15 therapies improve appetite/weight but functional/survival benefit is still being established in late-phase trials.

## Open problems

- Is GDF-15 a CAUSE of cachexia or a marker of underlying disease severity?
- Which patients (by CAC subtype) most benefit from anti-GDF-15?
- Interaction with IL-6 and the central melanocortin system.
