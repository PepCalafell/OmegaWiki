## Definition

Repeated high-platform stress (RHS) places mice on a small elevated platform for repeated daily sessions, triggering a stress response dominated by peripheral noradrenaline release (sympathetic nervous system activation) over HPA-axis corticosterone elevation. Compared to restraint (PPR) and cage-change paradigms, RHS produces the largest plasma noradrenaline rise and is the only paradigm of the three that exacerbates pre-established MC903 atopic-dermatitis-like skin inflammation in mice.

## Relevance to active research

Defines the stressor that selectively activates Pdyn⁺ sympathetic neurons in [[papers/sympathetic-eosinophil-axis-orchestrates-psychological-stress]]; the differential outcome of RHS vs PPR vs cage-change anchors the broader principle that distinct psychological stressors engage segregated sympathetic circuits and imprint distinct peripheral immune signatures.
