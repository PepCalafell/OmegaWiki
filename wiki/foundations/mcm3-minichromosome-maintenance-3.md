## Definition

MCM3 is a subunit of the heterohexameric MCM2–7 replicative helicase that is loaded onto origins during G1 to license DNA replication and unwinds the parental duplex during S phase. MCM proteins are widely used proliferation and replication-licensing markers.

## Intuition

MCM3 marks a cell's capacity to license and fire replication origins. Its HIF-1α-dependent downregulation in normoxic HCC cells links HIF-1α activity to the control of DNA-replication licensing during the cell cycle.

## Formal notation

- Gene: MCM3 (chr6p12.2); UniProt P25205
- Component of the CMG (Cdc45–MCM2/7–GINS) replicative helicase

## Key variants

- Other licensing-complex members: MCM2, MCM4, MCM5, MCM6, MCM7, CDC6, CDT1

## Known limitations

- One subunit of a larger complex; single-marker immunoblot readout of a multi-protein licensing program
- General proliferation marker — changes may reflect altered cycling rather than a direct HIF-1α target relationship

## Open problems

- Whether HIF-1α regulates replication licensing directly or via cell-cycle redistribution

## Relevance to active research

Representative DNA-replication-licensing protein shown to be downregulated in a HIF-1α-dependent manner under normoxia in Huh7 (not HeLa) cells, connecting normoxic HIF-1α to cell-cycle control. Relevant to hypoxia and cell-cycle themes.
