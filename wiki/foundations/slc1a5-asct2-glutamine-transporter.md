## Definition

Sodium-coupled neutral amino acid transporter that mediates uptake of glutamine, alanine, serine, threonine, and asparagine. Upregulated in many cancers, where it sustains the high glutaminolysis flux of proliferating tumour cells and outcompetes immune cells for available glutamine.

## Intuition

If glutamine is the second most important fuel after glucose, SLC1A5 is the gate. Tumour overexpression of SLC1A5 depletes TME glutamine, triggering ER stress in myeloid cells and forcing immunosuppressive polarisation.

## Formal notation

Glutamine + Na+(out) ⇌ Glutamine(in) + Na+(in) (electrogenic symport).

## Key variants

- ASCT2 (SLC1A5, glutamine-preferring).
- ASCT1 (SLC1A4, related but distinct substrate preference).
- Small-molecule inhibitors (V-9302) — limited clinical traction.

## Known limitations

- Genetic SLC1A5 knockout is partially compensated by other neutral amino acid transporters (SNAT1/2, LAT1).
- V-9302 selectivity has been challenged.

## Open problems

- Tumour-selective targeting that spares immune-cell glutamine uptake.
- Whether SLC1A5 inhibition can re-sensitise tumours to ICB clinically.

## Relevance to active research

- Driver of glutamine starvation in the TME.
- Mechanistic link to IRE1α/XBP1 ER stress in myeloid cells.
- Target for combination metabolic immunotherapy.
