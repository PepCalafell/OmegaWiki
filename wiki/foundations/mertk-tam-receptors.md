## Definition

The TAM family — TYRO3, AXL, MERTK — comprises three structurally related receptor tyrosine kinases that mediate efferocytosis (clearance of apoptotic cells), regulate innate immune output, and modulate cancer and viral biology. Their two principal ligands are GAS6 and protein S, which bridge phosphatidylserine (PtdSer) on apoptotic cells to the receptor's extracellular Ig domains. TAM receptor engagement on macrophages triggers engulfment of the PtdSer-displaying target plus an anti-inflammatory cytokine programme (TGFβ, IL-10, PGE2 up; TNF, IL-1β, IL-6 down).

## Intuition

Apoptotic cells flip phosphatidylserine to the outer leaflet but the macrophage cannot read PtdSer directly through TAM receptors — bridging proteins (GAS6 or protein S) bind PtdSer at one end and TAM receptors at the other. Engagement of TAM receptors triggers the engulfment programme and shuts down inflammatory output, ensuring quiet, daily clearance of apoptosis without triggering autoimmunity. Loss of MERTK (or MFGE8 / GAS6 / TIM4) causes accumulation of dying cells and SLE-like autoimmunity.

## Formal notation

- Family: receptor tyrosine kinases with Ig-like + fibronectin-III extracellular domains
- Genes: *TYRO3*, *AXL*, *MERTK*
- Ligands:
  - GAS6 — vitamin-K-dependent; binds all three TAMs
  - Protein S (PROS1) — vitamin-K-dependent; binds TYRO3 and MERTK
- Intermediary: GAS6/PROS1 Gla domain binds PtdSer on target cell; their LG domain binds TAM receptor Ig domain → bridges target to phagocyte
- Signal output: PI3K/AKT (survival, engulfment), STAT1 inhibition (anti-inflammatory), SOCS1/3 induction
- Function:
  - Efferocytosis machinery (canonical role)
  - Inhibition of TLR-driven inflammation (negative feedback after pathogen response)
  - Trans-phagocytic regulation of platelets (GAS6/PROS1 also affect platelet aggregation)
  - Cancer: AXL upregulation drives EMT, drug resistance, and immune evasion
  - Virology: AXL and MERTK engaged by enveloped viruses (Ebola, Zika, dengue) for viral entry via apoptotic mimicry
- Disease:
  - MERTK / TYRO3 / AXL triple KO: severe blindness (RPE efferocytic failure), spontaneous autoimmunity, sterility
  - Single MERTK KO: SLE-like autoimmunity
  - MFGE8 KO: autoimmune phenotype
  - GAS6 KO: defective efferocytosis, mild
  - AXL upregulation: EMT, gefitinib-resistant lung cancer

## Key variants

- Each TAM has distinct tissue distribution: TYRO3 in nervous system; AXL in hematopoietic + tumour cells; MERTK in macrophages + RPE.
- AXL splice variants: full-length vs membrane-truncated.
- Soluble TAM receptors (sMERTK, sAXL) — circulating decoys generated by ADAM proteases.

## Known limitations

- Bridging-ligand redundancy makes single-receptor knockouts only partially penetrant.
- Drug development is challenging because TAMs have homeostatic, anti-inflammatory roles that should not be blocked indiscriminately.
- AXL inhibitors in cancer have produced mixed clinical results.

## Open problems

- The relative contribution of efferocytosis-induced tolerance vs cytokine-mediated suppression.
- Whether AXL inhibitors should be combined with checkpoint inhibitors (TAM-driven immunosuppression in tumours).
- The role of vitamin K dependency on GAS6/protein S in clinical anti-coagulation contexts.

## Relevance to active research

For my hypoxia-NF-κB work this is contextual. NF-κB drives pro-inflammatory output that is normally suppressed by TAM-receptor signalling — so chronically NF-κB-active macrophages may have impaired efferocytic capacity. AXL is also induced by HIF1α in some tumour contexts, linking hypoxia to cancer-associated AXL biology and immunotherapy resistance. Worth tracking as a potential therapeutic node in hypoxia-driven tumour immune evasion.
