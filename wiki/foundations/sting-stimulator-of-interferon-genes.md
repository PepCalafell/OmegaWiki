## Definition

ER-resident transmembrane adaptor that binds cyclic dinucleotides (2′3′-cGAMP and bacterial CDNs). On ligand binding, STING oligomerizes, translocates ER→ERGIC, recruits TBK1, and licenses IRF3 (and NF-κB) activation, driving type I IFN and inflammatory cytokine transcription.

## Intuition

The "router" of cytosolic DNA sensing: routes the cGAS-generated cGAMP signal toward (a) canonical IRF3/type I IFN, (b) non-canonical NF-κB, (c) autophagy, (d) programmed cell death, (e) metabolic reprogramming. The relative weighting of these outputs is cell-type-dependent and underlies the divergent effects of STING agonism across the TME.

## Key variants

Multiple human STING allelic variants (HAQ, AQ, REF) with differential agonist binding. Germline activating mutations cause SAVI (STING-associated vasculopathy with onset in infancy). DMXAA fully activates only murine STING — a key cause of the translational gap for that compound.

## Known limitations

Tumor cells frequently silence STING epigenetically (promoter methylation, EZH2/KDM5-mediated histone marks). Chronic, low-level STING activation can produce pro-tumorigenic NF-κB output instead of canonical antitumor IRF3-IFN. T cells are uniquely sensitive to STING-induced cytotoxicity.

## Relevance to active research

Therapeutic target with substantial preclinical anti-tumor activity but a long history of clinical underperformance with direct agonists. Active strategies: ADCs, hydrogel-sustained release, ENPP1/TREX1 inhibitors that boost endogenous cGAMP, epigenetic priming with DNMT/EZH2 inhibitors, NK-cell combination therapies.
