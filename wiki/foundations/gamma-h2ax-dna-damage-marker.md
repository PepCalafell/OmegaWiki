## Definition

γH2AX is histone H2AX phosphorylated at Ser139, formed rapidly and abundantly at sites of DNA double-strand breaks. Foci of γH2AX (by immunofluorescence) or total γH2AX (by Western blot) serve as a sensitive, quantitative marker of DNA damage.

## Intuition

Each double-strand break nucleates a spreading patch of γH2AX over surrounding chromatin, amplifying the signal so that even a few breaks produce a detectable focus.

## Key variants

- Foci counting by immunofluorescence (per-cell quantification)
- Bulk γH2AX immunoblot
- Flow-cytometric γH2AX

## Known limitations

Also induced by replication stress and apoptosis, not exclusively by frank DSBs; foci resolution requires high-quality imaging.

## Open problems

- Distinguishing repairable from persistent/lethal damage by γH2AX kinetics

## Relevance to active research

Used with PARP1 and the comet assay by Arenillas et al. to show that prolonged hypoxia increases DNA damage in PC12 PPGL-derived cells.
