## Definition

Functional MRI is a non-invasive neuroimaging technique that detects regional changes in blood oxygenation (BOLD signal) as a proxy for neuronal activity, allowing spatial and temporal mapping of brain activity in response to stimuli or at rest. In cancer cachexia research, task-based fMRI (e.g., visual food stimuli) and resting-state fMRI are used to map hypothalamic and brainstem responses that distinguish anorexic from non-anorexic cancer patients.

## Intuition

fMRI lets you see WHICH brain regions respond DIFFERENTLY in cachectic vs non-cachectic patients, in vivo, without injecting tracers. Applied to appetite regulation, it has shown hypothalamic activity differs between lung-cancer patients with vs without anorexia, supporting CNS involvement in human CAC.

## Formal notation

- Contrast: BOLD (blood oxygen level dependent) — T2* sensitive to deoxyhemoglobin.
- Modalities: task-based (visual food cue paradigm), resting-state functional connectivity, structural MRI for grey/white matter morphometry.
- Spatial resolution ~2-3 mm; temporal resolution ~1-2 s (TR-dependent).

## Key variants

- High-field fMRI (7T) for hypothalamus-specific resolution.
- Multi-echo fMRI for improved signal in subcortical regions.
- Diffusion MRI / structural MRI for accompanying anatomical measures.

## Known limitations

- BOLD is an INDIRECT proxy for neuronal activity (hemodynamic coupling).
- Hypothalamic and brainstem signals suffer from physiological noise (cardiac, respiratory).
- Small cohorts in CAC fMRI studies (<25 patients) limit generalizability.
- Confounders: medications, fasting state, motion.

## Open problems

- Whether hypothalamic fMRI can serve as a pharmacodynamic biomarker in early-phase trials of central-acting CAC drugs (e.g., anamorelin, anti-GDF-15).
- Standardization of food-cue paradigms across centres.
- Mapping cachexia-related fMRI signatures to molecular biomarkers (cytokines, GDF-15).
