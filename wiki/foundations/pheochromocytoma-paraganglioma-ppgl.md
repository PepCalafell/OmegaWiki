## Definition

PPGLs are rare neural-crest-derived neuroendocrine tumors arising from chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla (pheochromocytoma) or from extra-adrenal sympathetic/parasympathetic paraganglia (paraganglioma). They are among the most heritable human tumors, frequently driven by single mutually-exclusive germline or somatic mutations.

## Intuition

PPGLs originate from oxygen-sensing, catecholamine-handling cells. Sympathetic (adrenal/abdominal) tumors typically secrete catecholamines; parasympathetic (carotid body / head-and-neck) tumors are usually biochemically silent.

## Key variants

- Sympathetic catecholamine-secreting PPGL (adrenal medulla, thoracic/abdominal paraganglia)
- Parasympathetic PPGL (carotid body, head and neck)
- Molecular clusters: pseudohypoxia (VHL, SDHx, EPAS1), kinase signaling (RET, NF1, HRAS), Wnt-altered

## Known limitations

Rare, therapeutic-orphan tumors; lack of robust biomarkers for metastatic behavior and of suitable animal models.

## Open problems

- Predicting metastatic risk
- Effective systemic therapy for metastatic disease

## Relevance to active research

The tumor type in which EPAS1 convergence with high-altitude adaptation was demonstrated; HIF2α inhibitors (belzutifan) are in clinical trials for PPGL.
