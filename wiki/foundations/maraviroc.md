## Definition

Maraviroc is a small-molecule, orally bioavailable antagonist of the chemokine receptor CCR5. It was originally developed and approved (2007) as an HIV entry inhibitor (CCR5-tropic HIV-1 uses CCR5 as a coreceptor) and is increasingly repurposed in immuno-oncology to block CCR5-dependent immune-cell trafficking.

## Intuition

CCR5 is used by several immune populations to home to inflamed tissue and lymph nodes. By occupying CCR5, maraviroc blunts CCR5-CCL5/CCL3/CCL4-driven chemotaxis. Its relatively short half-life (~16 h) makes it suitable for transient, windowed blockade rather than sustained pathway shutdown.

## Formal notation

Competitive/allosteric CCR5 antagonist; binds a transmembrane cavity of CCR5, stabilizing a conformation that prevents chemokine signaling.

## Key variants

- Other CCR5-pathway agents: leronlimab (anti-CCR5 mAb), cenicriviroc (dual CCR2/CCR5 antagonist).
- Combination immuno-oncology regimens (e.g. CCR5 blockade + anti-PD-1, PICCASSO trial).

## Known limitations

- Systemic, prolonged CCR5 blockade affects many CCR5-expressing populations (effector T cells, tissue-resident macrophages, neutrophils) with both pro- and antitumor functions, blunting net benefit.
- Short half-life requires careful timing for windowed effects.

## Open problems

- Optimal scheduling of transient CCR5 blockade relative to vaccination or checkpoint blockade.
- Whether selective targeting of monocyte-derived CCR5+ cells can be achieved pharmacologically rather than genetically.

## Relevance to active research

In [[chemokine-defined-macrophage-niches-establish-spatial]], a single pre-vaccination dose of maraviroc selectively blocked migration of antigen-bearing Ly6C⁺ moDCs to draining lymph nodes (~76% reduction) without impairing classical DC migration, converting an immunosuppressive monocyte-derived antigen-presentation step into a window of enhanced DC cross-priming and improving neoantigen vaccine efficacy.
