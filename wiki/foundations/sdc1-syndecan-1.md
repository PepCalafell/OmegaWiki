## Definition
Syndecan-1 (SDC1, also CD138) is a transmembrane heparan sulfate proteoglycan that modulates growth-factor signaling and cell adhesion. Highly expressed on plasma cells and epithelial-derived solid tumours, SDC1 is the antigen for the antibody-drug conjugate indatuximab ravtansine and is also shed into plasma, allowing soluble-CD138 biomarker measurements.

## Intuition
SDC1 sits at the cell surface where heparan sulfate chains co-receptor for FGF, HGF, VEGF and Wnt ligands; its high expression on solid tumours and accessibility make it both a biomarker (plasma CD138) and a therapeutic handle (ADC).

## Formal notation
- Domain layout: extracellular heparan-sulfate-modified ectodomain + single-pass TM + short cytoplasmic tail
- Shedding: matrix metalloproteinase-mediated release of ectodomain into plasma

## Key variants
- Membrane-anchored SDC1
- Soluble (shed) SDC1 ectodomain

## Known limitations
- Expression heterogeneity within and across cancer types.
- Indatuximab ravtansine clinical development has been concentrated in multiple myeloma to date.

## Open problems
- Identification of solid-tumour types within TPCPA-style atlases where SDC1 levels predict ADC response.

## Relevance to active research
Highlighted by TPCPA as a top solid-cancer-enriched proteome marker; its accessibility on cell surface and in plasma, plus existing ADC chemistry, makes SDC1 one of the most translatable hits from the atlas.
