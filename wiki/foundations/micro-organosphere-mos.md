## Definition

The micro-organosphere (MOS) is a high-throughput tumour-organoid platform that uses droplet emulsion microfluidics to generate thousands of small Matrigel droplets, each containing tumour and stromal cells from a patient-derived single-cell suspension. MOSs preserve infiltrating immune populations after enzymatic dissociation, enabling rapid (within 14 days of biopsy) multiplexed drug testing of anti-PD1 and bispecific antibodies.

## Intuition

MOS is the high-throughput counterpart to ALI and PDOTS: it sacrifices native architecture but recovers immune cell diversity in a microfluidic-scalable format suitable for clinical-decision timelines.

## Key variants

- Standard CRC MOS — bulk colorectal tumour reaggregation.
- Combination-therapy MOS — multiplexed ICB and small-molecule screening.

## Known limitations

- Native architecture disrupted by single-cell dissociation.
- Small cell number per sphere limits per-organoid imaging depth.
- Non-physiological ECM.

## Open problems

- Reproducibility across labs.
- Larger prospective ICB validation cohorts.

## Relevance to active research

Used by the Polak/Zhang/Kuo 2024 review as the canonical example of a high-throughput reconstituted immune organoid platform with correlative ICB-response data.
