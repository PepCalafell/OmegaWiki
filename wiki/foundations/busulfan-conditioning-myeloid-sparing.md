## Definition

A conditioning strategy that uses the alkylating agent busulfan to deplete hematopoietic progenitors and short-lived circulating myeloid cells while sparing long-lived, self-renewing tissue-resident macrophages — in contrast to lethal total-body irradiation, which replaces essentially the entire myeloid compartment (including resident macrophages) with donor bone-marrow-derived cells.

## Intuition

By comparing irradiation chimeras (all myeloid cells donor-derived) against busulfan chimeras (resident macrophages remain host-derived, circulating cells donor-derived), one can assign a function to host tissue-resident macrophages versus circulating/recruited cells. Reconstituting with a knockout donor (e.g. Ccl2⁻ᐟ⁻) under each regimen isolates the contribution of the gene in resident versus recruited compartments.

## Key variants

- Pairing with congenic CD45.1/CD45.2 markers to track host- vs donor-derived cells.
- Knockout-donor chimeras (Ccl2⁻ᐟ⁻, Ccr5⁻ᐟ⁻, Ccr2⁻ᐟ⁻) to map gene function by compartment.

## Known limitations

- Busulfan sparing of resident macrophages is partial and tissue-dependent.
- Pharmacologic off-target effects of busulfan distinct from irradiation can confound direct comparison.

## Open problems

- Generalizing the resident-vs-recruited dissection to organs where resident macrophage half-lives differ.

## Relevance to active research

In [[chemokine-defined-macrophage-niches-establish-spatial]], busulfan-conditioned Ccl2⁻ᐟ⁻ chimeras (which retain CCL2-producing host IMs) grew tumors like WT, whereas irradiated Ccl2⁻ᐟ⁻ chimeras (which lack CCL2 in all myeloid cells, including resident IMs) had ~7-fold lower metastatic burden — pinpointing IM-derived CCL2, not recMac/DC/endothelial CCL2, as the dominant driver of recMac recruitment.
