mTOR is a serine/threonine kinase that nucleates two complexes — mTORC1 (Raptor-containing; rapamycin-sensitive) and mTORC2 (Rictor-containing) — that integrate growth-factor, amino-acid, and energy signals to control protein synthesis, lipid metabolism, autophagy, and immune cell fate. In macrophages, mTORC1 drives SREBP-mediated lipogenesis, supports M2 polarization in some contexts, and is required for microglial glucose→glutamine switching under aglycemia. Rapamycin reduces alveolar-macrophage numbers via SREBP suppression.
