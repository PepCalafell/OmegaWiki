## Definition

The most common primary cancer of the liver, arising from hepatocytes, typically on a background of chronic liver injury (hepatitis B/C, alcohol, MASH/NASH, aflatoxin). Distinguished from intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (bile-duct origin) and combined HCC-CCA.

## Intuition

A cancer dominated by underlying chronic liver disease and resectability constraints. Surgical resection or ablation is the curative standard for early-stage disease, but recurrence is common; systemic therapy (sorafenib → atezo+bev IMbrave150 → other ICB combos) is the standard for unresectable disease.

## Key variants

- Early-stage / resectable HCC — neoadjuvant ICB trials (e.g., NCT03916627)
- Unresectable / advanced HCC — first-line atezo+bev (IMbrave150) or other regimens
- Etiology-stratified subtypes: HBV-, HCV-, MASH-, alcohol-driven HCC

## Known limitations

- Treatment response heterogeneous; reliable biomarkers for ICB response are lacking.
- Cirrhotic background complicates immunotherapy interpretation and surveillance.

## Open problems

- Cellular and molecular determinants of ICB response — central topic of Hamon et al. 2025.
- Prevention of recurrence after curative resection.
