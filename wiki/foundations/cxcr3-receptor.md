## Definition

CXCR3 is a G-protein-coupled receptor expressed on effector and memory T cells, NK cells, and other lymphocytes that binds the IFN-γ-induced ligands CXCL9, CXCL10, and CXCL11 to drive chemotaxis into inflamed and tumour tissue.

## Intuition

CXCR3 is the receptor arm of the CXCL9/10/11 axis: where CXCL9 marks a T-cell-recruiting microenvironment, CXCR3⁺ lymphocytes are the cells that respond to and accumulate along that chemokine gradient. The system is central to the recruitment of stem-like CD8 T cells underlying anti-PD-1/PD-L1 efficacy.

## Formal notation

CXCL9/10/11 → CXCR3 → Gαi signalling → lymphocyte chemotaxis and retention.

## Key variants

- Splice variants CXCR3-A (chemotactic) and CXCR3-B (angiostatic).

## Known limitations

- Receptor expression is dynamic with activation state, complicating its use as a static cell-identity marker.

## Open problems

- Dissecting CXCR3-dependent vs -independent routes of intratumoral T-cell recruitment during ICI therapy.

## Relevance to active research

The CXCR3–CXCL9/10/11 axis is proposed as essential for the functional CD8⁺ T-cell response under PD-1 blockade and is the mechanistic counterpart to CXCL9⁺ macrophage abundance as an ICI-response biomarker.
