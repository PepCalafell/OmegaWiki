## Definition

MNAM is the SAM-dependent N1-methylation product of nicotinamide (NAM), produced by NNMT. It is a stable methylated end-product secreted into blood and urine; circulating MNAM reflects systemic NNMT activity. MNAM has been reported to have independent biological activities (anti-inflammatory, vasoprotective, effects on hepatic glucose metabolism) beyond being a flux marker.

## Intuition

NNMT consumes NAM (an NAD+ precursor) and SAM to produce MNAM and SAH. Elevated MNAM is therefore a non-invasive readout of NNMT activity and a candidate biomarker of one-carbon-pathway activation in the liver and other NNMT-expressing tissues. The liver is the dominant source of plasma MNAM.

## Formal notation

- Formation: NAM + SAM →(NNMT)→ MNAM + SAH.
- Excretion: primarily renal, in urine.
- Reported bioactivities: PGE2-mediated vasoprotective effects; effects on hepatic SIRT1; complex roles in adipose / obesity biology.

## Key variants

- Plasma MNAM (steady-state biomarker).
- Urinary MNAM (cumulative biomarker of NNMT activity over collection window).
- Tissue MNAM (direct readout in liver / muscle / adipose biopsies).

## Known limitations

- MNAM bioactivity reports are heterogeneous; some effects may be artefacts of high pharmacological dosing.
- Renal clearance rate affects MNAM interpretation in patients with kidney disease.

## Open problems

- Whether plasma / urinary MNAM provides quantitative cachexia-severity grading in patients.
- Whether MNAM itself contributes mechanistically to cachexia phenotypes or is a passive end-product.
