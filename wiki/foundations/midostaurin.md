## Definition

Midostaurin is a staurosporine-derived multi-kinase (broad-spectrum) inhibitor, clinically approved for FLT3-mutation-positive acute myeloid leukemia and for advanced systemic mastocytosis (mutant KIT). Its low selectivity is characteristic — it inhibits FLT3, KIT, PKC, and many other kinases.

## Intuition

A classic example that low target selectivity does not preclude clinical approval: Midostaurin is broadly active across the kinome yet clinically useful, making it a useful probe for off-target/polypharmacology studies.

## Formal notation

- Designated clinical targets: FLT3, KIT (and PKC).
- Kinobeads (Klaeger et al. 2017): high affinity for TBK1 among its many targets.

## Key variants

- Staurosporine (parent), other indolocarbazole kinase inhibitors.

## Known limitations

- Broad polypharmacology produces diverse off-target effects.
- Cellular effect spectrum not predictable from intended-target annotation alone.

## Open problems

- Full mapping of which of its many targets drive efficacy vs toxicity in each indication.

## Relevance to active research

In [[papers/integrative-epigenome-based-strategy-unbiased-functional]], Midostaurin was used at the kinobeads EC50 for TBK1; it suppressed Ifnb1 and IFN-stimulated genes in LPS-stimulated mouse macrophages and in human THP-1 cells, reduced LPS-induced p-STAT1, and stabilized TBK1 in CETSA — implicating TBK1 inhibition as the mechanism. Its epigenomic effect was near-identical to Momelotinib.
