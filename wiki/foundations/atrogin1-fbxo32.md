## Definition

Atrogin-1 (FBXO32 / MAFbx) is an F-box-containing E3 ubiquitin-ligase subunit of the SCF complex that is highly expressed in skeletal and cardiac muscle and is the second canonical atrogene (with [[foundations/murf1-trim63]]). It is FOXO-regulated and upregulated by catabolic stimuli including denervation, fasting, glucocorticoids, sepsis and (in mouse models) cancer cachexia. Substrates include MyoD, eIF3-f, and other regulators of muscle anabolism — Atrogin-1 thus combines a degradative role with downregulation of protein synthesis machinery.

## Intuition

Atrogin-1 attacks the synthesis side as well as the breakdown side: by ubiquitinating MyoD and eIF3-f, it shrinks the muscle's anabolic capacity in addition to flagging proteins for destruction. In human CAC its expression is reported as elevated in some cohorts and unchanged in others — the field needs unified definitions to resolve.

## Formal notation

- Complex: SCF E3 ligase (SKP1-CUL1-F-box) where Atrogin-1 supplies the F-box substrate-recognition unit.
- Substrates: MyoD (myogenic TF), eIF3-f (translation initiation), calcineurin A, FOXO (negative feedback).
- Upstream: FOXO1/FOXO3 (canonical); attenuated by IGF-1/PI3K/AKT.

## Key variants

- Heart-specific paralogues with overlapping E3-ligase activity.

## Known limitations

- mRNA-protein-activity correlation is imperfect.
- Inhibition pharmacology is preclinical.
- Knockout mice have phenotype but tissue-specific roles complicate interpretation.

## Open problems

- Does Atrogin-1 inhibition rescue both muscle mass AND anabolic resistance to feeding?
- Patient-stratification: which CAC subtypes show Atrogin-1 dominance?
- Crosstalk with IL-6 / NNMT / one-carbon-metabolism programmes described in [[papers/multi-omics-profiling-cachexia-targeted-tissues]].
