## Definition

Cancer-associated fibroblasts are a heterogeneous and phenotypically plastic group of activated fibroblasts that constitute a major, generally pro-tumorigenic component of the tumour microenvironment. They secrete ECM components, growth factors (WNT, FGF), cytokines (IL-6, TGFβ) and chemokines that regulate tumour growth, immunity and therapy response.

## Key variants

- Myofibroblastic CAF (myCAF) — αSMA+, ECM remodelling.
- Inflammatory CAF (iCAF) — IL-6, CXCL12, immunosuppression.
- Antigen-presenting CAF (apCAF) — MHC II+.
- Tissue-specific CAFs (PDAC, CRC, breast, lung).

## Known limitations

- Heterogeneity makes single-marker definitions inadequate.
- Few selective targeting strategies clinically validated.

## Relevance to active research

CAF-secreted WNT promotes PDAC organoid growth and CRC EMT, while tumour-secreted IL-1 and TGFβ modulate distinct CAF subtypes — relevant to reconstituted PDO co-culture experiments.
