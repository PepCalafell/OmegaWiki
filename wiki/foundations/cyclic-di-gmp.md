## Definition

Cyclic di-GMP is a ubiquitous bacterial second messenger that regulates the transition
between motile and sessile (biofilm) lifestyles and many other behaviours. It is
synthesized by diguanylate cyclases (GGDEF domains) and degraded by phosphodiesterases
(EAL/HD-GYP domains).

## Intuition

A bacterial internal "switch" molecule: high c-di-GMP favours biofilm/sessility, low
favours motility/virulence. Opposing enzymes set its level.

## Formal notation

GGDEF domain (Pfam PF00990) = synthesis; EAL domain (PF00563) = degradation; PAS-fold
domain (PF08447) = ligand/environmental sensing often coupled to these enzymes.

## Key variants

GGDEF, EAL, HD-GYP enzyme classes; cyclic dinucleotides more broadly act as immune
regulators across kingdoms (cf. cGAS-STING).

## Known limitations

Local pools and effector-specific outputs make global level a coarse readout.

## Relevance to active research

GGDEF, EAL and PAS domains are enriched in commensal T3SS effector candidates (and
absent from pathogen effectors), suggesting c-di-GMP signalling participates in
interkingdom communication.
