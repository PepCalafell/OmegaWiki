## Definition

IRF1 is an interferon regulatory factor that acts mainly as a transcriptional activator of interferon-stimulated and immune-activation genes, particularly downstream of IFN-γ.

## Intuition

IRF1 drives pro-inflammatory, antigen-presentation, and IFN-γ-responsive programs, and is functionally antagonized by the repressor IRF2 at shared ISRE sites.

## Formal notation

IRF1 binds ISRE/IRF-E motifs to activate target transcription; competes with IRF2 for the same elements, with the IRF1:IRF2 balance setting output strength.

## Key variants

- Broader IRF family (IRF1–IRF9) with activator/repressor roles.

## Known limitations

- Inferred regulon activity (e.g. via SCENIC) is correlational, not perturbational.

## Open problems

- Direct demonstration that IRF1 switches macrophage interferon-state identity.

## Relevance to active research

Identified as the activating TF preferentially harbored by CXCL9+ (IFN-γ) TAMs, antagonistic to IRF2 in IFIT1+ (IFN-I) TAMs, implicating distinct transcriptional control of the two interferon-related macrophage states.
