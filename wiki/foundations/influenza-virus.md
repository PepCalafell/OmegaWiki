## Definition

Influenza viruses are segmented negative-strand RNA viruses (Orthomyxoviridae) causing seasonal and pandemic respiratory disease. Influenza is the most extensively studied virus in AHR research: TCDD-driven AHR activation increases viral titres and mortality, while ITE, FICZ and PCB126 produce divergent ligand-specific outcomes. AHR signalling in endothelium during FluV can reduce lung vascular leakage.

## Intuition

FluV is the canonical "ligand-specific outcome" infection for AHR: same receptor, four different ligands, four different phenotypes. The lesson generalises to other respiratory viruses including SARS-CoV-2.

## Key variants

- H1N1 (PR8 laboratory strain)
- H3N2 seasonal viruses
- H5N1 / H7N9 highly pathogenic avian influenza

## Known limitations

- Vaccine effectiveness varies year to year due to antigenic drift
- Antivirals (oseltamivir, baloxavir) have limited window of efficacy

## Open problems

- Whether ligand-selective AHR modulators could be deployed as host-directed adjuncts in severe influenza
- Cell-type-specific (epithelium vs endothelium) AHR-targeting strategies
