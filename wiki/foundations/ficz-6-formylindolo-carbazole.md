## Definition

FICZ is an endogenous, high-affinity AHR agonist generated by photo-oxidation of tryptophan. Despite high binding affinity comparable to TCDD, FICZ is rapidly metabolised by CYP1A1, producing transient (rather than sustained) AHR activation — biology this distinct from TCDD has been used to dissect AHR signalling.

## Intuition

FICZ is the canonical example of a "ligand that gets cleaned up": it activates AHR strongly but its own product, CYP1A1, degrades it, so the AHR target-gene response is short-lived. This makes FICZ a tool to model physiological (rather than toxicological) AHR signalling.

## Key variants

- Endogenous photo-derived FICZ
- Synthetic FICZ used as research reagent

## Known limitations

- Half-life so short that systemic delivery is impractical
- Pro- and anti-inflammatory effects reported depending on dose, tissue, and immune context

## Open problems

- Whether FICZ levels in skin contribute physiologically to UV-induced AHR signalling in keratinocytes
- The microbiota's contribution to systemic FICZ-like ligands

## Relevance to active research

Used throughout [[papers/aryl-hydrocarbon-receptor-rehabilitated-target-therapeutic]] as the prototypical transient AHR agonist; FICZ ameliorated IMQ-induced psoriasis-like inflammation and TNBS colitis but enhanced Th17 polarisation in EAE — illustrating the ligand- and context-specificity of AHR responses.
