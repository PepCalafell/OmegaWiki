## Definition

PKCα (gene *PRKCA*) is a conventional, calcium- and diacylglycerol-dependent serine/threonine protein kinase of the PKC family. It transduces signals from phospholipase-C-coupled receptors and participates in proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and immune regulation, including the modulation of NF-κB-induced genes.

## Intuition

As a conventional PKC, PKCα integrates second-messenger signals (Ca²⁺, DAG) and is a hub linking receptor activation to downstream transcriptional programs. In macrophages it is associated with anti-inflammatory processes and can negatively regulate NF-κB-driven inflammatory genes.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *PRKCA*; conventional PKC (cPKC), with PKCβ and PKCγ
- Maturation requires ordered phosphorylation: activation-loop T497 (by PDPK1), then turn-motif and hydrophobic-motif autophosphorylation
- Activation: Ca²⁺ + DAG binding recruits PKCα to membranes and relieves pseudosubstrate autoinhibition

## Key variants

- Conventional (α, βI/βII, γ), novel (δ, ε, η, θ), and atypical (ζ, ι/λ) PKC subfamilies
- Activation-loop phospho-T497 as a readout of catalytically competent enzyme

## Known limitations

- Broad substrate range and isoform redundancy obscure isoform-specific roles
- Phorbol-ester tools activate multiple cPKC/nPKC isoforms non-selectively

## Open problems

- How PKCα activity biases human macrophages toward immunosuppressive phenotypes
- Whether PKCα is druggable for macrophage reprogramming without systemic toxicity

## Relevance to active research

Identified by phosphoproteomic footprinting as a central kinase of in vitro immunosuppressive human macrophages and the highest-centrality node in the M2a signaling network; its activation-loop residue T497 shows higher phosphorylation in both M2a and M2c states relative to M1. It is a known substrate-relationship partner of PDPK1 and is reported to support a protumoral immunosuppressive macrophage phenotype.
