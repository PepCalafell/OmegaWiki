## Definition

NCOR2 (SMRT) is a transcriptional corepressor that bridges unliganded nuclear receptors and other transcription factors to histone-deacetylase complexes, repressing target genes. It participates in metabolic and inflammatory gene regulation and is implicated in shaping macrophage phenotypes.

## Intuition

NCOR2 is a scaffolding repressor: by recruiting HDAC activity to specific transcription factors it silences gene programs, and the balance of corepressor vs coactivator occupancy tunes inflammatory output. Its activity can suppress inflammation, fitting an immunosuppressive macrophage role.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *NCOR2*; corepressor partnering nuclear receptors (e.g. PPARγ) and HDAC3-containing complexes
- Regulatory phosphorylation (e.g. S149, S152) can modulate corepressor function/localization

## Key variants

- Paralog NCOR1 with overlapping but distinct repressor functions
- Splice variants altering receptor-interaction domains

## Known limitations

- Broad partner repertoire makes target-gene attribution context-dependent
- Phospho-regulation of NCOR2 is incompletely characterized

## Open problems

- How NCOR2 phosphorylation reprograms macrophages toward immunosuppression
- Whether NCOR2-HDAC axes are druggable for macrophage repolarization

## Relevance to active research

Identified as a central transcription regulator of the immunosuppressive M2a macrophage network through multi-omics network integration; two NCOR2 residues (S149, S152) showed higher phosphorylation in the M2a state of primary human macrophages.
