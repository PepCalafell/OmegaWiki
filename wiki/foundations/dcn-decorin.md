## Definition

Decorin (DCN) is a small leucine-rich proteoglycan (SLRP) of the extracellular matrix. It binds and sequesters TGFβ, EGFR, IGF1R, Met, and VEGFR2 ligands, and modulates collagen-fibril assembly. Through TGFβ sequestration at the ECM and cell surface, decorin exerts well-documented anti-fibrogenic effects.

## Intuition

In the healthy liver, hepatic stellate cells produce decorin and biglycan; these proteoglycans buffer ambient TGFβ to prevent premature stellate activation. NiCo (Agrawal 2024) predicts that Kupffer-cell-derived Tgfb1 induces stellate Dcn, which then sequesters the same TGFβ — a homeostatic negative-feedback loop maintaining stellate quiescence in normal liver. Validated in vivo by smHCR (Pearson r=0.59 for Tgfb1–Dcn in co-localized KC–HSC pairs) and in vitro by qPCR (Dcn dampens TGFβ-induced Col1a1, Pdgfrb in cultured HSCs).

## Known limitations

- Pleiotropic ligand-trapping range complicates clean attribution to TGFβ.
- Decorin's anti-tumour effects in cancer are context-dependent.

## Relevance to active research

Anti-fibrotic decoy molecule; central node in NiCo's predicted Tgfb1–Dcn liver homeostatic feedback loop.
