## Definition

BRAF is a serine/threonine kinase in the RAF family that activates MEK1/2 in the canonical RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK MAPK cascade. The somatic V600E activating mutation drives ~50% of cutaneous melanomas (and subsets of thyroid, colorectal, and other cancers). BRAF inhibitors (vemurafenib, dabrafenib, encorafenib) are mainstays of BRAF V600E melanoma therapy, but resistance is universal — partly via the non-genomic AHR-SRC axis.

## Intuition

BRAF V600E hyperactivates MAPK signalling; pharmacological inhibition initially shrinks tumours but selects resistance, including via parallel AHR-SRC signalling that bypasses the BRAFi blockade.

## Formal notation

- Catalytic kinase activity on MEK1/2 → ERK1/2 → downstream proliferation/survival programs.
- V600E: substitution in the activation loop, locks BRAF in an active monomeric state.

## Key variants

- BRAF V600E, V600K, fusion-BRAF, kinase-dead BRAF mutants.

## Known limitations

- Resistance is universal; clinical practice now combines BRAFi + MEKi.

## Open problems

- Combined BRAFi + AHR-SRC inhibition to forestall non-genomic resistance.
- Predictive biomarkers for non-genomic resistance.

Melanoma therapy; AHR-SRC non-genomic axis. See [[claims/ahr-src-axis-drives-brafi-resistance-melanoma]], [[foundations/vemurafenib]].
