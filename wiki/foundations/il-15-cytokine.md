## Definition

IL-15 is a four-α-helix-bundle common-β/γc-family cytokine that signals through IL-15Rα (high-affinity) presented in trans, together with IL-2Rβ/γc, activating JAK1/3 → STAT5. It is largely trans-presented (by monocytes/DCs to NK and CD8 T cells) rather than secreted, and drives NK and memory CD8 T-cell development, survival, homeostatic proliferation, and cytotoxicity.

## Intuition

IL-15 is the homeostatic survival/cytotoxicity cytokine for NK and memory CD8 T cells, overlapping with [[il-2-cytokine]] (shared IL-2Rβ/γc) but biased toward survival without strong Treg expansion. In the Human Cytokine Dictionary it is the least tissue-specific cytokine (TSI 0.07, affects all cell types) and the most prolific regulator of other cytokines (146 regulatory events), highlighting its hub role in the cytokine network.

## Key variants

- IL-15/IL-15Rα-Fc superagonist complexes (e.g. N-803/ALT-803)
- Trans-presented vs soluble IL-15
- IL-15 immunocytokines for tumor targeting

## Known limitations

- Trans-presentation biology complicates simple ligand–receptor modeling
- Systemic toxicity / cytokine-release at therapeutic doses
- Overlap with IL-2 signaling confounds attribution

## Open problems

- Optimal superagonist design for tumor NK/CD8 activation
- Role in autoimmune cytotoxic pathology (e.g. MS, SLE)

## Relevance to active research

A network hub in the [[human-cytokine-dictionary-dataset]]; enriched in SLE flare and MS cytotoxic programs via huCIRA. Central to NK/CD8 cytotoxic states relevant to tumor immunity. Compare with [[il-2-cytokine]].
