## Definition

ABCG2 is an ATP-dependent efflux transporter of the ATP-binding cassette family, historically studied as a multidrug-resistance pump (BCRP). It has been identified as an exporter of the immunometabolite itaconate, enabling macrophages to release intracellular itaconate into the extracellular space where it can act non-cell-autonomously on neighboring cells.

## Intuition

Itaconate produced inside macrophages is only useful to neighboring tumor cells if it can get out. ABCG2 is the door: high ABCG2 means efficient itaconate export and stronger paracrine anti-tumor signaling; low ABCG2 traps itaconate and blunts that crosstalk.

## Formal notation

Efflux activity is inferred functionally: ABCG2 knockdown attenuates the anti-proliferative activity of macrophage conditioned media, and ABCG2 mRNA levels are used as a surrogate for itaconate-export capacity in tumor tissue.

## Key variants

- Human ABCG2 (Q9UNQ0), homodimeric half-transporter.
- Broad substrate range (xenobiotics, chemotherapeutics, urate, itaconate).

## Known limitations

- ABCG2 is not itaconate-specific; its expression reflects general efflux capacity.
- Prognostic direction is context-dependent across cancer types.

## Open problems

- The full complement of transporters controlling itaconate import/export.
- Why ABCG2/itaconate export is protective in some tumors and adverse in others.

## Relevance to active research

High ABCG2 expression is associated with improved survival in lung and breast cancer (where itaconate is anti-tumor) but poor survival in colorectal and gastric cancer, and ABCG2 mRNA is reduced in lung tumor-associated macrophages — implicating impaired itaconate export in the lung tumor microenvironment ([[papers/irg1-itaconate-rewires-macrophage-lung-tumor]]).
