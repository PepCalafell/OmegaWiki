## Definition

Proton-coupled monocarboxylate symporter exporting/importing lactate (and pyruvate, ketone bodies). Together with related MCT family members (MCT4/SLC16A3 etc.), MCT1 governs lactate flux across the plasma membrane and contributes to extracellular acidification.

## Intuition

In glycolytic tumours, MCT1/MCT4 export lactate together with H+, contributing the bulk of TME acidification. In lactate-consuming cells, MCT1 imports lactate for use as a carbon source. Blocking MCT1 in CAR-T cells shifts cellular metabolism toward OxPhos and boosts antileukaemia cytotoxicity.

## Formal notation

Lactate− + H+ ⇌ lactate−/H+ symport across the plasma membrane.

## Key variants

- MCT1 (broad expression, low-affinity, bidirectional).
- MCT4 (hypoxia-induced, primarily efflux in glycolytic cells).
- MCT1 inhibitors (AZD3965 and analogues).

## Known limitations

- MCT1 inhibition is poorly tolerated in normal lactate-consuming tissues (heart, muscle, RBCs).
- Compensation by MCT4 limits durable response.

## Open problems

- Whether ex vivo MCT1 inhibition during CAR-T manufacturing carries durable benefit in patients.
- Selectivity of MCT inhibitors for tumour vs immune cells.

## Relevance to active research

- Driver of TME acidification.
- Target for CAR-T metabolic conditioning.
- Couples lactate signalling to OxPhos shift in immune cells.
