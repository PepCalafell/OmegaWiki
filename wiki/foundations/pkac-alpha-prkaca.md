## Definition

PKACα (gene *PRKACA*) is the principal catalytic subunit of cAMP-dependent protein kinase A (PKA), a serine/threonine kinase held inactive in a holoenzyme with regulatory subunits until cAMP binding releases the active catalytic subunit. PKA mediates a large fraction of cAMP-dependent signaling, including CREB-driven transcription.

## Intuition

PKACα is the catalytic engine of the canonical cAMP→PKA→CREB axis. In macrophages, cAMP/PKA signaling is broadly anti-inflammatory and is implicated in protumoral, immunosuppressive polarization.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *PRKACA*; catalytic subunit (Cα) of PKA holoenzyme (R₂C₂)
- Activation: 4 cAMP molecules bind the regulatory dimer → release of two active Cα subunits
- Activation-loop autophosphorylation at T198 is required for full catalytic activity

## Key variants

- Catalytic isoforms Cα (*PRKACA*), Cβ (*PRKACB*), Cγ (*PRKACG*)
- *PRKACA*–DNAJB1 fusion as the oncogenic driver of fibrolamellar carcinoma

## Known limitations

- Ubiquitous activity makes cell-type-specific PKA function hard to isolate
- Activation-loop phosphorylation is largely constitutive, complicating its use as an activity readout

## Open problems

- The specific contribution of PKACα to immunosuppressive macrophage programs
- Whether targeting cAMP/PKA can revert tumor-associated macrophage states

## Relevance to active research

Phosphoproteomic profiling of primary human macrophages found the PKACα activation-loop residue T198 phosphorylated at significantly higher levels in the M2c state than in M1, consistent with PKACα supporting a protumoral, immunosuppressive macrophage phenotype; PKACα is a substrate-relationship partner of PKCα in the M2 signaling network.
