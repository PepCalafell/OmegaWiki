## Definition

BCAT-IN-2 is a small-molecule inhibitor of branched-chain aminotransferase (BCAT), the first and reversible enzyme of BCAA catabolism. By blocking the leucine ⇌ α-ketoisocaproic acid (α-KIC) transamination, BCAT-IN-2 prevents conversion of supplied α-KIC back into leucine and blunts BCAA breakdown. It is used to dissect whether leucine itself (versus downstream catabolites) is the active species in a metabolic phenotype.

## Intuition

BCAT-IN-2 cuts the reversible leucine/α-KIC link. If a rescue by α-KIC requires its back-conversion to leucine, BCAT-IN-2 abolishes it — pinpointing leucine as the effector.

## Formal notation

- Target: BCAT; blocks BCAA ⇌ branched-chain α-keto acid transamination.
- Used in vitro (10 μM) and in vivo (5 mg/kg/day) in [[papers/mitochondrial-vhl-rewires-cell-metabolism-hypoxia]].

## Key variants

- Other BCAT inhibitors: gabapentin (weak), ERG240.

## Known limitations

- Tool-compound selectivity across BCAT1/BCAT2 not fully defined.

## Open problems

- Therapeutic potential of BCAT inhibition in ischemic disease and cancer.

## Relevance to active research

In [[papers/mitochondrial-vhl-rewires-cell-metabolism-hypoxia]], BCAT-IN-2 caused mitochondrial leucine buildup and increased glutaminolysis in renal tissue, and relieved ischemia-reperfusion injury (an effect blunted by GDH inhibitor R162).
