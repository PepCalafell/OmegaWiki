## Definition

CCR5 is a G-protein-coupled CC chemokine receptor whose ligands include CCL3 (MIP-1α), CCL4 (MIP-1β) and CCL5 (RANTES). It directs chemotaxis of monocytes, monocyte-derived cells, effector T cells, NK cells and other leukocytes, and is the major coreceptor for CCR5-tropic HIV-1.

## Intuition

CCR5 reads CCL5/CCL3/CCL4 gradients to move cells toward inflamed sites and into lymphoid tissue. In the monocyte/macrophage lineage it provides a CCR7-independent route into draining lymph nodes used by monocyte-derived dendritic cells (moDCs).

## Formal notation

Gi-coupled GPCR; ligand binding triggers β-arrestin recruitment and chemotactic signaling.

## Key variants

- CCR5Δ32 loss-of-function allele (HIV resistance).
- Pharmacological antagonism by [[maraviroc]]; dual CCR2/CCR5 blockade by cenicriviroc.

## Known limitations

- Broadly expressed across leukocyte subsets, so receptor-level perturbation is rarely cell-type-selective without genetic tools or competitive chimeras.

## Open problems

- Distinguishing CCR5's role in monocyte-derived suppressive cells from its role in effector T cells during tumor immunity.

## Relevance to active research

In [[chemokine-defined-macrophage-niches-establish-spatial]], CCR5 (not CCR7) mediates migration of Ly6C⁺ monocyte-derived DCs into tumor-draining lymph nodes, where they act as immunosuppressive antigen-presenting cells. Competitive Ccr2⁻ᐟ⁻:Ccr5⁻ᐟ⁻ bone-marrow chimeras and the CCR5 antagonist [[maraviroc]] were used to isolate this monocyte-lineage-restricted CCR5 function from conventional DCs.
