## Definition

SAT1 catalyses the acetyl-CoA-dependent N1-acetylation of spermidine and spermine, producing N1-acetylspermidine / N1-acetylspermine and CoA. SAT1 is the rate-limiting step in polyamine catabolism: N1-acetylated polyamines are either oxidised by polyamine oxidase (PAOX) to recycle to putrescine or exported from the cell. SAT1 activity also depletes acetyl-CoA pools, linking polyamine catabolism to broader metabolism.

## Intuition

Polyamines (putrescine, spermidine, spermine) are abundant cationic small molecules essential for proliferation, autophagy and translation. SAT1 is their off-switch: induction of SAT1 lowers cellular polyamines. SAT1 is induced under many stress conditions (cytokines, oxidative stress, DNA damage) and connects polyamine flux to the methionine cycle via methionine-derived dcSAM (decarboxylated SAM), the methyl donor of polyamine synthesis.

## Formal notation

- Reactions:
  - spermidine + acetyl-CoA → N1-acetylspermidine + CoA.
  - spermine + acetyl-CoA → N1-acetylspermine + CoA.
- Downstream: PAOX oxidises acetylated polyamines back to putrescine or spermidine + 3-acetamidopropanal + H2O2.
- Coupling to one-carbon: polyamine synthesis (putrescine → spermidine → spermine) uses dcSAM (derived from SAM via AMD1) → links to methionine cycle and SAM availability.
- Induction: cytokines, ROS, p53, ATF4 stress response.

## Key variants

- SAT1 vs SAT2 (related N1-acetyltransferase) — distinct gene; less well characterised.

## Known limitations

- SAT1 inhibitors not clinically advanced.
- Polyamine readouts (N1-acetylspermidine, putrescine ratios) require careful MS quantitation.

## Open problems

- Functional contribution of SAT1 to cachexia phenotypes (vs being a passive marker).
- Whether polyamine depletion via SAT1 induction contributes to muscle dysfunction.

## Relevance to active research

In [[papers/multi-omics-profiling-cachexia-targeted-tissues]] SAT1 is one of the cross-tissue commonly upregulated one-carbon-associated enzymes across multiple cachexia target tissues, linking the methionine cycle to polyamine catabolism in cachexia.
