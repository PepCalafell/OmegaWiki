## Definition

A high-precision external-beam radiotherapy technique delivering ablative doses of radiation (typically 5-25 Gy per fraction, 1-5 fractions) to a small, well-defined extracranial target using image guidance and multi-angle beam shaping. Used in HCC, lung, pancreas, prostate, oligometastases.

## Intuition

Treats radiation as a focal ablative therapy comparable to surgical resection or radiofrequency ablation, with the immunological side-effect of releasing tumor antigens and damage signals — basis for combination with ICB ("radio-immunotherapy").

## Key variants

- Single-fraction SBRT
- Multi-fraction hypofractionated SBRT (1-5 fractions)
- Combination with ICB (e.g., cemiplimab + SBRT in NCT03916627 HCC Cohort B2)

## Known limitations

- Target-size constraints (typically <5-7 cm).
- Liver toxicity in cirrhotic patients.

## Open problems

- Optimal fractionation and timing for ICB combination.
- Whether SBRT-driven immunogenic cell death modulates TAM landscape (e.g., TREM2 mac infiltration).
