## Definition

CCHD is a group of congenital heart defects in which deoxygenated and oxygenated blood mix, producing chronic systemic hypoxemia (low arterial oxygen saturation) often from birth. It affects roughly 1–2 per 1,000 live births.

## Intuition

Because the defective heart chambers mix oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, patients sustain chronic low SatO2 throughout life unless surgically corrected. This makes CCHD a natural human model of lifelong systemic hypoxia.

## Key variants

- Defects causing hypoxia since birth (severe complex CCHD)
- Eisenmenger syndrome — later (childhood/adult) onset hypoxia from pulmonary hypertension and shunt reversal
- Surgical status: complete repair (restores normoxia) vs palliated/unoperated (persistent hypoxia)

## Known limitations

Heterogeneous defect types and variable correction timing complicate exposure quantification.

## Open problems

- Whether early complete repair (normoxia restoration) is causally protective against tumor development

## Relevance to active research

Patients with CCHD have a ~6-fold increased risk of PPGL; they are the central human model in Arenillas et al. for studying systemic-hypoxia tumorigenesis and EPAS1 selection.
