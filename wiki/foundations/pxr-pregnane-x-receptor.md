## Definition

PXR (NR1I2) is a ligand-activated nuclear receptor that senses xenobiotics, steroids, and bile acids and induces expression of phase I/II/III xenobiotic-metabolising enzymes (CYP3A4, CYP2B6, UGTs, MRPs). PXR is highly promiscuous and shares substantial ligand overlap with AHR, raising the possibility that AHR-attributed pharmacology is in part PXR-mediated.

## Intuition

A second xenobiotic sensor that frequently recognises the same compounds as AHR — meaning "AHR ligands" identified by biochemistry alone often co-activate PXR in cells. Disentangling the two is necessary for clean mechanistic attribution.

## Formal notation

- DNA-binding-domain - hinge - ligand-binding-domain architecture (canonical nuclear receptor).
- Dimerises with RXR; binds PXRE/DR3/DR4 response elements.
- Major substrates of induced enzymes: CYP3A4 (~50% of drugs).

## Key variants

- PXR vs CAR (NR1I3) — overlapping but distinct ligand and target spectrum.

## Known limitations

- Cross-reactivity with AHR confounds many xenobiotic-receptor pharmacology studies.

## Open problems

- Combined AHR + PXR antagonism phenotype.
- Tissue-resolved measurement of co-activation.

## Relevance to active research

Drug-drug interactions; xenobiotic metabolism in cancer therapy; AHR pharmacology disambiguation. See [[claims/ahr-pxr-promiscuous-ligand-overlap]].
