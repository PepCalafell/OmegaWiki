## Definition

CUL4B is the scaffold of a Cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase (CRL4B) that — together with DDB1 and a substrate-recognition adaptor — directs target proteins to ubiquitin-dependent proteasomal degradation. In AHR signalling, ligand-activated AHR can serve as the substrate-recognition subunit of a CUL4B-based ligase, targeting estrogen receptor α (ESR1), androgen receptor (AR), and peroxisome-proliferator-activated receptor γ (PPARG) for degradation — a non-genomic AHR function.

## Intuition

The same AHR molecule that drives XRE-dependent transcription can, in parallel, dispose of competing nuclear receptors via the CRL4B machinery — coupling AHR activity to endocrine signalling without requiring transcription of new degraders.

## Formal notation

- CRL4B core: CUL4B + DDB1 + RBX1 + substrate-receptor (here: ligand-bound AHR).
- Targets via AHR-CUL4B: ESR1, AR, PPARG.
- Also regulates AHR itself: CUL4B contributes to AHR-AR-driven proteasomal turnover of AHR following ligand activation.

## Key variants

- CUL4A vs CUL4B: related; CUL4B can substitute for CUL4A in some CRL4 complexes.

## Known limitations

- The full physiological substrate scope of AHR-CUL4B beyond ESR1/AR/PPARG is unknown.

## Open problems

- Pocket-selective AHR ligands that preserve CUL4B activity while sparing canonical transcription.
- Mechanism of substrate selection within the AHR-CUL4B complex.

## Relevance to active research

Endocrine-resistant breast/prostate cancer (ESR1/AR degradation); adipocyte differentiation (PPARG). See [[claims/ahr-acts-as-cul4b-e3-ligase]], [[concepts/ahr-non-genomic-cytoplasmic-effects]].
