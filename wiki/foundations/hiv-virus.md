## Definition

HIV is a retrovirus that targets CD4+ T cells and macrophages, causing chronic infection and acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS). AHR binds the HIV-1 5'LTR and Tat protein to facilitate viral transcription. KYN and FICZ AHR agonists reactivate latent HIV in PBMCs of cART-treated individuals; AHR antagonists may reduce viral transcription and serve as adjuncts in cure strategies.

## Intuition

HIV uses AHR as a transcriptional co-factor at its own promoter — kynurenine, elevated in chronic HIV, is the host-derived agonist that reactivates virus. AHR antagonism is therefore a candidate "block-and-lock" approach.

## Key variants

- HIV-1 (group M, N, O, P)
- HIV-2 (West Africa)

## Known limitations

- Latent proviral reservoirs in long-lived CD4+ memory T cells are not targeted by cART
- AHR effects on HIV are cell-type-specific (CD4+ T cells pro-viral; macrophages anti-viral with FICZ)

## Open problems

- Whether AHR antagonism added to cART suppresses viral reactivation and shrinks reservoirs
- Mechanistic basis for AHR cell-type divergence in HIV
