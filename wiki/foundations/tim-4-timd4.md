## Definition

TIM-4 is a phosphatidylserine-recognizing receptor expressed by many mature tissue-resident macrophage populations. It mediates tethering of apoptotic cells during efferocytosis and is widely used as a marker of self-renewing, embryonically seeded resident macrophages (e.g. peritoneal, liver, splenic, cardiac).

## Intuition

TIM-4 positivity marks "settled" mature resident macrophages; recently recruited monocyte-derived macrophages are typically TIM-4 negative until they complete the residency program. Loss of TIM-4+ cells thus reports a failure of the monocyte-to-resident transition.

## Formal notation

n/a

## Key variants

- Tissue-restricted expression: peritoneal, liver Kupffer, splenic, cardiac resident macrophages.

## Known limitations

- Not expressed by all resident macrophage niches (e.g. microglia, alveolar macrophages use other markers).

## Open problems

- Relationship between TIM-4 acquisition and the upstream transcriptional/translational residency program.

## Relevance to active research

TIM-4 is a primary readout for mature resident macrophages; its loss across peritoneum, liver, spleen and heart in DHPS-deficient mice signals a global block in monocyte-to-RTM maturation.
