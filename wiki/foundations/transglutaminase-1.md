## Definition

Transglutaminase 1 (TG1), encoded by *TGM1*, is a membrane-anchored calcium-dependent enzyme expressed in differentiating keratinocytes. It catalyses ε-(γ-glutamyl)lysine isopeptide cross-links that covalently assemble the cornified envelope — the insoluble protein/lipid scaffold of the stratum corneum that provides the skin's permeability barrier. Loss of TG1 activity disrupts cornified-envelope formation and skin-barrier integrity.

## Intuition

TG1 is the "molecular stapler" of the outermost skin. Without functional TG1, corneocytes cannot build a sealed barrier, so water is lost and the skin scales and thickens — the phenotype of severe congenital ichthyosis. Because the defect is a single enzyme with a measurable activity readout, partial restoration of TG1 activity (even ~30% of wild-type) is expected to be clinically meaningful.

## Formal notation

- Reaction: protein-Gln + protein-Lys → ε-(γ-glutamyl)lysine isopeptide cross-link (Ca²⁺-dependent)
- Substrates: loricrin, involucrin, small proline-rich proteins; ω-hydroxyceramide lipid attachment
- Locus: chromosome 14q11.2
- Common pathogenic allele: *TGM1* c.877-2A>G (canonical splice-acceptor disruption → truncated, non-functional TG1)

## Key variants

- Splice-site mutations (e.g. c.877-2A>G) — most common ARCI-causing class at this locus
- Missense/nonsense mutations across the catalytic and substrate-binding domains
- ~one-third of autosomal recessive congenital ichthyosis (ARCI) cases map to *TGM1*

## Known limitations

- Activity is calcium- and differentiation-dependent; in vitro readouts may not capture full in vivo context
- Bystander-prone splice motifs complicate precise genetic correction
- Restoration threshold for clinical benefit not precisely defined (partial activity likely sufficient)

## Open problems

- Durability of restored TG1 activity after transient genome editing
- Whether partial correction in a subset of basal stem cells yields lasting barrier repair
- Competitive dynamics between corrected and uncorrected keratinocytes

## Relevance to active research

[[papers/editing-skin-place-vivo-genome-correction]] targets the most common *TGM1* mutation (c.877-2A>G) for in situ correction in human skin. A precision [[foundations/cytosine-base-editor]] (eTD-CBE) restored ~30% of wild-type TG1 enzyme activity in a 3D ARCI skin model — framed as likely sufficient to alleviate the most severe symptoms of [[foundations/autosomal-recessive-congenital-ichthyosis]].
