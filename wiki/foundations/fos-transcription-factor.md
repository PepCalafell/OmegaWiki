## Definition

FOS (c-Fos) is an immediate-early bZIP transcription factor that dimerizes with JUN-family proteins to form the AP-1 transcription factor complex, regulating proliferation, differentiation, and immune gene expression. In macrophages FOS has been reported to suppress inflammation.

## Intuition

FOS is a rapidly induced transcriptional switch that, as part of AP-1, converts signaling inputs (MAPK cascades) into gene-expression programs. Its anti-inflammatory role in macrophages makes it relevant to immunosuppressive polarization.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *FOS*; bZIP family; obligate heterodimer with JUN (AP-1)
- AP-1 binds TPA-response elements (TRE / AP-1 sites)
- Induced downstream of ERK/MAPK signaling as an immediate-early gene

## Key variants

- FOS family: FOS, FOSB, FOSL1 (FRA-1), FOSL2 (FRA-2)
- AP-1 composition (FOS/JUN partner choice) tunes target specificity

## Known limitations

- Context-dependent, even opposite, transcriptional effects depending on AP-1 partner and cell type
- Immediate-early kinetics make steady-state measurements poor activity proxies

## Open problems

- Mechanism by which FOS enforces an immunosuppressive macrophage transcriptional program
- Whether FOS/AP-1 can be modulated to revert tumor-associated macrophage states

## Relevance to active research

Network integration of proteomic, phosphoproteomic, and transcriptomic data in primary human macrophages identified FOS as a central transcription regulator of the immunosuppressive M2a state, alongside NCOR2 and PPARγ.
