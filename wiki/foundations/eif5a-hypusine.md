## Definition

eIF5A is a small, highly conserved translation factor that bears hypusine, a unique amino acid formed post-translationally on a single conserved lysine via [[foundations/deoxyhypusine-synthase-dhps]] and deoxyhypusine hydroxylase. Hypusinated eIF5A promotes translation elongation and termination, particularly across sequences (e.g. polyproline and diproline motifs) that otherwise stall the ribosome and can trigger transcript degradation.

## Intuition

Hypusine converts eIF5A into a ribosome-rescue factor: it lubricates elongation through difficult peptide stretches. The set of transcripts most dependent on hypusinated eIF5A is enriched for sequences that cause ribosome stalling, so loss of hypusination selectively depletes the proteins those transcripts encode.

## Formal notation

eIF5A-Lys → eIF5A-deoxyhypusine (DHPS, +spermidine aminobutyl) → eIF5A-hypusine (DOHH, +OH).

## Key variants

- Hypusinated (active) vs unmodified eIF5A.
- Isoforms eIF5A1 (ubiquitous) and eIF5A2 (tissue-restricted, oncogenic).

## Known limitations

- The full repertoire of hypusine-dependent transcripts in any given cell remains incompletely defined.
- Distinguishing transcriptional from translational contributions to a protein deficit is experimentally difficult.

## Open problems

- Which exact motifs confer hypusine dependency beyond polyproline.
- Whether eIF5A is the only hypusinated protein in mammalian cells.

## Relevance to active research

In macrophages, hypusinated eIF5A supports translation of cell-adhesion and signalling mRNAs (e.g. ST2/Il1rl1, Tnik) required for the monocyte-to-resident-macrophage transition, and earlier work links it to mitochondrial respiration and macrophage alternative activation.
