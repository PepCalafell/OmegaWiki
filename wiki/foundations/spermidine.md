## Definition

Spermidine is a ubiquitous triamine polyamine synthesized from putrescine (via spermidine synthase, SRM) along the arginine→ornithine→putrescine→spermidine→spermine biosynthetic axis. It is the obligate aminobutyl donor for hypusine synthesis on eIF5A.

## Intuition

Among polyamines, spermidine is special because it is the only substrate for [[foundations/deoxyhypusine-synthase-dhps]]. A cell's decision to route spermidine toward hypusination versus other polyamine-dependent processes couples polyamine metabolism to translational control.

## Formal notation

Putrescine + decarboxylated SAM → spermidine (SRM); spermidine + eIF5A → deoxyhypusine-eIF5A (DHPS).

## Key variants

- Endogenous biosynthesis vs uptake (diet, microbiota, dying cells).
- Acetylation/catabolism via [[foundations/sat1-spermidine-spermine-acetyltransferase]].

## Known limitations

- Intracellular vs extracellular pools are hard to measure in situ.
- Effects of spermidine supplementation are pleiotropic (autophagy, hypusination, translation).

## Open problems

- How tissue polyamine availability gates hypusine synthesis in health and disease.

## Relevance to active research

Spermidine availability sets the ceiling on hypusinated-eIF5A function, linking polyamine metabolism to the macrophage tissue-residency program and to T-cell lineage decisions.
