## Definition
OSCC is squamous cell carcinoma arising from the mucosal epithelium of the oral cavity. HPV-negative OSCC, mostly driven by tobacco and alcohol use, accounts for >95% of OSCC cases (HPV infection contributes to only 2–5% of OSCC, unlike the oropharyngeal subset).

## Intuition
Despite being grouped under HNSCC, oral OSCC has a distinct molecular and clinical profile from HPV-positive oropharyngeal SCC: worse prognosis, higher recurrence/metastasis risk, and a strong leading-edge biology that has been recognised pathologically for decades.

## Key variants
- HPV-negative (this page; tobacco/alcohol-driven)
- HPV-positive oropharyngeal SCC (better prognosis, distinct biology)
- Verrucous carcinoma (well-differentiated, low-grade variant)

## Known limitations
- 5-year survival in HPV-negative OSCC remains <50%
- Targeted therapies are largely ineffective; surgery/chemo/radio dominate care
- Heterogeneous tumour microenvironment complicates patient stratification

## Open problems
- Identifying tractable LE-targeting therapeutic strategies
- Predicting recurrence pre-emptively from primary tumour biology

## Relevance to active research
