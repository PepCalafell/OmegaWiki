## Definition

Momelotinib is a multi-kinase inhibitor approved for myelofibrosis (including the associated anemia), designated as a JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor and additionally acting on ACVR1/ALK2. It also engages TBK1 and its paralog IKBKE, a property exploited as a probe of TBK1-dependent interferon signaling.

## Intuition

Although clinically a JAK inhibitor, Momelotinib's additional binding to the TBK1/IKBKE axis gives it interferon-pathway effects beyond canonical JAK–STAT inhibition — a concrete instance of off-target activity shaping cellular outcome.

## Formal notation

- Designated clinical targets: JAK1, JAK2 (also ACVR1).
- Kinobeads / functional evidence: binds IKBKE; binds TBK1 at ~2.5-fold higher concentration than IKBKE.

## Key variants

- Other JAK inhibitors: Ruxolitinib, Filgotinib, Tofacitinib, Pacritinib, BMS-911543.

## Known limitations

- Multi-target activity complicates attribution of cellular effects to JAK inhibition alone.
- TBK1 engagement occurs only at higher concentrations than IKBKE.

## Open problems

- Therapeutic relevance of its TBK1/IKBKE activity for interferon-driven disease.

## Relevance to active research

In [[papers/integrative-epigenome-based-strategy-unbiased-functional]], Momelotinib (used at the EC50 for IKBKE) was the only JAK inhibitor to reduce Ifnb1 expression, behaved epigenomically like the TBK1 inhibitor Midostaurin, reduced LPS-induced p-STAT1, and stabilized TBK1 in CETSA only at 10×EC50 — consistent with its lower-affinity TBK1 binding.
