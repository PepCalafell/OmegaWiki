## Definition

Vemurafenib (PLX4032; Zelboraf) is an FDA-approved small-molecule selective inhibitor of BRAF V600E used in BRAF-mutant metastatic melanoma. Beyond its kinase target, vemurafenib displaces radiolabeled TCDD from the AHR PAS-B pocket, induces AHR nuclear translocation, but — unlike TCDD — blocks AHR-ARNT heterodimerisation and does not induce CYP1A1, consistent with occupancy of a distinct binding pocket and "biased" AHR activation.

## Intuition

A canonical example that "AHR binding" and "AHR canonical signalling" are dissociable: vemurafenib enters the AHR pocket but produces a fundamentally different cellular response than TCDD or FICZ — making it a useful probe of pocket-selective AHR pharmacology.

## Formal notation

- Primary target: BRAF V600E (and CRAF, wild-type BRAF at higher concentrations).
- AHR pharmacology: displaces TCDD; induces nuclear translocation; no AHR-ARNT proximity-ligation signal; no XRE-luciferase or CYP1A1 induction. TCDD or CH-223191 block vemurafenib-mediated AHR effects.

## Key variants

- Dabrafenib, encorafenib — newer BRAFi; whether they share the AHR-binding property has not been systematically established.

## Known limitations

- BRAFi resistance emerges in most patients; the AHR-SRC axis is one resistance mechanism.

## Open problems

- Cryo-EM of AHR-vemurafenib complex to confirm secondary-pocket occupancy.
- Whether other clinically used kinase inhibitors share this property.
