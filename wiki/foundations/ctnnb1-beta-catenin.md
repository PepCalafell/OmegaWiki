## Definition
β-catenin (encoded by CTNNB1) is a dual-function protein that scaffolds cadherin-based cell-cell junctions and serves as the principal transcriptional effector of canonical Wnt signaling. Cytosolic β-catenin levels are gated by an APC/Axin/GSK3β destruction complex; mutational stabilisation drives aberrant Wnt-target expression in colorectal and other cancers.

## Intuition
Two pools (junctional vs cytosolic/nuclear) with different functions; oncogenic dysregulation typically expands the cytosolic pool via APC loss, CTNNB1 N-terminal stabilising mutations, or O-GlcNAcylation.

## Formal notation
- Destruction complex: APC, Axin, GSK3β, CK1α
- Phosphorylation at Ser33/37/Thr41/Ser45 → β-TrCP recognition → proteasomal degradation
- Mutation hotspots: N-terminal phospho-sites (CTNNB1 exon 3)

## Key variants
- Wild-type β-catenin
- Exon-3 mutated stabilised β-catenin

## Known limitations
- Direct β-catenin inhibitors with clinical utility remain elusive.

## Open problems
- Selective transcriptional vs junctional inhibition.
- Targeting via PROTACs or upstream regulators (GFPT1/HBP / O-GlcNAc).

## Relevance to active research
Recurrent hub protein in pan-cancer WGCNA modules including TPCPA module 5; mechanistic anchor for the GFPT1 / hexosamine pathway / O-GlcNAc axis described in colon and pancreatic cancers.
