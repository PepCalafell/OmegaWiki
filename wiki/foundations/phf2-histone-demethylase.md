## Definition

A Jumonji-C-domain, PHD-finger histone demethylase (KDM7 family) that removes the repressive H3K9me2 mark (and acts on H4K20me3), functioning largely as a transcriptional coactivator. PHF2 is α-ketoglutarate- and Fe(II)-dependent.

## Intuition

Beyond transcriptional coactivation, PHF2 is a genome-stability factor: it promotes homology-directed repair by controlling resection of DNA double-strand breaks and regulates cell-cycle progression of proliferating cells. Loss of PHF2 raises genomic instability, which generates the cytosolic/micronuclear DNA that cGAS senses.

## Key variants

Reported as a tumor suppressor in some cancers (e.g., p53-associated contexts, breast) and as potentially oncogenic in others (e.g., leukemia) — its role is context-dependent on cancer type.

## Known limitations

The directionality of PHF2's role in tumorigenesis is conflicting across cancer types, complicating its use as a universal therapeutic target.

## Relevance to active research

Candidate target for boosting cGAS-STING signaling: reducing PHF2 (especially with PHF8) increases DNA damage and innate-immune activation in tumor cells, suggesting a route to convert "cold" tumors toward inflammatory phenotypes.
