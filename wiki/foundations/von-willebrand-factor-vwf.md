## Definition

Von Willebrand factor is a large multimeric glycoprotein central to haemostasis (platelet adhesion and factor VIII carriage). In haematopoiesis, expression of the Vwf gene marks a subpopulation of megakaryocyte-biased / platelet-primed haematopoietic stem cells (vWF+ HSCs) that sit at the apex of a non-canonical, rapid route to the megakaryocyte/platelet lineage.

## Intuition

vWF is best known as a clotting protein, but its transcription in rare HSCs flags lineage priming: vWF+ HSCs preferentially and quickly produce megakaryocytes/platelets, distinguishing them from multi-lineage-balanced vWF− HSCs.

## Key variants

- vWF-eGFP reporter mice used to prospectively isolate vWF+ HSCs.
- vWF+ "platelet-biased" vs vWF− "myeloid/lymphoid-balanced" HSC subsets.

## Known limitations

- vWF expression is graded, not strictly binary; marks a biased tendency, not an absolute fate.

## Open problems

- Whether vWF+ HSC bias reflects stable subtypes or a reversible primed state.

## Relevance to active research

- A central marker in [[papers/pseudodynamics-reconstructing-population-dynamics-time-resolved]]: vWF+ MK-biased HSCs are enriched early (Day 3–12) and decline over time, underpinning the inferred shift from megakaryocyte-biased to balanced homeostatic haematopoiesis.
