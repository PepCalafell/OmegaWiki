## Definition

Mycobacterium tuberculosis is the obligate intracellular acid-fast bacterium that causes human tuberculosis. It infects alveolar macrophages, evades phagosome maturation, and establishes granulomatous lung disease. Several Mtb-derived molecules engage host AHR — notably the naphthoquinone pigment phthiocol and a range of other small molecules generated during Trp metabolism inside infected macrophages.

## Intuition

Mtb is the canonical "intracellular survivor" pathogen. The Mtb-AHR interaction provides a host receptor with bacterial chemistry that informs macrophage polarisation and antimycobacterial cytokine production; Ahr-KO mice show higher early bacterial loads and earlier mortality.

## Key variants

- H37Rv reference strain (laboratory)
- Beijing/W lineage (hypervirulent, drug-resistant)
- MDR-TB and XDR-TB clinical isolates

## Known limitations

- Drug-resistance is a global public-health threat; host-directed therapies (HDT) including AHR-modulating compounds are under investigation as adjuncts
- BCG vaccine offers limited adult protection

## Open problems

- Whether AHR-agonist HDT (indirubin, FICZ, tapinarof-mimetics) improves TB outcomes
- Role of Mtb phthiocol in AHR-mediated host phagocytosis and granuloma formation
