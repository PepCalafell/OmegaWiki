## Definition

NOD2 is a cytosolic pattern-recognition receptor that senses muramyl dipeptide (MDP),
a peptidoglycan fragment of bacterial cell walls, and triggers NF-κB and inflammatory
signalling. It is a major Crohn's disease susceptibility gene.

## Intuition

An intracellular bacterial "smoke detector": when it detects MDP, it activates
inflammatory and antibacterial responses; loss-of-function variants impair this and
predispose to Crohn's disease.

## Formal notation

Loss-of-function and risk variants in NOD2/CARD15 are among the strongest genetic
associations with Crohn's disease.

## Key variants

Common Crohn's-associated NOD2 risk alleles (e.g. frameshift and missense variants).

## Known limitations

Genotype–phenotype relationships are incomplete; NOD2's role intersects complex
gene–microbiome interactions.

## Relevance to active research

"Cellular response to muramyl dipeptide" is among the functions most enriched among
human proteins targeted by commensal T3SS effectors, linking effector–host interactions
to the NOD2/Crohn's disease axis.
