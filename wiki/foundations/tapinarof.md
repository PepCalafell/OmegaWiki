## Definition

Tapinarof is a non-steroidal, topically applied small molecule (a natural hydroxylated stilbene) that acts as a selective AHR agonist. It is a natural metabolite of the symbiotic bacterium *Photorhabdus luminescens*. Tapinarof binds AHR–ARNT heterodimers, induces CYP1A1 and skin-barrier genes (filaggrin, loricrin, involucrin, hornerin) in keratinocytes, suppresses IL-17A/IL-17F in T cells, and additionally activates Nrf2-driven antioxidant programmes.

## Intuition

A natural stilbene scaffold gives tapinarof transient AHR activation (unlike persistent TCDD-like ligands) and dual AHR + Nrf2 effects, producing anti-inflammatory and barrier-restoring activity in skin without measurable systemic exposure.

## Key variants

- 1% cream formulation (psoriasis, atopic dermatitis)
- Original WBI-1001 formulation → reformulated GSK2894512 → final Dermavant cream

## Known limitations

- Approved only as topical (skin); systemic AHR activation in other tissues remains under-explored
- Contact dermatitis and folliculitis are most common adverse events in trials
- Long-term effects of chronic AHR agonism in skin still being followed

## Open problems

- Whether tapinarof's "remittive effect" in psoriasis (PSOARING-3: 41% complete clearance, durable ≥4 months) is generalisable to other AHR agonists
- Whether the same scaffold or related SAHRMs can target other epithelial barriers (gut, lung)

## Relevance to active research

Referenced as the **first and only FDA-approved AHR-targeting therapeutic** in [[papers/aryl-hydrocarbon-receptor-rehabilitated-target-therapeutic]]. Approved by the FDA in 2022 for adult plaque psoriasis (PSOARING 1/2/3 phase III). ADORING 1/2/3 phase III in atopic dermatitis showed efficacy; sNDA submitted 2024.
