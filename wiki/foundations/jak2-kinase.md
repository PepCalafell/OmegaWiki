## Definition

JAK2 is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase of the Janus kinase family that associates with cytokine receptors and, upon ligand-induced receptor dimerization, phosphorylates receptor tails and STAT transcription factors, driving the canonical JAK–STAT signaling axis central to inflammatory and hematopoietic responses.

## Intuition

JAK2 is the receptor-proximal engine of cytokine signaling: it converts extracellular cytokine binding into STAT phosphorylation and nuclear translocation. In macrophages the IFN-γ/JAK–STAT axis is a defining proinflammatory (M1) route.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *JAK2*; domains: FERM–SH2-like–pseudokinase (JH2)–kinase (JH1)
- Activates STAT1/3/5 among others; coupled to IFNGR, EPOR, GH receptor, etc.
- Regulatory phosphorylation includes S518 in addition to canonical activation-loop tyrosines

## Key variants

- JAK2 V617F gain-of-function (myeloproliferative neoplasms)
- JAK inhibitors (ruxolitinib, fedratinib) in clinical use

## Known limitations

- Shared STAT substrates with JAK1/JAK3/TYK2 complicate isoform attribution
- Pseudokinase-domain regulation is mechanistically intricate

## Open problems

- Precise role of non-canonical JAK2 serine phosphorylation (e.g. S518) in macrophage states
- Targeting JAK2 to tune macrophage proinflammatory output without broad immunosuppression

## Relevance to active research

Phosphoproteomics of primary human macrophages found JAK2 S518 phosphorylated at higher levels in the proinflammatory M1 state, consistent with JAK2's role in the JAK–STAT axis; JAK2 was also a high-centrality kinase node of the M1 signaling network.
