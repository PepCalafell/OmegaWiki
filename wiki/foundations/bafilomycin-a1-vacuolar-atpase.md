## Definition

Bafilomycin A1 is a macrolide antibiotic produced by Streptomyces species that selectively inhibits vacuolar-type H⁺-ATPase (V-ATPase), the proton pump responsible for acidifying endosomes, lysosomes, and phagolysosomes. Loss of lumenal acidification blocks lysosomal hydrolase activity, autophagosome–lysosome fusion, and phagolysosomal cargo degradation, making bafilomycin a canonical tool for blocking lysosomal degradation while leaving uptake intact.

## Formal notation

- Target: V0 subunit of V-ATPase
- IC50: low nM
- Typical concentration: 50-200 nM (in vitro, hours)
- Readout: LC3-II accumulation (autophagy block), lysosomal pH rise (LysoTracker), preserved engulfment with blocked cargo digestion
- Selectivity: high vs other ATPases; clean tool

## Known limitations

- Toxicity at sustained high doses
- Affects all V-ATPase-dependent compartments (endosomes, lysosomes, Golgi)
- Light- and temperature-sensitive

## Relevance to active research

Used in [[papers/macrophages-use-apoptotic-cell-derived-methionine]] (Ampomah 2022 *Nat Metab*) to dissociate AC engulfment (intact under bafilomycin) from phagolysosomal AC degradation (blocked) — establishing that the latter is required for Ptgs2/Tgfb1 induction and for 13C5,15N-SAM formation from labelled AC methionine.
