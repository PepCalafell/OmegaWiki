## Definition

P2RY12 is a Gi-coupled purinergic receptor for ADP, and one of the canonical markers of homeostatic, brain-resident microglia. Together with TMEM119, its expression distinguishes microglia (Mg-GAMs) from peripheral monocyte/macrophage-derived cells (Mo/Mφ-GAMs) in the CNS and in glioma.

## Intuition

P2RY12 senses extracellular nucleotides released by damaged/active neurons and drives microglial process motility and chemotaxis. High P2RY12 is a hallmark of the quiescent/surveilling microglial state; its loss accompanies microglial activation, disease-associated reprogramming, and (as shown in glioma) hypoxic stress.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *P2RY12* (human) / *P2ry12* (mouse). Protein: P2Y12 receptor.
- Transcriptionally controlled by the microglial identity factors SPI1/PU.1 and IRF8.

## Key variants

- Companion homeostatic markers: TMEM119, CX3CR1, TGFBR1, OLFML3.
- P2RY12 is pharmacologically the target of antiplatelet drugs (clopidogrel) in platelets, a distinct context from microglia.

## Known limitations

- Not strictly microglia-exclusive: low-level expression detected in macrophage lines (RAW264.7, BMDM) and modulated by environmental cues, which limits its reliability as a lineage marker under stress.

## Open problems

- The degree to which P2RY12 downregulation reflects loss of microglia versus transcriptional reprogramming of existing microglia in hypoxic tumor regions.

## Relevance to active research

Central homeostatic microglial marker whose hypoxia-driven chromatin closure (loss of accessibility at an SPI1/IRF8 motif promoter peak) is a key mechanistic finding in [[papers/hypoxic-stress-dysregulates-functions-glioma-associated]].
