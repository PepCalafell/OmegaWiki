## Definition

CSF1R is a class III receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) and the master receptor for macrophage development, survival, and tissue maintenance across nearly all vertebrate tissues. It binds two distinct ligands — CSF1 (M-CSF, broadly produced) and IL-34 (produced selectively by neurons and keratinocytes) — and signals via dimerization, autophosphorylation, and downstream PI3K-AKT, RAS-MAPK, and STAT pathways. Loss of CSF1R or its ligands causes tissue-specific or pan-tissue macrophage deficiencies and human disease (paediatric leukoencephalopathy, ALSP, osteopetrosis).

## Intuition

CSF1R is the macrophage's "I am alive" receptor. Without continuous trophic CSF1 or IL-34 signalling, most TRMs die or fail to develop. CSF1R is therefore the canonical drug target for *macrophage depletion* — small-molecule inhibitors (pexidartinib, BLZ945) and blocking antibodies (emactuzumab, axatilimab) are in clinical use for tenosynovial giant cell tumour and in trials for cancer immunotherapy and graft-vs-host disease.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *CSF1R* (chromosome 5q33; same locus as PDGFRB, KIT family)
- Domain structure: 5 immunoglobulin-like extracellular domains, single transmembrane helix, intracellular split kinase domain (canonical class III RTK architecture)
- Ligands: CSF1 (M-CSF; secreted and membrane-bound isoforms) and IL-34
- Downstream signalling: PI3K/AKT (survival), RAS-MAPK (proliferation), STAT1/3/5
- Tissue dependence:
  - Most TRMs (Kupffer, peritoneal, alveolar, etc.): require CSF1
  - Microglia + Langerhans cells: require IL-34 specifically
  - Osteoclasts: require CSF1 and RANKL together
- Diseases:
  - Bi-allelic LOF: paediatric leukoencephalopathy (Oosterhof 2019); CSF1R-LOF osteopetrosis
  - Hypomorphic / heterozygous: ALSP (Rademakers 2012)
- Therapeutics:
  - Small-molecule CSF1R kinase inhibitors: pexidartinib (FDA-approved for TGCT), BLZ945, JNJ-40346527
  - Anti-CSF1R blocking antibodies: emactuzumab, axatilimab, cabiralizumab
  - Anti-CSF1 antibody: lacnotuzumab

## Key variants

- CSF1 vs IL-34 selectivity: at the receptor level, CSF1 and IL-34 compete for the same binding site but have different tissue distributions.
- CSF1 isoforms: secreted (long), proteoglycan-anchored (intermediate), membrane-bound (short) — different tissue penetration and rescue capabilities.
- Engineered CSF1R kinase domain mutants (e.g. F807V) — used in CSF1R inhibitor resistance research.

## Known limitations

- CSF1R inhibitors cause anaemia (RBC clearance impairment), bone phenotypes, and cognitive concerns due to off-target microglia depletion.
- Patient response to anti-CSF1R cancer immunotherapy is heterogeneous; biomarkers for response are not established.
- IL-34 vs CSF1 mechanistic difference downstream of CSF1R is incompletely understood.

## Open problems

- Whether selective IL-34 blockers (sparing CSF1) can deplete microglia without disturbing other TRMs.
- The role of CSF1R in CHIP / clonal hematopoiesis-related myeloid malignancy.
- Combination strategies (CSF1R + checkpoint inhibitors) — clinical validation.

## Relevance to active research

CSF1R is upstream of every monocyte-to-macrophage differentiation experiment in vitro (M-CSF in growth medium = CSF1 → CSF1R agonism). For my hypoxia work, CSF1R is the "starting line" — every macrophage I study has been generated by CSF1 signalling. Whether hypoxia modulates CSF1R signalling itself is an open question. Clinically, CSF1R is also a hot cancer-immunotherapy target; understanding its biology is foundational for translating any TAM-modulation strategy.
