## Definition

Calreticulin (CALR) is a multifunctional ER chaperone that, when translocated to the cell surface (ecto-CALR), serves as a pro-phagocytic 'eat-me' signal recognised by phagocyte LRP1. Surface CALR on apoptotic/stressed cells drives their engulfment; on phagocytes, it can be exposed to facilitate target binding. Pharmacological surface-CALR induction is a feature of immunogenic cell death (ICD).

## Intuition

The functional counterpart to the CD47-SIRPα 'don't-eat-me' axis. When CALR is presented on the surface (of a target or of a phagocyte), phagocytosis is favoured.

## Key variants

- ER-resident CALR (canonical chaperone function)
- Surface (ecto-) CALR — pro-phagocytic 'eat-me' signal
- Mutant CALR in myeloproliferative neoplasms (essential thrombocythemia, primary myelofibrosis)

## Known limitations

- Surface vs intracellular CALR cannot be distinguished by scRNA-seq.
- ICD-induced ecto-CALR is transient and context-dependent.

## Open problems

- Mechanism of CALR translocation to the surface in lipid-handling phagocytes.
- Whether ecto-CALR on hepatic TREM2 macs (Hamon 2025) contributes mechanistically to pro-phagocytic clearance of tumor debris.
