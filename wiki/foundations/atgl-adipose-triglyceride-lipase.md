## Definition

ATGL (PNPLA2) is the first and rate-limiting enzyme of adipose lipolysis, hydrolysing triacylglycerol (TAG) to diacylglycerol (DAG) and free fatty acid (FFA). It works upstream of [[foundations/hsl-hormone-sensitive-lipase]] in the lipolytic cascade. Coactivator CGI-58 (ABHD5) is required for full activity; G0S2 inhibits. In mouse cancer cachexia models, ATGL is the primary lipase driving adipose-tissue loss, though human evidence for this primacy is limited.

## Intuition

ATGL is the first cut: triglycerides → diglycerides + free fatty acid. Without ATGL, lipolysis is stalled. In CAC mouse models, knocking out ATGL preserves adipose mass and partially rescues cachexia, motivating it as a candidate therapeutic target — but the human story is less clean.

## Formal notation

- Substrate: triacylglycerol (TAG).
- Coactivator: CGI-58 / ABHD5.
- Inhibitor: G0S2.
- Regulation: transcriptional by PPARγ, fasting; post-translational by CGI-58 binding.

## Key variants

- PNPLA family members (PNPLA3, PNPLA5) with related but distinct substrate specificities.

## Known limitations

- Atgl-null mice die early of cardiomyopathy from triglyceride accumulation.
- Pharmacological ATGL inhibitors are research tools (atglistatin) with mixed pharmacology.
- Human-CAC ATGL evidence does not consistently match mouse-CAC findings.

## Open problems

- Whether ATGL inhibition (or upstream β-AR antagonism) is a viable human CAC therapy without cardiac risk.
- Relative contribution of ATGL vs HSL in human CAC adipose wasting.
