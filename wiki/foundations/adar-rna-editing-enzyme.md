## Definition

ADAR1 catalyses adenosine-to-inosine (A-to-I) editing of double-stranded RNA. By editing endogenous dsRNA (notably inverted Alu/repeat elements), it marks self-RNA so that it is not sensed as viral by the cytosolic RNA sensors MDA5 (IFIH1) and PKR. Loss of ADAR unleashes an interferon-stimulated-gene (ISG) response driven by unedited self-dsRNA.

## Intuition

ADAR is a self/non-self editing switch: it keeps a cell's own double-stranded RNA "invisible" to the antiviral sensing machinery. Remove ADAR and the cell behaves as if virally infected, turning on type-I-interferon and ISG programs — the mechanism exploited to convert tumor-associated macrophages into an immunostimulatory ISG+ state.

## Formal notation

A→I deamination; inosine is read as guanosine by the translation and sensing machinery, so editing is scored as A/G mismatches in RNA-seq relative to genomic DNA.

## Key variants

- ADAR1 p110 (nuclear, constitutive) vs p150 (interferon-inducible, cytoplasmic, Zα domain).
- ADAR2 (ADARB1) — edits coding sites such as GRIA2 Q/R; ADAR3 (ADARB2) is catalytically inactive.

## Known limitations

- Complete ADAR1 loss is developmentally lethal via runaway interferon; effects are dose- and context-dependent.
- Editing-independent functions (protein interactions) complicate clean genetic interpretation.

## Open problems

- Selective pharmacological ADAR1 inhibition without systemic interferon toxicity.
- Whether myeloid-restricted ADAR loss is durably safe as an immunotherapy strategy.

## Relevance to active research

ADAR is the top actionable screen hit for reprogramming TAMs: myeloid-specific `Adar` knockout expands ISG+ TAMs, boosts CXCL10–CXCR3 and CD40–CD40L signalling to CD8+ T cells, slows tumor growth, and enhances anti–PD-1 response. It connects RNA-editing biology to macrophage-targeted immunotherapy.
