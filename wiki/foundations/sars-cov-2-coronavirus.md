## Definition

SARS-CoV-2 is the betacoronavirus responsible for COVID-19. In infected hosts, the virus drives an IFN-β/γ → IDO → kynurenine → AHR axis in alveolar epithelium that produces pathogenic mucin overproduction; pharmacologic AHR antagonism (CH-223191) reduces mucins and ameliorates lung pathology in murine models. AHR is upregulated in nasal swabs and BAL of high-viral-load COVID-19 patients, and CH-223191 suppresses SARS-CoV-2 replication in vitro.

## Intuition

SARS-CoV-2 exploits the host AHR pathway twice: (i) via direct upregulation in respiratory epithelium during high-load infection, and (ii) indirectly via the IFN-IDO-KYN-AHR-mucin axis driving pathology. AHR antagonism is therefore an attractive host-directed therapy.

## Key variants

- Wuhan-Hu-1 ancestral strain
- Alpha, Delta, Omicron lineages and sublineages

## Known limitations

- Continuing emergence of variants with altered antigenicity
- Antiviral resistance to nirmatrelvir/ritonavir possible

## Open problems

- Whether CH-223191 / IK-175 / BAY-2416964 are efficacious for severe COVID-19 in humans
- Spatially-restricted AHR antagonism to epithelium without blocking protective endothelial AHR
