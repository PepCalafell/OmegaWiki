## Definition

IL-32 is a proinflammatory cytokine expressed predominantly by T cells, NK cells, and monocytes, with several splice isoforms (α, β, γ, δ, ε); IL-32-β is a major isoform. Unlike classical cytokines it lacks a canonical signal peptide and has no single well-defined receptor; it acts intracellularly and extracellularly to induce NF-κB and p38 MAPK signaling and the production of TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and chemokines. Notably, IL-32 has no mouse homologue, making it accessible only in human and primate studies.

## Intuition

IL-32 is a human-specific amplifier of myeloid inflammation. In the Human Cytokine Dictionary it emerges as a dominant node connecting T cells to other lineages and as a switch that converts monocytes from an antiviral/Th1-recruiting state to a neutrophil-recruiting, IL-10-family-self-regulating inflammatory state. Its absence in mice is a key reason human and mouse cytokine networks diverge.

## Key variants

- IL-32α, IL-32β (major), IL-32γ (most active), IL-32δ, IL-32ε splice isoforms
- Membrane-associated vs secreted forms

## Known limitations

- No definitive cell-surface receptor identified
- No mouse ortholog → limited in vivo mechanistic models
- Isoform-specific functions incompletely resolved

## Open problems

- Receptor identity and signaling topology
- Isoform-specific roles in disease (SLE, RA, IBD, cancer)
- Therapeutic targetability

## Relevance to active research

Central to the signature finding of the [[human-cytokine-dictionary-dataset]] (IL-32-β myeloid reprogramming). Implicated in SLE flare (NK + non-classical monocytes) via huCIRA. A human-specific axis relevant to tumor myeloid inflammation.
