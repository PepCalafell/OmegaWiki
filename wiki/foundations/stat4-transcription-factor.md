## Definition
A member of the STAT family of latent cytoplasmic transcription factors. STAT4 is activated downstream of IL-12 (and IL-23, type I IFN in some contexts), where receptor-associated JAK kinases phosphorylate it; phospho-STAT4 dimerises, translocates to the nucleus, and drives transcription. Classically described as the master driver of Th1 differentiation and IFN-γ production in T and NK cells.

## Intuition
STAT4 is the canonical "IL-12 → Th1" switch. Its appearance outside the T/NK compartment — e.g. selectively induced in human macrophages by a TNF+PGE2+TLR2-ligand combination — marks an activation program distinct from the IFN-γ (STAT1) and IL-4 (STAT6) macrophage poles.

## Formal notation
- Canonical pathway: IL-12 → IL-12Rβ1/β2 → JAK2/TYK2 → STAT4 phosphorylation (Y693) → homodimer → GAS-element binding
- Signature target: IFNG (interferon gamma)

## Key variants
- STAT4α / STAT4β splice isoforms
- Phospho-STAT4 (pY693) as the active form

## Known limitations
- Most STAT4 biology is mapped in lymphocytes; its transcriptional targets in macrophages are poorly characterised.
- Detection in macrophages requires sensitive protein assays (low baseline expression).

## Open problems
- The macrophage-intrinsic gene program downstream of STAT4 under chronic-inflammatory (TPP-type) stimulation.
- Whether macrophage STAT4 contributes to granulomatous-disease pathology.

## Relevance to active research
Identified by [[papers/transcriptome-based-network-analysis-reveals-spectrum]] as a transcription factor selectively induced in chronic-inflammation (TPP/MTPP) human macrophages, distinguishing them from M1 (STAT1) and M2 (STAT6) states.
