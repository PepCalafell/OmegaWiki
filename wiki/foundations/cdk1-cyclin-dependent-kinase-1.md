## Definition

CDK1 is the master mitotic serine/threonine kinase that, in complex with cyclin B1, drives the G2→M transition and mitotic entry. Its activity peaks at G2/M. Beyond core cell-cycle control, CDK1 phosphorylates numerous substrates, including HIF-1α: CDK1-mediated phosphorylation of HIF-1α at Ser668 during G2/M can stabilize HIF-1α under normoxia by blocking its ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation.

## Intuition

CDK1 is the "mitotic clock" whose transient activation at G2/M can also gate a normoxic pulse of HIF-1α — coupling the cell cycle to metabolic (HIF-driven) reprogramming independently of oxygen levels.

## Formal notation

- Gene: CDK1 (chr10q21.2); UniProt P06493
- Active as CDK1–cyclin B1 holoenzyme; regulated by WEE1/MYT1 inhibitory phosphorylation and CDC25 activating dephosphorylation
- Substrate motif: [S/T]-P-X-[K/R]; phosphorylates HIF-1α Ser668

## Key variants

- Related mitotic/interphase CDKs: CDK2 (with cyclin E, G1/S), CDK4/6 (with cyclin D, G1)
- Partner cyclin: [[cyclin-b1-ccnb1]]

## Known limitations

- Highly pleiotropic kinase; specific substrate effects (e.g. HIF-1α stabilization) are hard to isolate from global mitotic arrest when inhibited
- Chemical inhibitors (e.g. [[ro-3306-cdk1-inhibitor]]) also block cell-cycle progression, confounding synchronization vs direct-substrate effects

## Open problems

- Full catalog of cell-cycle-phase-specific CDK1 substrates linking mitosis to metabolism

## Relevance to active research

Proposed mechanism for cell-cycle-dependent transient normoxic HIF-1α stabilization in HCC cells: normoxic HIF-1α appearance coincides with the CDK1/cyclin B1 peak at G2/M. Relevant to hypoxia-signaling and cell-cycle themes in the vault.
