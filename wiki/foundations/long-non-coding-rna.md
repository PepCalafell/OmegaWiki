## Definition

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are RNA transcripts longer than ~200 nucleotides that do not encode proteins but regulate gene expression. They are one of several classes of functional non-coding genomic elements — alongside promoters, enhancers, and gene-regulatory motifs — that were historically dismissed as "junk DNA" or the "dark matter" of the genome.

## Intuition

After the HGP draft showed only ~20,000 protein-coding genes, attention pivoted to the far larger non-coding portion. Variation in these regions does not change protein sequence, but it can perturb the regulatory networks that govern protein expression — making non-coding elements central to understanding disease.

## Formal notation

Not applicable.

## Key variants

- lncRNAs, microRNAs, and other non-coding RNAs.
- Cis-regulatory elements: promoters, enhancers, silencers, insulators.
- Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and other sequence variants concentrated in non-coding regions.

## Known limitations

- Functional annotation of most non-coding elements is incomplete.
- Establishing causal links between non-coding variation and phenotype remains difficult.

## Open problems

- Mapping the full regulatory network linking non-coding elements to protein expression.

## Relevance to active research

[[wealth-discovery-built-human-genome-project]] reports that discovery of non-protein-coding elements has outstripped protein-coding gene discovery by ~5x and shows no sign of slowing, and that the majority of functional sequences in the genome do not encode proteins.
