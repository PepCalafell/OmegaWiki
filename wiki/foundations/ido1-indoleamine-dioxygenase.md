## Definition

Rate-limiting enzyme of the kynurenine pathway, oxidising L-tryptophan to N-formylkynurenine and ultimately kynurenine. Upregulated in many cancers, in tumour-draining LN DCs, and by IFN-γ in macrophages; depletes tryptophan and produces immunosuppressive AhR-ligand metabolites.

## Intuition

A double-edged sword: by both starving T cells of tryptophan and bathing them in kynurenine (an AhR agonist), IDO1 builds an immunosuppressive corridor around the tumour. Clinical inhibition (epacadostat) was disappointing in phase 3 (ECHO-301), but mechanism remains compelling and new trials with biomarker-selected patients are ongoing.

## Formal notation

L-tryptophan + O2 → N-formyl-kynurenine → kynurenine.

## Key variants

- IDO1 (broadly expressed, IFN-γ-inducible).
- IDO2 (related enzyme, immunological role debated).
- TDO (hepatic, also tumour-expressed in glioma).

## Known limitations

- Phase 3 epacadostat trial (ECHO-301) failed to improve PFS/OS in metastatic melanoma combined with pembrolizumab.
- Functional redundancy with TDO/IDO2.

## Open problems

- Biomarker-driven patient selection for IDO1 inhibition.
- Combination strategies (with chemotherapy, vaccines, alternative ICB partners).

## Relevance to active research

- Connects amino-acid catabolism to AhR-mediated immunosuppression (see [[concepts/ahr-tam-immunosuppression-tumour]]).
- Renewed interest with better patient stratification and combination regimens.
