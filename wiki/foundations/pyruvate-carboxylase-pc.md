## Definition

Pyruvate carboxylase (PC) is a biotin-dependent mitochondrial enzyme that catalyses the ATP-dependent carboxylation of pyruvate to oxaloacetate (OAA), providing the dominant anaplerotic input to the TCA cycle and the principal entry point for gluconeogenic carbon in liver and kidney. Allosteric activation by acetyl-CoA links PC activity to acetyl-CoA availability.

## Intuition

PC is the "fill the cycle" enzyme, complementing PDH which is the "burn pyruvate for acetyl-CoA" enzyme. When PC and PDH are both active, glucose-derived carbon enters the TCA cycle through *both* the M+2 (PDH-derived acetyl-CoA → citrate) and M+3 (PC-derived OAA → citrate) routes — diagnostic of anaplerotic + oxidative co-activation.

## Formal notation

- Reaction: pyruvate + CO2 + ATP → OAA + ADP + Pi.
- Cofactor: biotin (covalent), Mg2+.
- Allosteric activator: acetyl-CoA.
- Tissue distribution: high in liver, kidney, adipose, β-cells; lower but present in muscle.

## Key variants

- Liver PC: gluconeogenesis-driving.
- Adipose PC: lipogenic anaplerosis.
- Muscle PC: under-appreciated in classical muscle biology, but isotope tracing (this paper) shows muscle PC is active in cachexia.

## Known limitations

- Selective PC inhibitors not clinically advanced.
- Compensatory anaplerotic routes (glutamine anaplerosis) make pure PC blockade phenotypically complex.

## Open problems

- Whether selective skeletal muscle PC inhibition could rescue cachexia phenotypes without compromising hepatic gluconeogenesis.
- Mechanistic regulation of muscle PC activity in cachexia (acetyl-CoA pools, biotin availability, PC mRNA induction).

## Relevance to active research

Identified as elevated in cachectic GC muscle in [[papers/multi-omics-profiling-cachexia-targeted-tissues]] via 13C6-glucose tracing (M+3 TCA isotopologues) and [1-13C]-pyruvate tracing in C2C12; INCA flux modelling confirms increased V9 (PC flux) and underlies the muscle glucose hypermetabolism phenotype.
