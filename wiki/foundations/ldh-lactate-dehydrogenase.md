## Definition

Tetrameric oxidoreductase interconverting pyruvate and lactate while regenerating NAD+/NADH. LDHA (M-subunit) favours pyruvate-to-lactate; LDHB (H-subunit) favours lactate-to-pyruvate. Frequently upregulated in aggressive cancers; serum LDH is a clinical correlate of tumour burden and aggressiveness.

## Intuition

Cancer cells running aerobic glycolysis dump excess pyruvate to lactate via LDHA, freeing NAD+ for continued glycolytic flux. The resulting lactate efflux acidifies the TME and supplies a paracrine carbon source for stromal and tumour cells.

## Formal notation

Pyruvate + NADH + H+ ⇌ Lactate + NAD+.

## Key variants

- LDHA-rich tumours: glycolytic, lactate-secreting.
- LDHB-rich tumours: lactate-consuming, OxPhos-leaning.
- LDH inhibitors (small molecules) explored to enhance CAR-T stemness.

## Known limitations

- Pharmacological LDH inhibition is broadly toxic in proliferating tissues.
- Serum LDH is non-specific (haemolysis, organ damage, inflammation).

## Open problems

- Selective inhibition of LDHA in tumours vs immune cells.
- Whether LDH-modulated lactate flux can rewire CAR-T memory formation in patients.

## Relevance to active research

- Biomarker of tumour aggressiveness.
- LDH inhibition enhances stemness and antitumour activity of CAR-T cells.
- Lactate efflux as immunosuppressive cue in the TME.
