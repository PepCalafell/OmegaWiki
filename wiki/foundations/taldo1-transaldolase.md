## Definition

TALDO1 is a key enzyme of the non-oxidative branch of the pentose phosphate pathway (PPP), reversibly transferring a three-carbon unit between sugar phosphates and feeding into NADPH regeneration. NADPH is required for reduced-glutathione synthesis and antioxidant defense. In HSCs, TALDO1 is enriched at the protein level and CRISPR KO reduces long-term hematopoietic potential.

## Intuition

NADPH from the PPP fuels antioxidant systems (glutathione cycle). In HSCs with low oxidative phosphorylation, the PPP becomes a critical source of NADPH; losing TALDO1 disrupts this and reduces stem-cell function — measurable by LTC-IC.

## Formal notation

Reaction: sedoheptulose-7-P + glyceraldehyde-3-P ⇌ erythrose-4-P + fructose-6-P. CRISPR KO in CD34+ BM cells in [[papers/mapping-early-human-blood-cell-differentiation]]: LTC-IC frequency dropped to 1/42 (vs 1/14 AAVS1 control), with minor effects on CFU formation.

## Key variants

- TALDO1 (single gene in humans).
- Related PPP enzymes: G6PD, PGD, TKT.

## Known limitations

- Bulk-level TALDO1 changes are small; the role in HSCs was previously inferred but not interrogated by KO in human cells.

## Open problems

- Whether the TALDO1 phenotype reflects general PPP collapse or a TALDO1-specific function.
- Therapeutic relevance in myeloid disease.

## Relevance to active research

- [[papers/mapping-early-human-blood-cell-differentiation]] — TALDO1 emerges as a functional regulator of long-term human HSPC potential by CRISPR KO.
