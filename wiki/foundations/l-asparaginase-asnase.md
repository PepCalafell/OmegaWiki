## Definition

Bacterial enzyme (from Escherichia coli or Erwinia chrysanthemi) that hydrolyses extracellular L-asparagine to L-aspartate and ammonia, depleting circulating asparagine. Approved for acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL, both T- and B-cell) and NK/T-cell lymphomas, which are auxotrophic for asparagine due to epigenetic silencing of asparagine synthetase (ASNS).

## Intuition

Tumours that cannot synthesise asparagine die when serum asparagine is removed. The treatment is metabolism-as-leverage: a single non-essential amino acid becomes selectively essential because of an acquired biosynthetic defect.

## Formal notation

L-asparagine + H2O → L-aspartate + NH3.

## Key variants

- Native E. coli ASNase (Elspar).
- PEGylated ASNase (Oncaspar) — extended half-life.
- Erwinia ASNase (Erwinaze) — used after E. coli ASNase hypersensitivity.

## Known limitations

- Hypersensitivity, pancreatitis, coagulopathy.
- Resistance via ASNS reactivation.
- Off-target depletion of asparagine in non-tumour tissues.

## Open problems

- Extension to non-haematological malignancies (DLBCL OxPhos subtypes).
- Pharmacodynamic markers of intratumoural asparagine depletion.

## Relevance to active research

- Active rediscovery for OxPhos-dependent DLBCL.
- Paradigm of "metabolic monotherapy" approved in oncology.
