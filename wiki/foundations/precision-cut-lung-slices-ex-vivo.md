## Definition

Precision-cut lung slices are thin (typically 200–500 µm), viable sections cut from fresh lung tissue that preserve the native three-dimensional architecture, cellular composition, and microenvironment. Tumor-derived PCLS (TD-PCLS) are prepared from patient lung cancer specimens and cultured ex vivo, allowing drug treatment of intact human tumor tissue with its resident immune and stromal cells still in place.

## Intuition

A TD-PCLS is a living slice of a real human tumor: it keeps tumor cells, macrophages, and matrix together, so a compound can be tested against the actual microenvironment rather than a purified cell line. This makes it a strong bridge between in vitro cell assays and in vivo relevance.

## Formal notation

Readouts are performed in situ on the cultured slice — proliferation (EdU), apoptosis (TUNEL), tumor-region identification (cytokeratin), immune phenotyping (CD80/CD206), and metabolite imaging (MALDI-MSI) — comparing treated vs vehicle slices from the same specimen.

## Key variants

- Healthy-lung PCLS (physiology/fibrosis modeling).
- Tumor-derived PCLS (TD-PCLS) for oncology drug testing.
- Living human lung slices for ex vivo lung cancer modeling.

## Known limitations

- Short viable culture window limits long-term studies.
- Specimen heterogeneity and limited tissue availability constrain replicate numbers.
- Lacks systemic circulation and recruited immune influx.

## Open problems

- Standardizing viability and readout timing across laboratories.
- Scaling to higher-throughput drug screening.

## Relevance to active research

TD-PCLS from human lung cancer specimens provided the ex vivo translational evidence that 4-octyl itaconate increases tissue itaconate, suppresses tumor-cell proliferation, and enriches anti-tumor (CD80+) TAMs in intact human tumor tissue ([[papers/irg1-itaconate-rewires-macrophage-lung-tumor]]).
