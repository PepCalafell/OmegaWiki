## Definition

MC38 is a chemically induced (1,2-dimethylhydrazine) colon adenocarcinoma cell line derived from C57BL/6 mice. It is one of the most widely used syngeneic preclinical models in cancer immunotherapy, displaying intermediate immunogenicity, microsatellite-instability-like features, response to anti-PD-1 / anti-CTLA-4, and abundant TAM infiltration.

## Intuition

MC38 sits in a "Goldilocks zone": immunogenic enough to respond to checkpoint blockade, suppressive enough that monotherapies leave room for combination benefit. Standard reference model for ICK/TAM-targeting agents.

## Key variants

- MC38 in WT C57BL/6 hosts
- MC38 in hTREM2 (humanised TREM2) hosts — required for testing human-TREM2-specific therapeutics (Park et al. 2021)
- MC38-Ova / MC38-CEA — antigen-tagged variants for TCR/CAR studies
- Companion model: MCA205 fibrosarcoma — orthogonal syngeneic model

## Known limitations

- MSI-like phenotype not representative of MSS colorectal disease (majority of human CRC)
- Cell-line drift between labs (sublines differ in checkpoint sensitivity)
- High intrinsic immunogenicity — translational gap with poorly inflamed human tumors

## Relevance to active research

Primary efficacy model in [[macrophage-targeted-immunocytokine-leverages-myeloid-nk]] (MiTE-144 monotherapy / +αCTLA-4 / +αPD-1 testing in hTREM2 mice). Standard model for TREM2 antagonist studies.
