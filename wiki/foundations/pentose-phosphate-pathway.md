## Definition

The pentose phosphate pathway is a glucose-6-phosphate-consuming pathway parallel to glycolysis, split into an irreversible oxidative branch (G6PD → PGD) that generates NADPH and CO₂, and a reversible non-oxidative branch (transketolase/transaldolase) that interconverts ribose-5-phosphate, ribulose-5-phosphate, xylulose-5-phosphate, sedoheptulose-7-phosphate, and glycolytic intermediates. It supplies ribose-5-phosphate for nucleotide biosynthesis and NADPH for reductive biosynthesis and antioxidant defense.

## Intuition

If glycolysis is the cell's power line, the PPP is its supply depot: it makes the sugar backbone of DNA/RNA and the reducing power that keeps glutathione and lipids topped up. Fast-dividing cells and stressed cells both need this depot, which is why blocking its entry enzyme (G6PD) is anti-proliferative and pro-oxidant.

## Formal notation

Oxidative flux can be tracked by [1,2-¹³C₂]-glucose tracing: M+1 labeling of pentose phosphates reports oxidative-branch flux, whereas M+2 labeling reports non-oxidative/glycolytic routing. The R5P/Ru5P ratio and the lactate M+2/M+1 ratio are used as readouts of oxidative PPP activity.

## Key variants

- Oxidative branch (NADPH-producing, irreversible).
- Non-oxidative branch (ribose-interconverting, reversible; transketolase/transaldolase).
- F-type vs L-type flux modes depending on cellular demand for ribose vs NADPH.

## Known limitations

- Branch fluxes are difficult to resolve without isotope tracing.
- NADPH and ribose demands are coupled but separable, complicating attribution of phenotypes to one output.

## Open problems

- Distinguishing ribose-limitation from NADPH-limitation as the dominant driver of PPP-dependent growth.
- Context-dependent PPP rewiring across tumor genetic backgrounds.

## Relevance to active research

Suppression of oxidative PPP flux is the primary metabolic consequence of IRG1/itaconate and 4-octyl itaconate treatment in lung cancer, linking immunometabolite signaling to nucleotide biosynthesis and redox control ([[papers/irg1-itaconate-rewires-macrophage-lung-tumor]]).
