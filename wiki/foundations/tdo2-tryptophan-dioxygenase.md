## Definition

TDO2 catalyses the first and rate-limiting step of liver tryptophan catabolism — oxidation of L-Trp to N-formylkynurenine, ultimately producing kynurenine (Kyn). Highly expressed in liver and testis at baseline; aberrantly upregulated in glioblastoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, melanoma, and other malignancies, where its product Kyn drives AHR activation and immunosuppression.

## Intuition

Alongside IDO1/IDO2 and IL4I1, TDO2 is one of the four enzymes whose differential expression determines which Trp-derived AHR agonist (Kyn, KynA, indole-3-aldehyde) dominates in a given tumour — and therefore which AHR-pathway therapy is most likely to work.

## Formal notation

- Substrate: L-tryptophan; product: N-formylkynurenine → kynurenine (Kyn).
- Tissue distribution: liver-dominant; testis (high); upregulated in subsets of glioblastoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, melanoma.

## Key variants

- TDO2 vs IDO1: TDO2 prefers L-Trp at high concentrations; IDO1 dominates under IFN-γ induction and low-Trp regimes.

## Known limitations

- TDO2 inhibitors (e.g. LM10) have not yet succeeded clinically.

## Open problems

- Patient stratification by Trp-enzyme expression (TDO2 vs IDO1 vs IL4I1) — likely critical for AHR-pathway trials.
- Combined TDO2 + IDO1 inhibition?
