## Definition

IRF8 is a transcription factor of the interferon regulatory factor family essential for the development and identity of myeloid lineages (monocytes, dendritic cells) and microglia. Together with SPI1/PU.1 it forms composite EICE/ISRE elements that specify microglial gene programs, including *P2ry12*.

## Intuition

IRF8 partners with PU.1 as a lineage-determining factor: where both bind, microglial identity genes are kept open and expressed. Loss of IRF8 (or of accessibility at IRF8/PU.1 composite sites) collapses the homeostatic microglial program.

## Formal notation

- Gene: *IRF8* / *Irf8*. Binds ISRE and, with PU.1, EICE motifs.
- Key target relevant here: the *P2ry12* promoter, whose accessibility at SPI1/IRF8 motifs is lost under hypoxic glioma co-culture.

## Key variants

- Cooperating factor SPI1/PU.1 ([[foundations/spi1-pu1-master-tf]]).

## Known limitations

- IRF8 is pleiotropic across myeloid lineages; effects on a specific gene depend on co-factor availability and chromatin context.

## Open problems

- Whether hypoxia acts on IRF8 activity, abundance, or only on chromatin accessibility at its binding sites in microglia.

## Relevance to active research

Microglial identity TF whose motif marks the hypoxia-closed *P2ry12* promoter peak in [[papers/hypoxic-stress-dysregulates-functions-glioma-associated]], linking hypoxic chromatin remodeling to loss of microglial identity.
