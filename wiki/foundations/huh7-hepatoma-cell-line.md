## Definition

Huh7 is an immortalized, well-differentiated human hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cell line established in 1982 from the liver tumor of a 57-year-old Japanese man. It is one of the most widely used in vitro models of liver cancer and hepatocyte biology, retaining many differentiated hepatic functions (lipoprotein and cholesterol metabolism, albumin secretion). Unlike HeLa (which lacks detectable endogenous HIF-2α), the parental Huh7 line expresses **both HIF-1α and HIF-2α** isoforms.

## Intuition

Think of Huh7 as a "metabolically liver-like" cancer cell: it carries the high biosynthetic/glycolytic potential of hepatocytes, which makes it a sensitive readout for HIF-1-driven metabolic reprogramming (glycolysis, steroid/cholesterol biosynthesis) even under well-oxygenated (normoxic) conditions.

## Formal notation

- Cellosaurus: CVCL_0336; JCRB provenance
- Genotype: p53 mutated (Y220C); expresses HIF-1α and HIF-2α
- Culture: DMEM + 10% FBS; used at passages 4–10 in the source paper

## Key variants

- HepG2 ([[hepg2-hepatoma-cell-line]]) — a second HCC-derived line used for orthogonal validation
- Huh7 HIF1A⁻/⁻ — CRISPR/Cas9 double-nickase knockout derivative with abolished HIF-1α

## Known limitations

- Single-donor, karyotypically abnormal immortalized line; does not capture HCC heterogeneity or the tumor microenvironment
- p53-mutant background; findings should be cross-validated in additional HCC models (as done here with HepG2)

## Open problems

- Extent to which Huh7-specific normoxic HIF-1α dependency generalizes across HCC subtypes and patient tumors

## Relevance to active research

Central in vitro model for the paper's finding that HIF-1α is essential for HCC survival and metabolic maintenance even under normoxia, in a cell-cycle-dependent manner. Relevant to hypoxia and liver-cancer metabolism themes in the vault.
