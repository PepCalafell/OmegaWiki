## Definition

LGALS9 (Galectin-9) is a tandem-repeat β-galactoside-binding lectin secreted by stromal, epithelial, and immune cells. It engages multiple receptors — HAVCR2 (TIM-3), CD44, CD45 (PTPRC) — to regulate T-cell apoptosis, Treg stability, and tumor immune evasion. Distinct from Galectin-3 ([[foundations/galectin-3]]) despite both being β-galactoside binders.

## Key variants

- LGALS9 / HAVCR2 (TIM-3) — induces T-cell apoptosis.
- LGALS9 / CD44 — stabilizes immunosuppressive Treg phenotype.
- LGALS9 / CD45 (PTPRC) — modulates T-cell receptor signaling.
- Anti-Gal-9 antibody therapy — augments intratumoral HAVCR2+ cytotoxic CD8+ T cells.

## Known limitations

- Multiple receptors and context-dependent biology complicate therapeutic targeting.
- Galectin family redundancy.

## Relevance to active research

Identified by [[papers/spatiotemporal-analyses-pan-cancer-single-cell]] as a key signaling axis through which CTHRC1+ ECM-remodeling fibroblasts exclude CD8+ T cells at the tumor leading edge, via LGALS9–CD44 and LGALS9–CD45 interactions. Anti-Gal-9 may synergize with anti-CAF / anti-TGFβ strategies.
